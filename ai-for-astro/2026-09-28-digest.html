Title: Self-Supervised Representation Learning: From Spectral Foundation Models to Auroral Emission Spectra
Authors: Matthieu Le Lain , Gaël Cessateur , Sébastien Lefèvre
arXiv ID: 2609.31206
Score: 9
Reason: This paper is directly about self-supervised spectral representation learning, pretraining a 1D ViT with a masked autoencoder on unlabeled spectra, and even tests transfer from astronomical spectral foundation models. It strongly matches your interests in spectral fitting, foundation models, and JWST spectroscopy analysis.
Short digest: The paper tackles the problem that auroral spectrographs record hundreds of thousands of emission spectra but only a few hundred can be expert-labeled, so most data go unused. The core idea is to pretrain a 1D Vision Transformer with a masked autoencoder, a self-supervised method that hides parts of the input spectrum and trains the model to reconstruct them, forcing it to learn useful internal representations without labels. Without any labels, the learned representation recovers emission-line intensity ratios that physicists use to diagnose precipitating particles, and with a simple linear probe it matches expert-designed features; fine-tuned, it beats the previous supervised classifier. For astronomy, this is highly relevant because JWST and ground-based spectrographs face the same label scarcity, and the paper explicitly tests whether astronomical spectral foundation models (SpectraFM, SpecFormer) transfer, finding that spectral-window overlap matters. A concrete workflow would be pretraining a masked-autoencoder ViT on unlabeled JWST NIRSpec or MIRI spectra and then fine-tuning for redshift estimation, AGN classification, or line-ratio diagnostics. The caveat is that auroral spectra are optical and relatively low-resolution compared to JWST, so you should check whether the architecture and pretraining scale up to noisier, higher-dimensional astronomical spectra.
Full-text inspection checklist: Check the masked-autoencoder architecture details and whether they scale to JWST spectral resolution and noise levels; Look for the transfer results from astronomical spectral foundation models and what spectral-window mismatch implies; Assess the linear-probe and fine-tuning protocols for label efficiency; See whether attribution methods identify physically meaningful spectral features

Title: AutoResearch at Production Scale: Failure Modes and a Multi-Agent Framework
Authors: Aparajith Chandran , Juwon Kim , Saurav Jha , Pablo Castells , Florian Hottier
arXiv ID: 2609.30541
Score: 8
Reason: Directly addresses LLM-driven automated research loops at production scale, including failure modes and scaffolding that transfer to astronomy research agents and automated scientific discovery workflows. The multi-agent framework and long-horizon campaign management are relevant to building reliable LLM agents for JWST/survey analysis pipelines.
Short digest: The paper asks how to make Karpathy's AutoResearch paradigm—an LLM that repeatedly edits a training script and keeps changes that improve a held-out metric—actually work when each iteration costs hours of multi-GPU compute and campaigns run for weeks. The main idea is a three-principle scaffolding called prevent, persist, redirect, which maps five observed failure modes (infrastructure fragility, agent memory decay, search-direction stagnation, iteration-cost asymmetry, and metric fixation) to structural remedies rather than just better prompts. For astronomy, this is directly relevant because automated discovery agents for JWST or Rubin will face the same long-horizon, expensive-iteration regime, and the failure taxonomy is a useful checklist for designing robust research agents. A concrete workflow would be to wrap an LLM agent around an SED-fitting or photometric-redshift pipeline, letting it propose and test configurations while the scaffolding prevents runaway or stagnating searches. The main caveat is that the study is on a book recommendation pipeline, so the specific remedies may need adaptation; also, the reported gains are against hand-tuned baselines, not against strong Bayesian optimization or human experts. Before investing time, check whether the scaffolding is described in enough detail to reimplement, and whether the failure modes are diagnosed with quantitative evidence rather than anecdotes.
Full-text inspection checklist: Look for the concrete definition and implementation of the prevent/persist/redirect scaffolding, and whether it is general enough to port to a non-recommendation domain.; Check how each failure mode is detected and quantified, and whether the diagnostics rely on metrics that would exist in an astronomy pipeline.; Look for the cost accounting: how many GPU-hours per experiment, how the agent decides to retain or discard a modification, and how the held-out metric is protected from overfitting.; Check whether the paper discusses transfer to domains with different data modalities or evaluation criteria, and whether any code or configuration is released.

Title: Epstein Files Engine: Agentic Search for Investigative Journalism
Authors: Duy K. Nguyen , Teresa Mondría Terol , Dylan Freedman , Zach Seward
arXiv ID: 2609.30611
Score: 8
Reason: This is a deployed LLM agent for investigative journalism over a large mixed-media corpus, with citation-rich answers and duplicate detection; it is a strong template for LLM agents doing literature review and document triage in astronomy.
Short digest: The paper describes an AI agent built by The New York Times to investigate a large release of mixed-media documents, translating reporter questions into SQL queries over document, archive, and headline corpora. The agent uses an LLM to plan queries and returns citation-rich answers that a reporter can verify, and more than 100 journalists used it, contributing to at least 20 published stories. A key technical piece is Diff, a text-and-visual duplicate matching method that amplifies novelty signals so the system surfaces genuinely new information rather than repeated content. For astronomy, this maps almost directly onto literature and archive triage: an agent that queries ADS, arXiv, and observatory logs, deduplicates near-identical results, and returns cited answers for a researcher. A concrete workflow could be an agent that searches a large JWST or HST document set, identifies novel claims, and produces a verifiable summary with links to source pages. The caveat is that the paper is a deployment report, not a controlled benchmark, and the duplicate-matching method is tuned to news documents and PDFs, so its behavior on scientific figures and spectra is untested.
Full-text inspection checklist: Look for the query-planning and citation-verification loop, and whether it can be adapted to ADS or arXiv APIs; Check the Diff duplicate-matching method and whether it handles figures, tables, and spectra rather than just text and images; See how reporters interacted with the agent and what failure modes were observed in practice; Check whether the system architecture is described in enough detail to reimplement for a scientific corpus

Title: Structure-Guided Masked Autoencoders for Ultra-High Resolution Scientific Image Understanding
Authors: Enzhi Zhang , Du Wu , Rui Zhong , Cong Ma , Isaac Lyngaas , Amir Koushyar Ziabari , Xiao Wang , Peng Chen , Tao Luo , Toshio Endo , Fumiyoshi Shoji , Kento Sato , Kentaro Uesugi , Takayuki Nonoyama , Ryuji Kiyama , Masahiro Yoshida , Masaru Tezuka , Tetsuya Ishikawa , Satoshi Matsuoka , Masaharu Munetomo , Mohamed Wahib
arXiv ID: 2609.30682
Score: 8
Reason: This paper is about structure-guided masked autoencoders for ultra-high-resolution scientific images, including gigapixel microscopy and X-ray CT. It is highly relevant to JWST and survey image analysis because it addresses self-supervised pretraining on large, structured scientific images with multi-scale morphology.
Short digest: The paper tries to make masked autoencoders practical for gigapixel scientific images, where random masking and uniform tokenization are poorly matched to structured, multi-scale morphology. It proposes SGMA, which combines a content-adaptive quadtree tokenizer that compresses gigapixel images into a fixed-length sequence with a structure-conditioned masking process that biases reconstruction toward spatially informative regions. A Damped Accumulation mechanism aggregates signal-dependent responses across the tree into a structure canvas used to guide masking, keeping the pretraining task compatible with standard ViT encoders. Across electron microscopy, whole-slide optical microscopy, and X-ray CT datasets, SGMA outperforms MAE baselines, achieving 95.68 percent Dice on an 8K x 8K x 28K dataset and 83.21 percent Dice on a 32K squared dataset, with up to 24.8 times inference speedup. For astronomy, this is directly relevant to JWST mosaics, Roman and Euclid survey images, and any gigapixel astronomical image where morphology is hierarchical and spatially structured. A concrete workflow would be pretraining SGMA on unlabeled JWST or HST mosaics, then fine-tuning for source detection, morphology classification, or anomaly detection. The main caveat is that the paper evaluates on microscopy and CT data, so you should check whether the quadtree tokenizer and masking strategy transfer to astronomical backgrounds, point sources, and noise properties.
Full-text inspection checklist: Check whether the quadtree tokenizer can handle astronomical point sources, extended galaxies, and diffuse emission; Look for the structure-conditioned masking details and whether they rely on domain-specific signal statistics that may differ in astronomy; Assess whether the method has been tested on images with high dynamic range or sparse sources like JWST deep fields; Check whether the paper provides code and pretrained models that could be adapted to astronomical datasets

Title: Learning What to Skip: Counterfactual Credit Assignment for Efficient Multi-Agent LLM Workflows
Authors: Jinfeng Xu , Zheyu Chen , Ziyue Peng , Zheng Lin , Shuo Yang , Jinze Li , Zheng Xing , Mengran Li , Victor C. M. Leung
arXiv ID: 2609.30734
Score: 8
Reason: This paper is directly relevant to my interest in LLM agents for scientific workflows, because it studies how to skip unnecessary components in multi-agent LLM pipelines while preserving accuracy. The counterfactual credit-assignment idea could transfer to astronomy research agents that plan, execute, verify, and summarize data-analysis tasks.
Short digest: The paper addresses the problem that multi-agent LLM workflows often execute every planning, execution, verification, and summarization step even when some steps are redundant or harmful. Its main idea is Learning What to Skip, which treats component omission as counterfactual credit assignment: full-workflow logs show the reward of the executed trajectory, while controlled skip interventions reveal what happens if a future step is omitted. The method learns action-specific safety models from these interventions and combines calibration with domain-native guards to decide when to skip. This matters for astronomy because an LLM research agent for JWST or survey analysis could waste tokens and time running redundant checks, or could overwrite a correct intermediate result. A concrete workflow would be an agent that plans a spectral-fitting or source-classification pipeline, executes steps, verifies outputs, and skips a verification or summarization step when the counterfactual safety model says it is safe. The main caveat is that the paper evaluates on math reasoning, multiple-choice QA, and code generation, so I should check whether the skip logic depends on task-specific guards that would need redesign for astronomical data.
Full-text inspection checklist: Look for the exact definition of counterfactual skip interventions and whether they require repeated full-workflow runs; Check whether the safety models are trained per task or can generalize across domains; Look for evidence on shared-error cases and whether agreement among agents is explicitly shown to be insufficient; Assess whether the method could be integrated into an astronomy LLM agent with tools for SED fitting, spectral fitting, or catalog querying

Title: ALF: An Active Learning Framework for Scientific Discovery
Authors: Shikha Surana , Alex Hawkins-Hooker , Olivia Gallup , Christoph Brunken , Jules Tilly , Paul Duckworth
arXiv ID: 2609.31197
Score: 8
Reason: Active learning for scientific discovery directly matches your interest in automated scientific discovery and efficient data acquisition, which is highly relevant for expensive JWST spectroscopy and survey follow-up. The modular offline/online framework could be adapted to astronomical observing or labeling campaigns.
Short digest: The paper addresses the problem that machine learning for science is usually limited by data, and producing high-quality labeled data under budget constraints is expensive. Active learning (AL) is the strategy of letting the model choose which examples to label next, which helps when labels require costly experiments, measurements, or simulations. The authors present ALF, a modular framework with five components that runs the full data-acquisition loop, supporting both offline experimentation against an existing dataset and online deployment against an oracle. For astronomy, this is directly applicable: JWST spectra, follow-up spectroscopy, and expert visual classifications are all expensive labels, so an AL loop could prioritize which targets to observe or label. A concrete workflow would be using ALF to select which candidate little red dots or high-redshift galaxy candidates get scarce spectroscopic follow-up. The caveat to check is whether the framework is general enough to plug into astronomical data loaders and whether the online oracle interface can wrap a telescope or simulation.
Full-text inspection checklist: Check whether the five modular components have clean APIs for custom astronomical datasets and oracles; Look for experiments on real scientific domains versus only standard AL benchmarks; Assess how it handles batch acquisition and budget constraints relevant to telescope time; See if uncertainty estimation and acquisition functions are customizable
