## 1. Beyond the Text: Verifying That Agent-Written Papers Are Backed by Their Artifacts

- arXiv ID: `2609.22111`
- Authors: Qiuhong Shen , Benlong Wu , Hanjin Liu , Yuang Qi , Kejiang Chen
- Score: 9
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22111
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.22111
- Subjects: Computation and Language (cs.CL) ; Multiagent Systems (cs.MA)
- Reason: Directly relevant to automated scientific discovery and research agents, addressing verification of agent-generated research claims against code and experiments. Highly applicable to building trustworthy LLM research agents for astronomy workflows.

Short digest:
The paper tackles the problem that LLM agents can now write research papers, but nobody reliably checks whether the reported results actually match the code and experiments behind them. It introduces ReAgent, an auditing framework that extracts structured scientific claims from a document and then checks the associated repository both statically (does the code implement the claimed methods and configs?) and dynamically (do the experiments actually reproduce the reported numbers?). The core idea is to combine two complementary views, because some inconsistencies only appear when you compare what the text claims against what the code does versus what running the code produces. For astronomy, this matters because as we build LLM agents that run pipelines, do SED fitting, or draft analysis notes, we need automated ways to catch hard-coded values, unimplemented steps, or unsupported claims before trusting outputs. A concrete workflow would be wrapping an agent that processes JWST imaging or spectra with a ReAgent-style audit that verifies each claimed measurement traces back to executable code and real data. The main caveat is that it relies on a curated benchmark of document-repository pairs and assumes the repository is available and runnable, which may not hold for many real astronomy projects.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Check what fraction of claims can be verified when the repository is incomplete or uses non-standard environments
- Look for the benchmark construction details and whether any astronomy or physical-science examples are included
- Examine how dynamic auditing handles stochastic or expensive experiments
- Check whether the audit report format could be adapted to trace astronomical measurement provenance

## 2. ScholarStack: Layered Research Asset Orchestration and Cross-Task Reuse for Scientific Agents

- arXiv ID: `2609.23735`
- Authors: ScholarSeed AI Team : Ao Zhang , Caoqinwei Gong , Guanglei Wang , Haifan Zhang , Hanwei Zhang , Jiayi Sheng , Jihai Zhang , Kai Ying , Liyun Dai , Tingyu Zhu , Wei Chen , Wei Luo , Wenkai Fang , Xiaoyu Qiu , Xue Jiang , Yi Wang , Yuan Cao , Zheng Yu , Wotao Yin
- Score: 9
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23735
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.23735
- Subjects: Artificial Intelligence (cs.AI)
- Reason: This paper directly addresses scientific agents and reusable research assets for literature-based tasks, which is highly relevant to my interest in LLM agents for literature review and automated scientific discovery in astronomy. The layered asset framework and cross-task reuse are exactly the kind of infrastructure that could support astronomy research workflows.

Short digest:
The paper addresses a practical inefficiency in scientific agents: most systems are built around single tasks, so the same papers are repeatedly retrieved, segmented, and interpreted, and understanding gained in one task is not reused in the next. ScholarStack proposes a layered research asset framework that compiles a paper collection into reusable, versioned, provenance-preserving assets at three levels: source-grounded paper-level statements, domain-level organization, and evidence-grounded cross-paper syntheses. A common access interface returns task-specific views at the evidence granularity each task needs, preserving study conditions, source traceability, and verification status. The authors instantiate this on four task families spanning ten settings and find that quality gains concentrate on tasks requiring cross-paper evidence, such as multi-paper question answering and literature review generation, while query-time token cost falls because assets are compiled once and reused. For astronomy, this is directly useful: a research agent that builds a persistent, provenance-tracked knowledge base over JWST and high-redshift literature could support literature reviews, claim checking, and cross-paper synthesis without re-reading the same papers repeatedly. A concrete workflow would be to compile an astronomy paper collection into ScholarStack-style assets and test it on tasks like summarizing evidence for a specific galaxy-population claim or tracking how a measurement has been revised across papers. The main caveat is that the paper is a systems framework, so I would need to check how well it handles the specialized terminology, figures, and tables common in astronomy papers, and whether the asset compilation is reproducible and auditable.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Check how the framework handles figures, tables, and equations, which are central in astronomy papers
- Look for details on provenance tracking and versioning, since reproducibility matters for scientific claims
- Check whether the evaluation includes domain-specific scientific literature or only general benchmarks
- Assess the cost and effort of compiling assets for a large astronomy corpus and whether the interface supports incremental updates

## 3. APEXA: Execution-Integrity Enforcement for Multi-Agent LLM Automation of Synchrotron Data Reduction

- arXiv ID: `2609.24165`
- Authors: Pawan K. Tripathi , Hemant Sharma , Andrew Chuang , Mathew J. Cherukara
- Score: 9
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24165
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.24165
- Subjects: Artificial Intelligence (cs.AI)
- Reason: Presents a deployed multi-agent LLM framework for synchrotron data reduction with execution-integrity enforcement, which is highly relevant to automated scientific discovery and LLM agents for facility-scale data pipelines.

Short digest:
This paper tackles the problem of automating synchrotron data reduction, a multi-step expert bottleneck, using LLM agents. The main AI idea is execution-integrity enforcement: a deterministic tool-layer guard that only surfaces results backed by an actually executed tool call, preventing the agent from fabricating calibration reports. They deploy a multi-agent framework with 61 tools, release a benchmark of facility tasks, and validate on real beamline data. For astronomy, this is directly relevant because JWST and other facilities face similar risks when LLM agents drive calibration or reduction pipelines. A concrete workflow would be applying the same guard and benchmark design to JWST pipeline steps such as detector calibration, spectral extraction, or image combination. The caveat is that the paper is a preliminary deployment report and large-scale agent scoring is left to future work.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Check how the execution-integrity guard is implemented and whether it can wrap existing astronomy pipeline tools
- Look for the benchmark taxonomy separating wasted compute from instrument damage and whether it maps to JWST operations
- Check the real beamline validation details and whether the natural-language prompt interface is reproducible
- Look for released traces or code that could be adapted to an astronomy data-reduction agent

For astronomy, this is directly relevant as a template for building domain-specific LLM assistants that combine retrieval over textbooks, papers, or documentation with structured reasoning. ## 6. Success Leaves Detours: Learning Executable Walkthroughs for Long-Horizon Agents

- arXiv ID: `2609.22120`
- Authors: Kaijie Chen , Chenyu Fang , Liang Yan , Bo Li , Bo Zhang , Peng Ye
- Score: 8
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22120
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.22120
- Subjects: Machine Learning (cs.LG) ; Computation and Language (cs.CL)
- Reason: Directly relevant to long-horizon LLM agents and executable procedure learning, which is central to building astronomy research agents that perform multi-step data analysis. The dependency-grounded walkthrough extraction could help agents reuse successful analysis pipelines.

Short digest:
The paper addresses the problem that self-improving agents learn from past trajectories, but sparse-reward trajectories contain failures, loops, and detours, and simple summaries lose the state conditions and action dependencies needed to actually execute the procedure. It proposes Trace, which detects progress anchors from rewards and persistent state changes, propagates credit to find valuable transitions, and estimates action prerequisites from cross-episode evidence, then uses backward dependency slicing to extract dependency-consistent action chains. The result is executable Walkthrough Memory encoding entry conditions, ordered state-action-effect steps, and completion/failure predicates that support reuse and programmatic verification. For astronomy, this is directly applicable to agents that learn to run JWST reduction pipelines, SED fitting, or catalog cross-matching, because it would let them extract and reuse verified multi-step procedures rather than raw logs. A concrete workflow would be having an agent learn executable walkthroughs for photometric redshift estimation or AGN selection from many past runs. The caveat is that it is tested on J-TTL, WebShop, and ScienceWorld, so transfer to real astronomical software environments needs validation.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Check how progress anchors and persistent state changes are detected in practice
- Look at the backward dependency slicing algorithm and whether it handles long tool chains
- Examine the ScienceWorld results for any physical-science-like task transfer
- Check whether Walkthrough Memory can be verified programmatically in a real software environment

## 8. RS-Claw-Evolution: Environment-Feedback-Driven Evolution for Lightweight Remote Sensing Agents in Long-Horizon Tasks

- arXiv ID: `2609.22258`
- Authors: Kai Ouyang , Dongyang Hou , Liangtian Liu , Zeyuan Wang , Ziyu Li , Chengfu Liu , Zichao Tang , Xuezhi Cui , Shengwu Ouyang , Wentao Yang , Hanwen Yu , Haifeng Li
- Score: 8
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22258
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.22258
- Subjects: Machine Learning (cs.LG) ; Artificial Intelligence (cs.AI); Computer Vision and Pattern Recognition (cs.CV)
- Reason: It develops environment-feedback-driven evolution for lightweight LLM agents on long-horizon remote sensing tasks, which is closely analogous to astronomical survey analysis workflows. The techniques for maintaining long-horizon state, learning from failure trajectories, and credit assignment in tool-use sequences are directly transferable to JWST or Roman data-analysis agents.

Short digest:
The paper tackles the problem that lightweight LLM agents based on compact models struggle with multi-step remote sensing tasks because they lose long-horizon state, underuse environmental feedback, and receive sparse optimization signals. RS-Claw-Evolution improves these agents in three stages: interaction evolution uses executable code to control observations and maintain intermediate states, experience evolution learns from failure-recovery trajectories while masking faulty actions, and decision evolution uses reinforcement learning with multi-dimensional environment rewards and turn-level advantage protection. The result is that a 4B-parameter agent reaches 65.9% accuracy on Earth-Bench, beating a 32B untrained baseline and approaching GPT-5. For astronomy, this is highly relevant because remote sensing agents and astronomical survey agents share the same structure: multi-step tool use over large image and catalog datasets with long horizons and sparse rewards. A concrete workflow could be adapting this framework to build a lightweight agent that plans and executes JWST image reduction, source detection, and catalog cross-matching steps. The caveat is that the domain is remote sensing, so I should check whether the environment rewards and tool interfaces are general enough to be redefined for astronomical data.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Check how the three evolution stages are implemented and whether the environment-feedback interface is domain-agnostic
- Look for the failure-aware trajectory generation and error-turn masking details, and whether they require labeled success/failure signals
- Check whether the reinforcement learning reward design could be adapted to astronomical task metrics
- Look for any discussion of transferring the framework to other geospatial or scientific domains
