## 1. FlowEvo: Self-Evolving Agents through the Co-Evolution of Workflows and Executable Skills

- arXiv ID: `2607.21596`
- Authors: Zeyu Ren , Ling Yue , Ran Li , Yishu Wang , Shengxiang Xu , Hanmo Liu , Shaowu Pan , Shimin Di
- Score: 9
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21596
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.21596
- Subjects: Artificial Intelligence (cs.AI)
- Reason: Directly addresses automated scientific workflow synthesis and skill reuse for LLM agents, which is core to your interest in research agents and automated discovery.

Short digest:
This paper tackles the problem that LLM agents solve complex tasks by constructing workflows, but the useful procedures discovered during execution are transient and not retained for future tasks. The main idea is FlowEvo, a training-free framework that compiles successful execution traces into reusable skill records stored in a skill bank, which can be retrieved and injected into future workflows. For astronomy, this could enable an agent to accumulate and refine analysis pipelines (e.g., for JWST SED fitting or spectral classification) over many sessions without retraining. You could imagine using it to build a personal research agent that learns your preferred data-reduction steps and automatically reuses them on new datasets. A key caveat is that the benchmarks are on interactive environments and code/math generation, so you would need to test whether the skill compilation and retrieval mechanisms transfer well to astronomy-specific tools and data formats.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Check whether the skill records can store astronomy-specific artifacts like Python scripts for SED fitting or SQL queries for catalog cross-matching.
- Look for details on the safety and replay checks during skill admission, which would be critical for ensuring reliability in scientific workflows.
- Examine the skill curation mechanism to see how it detects negative transfer, which is important if a skill learned on one survey (e.g., HST) harms performance on another (e.g., JWST).

For astronomy, SBI is crucial for inferring physical parameters from complex simulations (e.g., galaxy formation, SED fitting). For astronomy, this is directly relevant to building LLM agents that can query astronomical databases, literature, and catalogs to produce verifiable analyses. A concrete workflow would be an agent that retrieves JWST spectra, cross-matches with a knowledge graph of known AGN features, and generates an interpretable classification. Full-text inspection checklist:

- Score: 8
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22375

- Subjects: Artificial Intelligence (cs.AI)
- Reason: Directly addresses automated scientific ideation using LLM agents, which aligns with your interest in research agents and automated scientific discovery. The multi-agent framework for generating diverse, high-quality research ideas could be adapted for generating hypotheses in astronomy.

Short digest:
This paper tackles the problem that LLM-based idea generation systems often produce ideas that are either too similar or low-quality. It introduces IDEAgent, a multi-agent framework that treats research ideation as a Quality-Diversity (QD) search, using one set of agents to refine ideas for quality and another to ensure diversity through memory and comparison. For an astronomer, this could help generate novel research questions or analysis strategies for JWST data, such as exploring different interpretations of little red dots or AGN signatures. You could imagine using it to brainstorm new ways to combine multimodal data (images, spectra, catalogs) for source classification. A key caveat is that the framework is evaluated on computer science topics, so its effectiveness for astronomy-specific ideation would need to be tested.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Check the definition of 'Yield' metric and how quality threshold is set; could this be adapted for astronomy hypothesis evaluation?
- Look for the specific multi-objective feedback mechanisms used for repair and refinement; are they domain-agnostic or require tuning?
- Examine the diversity-promoting sequential memory; does it scale to many ideas or topics?
