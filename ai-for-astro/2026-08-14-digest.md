## 1. Test-Time Optimization of Query Embeddings with Ranking Aware Reward Maximization

- arXiv ID: `2608.12569`
- Authors: Tianyu Chen , Jiaxing Wu
- Score: 7
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12569
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.12569
- Subjects: Information Retrieval (cs.IR)
- Reason: The paper proposes a test-time optimization method for dense retrievers using ranking rewards, which is relevant to retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) and could improve document retrieval for astronomical literature or data. The method is model-agnostic and works with frozen encoders, making it applicable to closed-source models.

Short digest:
The paper addresses the problem of improving dense retrieval performance at test time without updating model weights. The main idea is to distill ranking rewards from a reranker or LLM judge into a lightweight learned vector in the embedding space, optimized purely from scalar scores. This matters for astronomy because it could enhance retrieval of relevant papers or data in RAG systems for literature review or data discovery. A concrete workflow could be using this to improve retrieval of astronomical papers or catalogs in a research agent. A limitation is that it requires access to ranking scores from a judge, which may be costly, and the optimal sharing scope depends on the reward budget.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Check whether the method requires a specific type of reranker or LLM judge, and how the reward budget is defined in practice.
- Look for experiments on retrieval tasks similar to scientific document retrieval, and whether the method generalizes to unseen domains.
- Examine the computational overhead of the optimization and whether it is feasible for large-scale astronomical catalogs.

- Score: 7
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12585

- Reason: The paper proposes a multi-model consensus system for evaluating reasoning traces, which is relevant to LLM agents for research and automated scientific discovery. It could improve the quality of reasoning in astronomy-related LLM applications, such as data analysis or literature review.

Short digest:
The paper addresses the problem of judging the quality of long reasoning traces in LLMs. The main idea is to use a jury of open-weight models with a moderated consensus mechanism to identify reasoning defects more accurately than single frontier models. This matters for astronomy because it could enhance the reliability of LLM-based reasoning in scientific workflows, such as interpreting JWST data. A concrete workflow could be using Reasoning Jury to evaluate reasoning traces from an LLM agent that analyzes astronomical spectra. A limitation is the cost of running multiple models, though it is lower than frontier models.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Check whether the method can be applied to reasoning traces in scientific domains, and if the defect definitions are general.
- Look for details on the deliberation mechanism and how consensus is reached.
- Examine the cost breakdown and whether it is feasible for large-scale use in astronomy.

## 4. Beyond Outcome Rewards: Step-Level Self-Distilled Policy Optimization for Deep Search Agents

- arXiv ID: `2608.12764`
- Authors: Haoze Wu , Chuqiao Kuang , Tianyi Zhuang , Xiaoguang Li
- Score: 7
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12764
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.12764
- Subjects: Machine Learning (cs.LG) ; Artificial Intelligence (cs.AI)
- Reason: Focuses on improving deep search agents via step-level reinforcement learning, directly relevant to building research agents for astronomy that perform multi-step literature or data searches. The method addresses credit assignment in long trajectories, a key challenge for automated discovery.

Short digest:
This paper tackles the problem of sparse reward in deep search agents, where only a single outcome reward is given for long trajectories. The main idea is Step-Level Self-Distilled Policy Optimization (SSPO), which uses evidence anchors (concise web snippets) as privileged information to provide step-level advantage weights during training, applied only to incorrect trajectories. This decouples what to update from how much to update, improving learning efficiency. For astronomy, this could enhance agents that search through catalogs or literature to identify high-redshift galaxies or anomalous sources, by providing finer-grained feedback on intermediate steps. One could imagine training an agent to navigate JWST data archives and extract relevant spectra, with SSPO improving its search strategy. The caveat is that it requires a source of privileged evidence anchors, which may not be readily available for all astronomical tasks.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Check whether evidence anchors are domain-agnostic and can be constructed from astronomical databases.
- Look for details on the overhead and stability of the method on longer trajectories.
- Assess whether the improvement is significant for tasks with noisy or incomplete evidence.

Short digest:

The main idea is ARAC-Bench, a framework with Academic Cognition Skills rubrics and a three-stage diagnostic protocol (Proposal, Experiment, Synthesis). It finds that even the best systems score only 67.9/100, showing a significant gap. For astronomy, this could help evaluate AI agents that propose and test hypotheses about galaxy formation or AGN evolution. One could imagine using ARAC-Bench to assess an agent designed to analyze JWST spectra and propose follow-up observations. The caveat is that the benchmark is general and may need adaptation to astronomy-specific research workflows.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Check whether the rubrics are domain-agnostic or can be customized for astronomy.
- Look for details on how the three stages are decomposed and scored.
- Assess whether the framework can provide actionable feedback for improving research agents.
