Harnessing the Collective Intelligence of AI Agents in the Wild for New Discoveries

Federico Bianchi , Yongchan Kwon , Aneesh Pappu , James Zou

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

This paper presents EinsteinArena, a platform for open distributed research where LLM-based agents collaborate on mathematical problems with verifiers and discussion forums. The key finding is that agents discovered 12 new state-of-the-art results, including improving the kissing number problem lower bound from 593 to 604 through iterative submissions and idea borrowing. For astronomy, this paradigm could be adapted to problems like optimizing photometric redshift estimation, improving SED fitting, or discovering new morphological classes in JWST data. The platform's verifier-based evaluation and agent-to-agent idea sharing could transfer to astronomical workflows where ground truth is available (e.g., simulated data or known classifications). A limitation is that the current focus is on mathematical problems, not astronomical data analysis, so adapting the framework would require defining verifiable astronomical tasks.

Reason

Directly addresses AI agents for scientific discovery with verifiable mathematical problems, highly relevant to automated scientific discovery methods for astronomy.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Look for details on how verifiers are constructed and whether they could be adapted for astronomical tasks like classification accuracy or redshift estimation.
  • Check the discussion forum structure to see if it supports sharing of partial results or failed attempts relevant to astronomy.
  • Inspect the agent interaction logs to understand how ideas are borrowed and whether this could work for multi-wavelength or multi-survey data analysis.
Abstract

Scientific discovery is often a collective process: researchers share partial results, inspect failed attempts, and build on each other's ideas over long time horizons. Recent AI systems have shown that language-model-based agents can make meaningful progress on open scientific problems, but most existing systems operate in isolation. In this paper, we present EinsteinArena, an agent-native platform for open distributed research and discovery. EinsteinArena provides agents with a live set of open problems, each with a solid verifier, public leaderboard, and problem-specific discussion forum where agents can ask questions and share insights. We focus on mathematical tasks that have garnered substantial research interest, where progress can be measured unambiguously. As of May 2026, agents on EinsteinArena have discovered 12 new state-of-the-art results better than any previous human or AI solutions. One notable example is the kissing number problem in dimension 11, where the platform improved the best known lower bound from 593 to 604. This advance did not come from a single agent or isolated run. Rather it arose through a sequence of submissions, public discussion, verifier refinement, and subsequent agent-to-agent borrowing of ideas. These results provide evidence that decentralized scientific discovery can emerge from open interaction among autonomous agents in the wild, demonstrating a new paradigm for collective AI-driven research.

One Token per Multimodal Evidence: Latent Memory for Resource-Constrained QA

Zhi Zheng , Ziqiao Meng , Hao Luan , Wei Liu , Wee Sun Lee

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

This paper tackles the problem of high token consumption in multimodal question answering, which makes retrieval-augmented generation expensive for resource-limited settings. The core idea is to compress each piece of evidence (text or image) into a single high-dimensional latent token using a small compressor model, then retrieve and use these latent tokens directly for answer generation without ever decoding back to raw text or images. For astronomy, this could enable efficient on-board or low-bandwidth analysis of JWST images and spectra, where sending full-resolution data is prohibitive. A concrete workflow would be compressing thousands of galaxy cutouts or spectral snippets into latent tokens, then querying them with natural language questions about morphology, redshift, or AGN features. A key caveat is that the compressor must be trained on domain-specific data (e.g., astronomical images) to preserve scientifically relevant features, and the method's performance on out-of-distribution astronomical data is untested.

Reason

Directly addresses multimodal QA with a latent memory approach that dramatically reduces token consumption, which is critical for resource-constrained astronomy workflows (e.g., on-board or low-bandwidth analysis). The method compresses images and text into single latent tokens, making it highly relevant for JWST/ALMA data where large image volumes are common.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Check whether the compressor model is tested on scientific or astronomical images, or only on natural images/benchmarks.
  • Look for ablation studies showing how the latent token dimension affects reconstruction fidelity and retrieval accuracy.
  • Examine the training objectives: are reconstruction, contrastive, and distillation losses balanced in a way that preserves fine-grained features needed for science (e.g., spectral lines, small morphological details)?
  • Check if the method supports multi-image or multi-spectrum queries, which would be needed for stacking or comparing multiple sources.
Abstract

External memory effectively grounds large language models (LLMs) and vision-language models (VLMs)-based question answering (QA) in relevant multimodal evidence. However, existing memory paradigms represent each memory item in raw text and image forms, so retrieval-based systems must pass the retrieved text or images to the generation LLMs/VLMs, resulting in high token consumption and storage pressure, making it unaffordable for resource-constrained applications. We propose Latent Memory, a latent-space memory paradigm that replaces each raw text or image evidence item with a single high-dimensional latent token produced by a small compressor LLM/VLM. Rather than retrieving raw evidence for generation, Latent Memory operates in a unified latent representation space: the query is embedded into this space to retrieve relevant latent tokens, and the retrieved latent tokens are directly prompted to a pretrained LLM or VLM for answer generation. To make each latent token simultaneously informative for reconstruction, retrieval, and generation, we train the compressor with reconstruction, contrastive, and distillation objectives in a unified end-to-end manner. Latent Memory is evaluated on seven text-only QA benchmarks (e.g., HotpotQA) and multimodal QA benchmarks, where it achieves competitive QA performance compared to advanced RAG baselines while consuming 3x to 10x fewer generator tokens. It can also deliver the strongest image-grounded QA performance on WebQA. Code is available at this https URL .

SPACR: Single-Pass Adaptive Training of Uncertainty-Aware Conformal Regressors

Soundouss Messoudi , Sylvain Rousseau , Sébastien Destercke

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

This paper addresses the problem of producing efficient (narrow) prediction intervals for regression models using conformal prediction. The authors propose SPACR, a method that directly trains uncertainty-aware regressors within a differentiable loss, jointly optimizing efficiency and validity without requiring predefined confidence levels during training. A single SPACR model yields valid prediction intervals at multiple confidence levels during inference. For astronomy, this is highly relevant for tasks like photometric redshift estimation, where accurate uncertainty quantification is critical. One could imagine using SPACR to train a model that predicts galaxy redshifts with well-calibrated confidence intervals, improving over standard conformal prediction methods. The method is general and can be applied to any regression model, including neural networks used in astronomy. However, the paper evaluates on standard regression datasets, not astronomical data, so domain-specific testing is needed.

Reason

Proposes a method for training uncertainty-aware regressors with conformal prediction, directly applicable to astronomical regression tasks like photometric redshift estimation or SED fitting.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Check if the method is evaluated on datasets with heteroscedastic noise, which is common in astronomical measurements.
  • Look for the computational cost of training compared to standard regression training.
  • Assess whether the method can be combined with deep learning architectures used in astronomy (e.g., CNNs for images, transformers for spectra).
Abstract

Conformal Prediction (CP) provides robust uncertainty guarantees for predictive models, but is typically applied post hoc, which misaligns model training with the conformal goal of producing efficient (i.e, narrow) intervals. We propose SPACR (Single-Pass Adaptive Conformal Regressor), a novel method for directly training uncertainty-aware regressors within a differentiable loss. SPACR jointly optimizes efficiency and validity without batch-splitting or a predefined confidence levels during training. As a result, a single SPACR model yields valid prediction intervals at multiple confidence levels during inference, avoiding the costly retraining required by methods like DOICR. Experiments on diverse datasets show that SPACR consistently gives tighter intervals and better coverage-efficiency trade-offs compared to standard CP and DOICR, while significantly reducing computational costs.

Less Context, More Accuracy: A Bi-Temporal Memory Engine for LLM Agents Where a Lean Retrieved Context Beats the Full History

Liuyin Wang

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

This paper addresses the problem of long-term memory for LLM agents, where replaying full history is expensive and less accurate due to distractors. It presents Engram, a dual-process memory engine using a bi-temporal data model: a fast write path appends episodes without LLM involvement, and an asynchronous path extracts atomic facts into a knowledge graph with provenance and supersession chains. A hybrid read path fuses dense, lexical, graph, and recency signals to retrieve a compact context that beats full-history baselines by 10.4 points on LongMemEval_S while using 8x fewer tokens. For astronomy, this could enable research agents that maintain persistent memory across sessions of literature review, data analysis, and coding, remembering past results and reasoning steps without reprocessing entire histories. A concrete workflow would be an agent that reads JWST papers, extracts facts about high-redshift galaxies, and retrieves relevant context when asked to compare results across sessions. A key caveat is that the benchmark uses general knowledge questions, not scientific or multimodal data, and the fact extraction may need domain-specific tuning for astronomical terminology and data types.

Reason

Memory engine for LLM agents that improves accuracy with fewer tokens, directly relevant to building research agents for literature review and data analysis.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Check whether the fact extraction works on scientific text with equations, citations, and domain-specific terms.
  • Look for evaluation on long-form scientific reasoning or multi-session tasks.
  • Examine the hybrid read path: does it support multimodal queries (images + text)?
  • Check the reproducibility harness and whether it can be adapted to custom scientific datasets.
Abstract

Long-term memory is the missing layer for LLM agents: across sessions they forget, and the common workaround -- replaying the whole history into the prompt -- is expensive, slow, and, as distractors accumulate, less accurate. Most memory systems win on cost or latency but still lose to the full-context baseline on accuracy, and benchmark numbers are reported on inconsistent, non-reproducible harnesses, so one system appears at wildly different scores across sources. We present Engram, an open-source, dual-process memory engine on a bi-temporal data model. A fast write path appends lossless episodes with no LLM on the critical path; an asynchronous path extracts atomic (subject, predicate, object) facts, builds a bi-temporal knowledge graph, and resolves contradictions without an LLM call per fact -- invalidating, never deleting, so every fact keeps provenance and a supersession chain. A hybrid read path fuses dense, lexical, graph, and recency/salience signals, applies a point-in-time ("as-of") filter, and assembles a compact, provenance-tagged context. On the full 500-question LongMemEval_S, graded by the official category-specific judge, Engram's lean configuration -- answering from a ~9.6k-token retrieved slice, never the full history -- scores 83.6% vs. 73.2% for full-context (+10.4 points, McNemar p < 10^-6) at ~8x fewer tokens (9.6k vs. 79k), with 0/500 errored. The gain needs a hybrid read path: facts alone lose recall, while facts plus retrieved chunks recover detail. We also contribute a neutral, in-repo evaluation harness with the official judge baked in and the full-context baseline in every table, publish the raw per-question logs, and document the measurement-integrity pitfalls (truncation, home-grown judges, full-history leaks) that silently distort memory benchmarks. Every number ships with a command to reproduce it.

Compile Once, Differentiate Everywhere: A Differentiable Meta-Circular Interpreter

Lucas Sheneman

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

This paper addresses the problem of making arbitrary programs differentiable for gradient-based optimization. It presents a compiler that translates a self-hosting subset of Scheme into differentiable computation graphs, enabling differentiable meta-circular interpretation (DMCI). This allows exact gradients to propagate through programs with closures, recursion, and data structures. The method is used for program-and-parameter co-search, where an LLM proposes program structures and DMCI calibrates their continuous parameters. This is highly relevant to astronomy for tasks like symbolic regression of physical laws, fitting complex models to data, or discovering new relationships in astronomical datasets. A concrete workflow would be using an LLM to propose a model for galaxy SEDs or light curves, then using DMCI to optimize the model's parameters against JWST data. A key caveat is that the method requires programs to be written in a specific Scheme subset, which may limit its adoption by astronomers who use Python.

Reason

Directly relevant to automated scientific discovery: enables gradient-based optimization of arbitrary programs, which could be used for model discovery in astronomy.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Check if the Scheme subset is expressive enough to represent common astronomical models (e.g., power laws, exponentials, SED templates).
  • Look for the computational cost of compiling and differentiating programs: is it feasible for large-scale data analysis?
  • Examine the battery capacity-fade and El Nino examples: do they demonstrate robustness to noise and missing data common in astronomy?
Abstract

The boundary between program execution and gradient-based optimization has long limited the use of code itself as a learnable scientific model. We present a compiler that translates a self-hosting subset of Scheme into differentiable computation graphs for autograd backends. Because the subset can compile its own evaluator, this yields differentiable meta-circular interpretation (DMCI): a compiled Scheme interpreter executes programs supplied as data, while reverse-mode autodiff propagates gradients to continuous constants embedded in those programs. The interpreter is compiled once, so new programs inherit differentiability without recompilation or custom gradient machinery, while retaining closures, recursion, and data structures. We prove that gradients through the compiled interpreter are correct almost everywhere and show that they match direct compilation to numerical precision across 171 recursive and higher-order program-seed pairs. We then use DMCI for program-and-parameter co-search, where a large language model proposes Scheme programs and exact gradients calibrate their continuous parameters through a single frozen interpreter. This enables OpenEvolve-style program search in which an outer loop proposes discrete program structures and DMCI supplies exact gradient-based calibration of each candidate's continuous parameters. On battery capacity-fade data, the search recovers a knee-like degradation structure and improves held-out extrapolation over hand-crafted baselines on the harder early-extrapolation split, matching them on the later split. On a high-dimensional El Nino inverse problem, DMCI optimizes an interpreted Kalman-filter likelihood where gradient-free search fails. These results extend symbolic regression and neurosymbolic search from closed-form expressions to executable, stateful programs, making model-generated code directly optimizable against data.
