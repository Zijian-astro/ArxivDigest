## 1. TRACES: A Benchmark for Epistemic Reliability in Scientific Reasoning by LLMs

- arXiv ID: `2608.11415`
- Authors: Valentin Rodionov , Shamil Assylbekov
- Score: 7
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11415
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.11415
- Subjects: Information Retrieval (cs.IR) ; Artificial Intelligence (cs.AI)
- Reason: Benchmark for epistemic reliability in scientific reasoning by LLMs; directly relevant to using LLM agents for literature review and scientific discovery in astronomy.

Short digest:
The paper introduces TRACES, a benchmark with 42 retracted or pseudoscientific papers, to test whether LLMs reject flawed premises in scientific reasoning. It finds that models engage with untenable premises in 95% of responses, indicating topic-keyed safety rather than robust epistemic competence. For astronomy, this is crucial for deploying LLM agents to review literature or design studies, as they may accept unreliable sources. The benchmark methodology could be adapted to astronomical pseudoscience or flawed claims.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Check if the probe corpus includes any astronomy-related papers.
- Look for the scoring methodology (IFR-a, IFR-i, EDI) and how to apply it.
- See if the benchmark is publicly available and extensible to new domains.

## 2. Beyond Single-Turn Confidence: Trajectory-Adapted Uncertainty Quantification for LLM Agents

- arXiv ID: `2608.11552`
- Authors: Dylan Bouchard , Mohit Singh Chauhan
- Score: 6
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11552
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.11552
- Subjects: Computation and Language (cs.CL) ; Artificial Intelligence (cs.AI); Machine Learning (cs.LG)
- Reason: Uncertainty quantification for LLM agents, which is relevant to research agents for scientific workflows.

Short digest:
This paper studies how single-turn uncertainty quantification methods transfer to LLM agents with interactive trajectories. It finds that token-probability scores are sensitive to aggregators, reflexive scores are good baselines, and self-consistency is often strongest. For astronomy, this could help build reliable research agents that flag uncertain results. A workflow might involve using it to assess confidence in automated literature reviews or data analysis. The main caveat is that it is tested on tool-use datasets, not scientific ones.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Check if the uncertainty methods could be applied to agent trajectories in scientific tasks
- Look for any guidance on aggregator choice that could be useful for multi-step analyses

## 5. Generation as Auxiliary Supervision: Enhancing Visual Understanding at Zero Inference Overhead via Decoupled Embedding Prediction

- arXiv ID: `2608.12209`
- Authors: Zhongbin Guo , Jiahao Xie , Dongling Xiao , Qianle Wang , Ruiqi Lu , Xiaomin He , Wanxuan Sun , Cheng Yang
- Score: 5
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12209
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.12209
- Subjects: Computer Vision and Pattern Recognition (cs.CV)
- Reason: The paper explores using generation as auxiliary supervision to improve visual understanding in multimodal LLMs, which is directly relevant to the interest in multimodal models for astronomy.

Short digest:
The paper proposes GAS, a framework that uses visual generation as auxiliary supervision to enhance visual understanding in MLLMs without inference overhead. It uses next embedding prediction and a decoupled mixture-of-transformers architecture. This is relevant to astronomy because it could improve multimodal models that combine images and text for tasks like source classification or description, and the zero-inference-overhead aspect is attractive for large surveys.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Check if the generation tasks are generalizable to astronomical images and captions.
- Look for any analysis on which visual understanding tasks benefit most, to see if they align with astronomical needs.
