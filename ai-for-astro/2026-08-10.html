ADIAS: Automated Design of Interactive Agentic Systems

Lekang Jiang , Bohan Tang , Stephan Goetz , Yiwen Guo

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

This paper addresses the inefficiency of current automated agent design methods, which organize repair progress around candidate agents rather than explicit issue states. The main idea is to maintain a persistent issue state that tracks repair progress and guides optimization, leading to more focused and effective modifications. This matters for astronomy because it could enable more robust and efficient AI agents for tasks like literature review, data analysis, and automated hypothesis testing. I could imagine applying this to build a research agent that iteratively improves its analysis of JWST data or its literature search strategy. However, the framework is evaluated on interactive benchmarks, so I should check if it scales to the long-horizon, complex workflows typical in astronomical research.

Reason

The paper introduces a framework for automated agent design, which is directly relevant to my interest in research agents and automated scientific discovery. The issue-centric optimization approach could improve the efficiency of AI-driven workflows for astronomy.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Check whether the persistent issue state mechanism is generalizable to domains beyond the tested benchmarks
  • Look for details on how the framework handles sparse or noisy feedback, which is common in scientific workflows
  • Assess the computational overhead of maintaining the issue state for large-scale agent systems
Abstract

Automated agent design improves agent harnesses through iterative revision, evaluation, and feedback summarization. Existing methods are largely candidate-centric: cross-round experience is organized around candidate agents, which leaves the repair progress implicit. This causes inefficient repair targeting, slow consolidation of partial progress, and propagation of ineffective interventions across rounds. Therefore, we formulate issue-centric agent optimization, in which repair progress is carried forward as an explicit persistent issue state to guide optimization, rather than re-derived from candidate history in each round. We instantiate the formulation in ADIAS, a framework for automated full-code agent design with two mechanisms. A persistent issue state maintains stable issue identities, lifecycle status, supporting evidence, and intervention-outcome histories. Issue-guided optimization uses this state to jointly propose repair targets and revision directions for subsequent focused full-code modification. Across five interactive benchmarks, ADIAS outperforms the strongest baseline by 25.2% on average and achieves consistent gains across four backbone models. Controlled ablations further show that removing persistent issue state or replacing issue-centric revision with candidate-centric policies leads to performance drops of up to 40.7%.

Science Edge Evaluation: SEE the Missing Step Toward Real Scientific Discovery

Taolin Han , Yuchen Zhang , Jinghang Wang , Yun Wu , Wai Yuet Chiu , Zhaohai Li , Yifei Zhang , Jinxin Wang , Yuhao Zhou , Chen Zhao , Jiajia Li , Jiaxin Li , Qile Jin , Kewei Sun , Shuang Wu , Weiqi Zhai , Renquan Lv , Junchao Li , Ruodan Chen , Qingteng Chen , Zhibo Yang , Hu Wei , Lin Qu , Shuai Bai , Bing Zhao

  • Look for any analysis of the types of reasoning errors made by the models.

Abstract

Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly involved in scientific discovery, yet it remains unclear whether they can support complex real laboratory science. Here we introduce Science Edge Evaluation (SEE), a multimodal benchmark of expert-curated questions grounded in peer-reviewed literature and experimental practice in chemistry, biology, and materials science. Evaluation of 19 multimodal large language models (MLLMs) shows that even the best-performing model reaches only 48.7% accuracy. Moreover, general-purpose models outperform science-specialized models on average. In the visual-agent evaluation, the use of tools increases the best accuracy to 52.7%. Tool use can expand the information available to models, but more information does not necessarily lead to reliable scientific reasoning. The key challenge is whether models can manage tool-derived information within the boundaries of the original experimental evidence. Together, these findings reveal that current MLLMs still cannot reliably make justified and evidence-bounded inferences from experimental results, which is an essential capability in real scientific discovery. Bridging this gap requires MLLMs to transition from explaining established scientific concepts to deriving novel and evidence-based insights from experimental data.

EMAS: Stabilizing Multi-Agent System Evolution through Evidence-Guided Revision

Chao Fei , Qingyi Si , Kaihua Liang , Yanghua Xiao , Panos Kalnis , Hongcheng Guo

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

The paper tackles the problem of static multi-agent systems that do not learn from experience during deployment. It introduces EMAS, which converts execution traces into structured diagnoses and only revises the system topology or prompts when the same issue recurs, validating each change against the current version. This matters for astronomy because research agents analyzing JWST data could similarly adapt their pipelines based on repeated failures, improving accuracy and reducing token costs. A concrete workflow would be an agent system that iteratively refines its data-reduction or classification steps based on feedback from validation metrics. Before investing, check whether the benchmarks and tasks are similar enough to astronomy workflows, and whether the validation criteria are practical for your use case.

Reason

The paper directly addresses automated multi-agent system design and evolution, which is relevant to LLM agents for scientific workflows. Its evidence-guided revision method could transfer to improving research agents for astronomy.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Check whether the method requires a validation set with ground truth, which may be hard to obtain for astronomy tasks.
  • Look for details on how diagnoses are structured and whether they are interpretable for domain-specific errors.
  • See if the token cost reduction is quantified in a way that transfers to large-scale survey data processing.
Abstract

Many methods for automated multi-agent system design optimize prompts and topologies during an initial design stage and then deploy the resulting system unchanged on subsequent samples. Experience from these samples is rarely consolidated into reusable system updates, while accuracy-oriented designs may incur high token costs. We introduce EMAS (Evolving Multi-Agent System), which uses this experience to revise MAS topology and prompts without updating LLM parameters, either to improve accuracy or to reduce cost. EMAS converts traces into structured diagnoses that specify a revision operation and target. It generates a candidate revision only when the same diagnosis recurs across samples and applies it only if paired validation against the current MAS meets the corresponding acceptance criterion. Across four benchmarks and two LLMs, EMAS attains the highest task-weighted overall accuracy for both backbones and is best or tied in six of eight model--benchmark settings. Within two evolution epochs, EMAS achieves relative gains of 6.30% and 20.10% in task-weighted accuracy on Kimi-K2-6 and Qwen3.6-27B, respectively. On MBPP with Qwen3.6-27B, EMAS raises accuracy from 55.09% to 89.12% while reducing token use per task by 62.2%. These results show that EMAS can turn experience from new samples into reusable updates to MAS topology and prompts.

Fast LapSum: Exact Differentiable Top-k at Million Scale

Łukasz Struski , Joanna Wojciechowicz , Jakub Antczak , Marcin Mazur , Kamil Książek , Jacek Tabor

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

This paper solves the problem of making the top-k operation differentiable and scalable to millions of elements, which is a bottleneck in sparse computation. The main idea is a linear-time solver that preserves an exact selection mass of k while being fully differentiable, with a probabilistic bracketing trick for extreme scales. For astronomy, this could enable training models that use sparse attention or routing on large datasets, such as processing millions of galaxy spectra or images. A concrete workflow might be using this in a transformer model for source classification or anomaly detection in survey data. However, the paper demonstrates on adversarial examples and image coding, so its utility for astronomical tasks needs validation.

Reason

Introduces a differentiable top-k operation that could be useful for sparse attention or routing in models applied to large astronomical datasets.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Check if the method works with typical deep learning frameworks like PyTorch and is easy to integrate.
  • Look for any numerical stability issues when gradients are propagated through the top-k operation.
  • Assess the memory footprint of the solver for very large inputs, as astronomical datasets can be huge.
Abstract

The top-$k$ operation is a fundamental building block of modern sparse computation, enabling token routing, expert activation, memory selection, and attention pruning. Yet standard hard top-$k$ blocks gradients, while existing continuous (soft) relaxations remain too costly for large-scale models. We introduce Fast LapSum, an exact-budget soft top-$k$ primitive whose GPU solver runs in linear time after sorting. Unlike prior linear-time methods such as DFTopK, which relax the normalization constraint, Fast LapSum is, to our knowledge, the first method to preserve an exact selection mass of $k$ while remaining fully differentiable end-to-end. Our solver combines a linear-time threshold computation with an analytical vector--Jacobian product, and for extreme scales employs probabilistic bracketing to sort only the uncertain middle band of kernel-noised scores. The resulting overhead is almost negligible: the solver processes $10^6$, $10^7$, and $10^8$ scores in $0.41$, $1.15$, and $5.23$\,ms, respectively. This makes exact soft top-$k$ practical for sparse routing, retrieval, and large-scale optimization. We demonstrate Fast LapSum on two demanding applications operating over millions of coordinates inside the training loop: generating megapixel sparse adversarial examples with an exact soft budget of ${\sim}0.02\%$ of an image's pixels, achieving an order-of-magnitude speedup over state-of-the-art methods, and training a fully differentiable sparse image coder from scratch.

Long-Horizon Agent Trajectory Attribution: A Unified Benchmark and Fine-Grained Annotation Framework

Jing Chen , Yang Sun , Li Zhang , Lin Xu , Jie Shi

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

This paper addresses the problem of attributing outcomes in long-horizon LLM agent trajectories to specific components like instructions, tool use, or memory. The main idea is a unified benchmark and annotation framework that labels the primary attribution component and chains of actions, with reference baselines for evaluation. For astronomy, this could help in building and debugging research agents that perform literature reviews or data analysis, by understanding which steps lead to success or failure. A concrete workflow might be using this framework to evaluate an agent that searches and analyzes JWST data. However, the paper is not astronomy-specific and requires adaptation to scientific workflows.

Reason

Focuses on LLM agent trajectory attribution, which could inform debugging and understanding of research agents in astronomy.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Check if the benchmark includes trajectories with tool use that are similar to scientific data analysis tools.
  • Look for any analysis of attribution in the presence of noisy or ambiguous observations.
  • Assess whether the annotation framework can be applied to custom agent trajectories in astronomy.
Abstract

Large language model (LLM) agents increasingly operate through long-horizon trajectories involving user instructions, tool use, external observations, and memory. Existing benchmarks primarily evaluate behavioral outcomes but provide limited support for fine-grained attribution analysis. We introduce trajectory attribution and develop a benchmark and annotation framework for this task. The benchmark organizes heterogeneous trajectories under a unified component schema and provides annotations of the primary attribution component, together with attack and execution chains where applicable. Instantiating the benchmark with trajectories from AgentDojo and the Stage and Canary settings of Agent3Sigma yields more than 1,300 annotated trajectories covering task-aligned actions, unsafe actions, and safety refusals. The benchmark defines two evaluation tasks, primary attribution localization and attribution-chain recovery, and provides reference baselines based on incremental trajectory contribution and component-level leave-one-out perturbation. It captures diverse attribution settings, including local and long-range attribution as well as structured attribution chains. Reference baseline results exhibit substantial performance differences across these settings, providing an initial characterization of the benchmark's attribution challenges. Beyond this initial instantiation, we release a reusable annotation skill that enables trajectories generated by new agent models to be standardized, annotated, and evaluated under the same framework.
