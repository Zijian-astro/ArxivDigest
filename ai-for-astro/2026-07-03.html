IsoSci: A Benchmark of Isomorphic Cross-Domain Science Problems for Evaluating Reasoning versus Knowledge Retrieval in LLMs

Samir Abdaljalil , Erchin Serpedin , Hasan Kurban

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

This paper asks whether chain-of-thought reasoning truly improves problem-solving or just relies on domain knowledge. It introduces ISOSCI, a benchmark of science problems that share the same logical structure but require different domain knowledge. The key finding is that 91.3% of reasoning gains are knowledge-dependent, not structure-invariant, challenging the assumption that reasoning alone helps. For astronomy, this means an LLM agent that appears to 'reason' about a JWST spectrum may actually just be retrieving memorized facts. Before using such an agent for source classification or SED fitting, you should verify it can handle novel logic, not just recall. A caveat is that the benchmark covers short-horizon procedural problems, not the open-ended synthesis needed for discovery.

Reason

Directly evaluates LLM reasoning vs. knowledge retrieval, which is central to building reliable scientific agents for astronomy workflows.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Check whether the benchmark includes any astronomy or physics problems, or only chemistry/biology.
  • Look for the specific problem pairs to see if they resemble tasks like photometric redshift estimation vs. spectral line identification.
  • Examine how they define 'reasoning-mode gains' and whether the metric applies to multi-step agentic workflows.
Abstract

We introduce ISOSCI, a benchmark of isomorphic cross-domain science problem pairs that separates reasoning ability from domain knowledge retrieval in LLM evaluation. Each pair shares identical logical structure but requires different domain-specific knowledge, enabling controlled attribution of reasoning-mode gains. Across five model pairs spanning four model families, we find that 91.3% of reasoning-mode gains are knowledge-dependent rather than structure-invariant (63/69 gains; Wilson 95% CI [82.3%, 96.0%]), directly challenging the assumption that chain-of-thought reasoning improves short-horizon procedural scientific problem-solving. Reasoning toggles on highly capable models provide less than 5 percentage points accuracy gain across all domains, and a reasoning-specialized model (o3-mini) that outperforms its standard counterpart on GPQA Diamond (+19.2 percentage points) underperforms on ISOSCI (-24.7 percentage points), showing that benchmark choice determines conclusions about reasoning utility. Grounded autonomous research: a fault-tolerant LLM pipeline from corpus to manuscript in frontier computational physics

Haonan Huang

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

This paper asks whether an LLM-based agent can autonomously conduct novel research in frontier computational physics, from literature review to manuscript generation, without hallucinating unverifiable results. The author presents a pipeline that processes 11,083 condensed-matter physics papers, conceives a research direction, reproduces published references, performs novel first-principles computations, and writes a publication-grade manuscript, all grounded in literature through 2,162 consultation events. This matters for astronomy because it provides a blueprint for autonomous research agents that could be applied to JWST data analysis, high-redshift galaxy studies, or literature review in astrophysics. The fault-tolerant design (redundancy, adversarial review, calibration checkpoints) is directly relevant to building reliable scientific discovery tools. A concrete workflow could be adapting this pipeline to analyze JWST spectra of little red dots or to autonomously search for AGN signatures in large survey catalogs. Caveats: the pipeline is demonstrated in condensed-matter physics, not astronomy, and requires significant domain-specific engineering for toolchains and calibration references.

Reason

This paper directly addresses autonomous scientific discovery using LLMs in a physics domain, with a fault-tolerant pipeline that could be adapted for astronomy. It demonstrates end-to-end automation from literature review to manuscript generation.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Look for the specific fault-tolerance mechanisms: fresh-context isolation, distributed grounding, adversarial review, and how they catch hallucinations.
  • Check the calibration checkpoint design: how does the agent reproduce published references and what constitutes a 'reproduction failure'?
  • Examine the corpus construction and literature consultation strategy: is it generalizable to astronomy literature (e.g., arXiv astro-ph)?
  • Assess the human intervention pattern: what fraction of the pipeline is autonomous vs. requiring bounded human input?
Abstract

Autonomous-research agents have demonstrated end-to-end LLM automation in machine-learning sandboxes where execution provides calibration. Frontier physical science differs categorically: physical reasoning underlies every methodology choice, toolchains are often underdocumented, and calibration must come from external literature anchors - which unscaffolded agents cite but do not confront, hallucinating plausible, unverifiable results from internal priors. We present a pipeline that runs end-to-end from a corpus of 11,083 recent condensed-matter physics arXiv papers to a publication-grade manuscript with three substantive physics findings (here on altermagnetic piezomagnetism): the agent autonomously conceives a research direction by mapping the corpus, calibrates methodology by reproducing published references, conducts novel first-principles computations, and writes the manuscript - grounded in literature throughout, across 47 fresh-context sessions in six phases sharing only on-disk state, with 2,162 literature-consultation events. Fault tolerance emerges from redundancy: fresh-context isolation, distributed grounding, and adversarial review catch what any single session misses; pre- and post-pilot stages are fully autonomous, and pilot requires bounded human intervention only at reproduction failures - operational knowledge curation, not scientific direction. Two paired failure modes - a pre-architecture baseline and a no-pilot ablation - isolate structurally enforced numerical confrontation at calibration checkpoints as the operative grounding mechanism. The primitives, characterized failure modes, and quantified intervention pattern lay a foundation for autonomous research in high-stakes scientific domains beyond computational physics.

PairCoder++: Pair Programming as a Universal Paradigm for Verified Code-Driven Multimodal and Structured-Artifact Generation

Junhao Chen , Xiang Li , Mingjin Chen , Boran Zhang , Henghaofan Zhang , Yibin Xu , Yuehan Cui , Fangsheng Weng , Fei Ma , Qi Tian , Ruqi Huang , Hao Zhao

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

This paper addresses the problem of LLMs generating incorrect or brittle code for structured artifacts (e.g., scientific figures, 3D scenes) by introducing a pair programming framework. Two agents (Driver and Navigator) collaborate: the Driver writes code, the Navigator reviews it against toolchain feedback (compilation, rendering), and they switch roles on errors. For astronomy, this could be used to automatically generate publication-quality figures from data, create 3D visualizations of galaxy fields, or produce reproducible analysis scripts. The method improves artifact quality across 17 benchmarks, including TikZ figures and Blender scenes. A limitation is the increased computational cost (2.9-9.2x single model cost), which may be acceptable for high-stakes scientific outputs.

Reason

Directly relevant to automated scientific discovery: uses LLM agents to generate verifiable code-driven artifacts (charts, figures, 3D scenes) with a review loop, which could be applied to astronomical data visualization and analysis.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Look for examples of scientific figure generation and whether the method handles domain-specific plotting libraries (e.g., matplotlib, astropy).
  • Check if the verification step can be adapted to astronomical toolchains (e.g., running SExtractor, comparing with catalog data).
  • Assess whether the role-switching mechanism is robust for long, multi-step analysis workflows.
Abstract

Code is the medium through which large language models generate structured artifacts: charts, scientific figures, vector graphics, CAD models, 3D scenes, and hardware designs are all produced by writing programs. In this regime single pass inference is brittle, because the compiler, renderer, or simulator that decides whether the artifact exists is invisible to the model. We present PairCoder, which grounds review in the toolchain and realizes it as two agent pair programming: a Driver agent writes the program, a Navigator agent reviews it against verification evidence (diagnostics, execution results, and renderings of the current artifact beside the target), and the two switch roles when errors persist. Across 17 public benchmarks and seven models from three vendors, PairCoder improves essentially every benchmark whose artifact is verifiable, on full official metric suites rather than execution alone (for example, Blender scene executability 0.20 to 0.78; TikZ compile rate up 10 to 30 points on every model), at 2.9 to 9.2 times single model cost (about 7 times overall). The improvements concentrate where the toolchain provides an informative oracle and the baseline leaves headroom, and the method ties or mildly regresses where the oracle is weak; we frame pair programming as a reliable recipe for verified code driven generation.

Hidden Forgetting in Continual Multimodal Learning: When Accuracy Survives but Grounding Fails

Qianyu Chen , Canran Xiao , Runxuan Tang

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

This paper asks whether continually adapted multimodal LLMs preserve not only answer accuracy but also how they use visual, textual, and other evidence channels. It identifies a failure mode called 'hidden evidence-use forgetting' where accuracy stays high but the model shifts to less grounded evidence. The authors propose RCL, a replay-free framework that freezes a previous checkpoint as a behavioral reference and uses counterfactual interventions to estimate and preserve evidence-reliance profiles. For astronomy, this is relevant because a model fine-tuned on new JWST data might still give correct classifications but silently stop using crucial spectral or morphological evidence. You could imagine using RCL to monitor whether a model fine-tuned on new galaxy types still properly attends to SED features versus relying on spurious correlations. A key caveat is that the method assumes access to a frozen prior checkpoint and requires counterfactual interventions, which may be computationally expensive for large models.

Reason

Directly studies continual learning for multimodal LLMs, which is relevant to adapting models to evolving astronomical tasks and datasets. The focus on preserving evidence grounding (not just accuracy) is critical for scientific applications where interpretability matters.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Look for the definition of 'evidence-reliance profiles' and how counterfactual channel interventions are implemented in practice.
  • Check whether the method is demonstrated on multimodal data types similar to astronomy (e.g., images+text, charts) or only on standard benchmarks.
  • Examine the computational cost of the reliance-preservation loss and whether it scales to large models or large astronomical datasets.
Abstract

Multimodal large language models must continually adapt to evolving tasks and domains, yet standard continual learning metrics mainly measure whether old answers remain correct, leaving the stability of multimodal grounding largely unexamined. We study this overlooked failure mode and ask whether a continually adapted MLLM can preserve not only what it answers, but also how it uses visual, textual, OCR, chart, and document evidence. We identify \emph{hidden evidence-use forgetting}, where answer accuracy is retained while the model silently shifts toward different or less grounded evidence channels, and propose \textsc{RCL}, a replay-free reliance-constrained continual learning framework. \textsc{RCL} freezes the previous checkpoint as a behavioral reference, estimates teacher and student evidence-reliance profiles through counterfactual channel interventions, and jointly optimizes task learning, prediction preservation, and reliance preservation without adding inference-time cost. Across CoIN, COAST, MCITlib, and an evidence-sensitive multimodal stream, \textsc{RCL} consistently improves final performance and reduces forgetting over replay-free, PEFT, routing, and memory-assisted baselines, while substantially lowering modality reliance drift, dominant evidence flips, and hidden forgetting rates. These results suggest that robust continual multimodal learning requires preserving the evidence path behind correct answers, not merely the answers themselves.

UA-ChatDev: Uncertainty-Aware Multi-Agent Collaboration for Reliable Software Development

Temitayo Olamilekan Ogunsusi , Lijun Qian , Xishuang Dong

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

This paper addresses the problem of hallucination propagation in multi-agent LLM frameworks for software development. It introduces UA-ChatDev, which integrates uncertainty quantification (based on token-level log probabilities) to trigger retrieval-based verification when agent responses are uncertain. For astronomy, this framework could be adapted to build reliable research agents that perform multi-step analysis tasks (e.g., querying databases, running code, interpreting results) with built-in verification. A concrete workflow would be a multi-agent system for automated SED fitting, where one agent selects models, another runs the fit, and a third checks for convergence and flags uncertain results. However, the framework is designed for software development, not scientific analysis, and would require significant adaptation.

Reason

This paper proposes an uncertainty-aware multi-agent framework for software development, which directly addresses the problem of hallucination propagation in LLM-based agents, a key challenge for scientific research agents.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Check if the uncertainty estimation mechanism (token-level log probabilities) is model-agnostic and could be applied to any LLM.
  • Look for details on the 'phase-aware threshold calibration' and how thresholds are set for different tasks.
  • Examine the SRDD benchmark tasks to see if any are analogous to scientific workflows (e.g., data analysis, report generation).
Abstract

Software development is a complex task that demands cooperation among agents with diverse roles. Large language models (LLMs) have enabled autonomous multi-agent software development frameworks that leverage role-based collaboration to automate requirements analysis, coding, testing, and refinement. However, existing approaches typically assume that intermediate agent outputs are equally reliable, leaving them vulnerable to hallucination propagation, where incorrect decisions generated in early development phases are transferred to downstream agents and negatively impact final software quality. To address this challenge, we propose UA-ChatDev, an uncertainty-aware multi-agent software development framework that integrates uncertainty quantification into agent interactions. It introduces a lightweight uncertainty estimation mechanism based on token-level log probabilities to assess the confidence of agent responses and employs phase-aware threshold calibration to selectively trigger retrieval-based verification when uncertainty exceeds acceptable levels. Extensive experiments on the SRDD benchmark demonstrate that UA-ChatDev consistently outperforms existing single-agent and multi-agent software development frameworks across completeness, executability, consistency, and overall quality metrics. Further ablation studies and communication analyses verify that uncertainty-aware interactions enhance code execution reliability.

An Optimisation Framework for the Well-Conditioned Training of Physics-Informed Neural Networks

Joseph Webb , Sadok Jerad , Coralia Cartis

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

This paper addresses the ill-conditioned optimization problem in physics-informed neural networks (PINNs) that limits their accuracy. It introduces DSGNAR, a doubly-sketched Gauss-Newton method with adaptive regularization, achieving relative errors as low as 3e-16 on benchmark PDEs. For astronomy, this could enable highly accurate PINN-based solutions to astrophysical PDEs, such as the Poisson equation for gravitational potential or the radiative transfer equation. A concrete workflow would be using DSGNAR to train a PINN that solves the hydrostatic equilibrium equation for a stellar model, achieving precision comparable to numerical solvers. However, the method is demonstrated on standard PDE benchmarks (Burgers, Navier-Stokes), not on astrophysical problems.

Reason

This paper proposes a second-order optimization framework for physics-informed neural networks (PINNs), achieving unprecedented accuracy. PINNs are directly applicable to solving differential equations in astrophysics, e.g., for modeling stellar structure or gravitational lensing.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Check if the paper provides any examples or benchmarks relevant to astrophysics (e.g., Poisson equation, radiative transfer).
  • Look for details on the computational cost and scalability of DSGNAR for high-dimensional problems.
  • Examine the sensitivity to network architecture and whether the method works with standard PINN architectures used in astronomy.
Abstract

Physics-informed neural networks (PINNs) have emerged as a promising route to solve partial differential equations, yet they have struggled to reach the precision of classical solvers. The obstacle is increasingly understood to be one of optimisation, owing to the severely ill-conditioned loss landscape. We present $\textbf{DSGNAR}$: Doubly-Sketched Gauss-Newton with Adaptive Ratio, a scalable second-order optimisation framework that confronts this ill-conditioning and, in doing so, obtains unprecedented accuracy and speed. $\textbf{DSGNAR}$ couples a doubly-sketched Gauss-Newton model with a novel strategy that carefully controls both regularisation and step length. Across a suite of problems spanning nonlinear, chaotic, multi-scale, high-dimensional, and Navier-Stokes, the framework greatly improves on the state of the art: able to attain relative $\ell_2$ errors as low as $3\times10^{-16}$ in double precision, improve contemporary results by five orders of magnitude on the canonical Burgers' equation, and as much as eight orders on a high-dimensional Poisson problem, while remaining markedly faster. We further show that, in single precision, solutions at the limit of round-off error can be obtained very quickly: Burgers' equation to $\ell_2^{\text{rel}} = 4.75 \times 10^{-7}$ in under ten seconds. The framework is also robust to the choice of architecture, arithmetic precision, and initial hyperparameters. The code is available at this https URL

Self-explainable Operator Learning for Discovering Spatial Patterns in Functional Data

Mojgan Alishiri , Amirhossein Arzani

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

This paper addresses the lack of interpretability in neural operator learning for physical systems. It reformulates operator learning as a linear combination of generalized functional linear models, enabling direct interpretability by decomposing predictions into contributions from input subdomains. For astronomy, this could be used to discover which spatial regions in a galaxy image or spectral cube drive a prediction (e.g., star formation rate, metallicity). A concrete workflow would be training the model on simulated JWST images to predict galaxy properties and then inspecting the spatial contributions to understand which features (e.g., spiral arms, nucleus) are most important. However, the method is demonstrated on fluid flow problems (blood flow, aerodynamics), not on astronomical data.

Reason

This paper proposes a self-explainable operator learning framework that discovers spatial patterns, which is directly relevant to interpretable analysis of astronomical data, such as identifying structures in galaxy images or spectral maps.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Check if the framework can handle 2D or 3D spatial data typical of astronomical images and cubes.
  • Look for details on the computational cost of the subdomain decomposition and whether it scales to large images.
  • Examine whether the method requires the input domain to be regularly gridded, which is typical for images but not for irregularly sampled data.
Abstract

Operator learning has emerged as a powerful tool for modeling complex physical systems in functional spaces. However, their neural network-based architectures make them opaque models, obscuring the reasoning behind their predictions. In this work, we introduce a self-explainable operator learning framework that overcomes this challenge by reformulating operator learning as a linear combination of generalized functional linear models expressed through integral equations. Exploiting the additive decomposability of these integral equations, we divide the input domain into subdomains and compute localized integrals to evaluate the contribution of each region to the final prediction. This decomposition enables direct interpretability where the model explains both inputs and outputs by linking specific input regions to corresponding output patterns, thereby revealing which spatial features drive predictions. We demonstrate the framework on function-to-scalar and function-to-function mappings in fluid flow problems involving blood flow and unsteady aerodynamics. The results show that the operator most often prioritizes regions with strong feature gradients, providing physically meaningful insight into the model's decision-making process. Comparisons with established post-hoc explainability methods demonstrate qualitative agreement while highlighting the key advantage of the proposed approach: explainability is embedded directly within the operator structure itself and does not require an external tool. Therefore, our framework provides a mathematically transparent and physically interpretable approach to uncover relationships within data, fostering trust in machine learning for scientific applications by enabling more informed data-driven analysis of physical systems.
