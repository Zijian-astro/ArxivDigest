## 1. LongDS-Bench: On the Failure of Long-Horizon Agentic Data Analysis

- arXiv ID: `2605.30434`
- Authors: Kewei Xu , Xiaoben Lu , Shuofei Qiao , Zihan Ding , Haoming Xu , Lei Liang , Ningyu Zhang
- Score: 8
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30434
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.30434
- Subjects: Machine Learning (cs.LG) ; Artificial Intelligence (cs.AI); Computation and Language (cs.CL); Multiagent Systems (cs.MA)
- Reason: Benchmark for long-horizon agentic data analysis directly addresses the need for reliable AI agents in scientific workflows, including astronomy.

Short digest:
This paper introduces LongDS, a benchmark for long-horizon, multi-turn data analysis where agents must maintain and update evolving analytical states. It finds that even the best model achieves only 48.45% accuracy, with performance dropping sharply over long horizons. For astronomy, this is highly relevant: an agent analyzing JWST data over many steps (e.g., iterative SED fitting, source classification) would need to maintain state correctly. A concrete workflow would be to test an LLM agent on a multi-step task like 'identify little red dots in a JWST field, then classify their AGN fraction, then compare with literature.' A caveat is that the benchmark tasks are from Kaggle notebooks, not astronomy-specific, so the types of state changes (e.g., counterfactual perturbation) may not match astronomical workflows.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Check whether the benchmark tasks include any scientific or data-analysis workflows similar to astronomy
- Look for an analysis of failure modes that could inform how to design robust astronomical agents

## 2. EvoGens: A Population-Based Heuristic Search Framework for Scientific Idea Generation

- arXiv ID: `2605.30961`
- Authors: Xu Li , Hanzhe Tu , Xinyi Li , Kuncheng Zhao , Xun Han , Zhonghui Liu
- Score: 8
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30961
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.30961
- Subjects: Computation and Language (cs.CL)
- Reason: Directly addresses scientific idea generation using LLMs, which aligns with automated discovery and research agents for astronomy.

Short digest:
The paper tackles the problem of generating novel and diverse research ideas using LLMs, which often suffer from semantic convergence. It introduces EvoGens, an evolution-inspired framework that treats idea generation as an evolutionary search over a population, using rank-based mutation, retrieval planning, and semantic-aware crossover. For astronomy, this could help generate hypotheses about high-redshift galaxies or AGN from literature and data. An astronomer could use it to brainstorm novel analysis methods for JWST data or to propose new theoretical models. The main caveat is that the evaluation is automatic and may not capture true scientific novelty; human expert validation is needed.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Look for examples of generated ideas in the paper to assess relevance to astronomy.
- Check whether the retrieval mechanism can be adapted to astronomical literature databases.
- Examine the diversity and novelty metrics to see if they align with scientific creativity.

## 3. LongTraceRL: Learning Long-Context Reasoning from Search Agent Trajectories with Rubric Rewards

- arXiv ID: `2605.31584`
- Authors: Nianyi Lin , Jiajie Zhang , Lei Hou , Juanzi Li
- Score: 8
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.31584
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.31584
- Subjects: Computation and Language (cs.CL) ; Artificial Intelligence (cs.AI); Machine Learning (cs.LG)
- Reason: The paper directly addresses long-context reasoning with reinforcement learning and search agent trajectories, which is highly relevant for building research agents for astronomy literature review and data analysis.

Short digest:
This paper addresses the challenge of long-context reasoning in LLMs, where models fail to locate and integrate key information in extensive distracting content. It introduces LongTraceRL, which uses search agent trajectories to build tiered distractors (high and low confusability) and a rubric reward that provides fine-grained entity-level process supervision. The method consistently outperforms baselines across five long-context benchmarks. This is directly relevant for building research agents that can reason over long astronomical papers or multi-document literature reviews. The approach could be adapted for tasks like synthesizing information from multiple JWST papers or extracting relevant details from survey documentation. A key caveat is that the method requires generating training data from search agent trajectories, which may need adaptation for astronomical literature.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Check if the tiered distractor construction method can be adapted for astronomical papers with varying relevance levels.
- Look for any analysis of how the rubric reward handles domain-specific entities and relationships in scientific text.
- Examine the scalability of the approach to very long documents (e.g., 100+ page survey documentation).

Short digest:
This paper introduces Autonomous Agentic Data Engineering, where LLMs autonomously plan, generate, and iteratively optimize training data to specialize a student model. Experiments show a 57.29% improvement in the student model's performance through iterative data adaptation. For astronomy, this could automate the creation of training datasets for tasks like galaxy morphology classification or photometric redshift estimation. A concrete workflow would be to have an LLM agent generate synthetic JWST images or spectra to train a classifier for little red dots. A caveat is that the paper's domains are not astronomy, and the method may require careful prompt engineering and validation to avoid generating incorrect training data.

## 5. On the impact of retrieved content representations in RAG Pipelines

- arXiv ID: `2605.30790`
- Authors: Jonathan J Ross , Bevan Koopman , Anton van der Vegt , Guido Zuccon
- Score: 7
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30790
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.30790
- Subjects: Information Retrieval (cs.IR) ; Artificial Intelligence (cs.AI); Computation and Language (cs.CL)
- Reason: Directly relevant to RAG pipelines, which are key for building research agents that retrieve and synthesize astronomical literature and data.

Short digest:
This paper investigates how the representation of retrieved documents affects RAG pipeline accuracy. It compares 14 different transformations (selection, summarization, reformulation) of retrieved documents, holding retrieval fixed. The key finding is that answer retention—whether the answer-bearing document still supports its answer after transformation—is the primary determinant of generator accuracy, not wording, structure, or length. For astronomy, this is crucial for building RAG systems that retrieve papers, catalogs, or data products. It suggests that preserving the core scientific content is more important than formatting. A caveat is that the experiments use general QA datasets, not scientific or astronomical data, so domain-specific answer retention needs verification.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Check if the answer retention metric is defined and measured in a way that transfers to scientific claims or data.
- Look for the specific transformations tested and whether any are particularly suited to technical/scientific text.
- Examine the controlled comparison setup to see if it can be replicated with astronomical documents.
- Check if the paper discusses limitations for domain-specific or technical language.
