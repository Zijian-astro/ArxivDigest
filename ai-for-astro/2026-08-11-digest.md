## 2. An AI Scientist that Doesn't Drift: Taste, Structure, and Falsifiable Findings in a Quadruped Navigation Research Loop

- arXiv ID: `2608.07542`
- Authors: Yiwen Zhang , Eloise Zeng , Jaeha Lee , Tony Yue Yu
- Score: 7
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07542
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.07542
- Subjects: Artificial Intelligence (cs.AI) ; Machine Learning (cs.LG); Multiagent Systems (cs.MA); Robotics (cs.RO)
- Reason: The paper addresses AI scientists that avoid drift by using a preference oracle and structured experiment cards, which is highly relevant to automated scientific discovery and research agents.

Short digest:
The paper presents an AI Scientist for studying generalization in quadruped navigation, with components to prevent drift: immutable experiment cards, specialized subagents, and a preference oracle (kkanbu) that holds user research taste. They find that the oracle changes direction but not score, and the loop remains honest without retconning. For astronomy, this matters because it offers a framework for autonomous research loops that could test hypotheses about galaxy evolution or AGN classification without drifting from the original question. A workflow could involve using such a loop to explore JWST data, with the oracle encoding the astronomer's preferences for interesting sources. However, the paper is in robotics simulation, so transferability to astronomical data analysis needs validation.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Check if the experiment card schema could be adapted to astronomical hypotheses and data.
- Look for details on how the preference oracle is constructed and whether it could encode astronomical research interests.
- Assess whether the loop could handle real astronomical datasets or only simulation.

## 5. Search-G1: Grounded Search Agents via Representation-Based Intrinsic Rewards

- arXiv ID: `2608.07531`
- Authors: Cheng Ruoxi , Ma Haoxuan , Zhang Hongyi , Zhang Junming , Duan Ranjie , Xia Qiaolin , Wang Hao , Lu Yu , Shi Haibo , Ma Xingjun
- Score: 6
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07531
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.07531
- Subjects: Computation and Language (cs.CL) ; Artificial Intelligence (cs.AI)
- Reason: The paper proposes a reward framework for search-augmented language agents, which is relevant to retrieval-augmented generation and research agents that could be used for astronomical literature review or data analysis.

Short digest:
The paper addresses the problem of when search-augmented language agents should retrieve external information and how to ground answers in retrieved evidence. It introduces Search-G1, a representation-based intrinsic reward framework that uses intervention-calibrated readouts to measure whether an answer relies on retrieved evidence, without needing process annotations or LLM judges. This matters for astronomy because research agents that query databases or literature could benefit from knowing when retrieval is necessary and when closed-book knowledge suffices, improving efficiency and reliability. A concrete workflow could be an agent that searches astronomical catalogs or papers to answer questions about high-redshift galaxies, using this reward to avoid unnecessary searches. However, the paper tests on generic question-answering benchmarks, so transferability to astronomical data and multimodal inputs needs verification.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Check if the reward framework is tested on multimodal or scientific data, or if it only works on text-based QA.
- Look for details on how the readouts are calibrated and whether they require domain-specific tuning for astronomy.
- Assess whether the method can handle long-context or multi-step reasoning typical in astronomical data analysis.
