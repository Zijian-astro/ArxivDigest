CASCADE: Case-Based Continual Adaptation for Large Language Models During Deployment

Siyuan Guo , Yali Du , Hechang Chen , Yi Chang , Jun Wang

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

This paper asks how LLM agents can continue learning from experience during deployment without retraining model parameters. The core idea is CASCADE, a framework that gives agents an explicit episodic memory and treats experience reuse as a contextual bandit problem, balancing exploration and exploitation with theoretical guarantees. For astronomy, this could enable a research agent that improves its ability to search literature, analyze JWST spectra, or classify sources over time as it encounters more data. Concretely, one could imagine deploying an agent to autonomously analyze a stream of new JWST observations, where it learns which analysis strategies work best from past successes and failures. A key caveat is that the paper tests on 16 diverse tasks (medical, legal, code, web) but not on scientific data analysis, so the transfer to astronomy workflows needs validation.

Reason

Directly addresses deployment-time learning for LLM agents via episodic memory, which is highly relevant to building research agents for automated scientific discovery in astronomy.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Check whether the episodic memory stores structured astronomical knowledge (e.g., source properties, SED templates) or only generic task descriptions.
  • Look for ablation studies showing how the exploration-exploitation trade-off is tuned and whether it can be adapted to long-running scientific workflows.
  • Examine the computational overhead of the episodic memory relative to the base LLM, especially for large-scale survey data.
  • Verify if the framework supports multimodal inputs (images, spectra) or only text, which is critical for JWST analysis.
Abstract

Large language models (LLMs) have become a central foundation of modern artificial intelligence, yet their lifecycle remains constrained by a rigid separation between training and deployment, after which learning effectively ceases. This limitation contrasts with natural intelligence, which continually adapts through interaction with its environment. In this paper, we formalise deployment-time learning (DTL) as the third stage in the LLM lifecycle that enables LLM agents to improve from experience during deployment without modifying model parameters. We present CASCADE (CASe-based Continual Adaptation during DEployment), a general and principled framework that equips LLM agents with an explicit, evolving episodic memory. CASCADE formulates experience reuse as a contextual bandit problem, enabling principled exploration-exploitation trade-offs and establishing no-regret guarantees over long-term interactions. This design allows agents to accumulate, select, and refine task-relevant cases, transforming past experience into actionable knowledge. Across 16 diverse tasks spanning medical diagnosis, legal analysis, code generation, web search, tool use, and embodied interaction, CASCADE improves macro-averaged success rate by 20.9% over zero-shot prompting while consistently outperforming gradient-based and memory-based baselines. By reframing deployment as an adaptive learning process, this work establishes a foundation for continually improving AI systems.

When Does Critique Improve AI-Assisted Theoretical Physics? SCALAR: Structured Critic--Actor Loop for Agentic Reasoning

Vasilis Niarchos , Constantinos Papageorgakis , Alexander G. Stapleton , Sokratis Trifinopoulos

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

The paper asks how researcher-agent interaction affects AI-assisted theoretical physics problem-solving. It introduces SCALAR, a pipeline where an Actor proposes solutions, a Critic provides iterative feedback, and a Judge evaluates the transcript. This matters for astronomy because similar agentic loops could be used to automate SED fitting, spectral classification, or literature review for JWST data. One could imagine using SCALAR to iteratively refine photometric redshift estimates or classify little red dots by having the agent propose and critique solutions. A key caveat is that the study uses quantum field theory problems, so transfer to astronomy workflows requires adaptation of the problem representation and evaluation metrics.

Reason

Directly studies LLM agents for theoretical physics reasoning with a structured critic-actor loop, highly relevant to research agents and automated scientific discovery.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Look for the specific prompting strategies used for the Critic (constructive vs. strict vs. adversarial) and whether they are described in enough detail to replicate.
  • Check if the paper provides the full transcript examples or only aggregate scores, to assess the quality of feedback.
  • Examine the Judge evaluation criteria: are they based on exact answer matching or partial credit, and could a similar rubric be designed for astronomy tasks?
Abstract

As large language models (LLMs) show increasing promise on research-level physics reasoning tasks and agentic AI becomes more common, a practical question emerges: How does the interaction between researchers and agents affect the results? We study this using SCALAR (Structured Critic--Actor Loop for AI Reasoning), an Actor--Critic--Judge pipeline applied to quantum field theory and string theory problems. The Actor proposes solutions, the Critic provides iterative feedback, and an independent Judge evaluates the transcript against reference solutions. We vary the Actor persona, the Critic feedback strategy, and the Actor model family and scale. Multi-turn dialogue improves over single-shot attempts throughout, but both the mechanism of improvement and the value of different prompting choices depend strongly on the Actor--Critic pairing. Increasing the scale within one model family (e.g. from the 8B-parameter DeepSeek-R1 variant to DeepSeek-R1 70B) improves some easier-problem behavior, but does not remove the hardest bottleneck we observe. Critic feedback strategy matters most clearly in the asymmetric Actor--Critic setting (e.g., a lightweight Haiku Actor guided by a stronger Sonnet Critic), where constructive feedback improves mean-score outcomes. In same-family Actor--Critic settings, strategy effects are weaker: lenient feedback is sometimes favored, while strict and adversarial feedback are not beneficial. Taken together, SCALAR provides a controlled testbed for evaluating which interaction structures help or hinder AI-driven scientific discovery.

Kurtosis-Guided Denoising Score Matching for Tabular Anomaly Detection

Victor Livernoche , Jie Zan , Reihaneh Rabbany

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

This paper tackles the problem of detecting anomalous points in tabular data, a common task in astronomy for finding rare or unusual sources in survey catalogs. The core idea is a denoising score matching (DSM) approach, where a neural network learns the 'score' (gradient of the data density) from noise-corrupted samples; a high score magnitude at a test point indicates it is inconsistent with the training distribution and thus anomalous. The key innovation is a per-feature noise scaling scheme based on kurtosis (a measure of tail heaviness), which adapts the noise level to each feature's distribution shape without requiring expensive hyperparameter tuning. For astronomy, this could be applied to detect anomalous galaxies, AGN, or artifacts in multi-wavelength catalogs from surveys like JWST, Roman, or Rubin. A concrete workflow would be to train K-DSM on a clean set of known source types (e.g., stars, normal galaxies) and then flag sources with high anomaly scores for spectroscopic follow-up or further inspection. A caveat is that the method is demonstrated on standard ML benchmarks, not astronomical data, so its performance on noisy, missing, or highly imbalanced astronomical catalogs needs verification.

Reason

Directly addresses anomaly detection in tabular data, which is highly relevant to astronomical source classification and outlier detection in survey catalogs. The kurtosis-based noise scaling is a practical, hyperparameter-light method that could transfer to JWST or Rubin/LSST data.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Check whether the method is evaluated on datasets with similar characteristics to astronomical catalogs (e.g., high dimensionality, missing values, class imbalance).
  • Look for ablation studies showing the impact of the kurtosis-based noise scaling versus fixed or other adaptive noise schemes.
  • Examine the sensitivity of the anomaly detection performance to the choice of the noise scale parameter and whether the method is robust to contamination of the training set.
Abstract

Denoising score matching (DSM) provides a way to learn data distributions by training a neural network to recover the score function, defined as the gradient of the log density, from noise-corrupted samples. Once trained, the score magnitude at a test point reflects how consistent that point is with the learned distribution, making it a natural anomaly signal. The key practical challenge is selecting the perturbation scale: too little noise yields unstable score estimates in sparse regions, while too much erases local structure and weakens anomaly sensitivity. This is compounded by the difficulty of hyperparameter tuning when anomalies are unknown and no validation set is available. We introduce kurtosis-based noise scaling (K-DSM), a per-feature scheme that sets noise levels from the shape of each marginal distribution, improving coverage of low-density regions and precision in high-density regions without extra model complexity. Contrary to prior claims that multi-scale or noise-conditioned training is necessary, we find that a carefully trained single-scale model is already a strong anomaly detector. On standard tabular anomaly detection benchmarks, K-DSM achieves state-of-the-art performance in the semi-supervised setting. When combined with a lightweight EMA-teacher filtering rule that removes low-density training points before each gradient step, it also achieves strong performance in the fully unsupervised (contaminated) setting, suggesting that simple, data-adaptive noise scaling enables robust anomaly detection while reducing reliance on hyperparameter tuning.

Discovering Ordinary Differential Equations with LLM-Based Qualitative and Quantitative Evaluation

Sum Kyun Song , Bong Gyun Shin , Jae Yong Lee

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

This paper tackles the problem of discovering governing differential equations from observational data, a core challenge in scientific machine learning. It proposes DoLQ, a method that uses a multi-agent LLM architecture to propose, optimize, and evaluate candidate equations. A Scientist Agent leverages an LLM to conduct both qualitative (physical plausibility) and quantitative (accuracy) evaluations. The method achieves superior performance on multi-dimensional ODE benchmarks. For astronomy, this could be applied to discover dynamical models from time-series observations, such as light curves or spectral variability. The multi-agent approach with LLM-based evaluation is a novel way to incorporate domain knowledge into equation discovery. However, the paper only tests on synthetic ODE benchmarks, not real astronomical data.

Reason

Directly relevant to automated scientific discovery using LLMs for equation discovery from observational data.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Check if the method can handle noisy or sparse observational data typical in astronomy.
  • Look for any experiments on real-world scientific datasets beyond synthetic ODEs.
  • Examine the LLM's ability to incorporate physical constraints (e.g., conservation laws) into the evaluation.
  • Check the computational cost and scalability to large datasets.
Abstract

Discovering governing differential equations from observational data is a fundamental challenge in scientific machine learning. Existing symbolic regression approaches rely primarily on quantitative metrics; however, real-world differential equation modeling also requires incorporating domain knowledge to ensure physical plausibility. To address this gap, we propose DoLQ, a method for discovering ordinary differential equations with LLM-based qualitative and quantitative evaluation. DoLQ employs a multi-agent architecture: a Sampler Agent proposes dynamic system candidates, a Parameter Optimizer refines equations for accuracy, and a Scientist Agent leverages an LLM to conduct both qualitative and quantitative evaluations and synthesize their results to iteratively guide the search. Experiments on multi-dimensional ordinary differential equation benchmarks demonstrate that DoLQ achieves superior performance compared to existing methods, not only attaining higher success rates but also more accurately recovering the correct symbolic terms of ground truth equations. Anisotropic Modality Align

Xiaomin Yu , Yijiang Li , Yuhui Zhang , Hanzhen Zhao , Yue Yang , Hao Tang , Yue Song , Xiaobin Hu , Chengwei Qin , Shuicheng Yan , Hui Xiong

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

This paper tackles the problem of the 'modality gap' in multimodal contrastive models, where representations from different modalities (e.g., images and text) are not interchangeable. The main finding is that the gap is not a simple shift but an anisotropic structure along a few dominant directions. The authors propose AnisoAlign, a method to correct this gap by aligning source representations to the target modality's distribution while preserving semantic structure. For astronomy, this is highly relevant: it could enable training multimodal models that combine JWST images, spectra, and catalog data without requiring large paired datasets. An astronomer could use this to build a model that, given a spectrum, retrieves similar images or vice versa. A caveat is that the method is demonstrated on general vision-language data, not astronomical data, and may require adaptation.

Reason

Directly addresses modality alignment for multimodal models, which is crucial for combining astronomical images, spectra, and catalogs.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Check if the anisotropic correction is computationally feasible for high-dimensional astronomical data (e.g., spectra with thousands of wavelength bins).
  • Look for any discussion of how to choose the number of dominant directions for correction, as this may be domain-dependent.
Abstract

Training multimodal large language models has long been limited by the scarcity of high-quality paired multimodal data. Recent studies show that the shared representation space of pretrained multimodal contrastive models can serve as a bridge, enabling models to perform multimodal training with unimodal data. However, the key premise of this paradigm remains insufficiently understood: can representations from different modalities be reliably interchanged? The core obstacle lies in the persistent Modality Gap in the shared space. In this work, we revisit the geometric nature of the modality gap. We find that modality representations already share compatible dominant semantic geometry. What truly hinders modality interchangeability is not a simple global shift, but an anisotropic residual structure concentrated along a small number of dominant directions. Based on this finding, we further propose the principle of anisotropic modality gap alignment: effective modality alignment should align with the target-modality distribution while preserving the semantic structure of the source modality. Guided by this principle, we propose an anisotropic geometric correction framework, AnisoAlign, for unpaired modality alignment. This framework leverages the internal geometric prior of the target modality and performs bounded correction on source-modality representations, thereby constructing substitute representations in the target modality. Experiments confirm its benefits in both geometric diagnostics and text-only MLLM training. Overall, this work recasts the modality gap from an empirical observation into a correctable, structured geometric phenomenon and provides a new representation alignment perspective for training multimodal models with unimodal data.

AgentEscapeBench: Evaluating Out-of-Domain Tool-Grounded Reasoning in LLM Agents

Zhengkang Guo , Yiyang Li , Lin Qiu , Xiaohua Wang , Jingwen Xv , Dongyu Ru , Xiaoyu Li , Xiaoqing Zheng , Xuezhi Cao , Xunliang Cai

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

This paper introduces AgentEscapeBench, a benchmark to test whether LLM agents can infer, execute, and revise novel tool-use procedures under long-range dependency constraints. The tasks involve a directed acyclic dependency graph over tools and items, requiring agents to track hidden state and propagate intermediate results. For astronomy, this is directly relevant because it provides a diagnostic framework for evaluating agents that could automate multi-step data analysis pipelines (e.g., fetching JWST images, running SED fitting, querying catalogs). A concrete workflow would be testing an agent on a simplified version of this benchmark before deploying it on real astronomical tasks. A key limitation is that the benchmark tasks are synthetic and not domain-specific; performance may not directly transfer to astronomical tool use without adaptation.

Reason

Directly evaluates LLM agents on long-horizon tool-use reasoning, which is highly relevant to building research agents for automated scientific workflows.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Check the dependency graph complexity and how it maps to real astronomical workflows (e.g., multi-catalog queries, sequential data processing).
  • Look for trajectory analysis breakdowns: which types of errors (state tracking, clue adherence, result propagation) are most common and how they might be mitigated.
  • Examine whether the benchmark supports custom tool definitions that could be adapted for astronomy-specific tools (e.g., astroquery, TOPCAT, or custom APIs).
  • Check the human performance baseline to calibrate expectations for agent capabilities on long-horizon tasks.
Abstract

As LLM-based agents increasingly rely on external tools, it is important to evaluate their ability to sustain tool-grounded reasoning beyond familiar workflows and short-range interactions. We introduce AgentEscapeBench, an escape-room-style benchmark that tests whether agents can infer, execute, and revise novel tool-use procedures under explicit long-range dependency constraints. Each task defines a directed acyclic dependency graph over tools and items, requiring agents to invoke real external functions, track hidden state revealed incrementally, propagate intermediate results, and submit a deterministically verifiable final answer. AgentEscapeBench includes 270 instances across five difficulty tiers and supports fully automated evaluation. Experiments with sixteen LLM agents and human participants show that performance drops sharply as dependency depth increases: humans decline from 98.3% success at difficulty-5 to 80.0% at difficulty-25, while the best model drops from 90.0% to 60.0%. Trajectory analysis attributes model failures mainly to breakdowns in long-range state tracking, clue adherence, and intermediate-result propagation. These findings suggest that current agents can often handle local tool use but still struggle with deep contextual dependencies. We hope AgentEscapeBench can serve as a diagnostic testbed for measuring current agent capabilities and informing future training efforts toward more robust general-purpose reasoning, action, and adaptation.

LLMs Improving LLMs: Agentic Discovery for Test-Time Scaling

Tong Zheng , Haolin Liu , Chengsong Huang , Huiwen Bao , Sheng Zhang , Rui Liu , Runpeng Dai , Ruibo Chen , Chenxi Liu , Tianyi Xiong , Xidong Wu , Hongming Zhang , Heng Huang

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

This paper tackles the problem of manually designing test-time scaling (TTS) strategies for LLMs, which is inefficient and unexplored. The key idea is an environment-driven framework, AutoTTS, that automatically discovers TTS strategies by constructing a tractable control space and providing cheap feedback. For astronomy, this could automate the design of reasoning strategies for LLM agents performing data analysis or literature review, improving accuracy-cost tradeoffs. A concrete workflow would be using AutoTTS to discover optimal branching and pruning strategies for an LLM agent analyzing JWST spectra. A limitation is that the framework is demonstrated on mathematical reasoning and may require adaptation to scientific domains with different feedback signals.

Reason

Directly addresses automated discovery of test-time scaling strategies for LLMs, which could be applied to astronomical reasoning tasks or scientific workflows.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Check if the discovered strategies generalize to held-out benchmarks and model scales.
  • Look for details on the feedback mechanism and how it can be adapted to scientific tasks.
  • Examine the cost and time requirements for discovery to assess feasibility for astronomy workflows.
Abstract

Test-time scaling (TTS) has become an effective approach for improving large language model performance by allocating additional computation during inference. However, existing TTS strategies are largely hand-crafted: researchers manually design reasoning patterns and tune heuristics by intuition, leaving much of the computation-allocation space unexplored. We propose an environment-driven framework, AutoTTS, that changes what researchers design: from individual TTS heuristics to environments where TTS strategies can be discovered automatically. The key to AutoTTS lies in environment construction: the discovery environment must make the control space tractable and provide cheap, frequent feedback for TTS search. As a concrete instantiation, we formulate width--depth TTS as controller synthesis over pre-collected reasoning trajectories and probe signals, where controllers decide when to branch, continue, probe, prune, or stop and can be evaluated cheaply without repeated LLM calls. We further introduce beta parameterization to make the search tractable and fine-grained execution trace feedback to improve discovery efficiency by helping the agent diagnose why a TTS program fails. Experiments on mathematical reasoning benchmarks show that the discovered strategies improve the overall accuracy--cost tradeoff over strong manually designed baselines. The discovered strategies generalize to held-out benchmarks and model scales, while the entire discovery costs only $39.9 and 160 minutes. Our data, and code will be open-source at this https URL .
