## 1. How do Self-Supervised Remote Sensing Vision Models Transfer to Downstream Tasks?

- arXiv ID: `2606.13896`
- Authors: Julia Romero , Qin Lv , Morteza Karimzadeh
- Score: 8
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13896
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.13896
- Subjects: Computer Vision and Pattern Recognition (cs.CV) ; Artificial Intelligence (cs.AI)
- Reason: Directly studies self-supervised remote sensing models, which are relevant to astronomical image analysis and transfer learning.

Short digest:
This paper investigates how self-supervised geospatial foundation models (GeoFMs) transfer to downstream tasks like classification and segmentation. It finds that model rankings change across tasks, and that intermediate transformer blocks often contain more task-relevant information than final layers. This matters for astronomy because similar self-supervised models could be applied to JWST or survey data for source classification or anomaly detection. The paper suggests that decoder design and fine-tuning can be as important as model choice, which is a practical insight for adapting such models to astronomical images. A limitation is that the study uses remote sensing data, not astronomical images, so transferability needs verification.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Look for layerwise probing results showing which blocks are most informative for different tasks.
- Check the segmentation case studies on PASTIS and Sen1Floods11 for decoder design comparisons.
- Examine CKA analysis to understand how fine-tuning changes model representations.

- Authors: Amin Bigdeli , Negar Arabzadeh , Radin Hamidi Rad , Sajad Ebrahimi , Charles L. A. Clarke , Ebrahim Bagheri
- Score: 8
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13905
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.13905
- Subjects: Information Retrieval (cs.IR) ; Computation and Language (cs.CL)
- Reason: Retrieval-augmented generation with iterative query expansion, directly applicable to literature review and data discovery in astronomy.

Short digest:
This paper addresses the problem of query expansion in retrieval-augmented generation, where generated expansions can cause retrieval drift. It introduces ADORE, an iterative framework that uses retrieval outcomes as feedback to refine queries. This matters for astronomy because it can improve literature search or data discovery from large archives like ADS or MAST. The method could be used to build a research agent that iteratively refines queries to find relevant papers or datasets. A limitation is that the evaluation is on general benchmarks, not astronomy-specific corpora.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Check the nDCG@10 improvements on BEIR and BRIGHT to assess robustness.
- Look for the relevance assessor design and how it evaluates retrieved documents.
- Examine the iterative process to see if it can be adapted to multi-turn scientific discovery workflows.

## 3. Decoupled Mixture-of-Experts for Parametric Knowledge Injection

- arXiv ID: `2606.14243`
- Authors: Baoqing Yue , Weihang Su , Qingyao Ai , Yichen Tang , Changyue Wang , Jiacheng Kang , Jingtao Zhan , Yiqun Liu
- Score: 8
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.14243
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.14243
- Subjects: Computation and Language (cs.CL)
- Reason: Directly addresses knowledge injection for LLMs via a modular expert architecture, which is relevant for building domain-specific scientific assistants for astronomy.

Short digest:
The paper tackles the problem of injecting external, domain-specific knowledge into large language models without catastrophic forgetting or costly retraining. It proposes Decoupled Mixture-of-Experts (DMoE), a modular architecture where external knowledge is stored in independently updatable expert modules, and a lightweight router activates relevant experts only when the base model lacks knowledge. This is relevant for astronomy because it could allow an LLM to incorporate specialized knowledge from JWST data analysis papers, catalogs, or simulation results without retraining the entire model. A concrete workflow would be using DMoE to augment a general-purpose LLM with expert modules for high-redshift galaxy SED fitting or AGN classification, enabling it to answer domain-specific queries accurately. A key caveat is that the method requires converting knowledge corpora into expert modules, which may need careful curation and preprocessing of astronomical literature or datasets.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Check whether the expert modules are designed to handle structured astronomical data (e.g., catalogs, spectra) or only text corpora.
- Look for details on the uncertainty-aware router mechanism and whether it can be adapted to multi-modal inputs (images + spectra).
- Examine the computational overhead of adding multiple expert modules and whether inference latency is acceptable for interactive use.

## 4. From Chatbot to Digital Colleague: The Paradigm Shift Toward Persistent Autonomous AI

- arXiv ID: `2606.14502`
- Authors: Yongheng Zhang , Ziang Liu , Jiaxuan Zhu , Shuai Wang , Xiangqi Chen , Haojing Huang , Jiayi Kuang , Siyu Chen , Ao Shen , Hao Wu , Qiufeng Wang , Qian-Wen Zhang , Junnan Dong , Wenhao Jiang , Ying Shen , Hai-Tao Zheng , Yinghui Li , Di Yin , Xing Sun , Philip S. Yu
- Score: 8
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.14502
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.14502
- Subjects: Artificial Intelligence (cs.AI)
- Reason: Directly discusses the evolution of LLMs from chatbots to persistent autonomous agents with reasoning, memory, and tool use, which is core to building research agents for astronomy workflows.

Short digest:
This paper argues that LLMs are transitioning from simple chatbots to persistent autonomous AI systems (Digital Colleagues) that can reason, act, remember, and improve themselves. The key idea is a two-part shift: (1) at the cognitive core, moving from fast next-token prediction to deliberate thinking using chain-of-thought, reflection, and reinforcement learning; (2) at the tool-use level, moving from ad-hoc tool calling to persistent Workspaces with reusable Skills, verification loops, and governance. For astronomy, this paradigm could enable an AI agent that maintains a long-term workspace for analyzing JWST data, remembers past fitting choices, reuses validated analysis pipelines, and self-corrects. A concrete workflow would be an agent that ingests a JWST NIRSpec spectrum, runs SED fitting, checks for AGN signatures, writes a summary, and stores the procedure for reuse on the next source. A key caveat is that the paper is a conceptual framework, not a ready-to-use system, so one must check whether the proposed 'Workspace + Skill' architecture has been implemented and tested on real scientific tasks.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Look for any implemented system or code release that demonstrates the Workspace+Skill paradigm on a concrete task.
- Check whether the paper discusses evaluation metrics relevant to scientific reproducibility, such as task completion rate or error recovery, not just benchmark scores.
- See if the paper addresses how the persistent memory handles domain-specific data formats (e.g., FITS files, spectra, catalogs) or if it is purely text-based.

- arXiv ID: `2606.13904`
- Authors: Austin Senna Wijaya , Jiaxiang Liu , Haonan Wang , Eugene Wu
- Score: 7
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13904
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.13904
- Subjects: Computation and Language (cs.CL) ; Artificial Intelligence (cs.AI); Databases (cs.DB)
- Reason: LLM agent framework for question answering over data lakes, relevant to scientific data discovery and analysis.

Short digest:
This paper addresses the problem of diagnosing failures in LLM agents for exploratory question answering over data lakes. It introduces SANA, a diagnostic ablation framework that identifies bottlenecks in search, planning, and data analysis. This matters for astronomy because similar agents could be used to query large astronomical catalogs or literature. The paper shows that data analysis is a consistent bottleneck, which is a key insight for building astronomy-specific research agents. A limitation is that the benchmarks are not astronomy-specific, so the findings may not directly transfer.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Look for the ablation results showing which component (search, planning, data analysis) is the main bottleneck.
- Check the benchmark details to see if they involve scientific or tabular data similar to astronomical catalogs.
- Examine the agent design and whether it can be adapted to multi-wavelength or multi-survey data.
