Title: Multimodal Hybrid Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Scientific Document Understanding using Open-Source SLMs
Authors: Alexandru-Andrei Saucă , Ana-Luiza Rusnac
arXiv ID: 2607.24799
Score: 8
Reason: The paper presents a multimodal RAG system for scientific documents using open-source SLMs, which directly addresses the need for reliable information extraction from astronomical literature and data.
Short digest: This paper asks how to build a multimodal RAG system for scientific document understanding that avoids hallucinations without fine-tuning. It proposes an architecture with a multimodal ingestion pipeline that uses a vision-language model to generate textual summaries of tables and figures, combined with HNSW-based semantic search and GIN-based lexical search unified through Reciprocal Rank Fusion and Cross Encoder reranking. Title: SepPrune:A Separator-based Pruning Framework for Efficient Multimodal Large Language Models
Authors: Yuchen Wang , Qihui Zhu , Yang Liu , Xiaoyan Sun , Siying Wu
arXiv ID: 2607.25818
Score: 8
Reason: This paper proposes a training-free vision token pruning method for multimodal LLMs, which is directly relevant to reducing computational cost when processing high-resolution astronomical images with vision-language models.
Short digest: The paper asks how to reduce the computational cost of multimodal LLMs that generate many vision tokens for high-resolution inputs. It proposes SepPrune, a training-free method that uses modality separator tokens as queries to rank and select informative vision tokens, removing 80.2% of vision tokens while retaining 96.3% of original accuracy. This is highly relevant for astronomy because JWST, Roman, and other surveys produce high-resolution images that would generate massive token counts in vision-language models. For example, a multimodal model analyzing JWST images of high-redshift galaxies could use SepPrune to focus on informative regions while drastically reducing compute. A key caveat is that the method is demonstrated on Qwen2.5-VL-7B with general-domain images, so performance on astronomical images with different visual characteristics (e.g., faint sources, noise, artifacts) needs validation.
Full-text inspection checklist: Check if the pruning ratio and accuracy retention hold for astronomical images with sparse sources and varying signal-to-noise.; Look for any analysis of which types of visual information are preserved or lost during pruning, especially for small or faint objects.; Check whether the method requires a specific model architecture or can be applied to other multimodal LLMs used in astronomy.
