Evaluating RAG Metrics in Applied Contexts: An Experiment, Its Findings and Its Limitations

Quentin Brabant

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

This paper empirically evaluates several RAG evaluation metrics (Ragas, DeepEval, RAGChecker, Opik) using a human-annotated question-answering dataset from business data. It compares automated metrics to human scores and standard recall, finding correlations and limitations. For astronomy, RAG systems are increasingly used for literature review, data analysis, and research agents. Understanding which metrics reliably reflect answer quality is essential for deploying RAG in scientific workflows. However, the study uses business data, not astronomical text, so the findings may not fully generalize to domain-specific jargon and complex scientific queries. The paper also highlights methodological limitations and suggests future work.

Reason

Directly evaluates RAG metrics, which is crucial for building reliable retrieval-augmented generation systems for astronomical literature review and data analysis.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Check if the correlation analysis between automated metrics and human scores is presented clearly, with effect sizes and confidence intervals.
  • Look for any discussion of domain-specific evaluation needs, such as handling technical terminology or numerical precision.
  • See if the paper provides recommendations for choosing metrics in applied contexts, which could guide astronomy RAG system development.
Abstract

This paper reports an empirical study evaluating the relevance of several RAG metrics. The experiment is based on a question-answering dataset created by human annotators from business data. The generated responses and retrieved spans of a RAG system are scored using evaluation metrics from four libraries (Ragas, DeepEval, RAGChecker, Opik). These metrics are compared to scores given by two evaluators, as well as to standard metrics such as recall. An analysis of correlations is conducted. Finally, we highlight certain limitations of our methodology, compare it to those used in the literature, and suggest some avenues for future research. This paper is an English translation of a paper originally published in the French-speaking workshop EvalLLM (Brabant, 2026).

TimEE: End-to-end Time Series Classification via In-Context Learning

Jaris Küken , Shi Bin Hoo , Martin Mráz , Frank Hutter , Lennart Purucker

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

This paper introduces TimEE, a 4.5M-parameter foundation model for end-to-end time series classification via in-context learning. Given a labeled support set and a query time series, it outputs a predicted class distribution in a single forward pass with no per-dataset training required. It is meta-trained exclusively on synthetic TSC tasks and achieves state-of-the-art performance on the UCR benchmark. For astronomy, this could classify variable star light curves, transient types, or spectral classes without retraining for each survey. A concrete workflow would be using TimEE to classify JWST NIRSpec spectra or LSST light curves by providing a few labeled examples from a known catalog. A key caveat is that the model is trained on synthetic data with structured distributional shifts, which may not match the noise and irregular sampling of real astronomical time series.

Reason

Directly relevant to time series classification via in-context learning, which could be applied to astronomical light curves, spectra, or time-domain surveys.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Check if the model handles irregularly sampled or noisy time series common in astronomy.
  • Look for the synthetic data generation process: does it produce realistic astronomical-like patterns?
  • Examine the support set size: how few labeled examples are needed for good performance on astronomical tasks?
  • Check if the model can be fine-tuned or adapted to domain-specific time series.
Abstract

Time series classification (TSC) is dominated by a two-stage paradigm: train a feature encoder -- either from scratch on the target dataset or via pretraining on large corpora -- and then fit a task-specific classifier on top. While effective, this decoupling optimizes representation learning independently of the classification objective, requires per-dataset training, and prevents the model from exploiting label information during inference. We introduce TimEE, a 4.5M-parameter foundation model for end-to-end TSC via in-context learning. Given a labeled support set and a query time series, TimEE directly outputs a predicted class distribution in a single forward pass with no per-dataset training required. Following the prior-data fitted network (PFN) framework, TimEE is meta-trained exclusively on synthetic TSC tasks, where each task contains time series with distinct class identities arising from structured distributional shifts in the generative process. Despite seeing no real time series during pre-training, TimEE ranks first in ROC AUC (and third on accuracy) on the UCR benchmark among all compared methods, which include both foundation models and supervised deep learning baselines. To our knowledge, TimEE is the first purely synthetic-pretrained model to reach state-of-the-art performance on the UCR benchmark. These results establish end-to-end ICL with synthetic priors as a compelling, largely unexplored direction for TSC, with scaling, prior design, and richer generation mechanisms as natural avenues for improvement. From Noisy Traces to Root Causes: Structural Trajectory Analysis and Causal Extraction for Agent Optimization

Ying Chang , Jiahang Xu , Xuan Feng , Chenyuan Yang , Peng Cheng , Yuqing Yang

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

This paper introduces STRACE, a framework that extracts high-signal optimization contexts from noisy execution traces of LLM agents. It mines failure patterns and performs causal localization to identify root causes, improving agent success rates by 1.4x on a formal verification task. For astronomy, this could be used to debug and improve LLM agents that perform multi-step data analysis, such as a pipeline that downloads JWST data, runs a reduction script, and fits a model. A concrete workflow might involve using STRACE to identify why an agent fails to correctly deblend a crowded field. A key caveat is that the method is demonstrated on a formal verification task, not scientific data analysis.

Reason

Directly addresses optimization of long-horizon agents by extracting causal information from noisy traces; highly relevant for improving LLM agents for complex astronomical workflows.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Check if the causal localization method is general enough to handle the diverse types of errors in scientific workflows (e.g., numerical instability, data format issues).
  • Look for details on how the 'textual dependency graph' is constructed and if it can incorporate domain-specific knowledge.
Abstract

The optimization of long-horizon agents increasingly relies on reflection-based mechanisms, where a large language model (LLM) acts as an optimizer to diagnose agent failures and improve agent policies. However, real execution traces are difficult to use directly for optimization: large trace collections are often redundant and heterogeneous, making optimization inefficient and prone to overfitting to low-value failures; meanwhile, each individual trajectory also contains many irrelevant steps, while naive context reduction methods such as truncation or sliding windows can discard causally important evidence and produce misleading optimization signals. To resolve this dilemma, we introduce STRACE (Structural TRajectory Analysis and Causal Extraction), a framework that constructs high signal-noise optimization contexts for more precise and effective optimization. At the batch level, STRACE mines failure patterns to filter redundant traces and retain representative failures; within each selected trace, it performs causal localization over a textual dependency graph to remove non-causal steps and identify the true root-cause module for optimization. Empirical results demonstrate that STRACE significantly outperforms standard context-filtering baselines. Notably, on a challenging formal verification task (VeruSAGE-Bench), it successfully optimizes human-expert designed agents, delivering $1.4\times$ success-rate improvement (42.5% to 58.5%). Naming the Concepts Classifiers Rely On: Language-Anchored Decomposition for Faithful Explanation

Ahsan Habib Akash , Dipkamal Bhusal , Stacey Jones , Donald A. Adjeroh , Binod Bhattarai , Prashnna Kumar Gyawali

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

This paper proposes Language-Anchored Decomposition (LAD), a post-hoc framework that produces named, faithful concept explanations for deep neural networks without retraining. It uses a large language model to propose concept vocabularies, CLIP to localize them in images, and non-negative matrix factorization to reconstruct encoder activations. The concepts are human-interpretable and decision-relevant. For astronomy, this could explain what features a galaxy classifier uses (e.g., spiral arms, color gradients, bulge size) in natural language. The method could be applied to JWST image classifiers or morphological models, providing insights into model behavior. However, the paper tests on natural and medical images, not astronomical data, and CLIP may not perform well on astronomical images without fine-tuning.

Reason

Post-hoc concept explanation with LLM and CLIP is highly relevant for interpreting deep learning models in astronomy, e.g., galaxy morphology classifiers.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Check if the concept vocabulary generation can be guided by domain-specific terms (e.g., 'tidal tail', 'star-forming region').
  • Look for evaluation of concept faithfulness on scientific classification tasks, not just natural images.
  • See if the method requires a pretrained CLIP model that works on astronomical images, or if fine-tuning is needed.
Abstract

Deep neural networks are widely deployed in high-stakes visual applications where interpretability is critical, yet existing explanations face a trade-off: post-hoc concept methods recover factors that are faithful to a model's behavior but unnamed, while naming and by-design methods attach human-readable concepts only by retraining or altering the classifier. We propose Language-Anchored Decomposition (LAD), a post-hoc framework that delivers concepts which are simultaneously named, faithful, and obtained without modifying the model. For each class, a large language model proposes a concept vocabulary that CLIP-based similarity maps localize across image regions. Inverting standard non-negative matrix factorization, LAD fixes these language-grounded maps as the coefficient matrix and learns only a concept basis that reconstructs the frozen encoder's activations, so naming becomes a structural constraint and the model's own feature geometry determines which concepts are retained. Removing this anchor preserves accuracy but collapses attribution faithfulness. Across natural-image, scene, and medical-imaging benchmarks, LAD produces spatially precise explanations that are decision-relevant under both concept insertion and deletion, while uniquely providing stable, human-interpretable concept names.

SpaCellAgent: A Self-Evolving LLM-Based Multi-Agent Framework for Trajectory Analysis

Songhan Wang , Haoang Chi , He Li , Zhiheng Zhang , Jiayan Yuan , Cheems Wang , Hao Peng , Xinwang Liu , Wenjing Yang

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

This paper introduces SpaCellAgent, an LLM-based multi-agent framework that automates end-to-end spatiotemporal analysis and narrative generation for single-cell transcriptomics. The core idea is using multiple LLM agents for strategic workflow planning, a dynamic tool-orchestration engine for adaptive algorithm selection, and a self-evolution module that iteratively refines performance through feedback. For astronomy, this approach could automate complex multi-step analyses like SED fitting, spectral classification, or photometric redshift estimation across large survey datasets. A concrete workflow would be using this framework to automatically select and run the best deblending or source classification tools on JWST NIRCam images, adapting to different data quality regimes. A key caveat is that the framework is demonstrated on biological data, so adapting it to astronomy would require building a new tool library and validation metrics specific to astronomical tasks.

Reason

Directly relevant to LLM-based multi-agent frameworks for automating scientific workflows, which can transfer to astronomical data analysis pipelines.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Check if the multi-agent architecture is modular enough to swap in astronomy-specific tools (e.g., EAZY, CIGALE, SExtractor).
  • Look for how the self-evolution module handles feedback—does it require human-in-the-loop or can it use automated validation metrics?
  • Examine the tool-orchestration engine: does it support heterogeneous data types (images, spectra, catalogs) as in astronomy?
Abstract

Spatial and Single-cell transcriptomics are transformative in deciphering cellular dynamics. As the fundamental paradigm for reconstructing cell developmental paths, trajectory inference (TI) is critical. However, existing methods require extensive manual intervention and proficiency in heterogeneous tools, posing a significant barrier to efficient TI analysis. To bridge this gap, we propose SpaCellAgent, an autonomous large language model (LLM) multi-agent framework that automates end-to-end spatiotemporal analysis and narrative generation. SpaCellAgent utilizes a multi-agent architecture for strategic workflow planning, a dynamic tool-orchestration engine for adaptive algorithm selection, and a self-evolution module that iteratively refines performance through feedback. We evaluate SpaCellAgent on six heterogeneous datasets encompassing complex temporal developmental trajectories, diverse sequencing platforms, and spatially-resolved tissue architectures. SpaCellAgent consistently demonstrates over 40\% improvement in analytical efficiency while maintaining expert-aligned performance. By converting natural language specifications into optimized analytical workflows and fully automating the pipeline, SpaCellAgent democratizes advanced spatiotemporal modeling and establishes a scalable, agent-driven paradigm for computational biology.
