Frozen Foundation-Model Embeddings Discard Small-Lesion Signal in Chest Radiography: Implications for Pre-Deployment Evaluation

Raajitha Muthyala , Zhenan Yin , Alekhya Jilla , Frank Li , Theo Dapamede , Bardia Khosravi , Mohammadreza Chavoshi , Judy Gichoya , Saptarshi Purkayastha

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

This paper systematically probes frozen vision transformer (ViT) embeddings to understand where small-scale, low-contrast signal is lost in chest radiography. It finds that global aggregation (CLS token or patch-mean) discards small-lesion signal, but this signal is recoverable from patch tokens when a region of interest is known. For astronomy, this is crucial: JWST images contain small, faint sources (e.g., high-redshift galaxies, AGN) that could be similarly suppressed by frozen foundation models. A concrete workflow would be to use patch-level features instead of global embeddings for detecting and characterizing little red dots or other compact sources in JWST images. A key caveat is that the study is on medical images, not astronomical ones, and the nature of 'signal' (lesions vs. galaxies) differs; the findings need validation on astronomical data.

Reason

Directly investigates signal loss in frozen foundation model embeddings for medical imaging, with clear parallels to astronomical imaging where small, low-contrast sources (e.g., high-redshift galaxies, little red dots) are critical.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Check if the paper provides any analysis of the spatial scale at which signal is lost—is there a characteristic size threshold?
  • Look for the perturbation panel details: what types of small-scale perturbations were tested, and how do they relate to astronomical sources (e.g., PSF size, surface brightness)?
  • Examine the region-aware probe: does the method require a perfect region of interest, or is it robust to imperfect localization?
Abstract

Frozen vision-transformer (ViT) foundation-model embeddings increasingly serve as the substrate for downstream chest-radiography (CXR) pipelines, yet where small-scale, low-contrast signal is retained or lost in the frozen forward pass has not been systematically quantified across architectures, pretraining domains, and objectives. We probed five frozen ViTs (RAD-DINO, DINOv2-B/14, DINOv3 ViT-7B, BiomedCLIP, MedSigLIP) and a frozen DINO-pretrained ResNet-50 architectural control across three large CXR cohorts (NIH-CXR14, MIMIC-CXR, Emory-CXR; aggregate pool n=492,724) and ChestX-Det10 (n=3,543; 1,462 small-lesion bounding boxes across Calcification, Nodule, Mass). Each model was evaluated with a small-scale-perturbation panel and a region-aware bounding-box-stratified probe on real lesions, comparing three pooling modes from the same forward pass: classification token (CLS), patch-mean (mean over all final-layer patch tokens), and bounding-box-restricted patch-local. On the perturbation panel, CLS embeddings sat at the chance floor (area under the ROC curve [AUC] 0.500-0.524); patch-mean was indistinguishable from CLS on iso-blur and reticular-fine cells but rose with CLS on larger directional-blur footprints, while disease AUC on globally decided tasks ranged 0.642-0.913. Patch-local probes recovered AUC ~1.0 from the same forward pass (per-model mean improvement +0.412 to +0.488); the ResNet-50 control reproduced the chance floor. On ChestX-Det10, image-level CLS classification showed within-class small-versus-large stratum gaps up to +0.243 AUC; bounding-box-level patch-local pooling on the same forward pass recovered AUC >= 0.899 on every (model x class) cell. Frozen ViT embeddings silently suppress small-scale signal at the global-aggregation step; the signal is recoverable from patch tokens conditional on a region of interest.

TimeRouter: Efficient and Adaptive Routing of Time-Series Foundation Models

Kanghui Ning , Yushan Jiang , Kashif Rasul , Anderson Schneider , Yuriy Nevmyvaka , Dongjin Song

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

This paper presents TimeRouter, a framework for efficiently routing between multiple pretrained time-series foundation models (TSFMs) without invoking an LLM at inference time. It uses a lightweight learned routing head, selective gating, and ensemble fallback to adaptively select the best expert model for each forecasting task. For astronomy, this is directly applicable: different TSFMs may excel at different types of light curves (e.g., periodic variables, stochastic AGN, transients). A concrete workflow could involve using TimeRouter to combine several TSFMs for classifying variable stars or predicting AGN flares from LSST or TESS data. A key caveat is that the paper evaluates on the GIFT-EVAL benchmark (general time-series), not astronomical datasets, and the TSFMs used may not be pretrained on astronomical data.

Reason

Directly addresses routing between time-series foundation models, which is highly relevant for astronomical time-series analysis (e.g., light curves, variable stars, AGN variability) and for building agentic systems.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Check if the paper provides any analysis of which TSFMs are selected for different time-series characteristics (e.g., periodicity, trend, noise level).
  • Look for details on the routing head architecture—is it interpretable? Can we see why a particular model is chosen?
  • Examine the computational overhead of the routing framework: is it truly lightweight enough for real-time or large-scale astronomical surveys?
Abstract

Time-series foundation models (TSFMs) are increasingly explored as predictive experts within emerging agentic time-series systems. However, TSFMs exhibit heterogeneous inductive biases, and no single model consistently dominates across forecasting regimes, making expert selection a critical challenge. Existing systems often delegate this decision to LLM-based controllers, incurring substantial inference overhead. We present TimeRouter, an efficient routing framework that leverages empirical complementarity across a pool of pretrained TSFMs through lightweight discriminative routing, selective gating, and ensemble fallback. Concretely, TimeRouter combines a learned routing head, a selective gate, and an ensemble fallback, enabling adaptive expert selection without invoking an LLM at inference time. TimeRouter achieves state-of-the-art performance on the GIFT-EVAL leaderboard, with an LB MASE of 0.6765. Beyond benchmark performance, our ablation studies provide empirical insights into TSFM routing design, highlighting the importance of pool composition and selective gating. Taken together, these results position TimeRouter as a modular and lightweight routing layer for future agentic time-series systems built upon foundation-model pools. Parameter-Efficient Adapter Tuning for Tabular-Image Multimodal Learning

Jiaqi Luo

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

1. The paper tackles the problem of efficiently combining tabular data (like structured catalog attributes) with image data for multimodal learning, without the high cost of full fine-tuning. 2. The main idea is TI-Adapter, which freezes pretrained encoders and inserts small trainable adapter modules after the tabular embedding and within the image branch, achieving competitive performance with far fewer parameters. 3. For astronomy, this could enable efficient fine-tuning of models on JWST or Roman images paired with photometric or spectroscopic catalog features, such as redshift, magnitude, or color. 4. A concrete workflow would be taking a pretrained vision encoder (e.g., for galaxy morphology) and a tabular encoder for catalog data, then using TI-Adapter to jointly fine-tune on a specific survey like COSMOS or CEERS. 5. A key caveat is that the paper tests on 20 general tabular-image datasets, not astronomical ones, so performance on high-redshift galaxy or AGN classification tasks is unknown and would require validation.

Reason

Directly addresses tabular-image multimodal learning with parameter-efficient adapters, which could be applied to astronomical datasets combining image (e.g., JWST) and tabular (e.g., photometric catalog) data.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Check if the adapter architecture supports different types of tabular features (e.g., continuous, categorical) common in astronomical catalogs.
  • Look for ablation studies on adapter placement and whether the method scales to very large tabular feature spaces (e.g., hundreds of photometric bands).
  • Examine whether the method can handle missing data in tabular inputs, which is common in astronomical surveys.
Abstract

Tabular-image multimodal learning aims to improve predictive modeling by jointly using structured tabular attributes and visual data. Although pretrained encoders provide strong modality-specific representations, full fine-tuning can be computationally expensive, while keeping encoders frozen may limit task-specific adaptation. We propose the Tabular-Image Adapter (TI-Adapter), a modality-specific adapter-based fine-tuning framework for efficient multimodal adaptation. TI-Adapter freezes the pretrained tabular encoder and learns an adapter after the extracted tabular embedding, while adapting the image branch with embedding-level and bottleneck-level adapters instead of full fine-tuning. Experiments on 20 tabular-image datasets show that TI-Adapter achieves competitive or better predictive performance than full fine-tuning while using substantially fewer trainable parameters. Ablation studies further demonstrate the importance of adapter placement for balancing performance and practical efficiency.

7 2606.11522

Search Discipline for Long-Horizon Research Agents

Adithya Srinivasan , Devesh Paragiri

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

This paper identifies a fundamental problem in autoresearch agents: when scientific validity is multi-dimensional (e.g., across regions or cohorts), optimizing an aggregate metric can select a candidate that inverts the structure underneath, breaking the model for important subgroups. It demonstrates this inversion on an ecosystem model where the top-scoring candidate collapses boreal regions while a slightly lower-scoring one preserves them. The proposed solution is a search-discipline protocol with an external control loop that audits candidates on disaggregated behavior after the agent has decided. For astronomy, this is directly relevant: an agent optimizing a global metric like average photometric redshift accuracy might select a model that fails for high-redshift galaxies or little red dots. The external audit loop could be implemented to check performance on specific subpopulations (e.g., by redshift, color, or morphology) before accepting a candidate model or pipeline.

Reason

Paper directly addresses a critical failure mode in research agents that optimize aggregate metrics, which is highly relevant for automated scientific discovery in astronomy where multi-dimensional validity (e.g., across redshift bins, galaxy types) is common.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Look for the formal definition of 'inversion' and how to detect it in practice, especially for astronomical metrics like classification accuracy across redshift bins.
  • Check whether the external control loop requires a separate validation dataset or can work with the same data used for optimization.
  • Examine the computational overhead of the audit loop: does it require full re-evaluation on all subgroups, or can it be approximated?
  • Look for examples of how the protocol handles multiple competing validity dimensions (e.g., accuracy vs. completeness vs. physical plausibility).
Abstract

Autoresearch agents now propose, evaluate, and select scientific candidates against a metric, and that metric is usually an aggregate reduced over a heterogeneous space of regions, slices, or cohorts. We show that when scientific validity lives in that disaggregated structure, the aggregate can rank the wrong candidate first. The headline number improves while the structure underneath inverts, so a decision made on the number accepts a candidate that quietly breaks the model. The failure is not domain-specific. It appears wherever a candidate's validity is multi-dimensional but its verifier is a single reduction. We demonstrate the inversion on a fire-model task in the Ecosystem Demography model. The highest-scoring candidate and a slightly lower one are within noise of each other on global score, yet the top-scoring one collapses the protected boreal regions while the other preserves them. What separates them is the per-region behavior, not the headline number. This decision should not be left to the agent that produced the candidates. The agent optimizing the score is the last party likely to catch the score being wrong, and a prompt has no remaining turn once the agent has stopped. We move the decision to an external control loop that audits each candidate on its disaggregated behavior and acts after the agent has decided. It can demote a candidate the agent would have accepted, and it can reopen a run the agent had declared finished. Our contribution is the inversion finding itself, and a search-discipline protocol that decides on reviewable candidate-effect evidence instead of the score.

Physics-Distilled Neural Network enabled by Large Language Models for Manufacturing Process-Property Predictive Modeling

Ge Song , Kiarash Naghavi Khanghah , Anandkumar Patel , Rajiv Malhotra , Hongyi Xu

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

This paper addresses the problem of predicting process-property relationships in manufacturing with limited data. The key idea is to use LLMs to extract analytical physics priors from scientific literature, which are then integrated into a privileged teacher model (with a Graph-Masked Attention layer). This teacher's knowledge is distilled into a lightweight student predictor for fast, real-time inference. For astronomy, this is highly relevant: one could use LLMs to extract physical priors (e.g., SED templates, dust attenuation laws) from the literature and distill them into a model for predicting galaxy properties from sparse photometry. A concrete workflow could be using an LLM to read papers on high-redshift galaxy SEDs, extract prior knowledge, and train a fast student model for photometric redshift estimation. A key caveat is that the paper demonstrates this on manufacturing processes, not astronomical data, and the quality of LLM-extracted priors may be lower for complex astrophysical phenomena.

Reason

Uses LLMs to extract physics priors from literature and distills them into a lightweight model for process-property prediction, directly relevant to automated scientific discovery and data-scarce scenarios in astronomy.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Check how the LLM is prompted to extract physics priors—is the process automated and reproducible?
  • Look for the Graph-Masked Attention layer details: can it handle the irregular sampling and missing data common in astronomical catalogs?
  • Examine the distillation procedure: is the student model interpretable, or is it a black box? How is the uncertainty in the teacher's knowledge propagated?
Abstract

Predicting process-property relationships in manufacturing is often challenged by high experimental costs and the limited interpretability of complex 'black-box' models. This paper proposes a novel knowledge distillation framework designed to achieve high-accuracy predictions in data-scarce scenarios. The framework integrates analytical physics priors, which are systematically extracted from scientific literature via Large Language Models, into a privileged teacher model. We employ a Graph-Masked Attention layer to capture the complex physical dependencies among input variables showing strict setpoints or a combination of static and high-frequency temporal signatures. This privileged knowledge is distilled into a lightweight student predictor for inference. The feasibility and robustness of the framework are evaluated through a comprehensive experiment across five diverse manufacturing processes. To ensure statistical reliability, given the small dataset sizes, a repeated K-fold cross-validation technique is employed to quantify model stability and generalization. Results indicate that the proposed framework consistently achieves high predictive accuracy across all evaluated domains. Most importantly, the architecture demonstrates significant fault tolerance by maintaining robust predictive performance even in scenarios where LLM-derived analytical priors are suboptimal or incomplete. Furthermore, the student predictor achieves an inference frequency exceeding 6000 Hz, which facilitates real-time edge deployment on standard industrial hardware. This work provides a scalable solution for bridging the gap between theoretical physics and real-time industrial monitoring in data-limited environments.
