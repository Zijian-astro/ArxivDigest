## 1. Agentic Publication Protocol: An Attempt to Modernize Scientific Publication

- arXiv ID: `2606.27386`
- Authors: Sirui Lu , Xiao-Liang Qi
- Score: 9
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27386
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.27386
- Subjects: Digital Libraries (cs.DL) ; Artificial Intelligence (cs.AI)
- Reason: Directly proposes an agentic publication protocol for packaging papers with code, data, and agent instructions, highly relevant for reproducible research and scientific workflows.

Short digest:
This paper asks how to modernize scientific publication to include not just static manuscripts but also operational know-how (code, data, reproducibility instructions) that can be used by LLM agents. It proposes the Agentic Publication Protocol (APP), a lightweight repository format that packages a paper with code, data, environment info, and an agent-facing instruction file. The protocol allows an agent to explain the work, reproduce results, and support follow-up research. For astronomy, this is extremely relevant because it could enable fully reproducible workflows for JWST data analysis, where an agent could re-run SED fitting or spectral extraction from a published paper. A concrete workflow could be using APP to publish a paper on high-redshift galaxy classification, allowing other researchers to run the same analysis on new data. A key caveat is that the protocol is new and may require community adoption and tooling support.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Check the detailed specification of the agent-facing instruction file and how it handles environment setup.
- Look for example repositories or case studies that demonstrate the protocol in action.
- Examine the discussion of agent skills and how they are evaluated.

## 2. CalBrief: A Pilot Diagnostic Benchmark for Evidence-Calibrated Scientific Briefing with Large Language Models

- arXiv ID: `2606.27383`
- Authors: Yu Fu , Yongqi Kang , Yong Zhao
- Score: 7
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27383
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.27383
- Subjects: Digital Libraries (cs.DL) ; Artificial Intelligence (cs.AI); Computation and Language (cs.CL)
- Reason: Directly addresses evidence-calibrated scientific briefing with LLMs, relevant for literature review and research agent workflows.

Short digest:
This paper asks whether LLMs can calibrate research takeaways to the strength and scope of supporting evidence, a task called evidence-calibrated scientific briefing. It introduces CalBrief, a framework for generating package-level takeaways with evidence strength, scope boundaries, and missing-evidence caveats. The main finding is that structured organization improves role and gap reasoning, but an explicit strength-calibration policy is systematically over-conservative. For astronomy, this is highly relevant because it could help automate literature review and synthesis, e.g., summarizing findings from multiple papers on high-redshift galaxies. A concrete workflow could be using CalBrief to generate a briefing on JWST observations of little red dots, with calibrated confidence statements. A key caveat is that the benchmark uses only 16 evidence packages, and the over-conservatism issue may require tuning for astronomy-specific literature.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Look for the specific evaluation schema and how 'evidence strength' is defined and annotated.
- Check whether the paper provides examples of generated takeaways and how they compare to human-written ones.
- Examine the diagnostic analysis that separates conservatism into label space expansion vs. pipeline policy.

## 3. When Search Agents Should Ask: DiscoBench for Clarification-Aware Deep Search

- arXiv ID: `2606.27669`
- Authors: Yiling Tao , Shihan Deng , Meiling Tao , Pengzhi Wei , Zhichao Hu , Zhihao Zhu
- Score: 7
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27669
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.27669
- Subjects: Computation and Language (cs.CL)
- Reason: The paper directly addresses a key limitation of LLM-based search agents: handling ambiguous or underspecified queries. This is highly relevant for building research agents for astronomy that can interact with users to clarify scientific questions.

Short digest:
This paper addresses the problem that LLM-based search agents often fail when user queries are vague, underspecified, or factually incorrect, because ambiguity propagates along multi-step reasoning chains. It introduces DiscoBench, a benchmark for evaluating whether search agents can proactively identify ambiguity, ask effective clarification questions, and recover correct reasoning paths through user interaction. The benchmark covers 211 samples across 11 domains with four ambiguity types. For astronomy, this is directly relevant for building research agents that assist astronomers with literature review, data analysis, or experimental design. A concrete workflow would be to use a clarification-aware agent to help an astronomer refine a vague query like 'find papers on high-redshift galaxies' into specific criteria (e.g., redshift range, JWST data, AGN content). A key limitation is that the benchmark is domain-general and may not cover astronomy-specific ambiguities (e.g., instrument names, coordinate systems). The paper does not provide a ready-to-use agent, only an evaluation framework.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Check the ambiguity types covered (e.g., missing constraints, conflicting information) and whether they map to astronomy use cases.
- Look for the user simulator design and whether it can be adapted to astronomy-specific interactions.
- Examine the evaluation metrics (task utility, ambiguity detection, interaction strategy, cost efficiency) and whether they are relevant for scientific research agents.
- Check if the benchmark includes any scientific or technical domains that could inform astronomy adaptation.

## 4. The Weakest Link Tells It All: Outcome-Supervised Process Reward Modeling via Learnable Credit Assignment

- arXiv ID: `2606.27739`
- Authors: Tianyu Jia , Yue Fang , Hongxin Ding , Rihong Qiu , Zhibang Yang , Zhijing Wu , Xu Chu , Junfeng Zhao , Yasha Wang
- Score: 7
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27739
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.27739
- Subjects: Machine Learning (cs.LG)
- Reason: Addresses process reward modeling for LLM reasoning, which is directly relevant for building research agents that can verify their own reasoning steps in scientific workflows.

Short digest:
This paper tackles the problem of training process reward models (PRMs) for LLM reasoning without expensive stepwise annotations. It proposes LCA, an outcome-supervised PRM framework that uses a learnable credit assignment mechanism based on the 'weakest link' principle, formalized as a Multiple Instance Learning problem. This could enable building research agents that can identify which reasoning steps are incorrect when solving complex astronomical problems, such as interpreting spectra or planning observations. One could imagine using LCA to train an agent that checks its own logic when fitting SEDs or classifying sources. A caveat is that the method is evaluated on math reasoning tasks and may need adaptation for scientific reasoning with domain-specific knowledge.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Look for experiments on multi-step reasoning tasks with long chains, similar to scientific workflows.
- Check whether the credit assignment can handle heterogeneous reasoning steps (e.g., mixing numerical calculations with logical deductions).
- Examine the Bayes consistency proof: does it rely on assumptions that hold for scientific reasoning?

## 5. SHIFT: Gate-Modulated Activation Steering for Knowledge Conflict Mitigation in Retrieval-Augmented Generation

- arXiv ID: `2606.27786`
- Authors: Ruochang Li , Pengcheng Huang , Zhenghao Liu , Yukun Yan , Huiyuan Xie , Yu Gu , Ge Yu , Maosong Sun
- Score: 7
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27786
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.27786
- Subjects: Computation and Language (cs.CL) ; Artificial Intelligence (cs.AI)
- Reason: Directly addresses knowledge conflict in retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), which is highly relevant for building research agents that combine retrieved astronomical literature with parametric knowledge.

Short digest:
This paper tackles the problem of conflicts between retrieved context and parametric knowledge in retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) systems. It introduces SHIFT, a framework that reformulates neuron-level modification as learnable gate modulation, using fewer than 0.01% trainable parameters to adaptively regulate internal activations. The main result is effective mitigation of knowledge conflicts across six datasets while preserving general model capabilities. This matters for astronomy because research agents that retrieve papers or data from archives (e.g., ADS, MAST) and combine them with pre-trained knowledge could benefit from this conflict resolution. A concrete workflow could be an agent that retrieves JWST papers and synthesizes findings with its own knowledge about galaxy formation. A limitation is that the method is evaluated on general-domain QA datasets, not on scientific or astronomical data.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Check if the gate modulation approach is domain-agnostic or requires task-specific tuning.
- Look for any discussion of scaling to long-context retrieval scenarios common in scientific literature review.
- Check whether the method handles conflicting numerical or spectral data, not just textual facts.
