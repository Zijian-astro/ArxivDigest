A Unified Framework for Context-Aware and Relation-Aware Graph Retrieval-Augmented Generation

Haoyang Zhong , Yifei Sun , Antong Zhang , Chunping Wang , Lei Chen , Yang Yang

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

This paper introduces HyGRAG, a hierarchical graph RAG framework that integrates contextual and relational information by constructing summaries over hybrid graphs with chunk and entity nodes. It improves multi-hop reasoning accuracy by 9.7%. For astronomy, this could enable an LLM agent to retrieve and synthesize information from diverse sources like papers, catalogs, and images, aiding in literature review or cross-matching multi-wavelength data. The dynamic update capability is useful for evolving surveys like JWST. However, the paper's evaluation is on general QA datasets, not astronomy-specific tasks.

Reason

Proposes a hierarchical graph RAG framework that could be applied to astronomical literature review, data analysis, and multi-modal knowledge integration.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Check if the hierarchical graph construction can handle astronomical entities (e.g., galaxies, AGN) and their relationships.
  • Look for details on how LLM-based summaries are generated and whether they are reliable for scientific content.
  • Assess the computational cost for large-scale astronomical corpora and whether the method scales.
Abstract

Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) has emerged as a paradigm for enhancing large language models (LLMs) with external knowledge, yet existing graph-based methods face a fundamental limitation: entity-centric and chunk-centric approaches operate on representations anchored to original text without true knowledge fusion. While entity-centric methods connect logically related content and chunk-centric methods preserve context, both retrieve information separately through similarity search, missing emergent understanding from their synthesis. In this paper, we propose HyGRAG, a hierarchical graph RAG framework that transcends source documents by addressing three core challenges: constructing summaries that genuinely integrate contextual and relational information, leveraging these synthesized representations to access emergent knowledge during retrieval, and efficiently updating hierarchical structures for dynamic corpora. Specifically, we design hierarchical index structures over hybrid graphs with both chunk and entity nodes, then iteratively cluster them and generate LLM-based summaries. Then, we design context and relation-aware retrieval that searches across all abstraction levels while expanding through community membership. Moreover, we enable dynamic knowledge update through attachment-based algorithms with only local re-summarization. Experimental results show that HyGRAG improves the average accuracy of multi-hop reasoning tasks by 9.7%, while maintaining reasonable efficiency.

HLS-GPT: A Generative Pretrained Transformer (GPT) for Continental-Scale NASA Harmonized Landsat and Sentinel-2 (HLS) Reflectance Reconstruction Across All Bands on Arbitrary Dates

Junjie Li , Hankui K. Zhang , David P. Roy

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

This paper addresses the problem of reconstructing missing or irregular satellite reflectance observations across all spectral bands and any date. It presents HLS-GPT, a hierarchical Transformer model trained on 9 years of NASA HLS data from 0.25 million pixels across the US. The model uses a random cropping and masking strategy to learn to reconstruct masked reflectance values, achieving RMSE below 0.026 for all bands. This is directly relevant to astronomy because similar Transformer-based methods could be applied to reconstruct missing photometric data from surveys like JWST, Roman, or Rubin. For example, one could train a model to predict missing photometric bands or fill in gaps in light curves for variable sources. The method is designed for single-pixel time series, which could be adapted for astronomical source photometry. A key caveat is that the model is trained on Landsat/Sentinel-2 data, so adaptation to astronomical instruments would require retraining on appropriate data.

Reason

Directly applies a generative pretrained Transformer to reconstruct satellite reflectance time series, highly relevant to astronomical survey data analysis.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Check the hierarchical Transformer architecture details to see if it can handle different spectral band configurations like JWST NIRCam filters.
  • Look for the masking strategy and how it handles irregularly sampled observations, which is common in astronomy.
  • Examine the evaluation metrics (RMSE, relative RMSE) to assess if they are appropriate for astronomical photometric reconstruction.
  • Check if the model can be applied to multi-pixel images or only single-pixel time series.
Abstract

Recent deep learning methods for Landsat and Sentinel-2 reflectance time series reconstruction remain limited by restricted spectral coverage, limited geographic scalability, or patch-based designs with short temporal contexts. We present HLS-GPT, a large-scale generative pretrained Transformer model for reconstructing NASA Harmonized Landsat Sentinel-2 30 m surface reflectance for all bands, any date, and any pixel location. HLS-GPT uses a hierarchical Transformer architecture to handle the different spectral band configurations of Landsat and Sentinel-2 and operates on single-pixel 12-month time series. To capture geographic and seasonal variability, the model was trained with nine years of HLS time series from more than 0.25 million training pixels across the conterminous United States. A random cropping and masking strategy extracts 12-month periods with varying start dates across epochs, masks 50% of valid observations, and trains the model to reconstruct the masked reflectance values from the remaining observations. Evaluation using more than 62,000 independent test pixels shows robust reconstruction under diverse land surface conditions, including complex crop phenology and sparse, irregular observations. Leave-one-observation-out evaluation achieved reconstruction RMSE below 0.026 for all HLS spectral bands, with relative RMSE below 35% for visible bands and below 13% for other bands. Red-edge band errors were comparable to red and near-infrared errors despite the absence of red-edge bands on Landsat. Sensitivity analyses that randomly masked 10% to 90% of test observations showed only modest degradation when 10% to 50% of observations were masked, with all-band RMSE below 0.028. Image reconstruction over nine independent 109 by 109 km CONUS HLS tiles further demonstrates that HLS-GPT outperforms two conventional methods and the NASA-IBM Prithvi model.

GeoDisaster: Benchmarking Orchestrated Agents for Operational Disaster Geo-Intelligence

Maram Hasan , Aman Verma , Savitra Roy , Hariseetharam Gunduboina , Daksh Jain , Muhammad Haris Khan , Subhasis Chaudhuri , Biplab Banerjee

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

This paper introduces GeoDisaster, a benchmark for operational geospatial disaster reasoning, and proposes an orchestrated multi-agent framework with 18 disaster-oriented tools. The agents coordinate through explicit execution contracts and are trained via failure-aware supervised fine-tuning and reinforcement learning. For astronomy, this agentic approach could be adapted to coordinate specialized tools for JWST data analysis, source classification, or multi-wavelength catalog matching. A concrete workflow could involve an agent that retrieves relevant literature, runs SED fitting, and queries catalogs to classify a high-redshift galaxy candidate. A limitation is that the framework is designed for disaster response and may require significant adaptation for astronomical use cases.

Reason

Introduces a multi-agent framework for disaster geo-intelligence that could transfer to astronomical data analysis workflows.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Examine the multi-agent coordination mechanism and whether it can be generalized to astronomical tools
  • Check if the tool-use and evidence-grounding methods are model-agnostic and could work with astronomical databases
  • Look for details on the reinforcement learning reward design that might transfer to scientific reasoning tasks
Abstract

Remote-sensing vision-language models (RS-VLMs) have advanced Earth-observation analysis toward visual interpretation and instruction-following, yet fall short of operational geo-intelligence, which demands tool-grounded spatial reasoning and structured, evidence-backed decisions. We introduce GeoDisaster, an operational geospatial disaster reasoning benchmark with 2,921 verified instances across 43 question types and five task families: deforestation monitoring, multi-hazard analysis, building-damage assessment, flood-safe routing, and Sentinel-1 SAR flood monitoring. Instances integrate heterogeneous EO/GIS evidence-optical and SAR imagery, raster masks, vector geometries, road networks, and exposure layers-spanning hazard detection, damage assessment, exposure estimation, and diagnostic report generation. Ground-truth answers are grounded in executable geospatial workflows and deterministic consistency checks, removing the need for language-model annotation. We further propose an orchestrated multi-agent framework with 18 disaster-oriented tools, where role-specialized agents coordinate through explicit execution contracts, aligned via Role-Contract Expectation Alignment (RCEA): failure-aware supervised fine-tuning combined with contract-grounded reinforcement learning over dense step-level signals. Experiments show that GeoDisaster challenges existing RS-VLMs and agentic systems, while RCEA improves tool use, evidence grounding, state consistency, and decision generation.

Dissecting model behavior through agent trajectories

Gaurav Gupta , Vatshank Chaturvedi , Jun Huan , Anoop Deoras

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

This paper argues that AI agent performance depends not just on the model but on the harness (the system that connects the model to tools and execution loops), and introduces the 'intent-execution gap'—the mismatch between what the model intends and what the harness executes. The authors develop a simple harness called Simple Strands Agent (SSA) that reproduces or improves on pass@1 performance across multiple model families on agentic benchmarks (SWE-Pro, SWE-Verified, Terminal-Bench-2). They then analyze 138k trajectories to reveal model-level differences in problem-solving behavior, such as edit frequency, testing activity, and phase transitions. For astronomy, this is highly relevant because building research agents for tasks like literature review, data analysis, or running pipelines requires careful harness design. The trajectory analysis methodology could be used to debug and optimize astronomy-specific agents. One could imagine using SSA to build an agent that queries astronomical databases, runs analysis code, and writes reports, then analyzing trajectories to improve its performance. A key caveat is that the benchmarks are software engineering tasks, not astronomy, so the specific patterns may not transfer directly. The harness design principles are generic, but adapting to astronomy tools (e.g., TOPCAT, IRAF, or Python libraries) would require customization.

Reason

This paper analyzes AI agent trajectories to understand the 'intent-execution gap' between model capabilities and harness behavior. This is directly relevant to building effective research agents for astronomy, as it provides tools for debugging and optimizing agent performance on scientific workflows.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Check the SSA harness design: what tools and execution loops does it support? Could these be adapted for astronomical tools (e.g., querying Vizier, running SExtractor)?
  • Look for the trajectory analysis methodology: how are code state-spaces represented? This could be used to analyze astronomy agent behavior.
  • Examine the model-specific preferences identified: do they suggest that certain models are better for certain types of tasks (e.g., coding vs. reasoning)?
  • Check the evaluation benchmarks: are they purely software engineering, or could the methodology be applied to scientific workflows?
Abstract

AI agent performance is not just a modeling problem, it is fundamentally a systems problem. The advanced capabilities of models are realized through agent harnesses. Therefore, a gap between model assumptions and harness behavior can easily prevent the model's full capabilities from translating into agent performance. We formalize this as the `intent-execution' gap: the mismatch between what the model intends and what the harness executes, and vice versa. We argue that minimizing this intent-execution gap is as important as other aspects of harness design such as tools and execution loops. To illustrate the impact of this harness-model alignment, we develop a simple and customizable harness called `Simple Strands Agent' (SSA). SSA aims to find the bulk of common patterns which generalize across different model families (such as Claude, Gemini, GPT, Grok, Qwen), as well as a small number of model-specific preferences. We make two contributions: (i) we $\textbf{reproduce or improve on the pass@1}$ performance reported by diverse model-provider families on popular agentic benchmarks (SWE-Pro, SWE-Verified and Terminal-Bench-2), and (ii) building on an $\textbf{analysis of 138k trajectories generated by SSA}$, we look beyond the $\texttt{pass@1}$ numbers which tend to be relatively even across frontier models. By representing agent trajectories in code state-spaces, we observe model-level differences in problem-solving behavior. Finer-grained metrics such as edit frequency, testing activity, and phase-transitions reveal how individual models allocate effort across different stages of autonomous problem solving.

SpatioTemporal Causal Network Diagnostics for Geographic Tipping Point Early Warning

Zhaoyuan Yu , Zhangyong Liang

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

This paper addresses the problem of early warning for geographic tipping points, such as ecosystem collapse or ice sheet melting, using spatiotemporal data. The main idea is to represent the geographic field as a directed causal network and detect vulnerable subnetworks using transfer entropy and dynamic mode decomposition. For astronomy, this method could be applied to detect early warning signals for transient events or phase transitions in astrophysical systems, such as AGN variability or galaxy evolution. The framework is scalable and interpretable, making it suitable for large survey data like LSST. A caveat is that the method is validated on sea-surface temperature data, not astronomical data, and may require adaptation for different noise characteristics.

Reason

Spatiotemporal causal network for early warning of tipping points is highly relevant for studying climate or astrophysical systems, and the method could transfer to astronomical time series analysis.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Look for details on how transfer entropy is computed for large spatial grids
  • Check if the method can handle irregularly sampled time series common in astronomy
  • Examine the sensitivity to hyperparameters like subnetwork size and significance thresholds
Abstract

Geographic tipping points in ecosystems, climate subsystems, or ice sheets pose severe challenges for localized early warning. Classical spatial indicators such as Moran's I summarize global spatial structure, but they struggle with three issues: spatial dilution, Euclidean assumptions, and correlated noise. This paper introduces SpatioTemporal Causal Network Diagnostics (ST-CND), a framework that addresses these three issues by representing the geographic field as a time-evolving directed causal network. The core workflow is as follows: (1) infer which spatial nodes help predict other nodes via transfer entropy, replacing fixed Euclidean neighborhoods with data-driven information-flow topology; (2) estimate local recovery rates within each candidate subnetwork via dynamic mode decomposition; and (3) identify the most vulnerable subnetwork by combining three signals, namely high internal fluctuation, high internal synchronization, and low external coupling, thereby suppressing false alarms from spatially correlated noise. Validated on synthetic bifurcations and two observational sea-surface temperature benchmarks, namely Indo-Pacific SST and North Atlantic AMOC, ST-CND delivers localized and interpretable warnings. On the AMOC task, it achieves an AUROC of 0.783 and a critical-subnetwork IoU of 0.378, outperforming recurrence-network and lambda-AR1 baselines. The framework provides an interpretable and scalable pipeline for spatial early warning in Earth system science.
