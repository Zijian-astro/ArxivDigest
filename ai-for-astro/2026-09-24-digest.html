Title: Just-in-Time Memory: Learning to Curate Task-Adaptive Memory for LLM Agents
Authors: Yefan Zhou , Yang Li , Zeyu Leo Liu , Semih Yavuz , Shafiq Joty
arXiv ID: 2609.27334
Score: 8
Reason: This paper is directly about LLM agent memory curation, proposing read-time task-adaptive memory instead of write-time summarization. It is highly relevant to research agents and automated scientific workflows that must reuse past analysis experience.
Short digest: The paper tackles a core problem in agentic memory: most systems decide what to remember at write time, distilling a trajectory into a fixed artifact before the future query is known, which irreversibly discards information. Its main idea, Just-in-Time Memory (JitMem), keeps raw trajectories and defers curation to read time, when the current task is known, so a memory curator can synthesize a compact payload tailored to the immediate need. Because that payload is consumed on the same task, the curator can be trained directly from immediate task success, avoiding long-horizon credit assignment. JitMem outperforms no-memory and write-time memory baselines on ALFWorld, WebShop, and tau-squared-bench, and even an untrained curator is competitive, showing that read-time curation itself drives much of the gain. For astronomy, this is relevant to research agents that accumulate experience across many data-analysis sessions, such as repeated SED fitting, spectral line searches, or catalog cross-matching, where the right past experience depends on the current target. A concrete workflow would be to store full analysis traces and let a curator assemble task-specific context when a new JWST target or question arrives. The caveat is that the benchmarks are text-based interactive environments, so it is untested whether read-time curation handles numerical, image, or spectral evidence well.
Full-text inspection checklist: Check how the memory curator is trained and whether it requires task-specific fine-tuning or works zero-shot; Look for the comparison between untrained and trained curators to gauge how much training actually adds; Check whether the method handles multimodal or numerical traces, or only text-based trajectories; Assess whether the read-time curation cost is acceptable for long scientific workflows

Title: False-science induction in autonomous scientific discovery

arXiv ID: 2609.27883
Score: 8
Reason: This paper is highly relevant to automated scientific discovery, showing how record-integrity failures can mislead closed-loop discovery systems, which directly applies to AI-driven astronomy pipelines and neural surrogates for physical measurements.
Short digest: The paper addresses a subtle failure mode in closed-loop autonomous discovery, where legitimate physical objects and measurements are paired incorrectly, causing neural surrogates to learn record-induced associations that do not reflect the true object-outcome relationship while marginal data distributions remain unchanged. It calls this false-science induction and shows across green fluorescent protein fitness and materials band-gap prediction loops that coherent paired misbinding systematically redirects experimental budgets toward low-performing basins, whereas same-volume random swaps have negligible effects. The key finding is that error coherence, not raw error frequency, controls budget misallocation, and the resulting binding identifiability boundary supports monitored-axis quarantines and feedback-conflict triage. For astronomy, this is directly relevant to AI-driven discovery loops that pair observations with labels, spectra with redshifts, or images with catalog entries, where a systematic misbinding could silently corrupt a neural surrogate and waste telescope time. A concrete workflow would be a closed-loop system that selects high-redshift galaxy candidates for follow-up spectroscopy, where a misbinding between photometric measurements and object identities could redirect observations toward a low-value region. The caveat is that the domains are biology and materials, so the specific diagnostics would need to be re-derived for astronomical data streams, but the conceptual warning is strong and transferable.
Full-text inspection checklist: Check whether the misbinding mechanism is formalized in a way that maps onto astronomical data association problems; Look for the monitored-axis quarantine and feedback-conflict triage procedures and whether they are implementable; Assess whether the experiments include any surrogate model architecture resembling astronomical SED or image models; Check whether the paper provides diagnostics for detecting coherent misbinding before budget is wasted
