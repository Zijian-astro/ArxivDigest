## 1. SciToolAgent-Evo: An Ontology-Aware Self-Evolving Agent for Open-World Scientific Tool Acquisition

- arXiv ID: `2607.28692`
- Authors: Yuqi Tang , Chenyi Zhou , Libin Wang , Keyan Ding , Qiang Zhang , Huajun Chen
- Score: 7
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28692
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.28692
- Subjects: Artificial Intelligence (cs.AI) ; Computation and Language (cs.CL)
- Reason: Directly relevant to LLM agents for scientific workflows and tool acquisition, which is a key interest. The ontology-aware self-evolving design could transfer to astronomy tool integration.

Short digest:
This paper tackles the problem of LLM agents being limited to predefined tool sets in dynamic scientific environments. It introduces an agent that uses an evolving memory and an ontology graph to acquire new tools, balancing exploration and exploitation via a bandit algorithm. For astronomy, this could help agents automatically discover and integrate new analysis tools for JWST or survey data. A concrete workflow would be an agent that learns to use new SED-fitting or source-classification codes on demand. However, the benchmark is generic scientific tasks, so transfer to astronomy-specific tools and data may require adaptation.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Check if the ontology graph can be customized for astronomy-specific tools and datasets
- Look for examples of tool acquisition in scientific domains beyond the provided benchmark
- Assess the computational overhead of the self-evolving memory in long-running astronomy workflows

## 4. Uncertainty-Aware Deepfake Detection via Multi-View Structural Learning

- arXiv ID: `2607.28769`
- Authors: Muhammad Umar Farooq , Kutub Uddin , Awais Khan , Khalid Malik
- Score: 2
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28769
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.28769
- Subjects: Computer Vision and Pattern Recognition (cs.CV)
- Reason: The paper focuses on deepfake detection, which is outside astronomy and astrophysics. The uncertainty-aware multi-stream approach is generic but not directly transferable to astronomical workflows.

Short digest:
This paper addresses the problem of detecting deepfakes under distribution shift, where models are often overconfident. The main idea is to combine visual, semantic, and structural evidence streams and use disagreement among them to calibrate uncertainty. This matters for astronomy only as a generic example of uncertainty estimation, but the domain and methods are not tailored to astronomical data. One could imagine adapting the disagreement-based uncertainty idea to multi-wavelength source classification, but the paper's focus on facial attributes and deepfakes limits direct transfer. A key caveat is that the method relies on domain-specific semantic constraints that do not exist in astronomical contexts.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Check whether the uncertainty calibration method is domain-agnostic or relies on face-specific semantics.
- Look for any discussion of how the multi-stream approach could generalize to non-visual data.
