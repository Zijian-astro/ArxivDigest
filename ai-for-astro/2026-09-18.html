ScientistTwo: Pioneering the Human Knowledge Frontier with Autonomous AI

Jaehyun Nam , Jinsung Yoon , Yanzhou Pan , Yubo Wang , Rui Meng , Parthasarathy Ranganathan , Tomas Pfister

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

The paper asks whether AI can autonomously push the frontier of scientific knowledge, given a fundamental problem from a human expert. The core idea is ScientistTwo, a multi-agent framework that establishes baselines, formulates hypotheses, coordinates specialized agents through an end-to-end discovery cycle, runs experiments with diverse datasets and metrics, performs automated ablation studies, and validates findings via a simulated peer-review rebuttal engine. In plain language, it is an AI system that tries to do the whole research loop by itself, from problem statement to paper and code. For astronomy, this is highly relevant because such a framework could be pointed at open problems like little red dot classification, high-redshift galaxy property inference, or AGN selection, and could automate literature review, hypothesis generation, and pipeline experiments. A concrete workflow could be adapting the multi-agent architecture to a JWST-focused discovery task, with agents for literature retrieval, data reduction, SED fitting, and result validation. The main caveat is that the benchmark is top-tier ML conferences, so you should check whether the framework can handle domain-specific physical constraints, noisy astronomical data, and the longer iteration cycles typical of observational science.

Reason

It presents a fully autonomous multi-agent framework for scientific discovery, which is directly aligned with your interest in research agents and automated scientific discovery methods for astronomy.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Look for how the multi-agent framework handles domain-specific physical constraints and whether it can incorporate astronomical priors
  • Check whether the automated peer-review engine is validated against real expert review or only AI review agents
  • Assess whether the framework can work with observational data that are noisy, heterogeneous, and not pre-cleaned
  • Check whether the codebase and agent orchestration are released and adaptable to astronomy workflows
Abstract

Scientific discovery is defined by the ability to identify the boundaries of existing knowledge and venture into unexplored territory. The ultimate vision for AI in science is problem-driven autonomous research: given a fundamental challenge by a human expert, the AI independently navigates the scientific landscape, uncovers theoretical and empirical bottlenecks, and systematically expands the frontier of knowledge. In this paper, we introduce ScientistTwo, a fully autonomous multi-agent framework designed to realize this vision. Specifically, ScientistTwo takes an initial problem as input, establishes state-of-the-art baselines, formulates novel hypotheses, and coordinates specialized agents to orchestrate an end-to-end discovery cycle without human intervention. Moreover, the framework rigorously conducts experiments using diverse datasets and metrics, refines methodologies through automated ablation studies, and validates research findings via a closed-loop simulated peer-review rebuttal engine. To evaluate ScientistTwo's capabilities against the highest standards of human scientific achievement, we benchmark it across papers accepted at top-tier conferences such as ICLR, ICML, and NeurIPS. As a result, ScientistTwo autonomously generates expert-level, publishable papers and fully verified, executable codebases. Its solutions consistently outperform human state-of-the-art models, and achieve higher average review ratings than human-authored papers under automated AI review agents. These results show that ScientistTwo is not merely an assistive tool but an autonomous scientific pioneer capable of pushing the frontiers of human discovery. Project website: this https URL

Less Is More: Graph-free Multimodal RAG via Multi-signal Late Fusion

Tithi Rakshit , Hongkuan Zhou , Lavdim Halilaj , Yuqicheng Zhu

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

The paper addresses the high cost and maintenance burden of graph-based multimodal retrieval-augmented generation for cross-document question answering. TrioRAG instead retrieves independently using three signals, the question, an anchor image, and a VLM-enhanced query generated from both, over a shared multi-vector index of page text and page images, and combines results with late fusion. It also introduces AutoQA, a multimodal automotive benchmark grounded in noisy web images, and reports that TrioRAG matches or outperforms graph-based systems while reducing cost and speeding up inference by 1.6 to 2.3 times. For astronomy, this is directly useful for building a literature assistant that can answer questions spanning text, figures, and tables in JWST or high-redshift papers. A concrete workflow would be to index a corpus of astronomy PDFs with page images and text, then query with an image of a spectrum or a plot plus a natural-language question. The main caveat is that the benchmark is automotive and web-image-based, and the paper notes image retrieval alone has low recall, so text-derived signals do most of the work.

Reason

TrioRAG is a graph-free multimodal RAG framework combining text, images, and VLM-enhanced queries, which is directly relevant to multimodal retrieval over astronomical literature and figures.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Check how the multi-vector index handles page images with plots, spectra, and multi-panel figures
  • Look for ablations separating the contribution of the VLM-enhanced query from the anchor image
  • Assess whether the framework can be adapted to a scientific PDF corpus with equations and tables
  • Check the reported failure modes when image retrieval recall is low
Abstract

Graph-based retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) is widely used for multimodal, cross-document question answering. However, building corpus-level graphs is expensive, slow to query, and difficult to maintain. We present TrioRAG, a graph-free multimodal framework that integrates evidence from three complementary signals: the question, the anchor image, and a VLM-enhanced query generated from both. Each signal retrieves independently over a shared multi-vector index of page text and page images, and the results are combined through late fusion. Further, we introduce AutoQA, a multimodal automotive benchmark whose questions are grounded in noisy, web-sourced images rather than clean document-sourced figures. Its questions require reasoning across manuals. We position it as a model-curated testbed rather than a human-validated gold standard. Across three benchmarks, TrioRAG matches or outperforms graph-based systems while reducing total cost and accelerating per-query inference by 1.6-2.3 times. By construction, AutoQA grounds its questions in out-of-corpus web images. In this setting image retrieval reaches only 19.3% document-level recall, while text-derived signals, especially the VLM-enhanced query, keep retrieval robust.

Bayesian Optimization with Rich Auxiliary Information via LLMs

Tejus Gupta , Efe Mert Karagözlü , Rohit Sonker , Barnabás Póczos , Jeff Schnieder

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

The paper tackles the problem that Bayesian Optimization (BO), a standard tool for optimizing expensive black-box functions, usually ignores rich side information such as training curves, expert notes, or images. The main idea is to let a large language model read that auxiliary information and propose where the optimizer should look next, effectively using the LLM as a prior over promising regions. They build three concrete methods for feeding this auxiliary context into BO and show gains on hyperparameter-optimization benchmarks and a real nuclear-fusion control task. For astronomy, this is interesting because observing runs, instrument tuning, and pipeline hyperparameter searches are expensive and often come with human notes, diagnostic plots, or partial spectra that a standard optimizer discards. A plausible workflow is using an LLM-guided BO loop to choose exposure times, filter choices, or pipeline settings for JWST or Roman observations, conditioning on prior spectra or quick-look images. The caveat to check is whether the LLM's auxiliary signal is trustworthy and calibrated, and whether the method needs large amounts of task-specific text or image context that may not exist for your survey.

Reason

Directly relevant to using LLMs as scientific assistants that inject auxiliary context (notes, images, curves) into optimization loops, which maps onto astronomical experiment design and hyperparameter tuning.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Check how auxiliary information is encoded and passed to the LLM, and whether it handles images, spectra, or only text
  • Look for the fusion-task setup to judge whether the optimization is truly black-box and expensive like an observing decision
  • Examine whether they report calibration or failure modes when the LLM prior is wrong or misleading
  • See if code or prompts are released so the method could be adapted to an astronomical optimization loop
Abstract

Bayesian Optimization (BO) is widely used for optimizing expensive black-box functions, yet many real-world optimization problems contain substantially richer information than function evaluations alone. Examples include training curves in hyperparameter optimization, expert notes and images in scientific experimentation, and prior knowledge about where optima may lie. We show that large language models (LLMs) can effectively leverage such rich auxiliary information to guide optimization. Motivated by these findings, we develop three methods for incorporating auxiliary information into BO using LLMs. Across hyperparameter optimization benchmarks and a real-world nuclear fusion optimization task, our methods consistently outperform both standard BO and existing LLM-based optimization approaches. Our results demonstrate the effectiveness of LLMs for leveraging rich auxiliary information in BO.

FootprintRAG: Visual Analytics for Evidence Context Refinement in RAG-based Scientific Literature Exploration

Xingyu Liu , Yu Dong , Qizhen Yu , Shiyu Cheng , Zhe Wang , Guan Li , Guihua Shan , Dong Tian , Christy Jie Liang , Quang Vinh Nguyen

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

The paper addresses the problem that RAG systems for scientific literature often hide how they retrieve, rerank, and filter evidence, leaving users unable to inspect or revise the evidence context. FootprintRAG treats the evidence context as an explicit, inspectable object, parses literature into text and figure units, expands queries, retrieves and assesses evidence iteratively, and surfaces supplementary candidates through coordinated visual analytics views. This matters for astronomy because literature review and evidence tracking are essential for JWST follow-up, anomaly interpretation, and reproducible research. A concrete workflow is to use FootprintRAG to explore the little red dots literature, compare retrieval directions, and trace generated summaries back to specific text or figure evidence. The main caveat is that the system is evaluated on general scientific literature, so I should check whether it handles astronomy-specific terminology, figures, and arXiv formatting well.

Reason

FootprintRAG is directly relevant to retrieval-augmented generation, LLM agents for literature review, and evidence provenance in scientific literature exploration, which are core to my interests in research agents and automated scientific discovery.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Check whether the evidence units include figures and tables from astronomy papers
  • Look for user-study metrics on evidence recovery and provenance tracing
  • Assess whether the system supports domain-specific queries and terminology
  • Check whether the code is available and can be run on an astronomy corpus
Abstract

Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) is increasingly used to ground large language model (LLM) outputs in scientific literature. However, in open-ended literature exploration, the evidence context used for generation is often produced through hidden retrieval, reranking, assessment, and filtering steps. Users may receive retrieval summaries without knowing how the system constructed the evidence context, which evidence units were retained or discarded, or whether potentially useful evidence was excluded before synthesis. We present FootprintRAG, an LLM-agent-powered visual analytics system for evidence context refinement in RAG-based scientific literature exploration. The core idea is to treat the RAG evidence context as an explicit, inspectable, and revisable analytical object before generation. FootprintRAG parses scientific literature into text and figure evidence units, expands an initial query into parallel query variants, retrieves and assesses evidence across iterative rounds, and surfaces ERS-ranked supplementary candidates from the corpus-level evidence space. Through coordinated views, the system connects retrieval trajectories, evidence-state revision, and provenance-aware summary generation into a user-steerable workflow. We evaluate FootprintRAG through two case studies, a user study, and a workflow-level comparison with representative RAG systems. The results show that FootprintRAG helps users compare retrieval directions, revise candidate evidence, recover potentially overlooked evidence, and trace generated summaries back to supporting evidence units. Scientific Image Quality Assessment via Multi-modal Retrieval-Augmented Generation

Yinuo Zhang , Bingshuo Liu , Zhiying Tu , Dianhui Chu , Qingbin Liu , Xi Chen , Jiang Bian , Xiaoyan Yu , Dianbo Sui

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

The paper addresses scientific image quality assessment, where the goal is both to understand image content and to assign a quality score, as part of the SIQA challenge. The core idea is a retrieval-augmented generation framework that builds a multimodal index combining textual semantics with fine-grained visual features, then uses multi-route retrieval and fusion to give a large language model relevant reference cases before it judges a new image. In plain language, it lets the model look up similar previously judged scientific images and their expert assessments, then reason by analogy rather than from scratch. For astronomy, this is directly relevant to assessing JWST image quality, flagging artifacts, or evaluating whether a detected source is real, since the model can retrieve analogous cases from a curated reference set. A concrete workflow could be building a multimodal index of labeled JWST cutouts or spectra and using RAG to help an agent classify artifacts, assess data quality, or support anomaly triage. The main caveat is that the paper is a challenge submission, so you should check whether the retrieval corpus and evaluation are general enough for astronomical images rather than the specific SIQA dataset.

Reason

It proposes a multimodal RAG framework for scientific image quality assessment, directly relevant to multimodal models, RAG, and scientific image analysis, with clear potential for JWST imaging quality control and source evaluation.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Look for how the multimodal index represents fine-grained visual features and whether it can handle astronomical image characteristics like low surface brightness or noise
  • Check the multi-route retrieval and fusion mechanism to see if it supports multiple image types or only the challenge data
  • Assess whether the quality scoring is calibrated against human expert judgments and whether the criteria transfer to astronomy
  • Check whether the framework is open-sourced and whether the retrieval corpus can be replaced with astronomical references
Abstract

This paper proposes a Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) framework for scientific image quality assessment, designed to simultaneously address both the understanding track (SIQA-U) and the scoring track (SIQA-S) of the SIQA challenge. We construct a multimodal index that integrates textual semantics with fine-grained visual features, and develop a multi-route retrieval and fusion mechanism to provide large language models with highly relevant reference cases, thereby enhancing their capability to evaluate complex scientific images. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed framework effectively aligns with the judgment criteria of human experts. Ultimately, our method achieves 1st place in the SIQA-U track of the SIQA challenge at the ICME 2026 Grand Challenges.

AutoData: Agentic Search for Pre-training Data Selection

Yan Meng , Dhruv Srikanth , Bingchen Zhao , Zhengyao Jiang , Yuxiang Wu

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

The paper addresses the problem that data selection for LLM pre-training remains largely outside the agentic optimization loop, while model and training code can already be edited by agents. It introduces AutoData, an agent that searches directly over executable selection algorithms, including scoring, stratification, and stochastic selection rules, using validation feedback from a proxy model to discover feature interactions automatically. The main claim is that within an overnight search, AutoData discovers a selection algorithm that outperforms human-designed curation pipelines and transfers to larger scales, improving the downstream CORE metric. For astronomy, this is relevant because building training sets for astronomical foundation models or multimodal models requires selecting which images, spectra, or catalogs to include, and an agentic search over selection rules could automate that curation. A concrete workflow could be using AutoData-style search to select JWST or Rubin training samples that maximize downstream performance on galaxy classification or anomaly detection. The caveat is that the paper is about text pre-training data, so you would need to adapt the per-document features and proxy model to astronomical data modalities and evaluation metrics.

Reason

This paper is about LLM agents for automated pre-training data selection, which is directly relevant to automated scientific discovery, research agents, and data-centric machine learning. The idea of searching over executable selection algorithms could transfer to curating astronomical training sets for foundation models.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Check whether the executable selection algorithms are general enough to operate on astronomical image or catalog features
  • Look for the proxy model design and whether a similar small-scale proxy could be built for astronomical tasks
  • See whether the search procedure requires large-scale compute or can run overnight on modest resources
  • Assess whether the discovered selection rules are interpretable and whether they could introduce survey-specific biases
Abstract

LLM agents have recently shown promise in automating machine learning engineering by editing model and training code under execution feedback. Data, however, remains largely outside this agentic optimisation loop. We frame pre-training data selection as heuristic engineering over per-document features, i.e., lexical statistics, categorical labels, and perplexity. We introduce AutoData, an agent that searches directly over executable selection algorithms. Unlike prior data mixture methods that optimise weights over a fixed set of domains, AutoData searches a richer program space of scoring, stratification, and stochastic selection rules, discovering feature interactions automatically by iteratively refining algorithms with validation feedback from a proxy model. Within an overnight search, AutoData discovers a selection algorithm that outperforms existing human-designed curation pipelines. Despite being searched only on this small proxy, the discovered recipe transfers to larger scales and improves the downstream metric CORE. These results suggest that data engineering can be treated as an agentic machine learning problem, extending autonomous research from model and training-code optimization to the data.

F$^{2}$DR: A Fine-Grained Full-Pipeline Reward Framework for DeepSearch Workflows

Bojian Xiong (Tianjin University) Wentao Ding (Baidu Inc.) Yujing Lu (Baidu Inc.) Shaowei Zhang (Tianjin University) Ling Shi (Tianjin University) Jing Liao (Baidu Inc.) Yan Wang (Baidu Inc.) Short Digest

The paper addresses the problem that existing reward models and benchmarks for LLMs are built for static single-turn tasks, while DeepSearch agents operate in iterative loops of planning, reflection, retrieval, and answer generation. The main idea is F2DR, a fine-grained full-pipeline reward framework that scores DeepSearch workflows along three dimensions: content, trajectory, and answer, plus a dedicated benchmark called DeepSearch RM-Bench for evaluating reward models in this setting. This matters for astronomy because a research agent doing literature review or multi-step data analysis needs process-level feedback, not just a final-answer score, to learn reliable workflows. Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Look for the exact definition of the Content, Trajectory, and Answer reward dimensions and whether they are domain-agnostic
  • Check whether DeepSearch RM-Bench includes tool-use or code-execution trajectories or only retrieval and QA
  • Examine how the reward model handles long-horizon credit assignment and whether it could support scientific workflows

Abstract

With the widespread industrial deployment of Large Language Models (LLMs), DeepSearch has emerged as the dominant paradigm for resolving complex user queries. It typically operates through an iterative closed-loop workflow consisting of planning and reflection, information retrieval, and answer generation. However, existing reward models (RMs) and evaluation benchmarks are primarily designed for static single-turn tasks, failing to capture the full-pipeline complexity of DeepSearch workflows. To address this limitation, we propose F2DR, a fine-grained full-pipeline DeepSearch reward framework. F2DR evaluates DeepSearch workflows across three dimensions: Content, Trajectory, and Answer, enabling comprehensive process-level assessment. We further construct DeepSearch RM-Bench, a dedicated benchmark for evaluating RMs in DeepSearch scenarios. Extensive experiments demonstrate that F2DR achieves significantly higher evaluation consistency than self-evaluation-based baselines, while DeepSearch RM-Bench exhibits strong discriminative capability across existing open-source RMs. We will publicly release the complete DeepSearch RM-Bench dataset soon.

UnifiedPlayers: Enhance Tool-Integrated Reasoning in Agentic Reinforcement Learning

Wenjie Liao , Liangjie Zhao , Zehong Cao

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

The paper tackles the problem of training tool-using AI agents without extensive human-annotated data, where existing self-evolving methods separate trajectory generation from evaluation and rely on static verifiers that cannot adapt to new failure modes. The core idea is UnifiedPlayers, a cooperative framework with three specialized roles: a Planning Player that generates tasks, an Execution Player that produces multi-turn trajectories with Python tool calls, and an Evaluation Player that builds executable verifiers, all coordinated through role-specific rewards under GRPO (a reinforcement learning algorithm that optimizes policies using group-relative advantages). For astronomy, this could enable agents that autonomously write and validate Python code for JWST data reduction, spectral fitting, or catalog cross-matching, where the agent generates its own training tasks and verifies its outputs without human labeling. A concrete workflow would be training an agent to perform SED fitting on high-redshift galaxy photometry, where the Execution Player writes fitting code and the Evaluation Player checks residuals and physical plausibility. The main caveat is that the method is validated only on mathematical and general reasoning benchmarks, so I would need to check whether the verifier construction generalizes to scientific domains where correctness is harder to formalize than arithmetic.

Reason

This paper directly addresses agentic reinforcement learning with tool-integrated reasoning, which is highly relevant to my interest in LLM agents for scientific workflows and automated discovery. The self-evolving training approach with executable verifiers could transfer to astronomical data analysis agents that need to generate and validate their own workflows.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Check whether the executable verifiers are domain-specific or general-purpose, and whether they could be adapted to astronomical validation criteria like chi-squared residuals or physical parameter bounds
  • Look for details on how the Planning Player generates tasks and whether task diversity is maintained over training
  • Examine the GRPO implementation details and whether the role-specific rewards could be redesigned for scientific workflow objectives
  • Check the computational cost of training three separate players and whether it is feasible for a small research group
Abstract

Self-evolving methods reduce the need for human-annotated trajectories by allowing tool-using agents to generate their own training data. Yet existing methods typically separate trajectory generation from evaluation, relying on static verifiers that cannot adapt to emerging failure modes or self-consistency signals that may reinforce errors shared across trajectories. Jointly adapting planning, execution, and evaluation offers a promising alternative, but introduces a fundamental coordination challenge: each component continuously changes the data or feedback used to train the others. We address this challenge with \textbf{UnifiedPlayers}, a cooperative framework comprising a Planning Player that generates tasks, an Execution Player that produces multi-turn trajectories with Python tool calls, and an Evaluation Player that constructs executable verifiers. We design role-specific rewards that coordinate the three players toward a shared learning objective under GRPO. Across two model backbones and twelve reasoning benchmarks, UnifiedPlayers outperforms the strongest prior baseline by at least 3.5\% on mathematical reasoning and 3.9\% on general reasoning tasks. Moreover, the learned verifier achieves 84.2\% adversarial detection accuracy, while its reward signal exhibits 2.03$\times$ higher per-question variance than a self-consistency baseline, providing more discriminative verifications. These results highlight cooperation among specialized players as a promising path toward self-enhanced tool-integrated agents.
