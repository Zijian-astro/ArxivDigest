GenSBI: Generative Methods for Simulation-Based Inference in JAX

Aurelio Amerio

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

This paper presents GenSBI, an open-source library in JAX for simulation-based inference (SBI) using flow matching, score matching, and denoising diffusion models. SBI is a powerful framework for estimating parameters from complex simulations where the likelihood is intractable, which is common in astronomy (e.g., galaxy formation models, SED fitting). The library offers transformer-based architectures and a unified interface for posterior, likelihood, and joint density estimation, with built-in calibration diagnostics. This is directly relevant to astronomy because it could be used to infer physical parameters of high-redshift galaxies from JWST data by training on simulated spectra or images. A concrete workflow would be using GenSBI to train a neural posterior estimator on simulated galaxy SEDs, then applying it to real JWST/NIRSpec observations to infer stellar mass, age, metallicity, and dust. A key caveat is that the library is JAX-based, which may require migrating from PyTorch workflows, and the benchmarks are on simple SBI tasks (SBIBM), not astronomical simulations. Prerequisites include familiarity with SBI concepts and JAX.

Reason

Directly provides a JAX-based library for simulation-based inference using flow matching and diffusion models, which is highly relevant for SED fitting, spectral fitting, and parameter estimation in astronomy.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Check if the library supports custom embedding networks for domain-specific features (e.g., spectral line indices or photometric bands)
  • Look for documentation on integrating with astronomical simulation codes (e.g., FSPS, CIGALE, or BEAGLE)
  • Examine the posterior calibration diagnostics (SBC, TARP, LC2ST) for applicability to multi-modal or degenerate posteriors common in SED fitting
Abstract

Flow and diffusion generative models have established themselves as widely adopted density estimators for simulation-based inference (SBI), extending naturally from neural posterior estimation to likelihood and joint density estimation. Their principled optimization objectives and freedom from architectural constraints have driven rapid adoption across the natural sciences. Yet the most widely used SBI libraries remain PyTorch-based, leaving researchers who develop their forward models and analysis pipelines in JAX without a native option. We present GenSBI, an open-source library that implements flow matching, score matching, and denoising diffusion entirely in JAX. The library offers three transformer-based architectures - SimFormer, Flux1, and a novel Flux1Joint that extends gate-modulated transformer blocks to joint density estimation - all interchangeable through a unified interface that decouples generative method, neural backbone, and inference mode. GenSBI provides an end-to-end workflow from training through posterior calibration (SBC, TARP, LC2ST) and supports custom architectures with domain-specific embedding networks. We validate the framework on standard SBI benchmarks, achieving near-ideal mean C2ST scores (0.50-0.56, where 0.50 is ideal) on SBIBM tasks with minimal per-task tuning and well-calibrated posterior coverage across all tested configurations. The code is publicly available at this https URL .

Eliot: Interactively $\underline{E}$xploring Fast-Changing Scientific $\underline{Li}$terature Trends with $\underline{O}$nline Da$\underline{t}$a and Learning

Bernardo A. Denkvitts , Nitin Gupta , Biplav Srivastava

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

The paper presents Eliot, an interactive system for traceable exploration of evolving scientific literature. It retrieves arXiv papers at query time, clusters them into themes, and visualizes publication-year distributions. The system uses MiniLM embeddings, UMAP, and Agglomerative Clustering as a practical default. This is directly applicable to an astronomer wanting to track trends in JWST, high-redshift galaxies, or AI methods for astronomy. I could imagine using it to explore how 'little red dots' or 'AGN feedback' research has evolved. A caveat is that it only uses titles and abstracts, not full text, and the clustering quality depends on the query terms.

Reason

Directly addresses literature trend exploration using clustering and temporal analysis, which is highly relevant for an astronomer tracking fast-moving areas like JWST or high-redshift galaxies.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Check the clustering quality metrics and whether they are robust across different astronomy subfields
  • Look for the temporal visualization details and whether they support custom date ranges
  • Examine the query-time retrieval API and whether it supports arXiv categories or custom filters
  • Check if the system can be extended to include full-text or citation data
Abstract

The rapid growth of scientific publishing has made it increasingly difficult to track how fast-moving areas evolve. Search engines and LLM-based assistants retrieve or summarize papers, but often hide how the corpus was selected, organized, or connected to temporal patterns. We present $\texttt{Eliot}$, a publicly deployed interactive system for traceable exploration of evolving scientific literature. Motivated by two studies on Large Language Models (LLMs) and Automated Planning and Scheduling (APS), $\texttt{Eliot}$ generalizes literature-evolution analysis beyond hand-built taxonomies and domain-specific scripts. Given explicit query terms and filters, it retrieves arXiv papers at query time, represents each paper by title and abstract, clusters the corpus into themes, assigns representative keywords, and visualizes each cluster's publication-year distribution. We evaluate $\texttt{Eliot}$ as both an applied system and an interactive research aid. An offline configuration study across eight arXiv domains compares document representations, dimensionality reduction methods, and clustering algorithms using intrinsic clustering and topic-coherence metrics; the results support MiniLM embeddings with 10-dimensional UMAP and Agglomerative Clustering as a practical default. A scenario-based survey and expert focus group assess interpretability and use contexts: participants rated cluster labels as meaningful in 85% of scenario responses, and feedback indicated that $\texttt{Eliot}$ is most valuable for auditable overviews of rapidly changing technical areas. These results suggest that query-time clustering and temporal inspection can complement search and generation tools by helping researchers inspect and refine the evidence behind literature trends.

SkillGrad: Optimizing Agent Skills Like Gradient Descent

Hanyu Wang , Yifan Lan , Bochuan Cao , Lu Lin , Jinghui Chen

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

This paper tackles the problem that agent skills (reusable procedural knowledge for LLM agents) are often unreliable, incomplete, or outdated when downloaded or self-generated. The authors propose SkillGrad, a framework that treats the skill package as a structured parameter to optimize using a gradient-descent-like process: task executions provide trajectory-level loss, automatic diagnoses provide text-based gradients, and a momentum agent accumulates recurring patterns to stabilize optimization. For astronomy, this could enable automated refinement of data-analysis workflows, such as tuning a multi-step pipeline for JWST source classification or SED fitting. A concrete workflow would be using SkillGrad to iteratively improve an agent that retrieves and processes astronomical catalogs, reducing manual debugging. However, the method is evaluated on spreadsheet and table QA tasks, so its transfer to astronomy-specific tools (e.g., astropy, photutils, or JWST pipeline steps) would require adapting the skill representation and diagnosis feedback to domain-specific APIs.

Reason

Directly addresses optimizing LLM agent skills via a gradient-descent-inspired framework, which is highly relevant to automated scientific discovery and research agents for astronomy.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Check whether the skill package format (structured files) can represent astronomy workflows like multi-step data reduction or model-fitting scripts.
  • Look for details on how the text-based gradients (diagnoses) are generated and whether they could incorporate domain-specific error messages (e.g., from JWST pipeline failures).
  • Examine the momentum memory overlay mechanism to see if it can capture recurring issues in astronomical data processing (e.g., persistent calibration artifacts).
Abstract

Agent skills provide a lightweight way to adapt LLM agents to specialized domains by storing reusable procedural knowledge in structured files. However, whether downloaded from third parties or self-generated, these skills are often unreliable, incomplete, or outdated. Existing skill-evolution methods often address these deficiencies through heuristic reflections without an explicit optimization formulation. In this paper, we propose SkillGrad, a gradient-descent-inspired framework for optimizing agent skills. SkillGrad treats the skill package as a structured parameter to optimize in a gradient descent fashion: task executions provide trajectory-level loss evidence, automatic diagnoses then provide text-based gradients that indicate the correction directions. To stabilize optimization across iterations, a momentum agent accumulates recurring diagnostic patterns into a persistent memory overlay. Finally, an LLM-based patcher executes the parameter update by applying layer-aware edits to the skill package. Evaluated on SpreadsheetBench Verified and WikiTableQuestions, SkillGrad consistently outperforms training-based skill evolution baselines across two backbone LLMs, improving over the strongest training-based baseline by $6.7$ percentage points on average. Ablations further show that momentum and contrastive diagnosis both contribute to the final skill quality.

ResearchLoop: An Evidence-Gated Control Plane for AI-Assisted Research

Yihan Xia , Taotao Wang

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

This paper aims to solve the problem of unverifiable claims in AI-assisted research by introducing an evidence-gated control plane. The core idea is a structured protocol that treats research questions, task contracts, evidence objects, and claim ledgers as durable project state, ensuring every claim is backed by evidence before it can be included in a paper. This matters for astronomy because it provides a framework for building trustworthy AI agents that can perform literature review, data analysis, and code generation while maintaining auditability and reproducibility. An astronomer could imagine using ResearchLoop to manage a project where an LLM agent searches JWST data for little red dots, fits SEDs, and writes a paper, with every claim automatically linked to the evidence. A key limitation is that the paper is a technical report of a protocol, not a deployed system, and its effectiveness depends on the quality of the underlying AI models and the user's ability to define task contracts.

Reason

Directly addresses AI-assisted research with an evidence-gated control plane, highly relevant for automated scientific discovery and reproducible workflows in astronomy.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Look for the protocol specification details: how are 'evidence objects' defined and verified? Can they include astronomical data products like spectra or cutouts?
  • Check the claim-admission algorithm: does it require human-in-the-loop verification, or is it fully automated? How does it handle uncertainty in evidence?
  • See the experimental record (V0-V9): are there any case studies involving scientific data analysis (e.g., from SciCode) that demonstrate the workflow on a realistic astronomy problem?
  • Examine the 'insight-compounding mechanism': does the system allow building on previous results, and how does it prevent error propagation?
Abstract

AI-assisted research compresses ideation, implementation, evaluation, and manuscript writing into a single interactive loop. This compression is useful, but it also creates a publication risk: paper claims can become easier to state than to audit. We present ResearchLoop, an evidence-gated control plane for AI-assisted computational research. ResearchLoop treats research questions, task contracts, evidence objects, claim ledgers, closeouts, and paper bindings as durable project state, realized here as a repository-backed runtime. This technical report provides the complete protocol specification, state model, transition rules, claim-admission algorithm, and insight-compounding mechanism. It also reports the full experimental record spanning nine versions (V0--V9), including a self-hosting case study, a controlled task-suite study with component ablations, a mathematical olympiad evaluation, and a supplementary SciCode boundary experiment evaluated with the official generated-code harness. All artifacts, manifests, and verification reports are preserved in the project repository.

From paper to benchmark: agentic, framework-based reproduction of under-specified methods in machine health intelligence

Raffael Theiler , Ludovico Comito , David Leko , Leandro Von Krannichfeldt , Lev Telyatnikov , Olga Fink

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

This paper tackles the challenge of reproducing under-specified machine learning methods from published papers. It introduces an agentic framework that translates papers into a shared benchmark framework via a slot-binding interface, mapping equations and protocols into structured components. The approach ensures consistent and comparable benchmarking across papers. For astronomy, this could automate the reproduction of methods for source classification, SED fitting, or spectral analysis from the literature. The framework explicitly records unresolved assumptions, which is crucial for trust in automated scientific workflows. A key caveat is that the method is demonstrated on industrial PHM papers, not astronomy, so adaptation to astronomical methods and datasets would be needed.

Reason

Directly addresses agentic reproduction of scientific methods, which is highly relevant for automated scientific discovery and reproducible research in astronomy.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Check if the slot-binding interface can be extended to astronomical tasks like photometric redshift estimation or galaxy morphology classification.
  • Look for evaluation on papers with incomplete reporting of preprocessing or evaluation protocols, which is common in astronomy.
Abstract

Industrial Prognostics and Health Management (PHM) provides a representative case study for a broader challenge in applied machine learning: translating published papers into executable, benchmark-ready implementations. Reproducing under-specified methods in PHM is particularly difficult due to restricted access to industrial datasets, incomplete reporting of preprocessing and evaluation protocols, and implicit design choices (e.g., windowing, target construction, data splits) that critically affect performance. Existing paper-to-code systems generate implementations for individual papers, but these artifacts are often not directly comparable due to inconsistencies in assumptions and evaluation settings. We introduce \emph{agentic, framework-based PHM paper reproduction}, where an agent translates a paper into a shared PHM benchmark framework via a \emph{slot-binding interface}. This interface maps equations and protocol descriptions into structured components (task definitions, dataset adapters, windowing, targets, models, and evaluators), while explicitly recording unresolved assumptions. The resulting implementations are validated against standardized task contracts and evaluation hooks, enabling consistent and comparable benchmarking. We evaluate this approach on 16 PHM papers, comparing framework-enhanced, skill-based and prompt-based agentic reproduction against a recent framework-free paper-reproduction agent. We assess reproduction success, model-based code evaluation, framework binding of paper assumptions, and cross-paper benchmark comparability under standardized protocols. Our results show that coupling agentic generation with a shared framework transforms paper reproduction from isolated code synthesis into executable, assumption-aware, and systematically comparable benchmark implementations.
