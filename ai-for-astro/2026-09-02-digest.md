## 1. Scientific Agent Skills: A Library of Procedural Knowledge for Research Agents

- arXiv ID: `2609.00065`
- Authors: Timothy Kassis , Vinayak Agarwal , Yuhuan He , Darshil Patel , Aubrey M. Brueckner
- Score: 8
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00065
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.00065
- Subjects: Computation and Language (cs.CL) ; Artificial Intelligence (cs.AI)
- Reason: Directly relevant to LLM agents for scientific workflows, providing a library of procedural knowledge that could enhance research agents in astronomy. The focus on defensible analyses and field-accepted procedures aligns with reproducible research interests.

Short digest:
This paper addresses the problem that language-model agents often return working code but not analyses that are defensible according to field standards. It introduces an open library of 163 procedural skills, each with a human-readable instruction file that agents load on demand, covering areas like study design and scientific communication. For astronomy, this could help agents follow best practices in data analysis, such as using authoritative catalogs or standard SED-fitting procedures. I could imagine using it to build an agent that performs literature review or data reduction with built-in checks for methodological correctness. However, the paper reports no task-level evaluation, so its practical utility remains unproven and needs testing.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Check whether the library includes any astronomy or astrophysics-specific skills
- Look for examples of how a skill is structured and loaded by an agent
- Assess the licensing and ease of adapting skills to custom research workflows

## 2. Explore Before Committing: Hypothesis-Guided Search for Deep Research Agents

- arXiv ID: `2609.01294`
- Authors: Ruochen Zhou , Zhengyu Chen , Luan Zhang , Siyang Gao , Yee Whye Teh , Shiqi Chen
- Score: 8
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01294
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.01294
- Subjects: Computation and Language (cs.CL)
- Reason: Directly relevant to deep research agents, which could be used for literature review and automated scientific discovery in astronomy.

Short digest:
This paper addresses a failure mode in deep-research agents where they commit to an early search direction without gathering enough comparative evidence. The main idea is to generate lightweight hypotheses as soft search hints, explore them in bounded independent branches, and compare evidence before committing, improving search efficiency and accuracy. For astronomy, this is highly relevant because it could enhance research agents that search literature or data for astrophysical questions, such as finding properties of high-redshift galaxies. A concrete workflow could be using such an agent to conduct a systematic literature review on little red dots or AGN properties. The caveat is that the method is tested on general benchmarks, not astronomy-specific, so it needs validation on domain-specific search tasks.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Look for details on how hypotheses are generated and how branch exploration is bounded
- Check if the method could be adapted to search astronomical databases or literature
- See if the training data or benchmarks include any scientific domains

## 3. Beyond Language Priors: Diagnosing and Fixing Visual-Origin Hallucinations in Multimodal LLM

- arXiv ID: `2609.00231`
- Authors: Peiyang Xu , Xiaopei Zhu , Jun Zhu , Xiaolin Hu
- Score: 7
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00231
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.00231
- Subjects: Computer Vision and Pattern Recognition (cs.CV)
- Reason: Directly addresses visual-origin hallucinations in multimodal LLMs, which is crucial for reliable analysis of astronomical images where subtle features may be misrepresented. The proposed ACFT method for reducing hallucinations could improve trust in LMM outputs for JWST data interpretation.

Short digest:
This paper challenges the common belief that hallucinations in multimodal large language models (MLLMs) are solely due to language priors, showing instead that incorrect visual feature extraction and image-text misalignment also play a key role. The authors introduce Adversarial Contrastive Fine-Tuning (ACFT), which uses targeted adversarial perturbations to create aligned positive-negative pairs for contrastive learning, reducing hallucinations without extra inference cost. For astronomy, this matters because LMMs are increasingly used to interpret JWST images, and reducing visual-origin hallucinations could make their outputs more reliable for tasks like identifying high-redshift galaxies or AGN. A practical workflow could involve fine-tuning an LMM on astronomical image-caption pairs with ACFT to improve accuracy in source classification. However, the method is tested on COCO, so its transferability to astronomical data needs validation, and the adversarial perturbations may not capture the unique noise characteristics of JWST images.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Check whether the ACFT method is evaluated on any non-COCO datasets or if the authors discuss domain transfer limitations.
- Look for details on how the adversarial perturbations are generated and whether they could be adapted to astronomical image noise properties.
- Examine the diagnostic metrics (cosine similarity, Smooth Grad-CAM) for evidence that visual-origin hallucinations are indeed reduced in practice.

## 4. SCoNE: Selective Context-aware Neuron Editing for Robust Retrieval-Augmented Generation

- arXiv ID: `2609.00689`
- Authors: Chaewon Kim , Seo Yeon Park
- Score: 7
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00689
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.00689
- Subjects: Computation and Language (cs.CL)
- Reason: Addresses retrieval noise robustness in RAG, which is directly relevant to using LLMs for literature review and data analysis in astronomy, where retrieved documents can be noisy.

Short digest:
This paper tackles the problem of retrieval noise in Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG), where irrelevant context can distract LLMs and cause hallucinations. The main idea is SCoNE, a training-free method that selectively edits feed-forward neurons in LLMs to strengthen context-aware processing, identified by high attribution and cross-input variability. For astronomy, this is relevant because when using RAG for literature review or data analysis, retrieved papers or catalogs may contain irrelevant information. The workflow could involve applying SCoNE to improve the robustness of an astronomy-focused RAG system that retrieves papers or data descriptions. However, the method requires mining samples and may need adaptation to astronomical text, and its effectiveness on domain-specific corpora is untested.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Check if the method is evaluated on any scientific or domain-specific question-answering tasks.
- Look for details on how many mining samples are needed and whether they can be obtained from astronomical literature.
- Examine if the neuron editing is stable across different prompts and domains.

## 5. Agentic Multimodal Models for Environmental Hyperspectral Unmixing

- arXiv ID: `2609.01289`
- Authors: Michał Cholewa , Luca Ciampi , Nicola Messina , Przemysław Głomb , Giuseppe Amato
- Score: 7
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01289
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.01289
- Subjects: Computer Vision and Pattern Recognition (cs.CV)
- Reason: Uses a large vision-language model as an agent to refine hyperspectral unmixing, which is analogous to astronomical source decomposition and could transfer to JWST spectral analysis.

Short digest:
This paper addresses the problem of hyperspectral unmixing in remote sensing, where mixed pixels are decomposed into material signatures and abundances. The main idea is to use a large vision-language model (LVLM) as an agent that iteratively refines outputs from traditional unmixing pipelines by merging or discarding endmembers based on spectral and spatial evidence. For astronomy, this is relevant because hyperspectral unmixing is similar to decomposing spectra of galaxies or AGN into components, and the agentic approach could be adapted to refine SED fitting or spectral decomposition. A concrete workflow could be using such an agent to refine photometric or spectral decompositions of high-redshift galaxies from JWST data. The caveat is that the paper is tested on remote sensing datasets, not astronomical ones, so transferability needs validation.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Look for details on how the LVLM agent gathers evidence and makes merge/discard decisions
- Check if the framework is algorithm-agnostic and could be applied to astronomical spectral fitting tools
- See if the tool-use patterns (e.g., spectral library retrieval) have analogs in astronomy
