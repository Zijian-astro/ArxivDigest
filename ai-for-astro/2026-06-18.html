From Specification to Execution: AI Assisted Scientific Workflow Management

Komal Thareja , Hamza Safri , Rajiv Mayani , Anirban Mandal , Ewa Deelman

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

This paper aims to automate scientific workflow design, implementation, and debugging using LLMs. The core idea is a structured specification phase that separates workflow intent, design, and implementation, combined with an LLM-based debugging agent and integration with the Pegasus workflow management system via a Model Context Protocol (MCP) layer. For astronomy, this is directly applicable to building automated pipelines for JWST data reduction, source classification, or SED fitting, where a user could describe the desired analysis in natural language and the system generates and executes the workflow. A concrete workflow would be describing a multi-step analysis of JWST NIRSpec data (e.g., extract spectra, fit emission lines, classify AGN) and having the system generate and run the pipeline. A key caveat is that the evaluation uses a federated learning workflow for medical imaging, not astronomy, so the system's performance on astronomical workflows is untested.

Reason

This paper directly addresses AI-assisted scientific workflow management using LLMs, which is a core interest in research agents and automated scientific discovery for astronomy.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Check if the MCP layer is extensible to other workflow management systems commonly used in astronomy (e.g., Luigi, Airflow, or custom scripts).
  • Look for details on how the LLM-based debugging agent handles domain-specific errors (e.g., missing calibration files, incorrect data formats).
  • Examine the structured specification phase to see if it can capture astronomical domain knowledge (e.g., data reduction steps, calibration requirements).
Abstract

Scientific workflow management systems (WMS) support scalable and reproducible execution of complex pipelines, but workflow design, implementation, and debugging remain largely manual and require significant expertise. Recent approaches using large language models (LLMs) show promise for workflow generation from natural language, but often rely on direct code synthesis, which limits transparency, reproducibility, and integration with workflow systems. We present an AI-assisted approach to scientific workflow management that combines specification-driven workflow generation, automated debugging, and distributed execution. The method introduces a structured specification phase that separates workflow intent, design, and implementation, allowing validation prior to code generation. We also develop an LLM-based debugging agent that diagnoses and resolves failures across multiple system layers. To support distributed execution and user interaction, we integrate Pegasus, a widely used WMS, with a Model Context Protocol (MCP) layer, providing a unified interface for workflow submission, monitoring, and control. We evaluate the approach using a federated learning workflow for medical imaging, chosen for its parallel, iterative, and dependency-intensive structure. The system generated and executed large-scale workflows with thousands of jobs, reduced debugging effort, and allowed non-expert users to construct workflows with expert-level design patterns. These results indicate that end-to-end AI-assisted workflow generation and execution is feasible, and point toward AI-driven platforms for managing the scientific workflow lifecycle.

Externalizing Research Synthesis and Validation in AI Scientists through a Research Harness

Zijian Wang , Hanqi Li , Ziyue Yang , Zijian Hu , Shenghan Zuo , Yunzhe Zhang , Da Ma , Danyu Luo , Chenrun Wang , Jing Peng , Tiancheng Huang , Sijia Guo , Huayang Wang , Zichen Zhu , Senyu Han , Yilu Cao , Kai Yu , Lu Chen

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

This paper addresses the problem that AI systems automating scientific workflows often keep reasoning implicit inside model inference, leading to claim drift. The main idea is Xcientist, a research harness that externalizes literature evidence, idea states, implementation plans, and validation into inspectable, contract-governed processes, preserving traceable trajectories from problem formulation to mechanism design. For astronomy, this could enable automated literature review, hypothesis generation, and validation for JWST data analysis or high-redshift galaxy studies. A concrete workflow could be using Xcientist to synthesize evidence on little red dots, generate SED fitting plans, and validate results against literature. The main caveat is that Xcientist is demonstrated on memory systems, traffic forecasting, and physics-informed neural networks, not astronomy, so adaptation may require domain-specific contracts.

Reason

Directly addresses AI scientists for automated research workflows, which is a core interest.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Look for the specific contract structures and how they encode domain knowledge
  • Check whether the system supports multi-modal data (images, spectra, catalogs) as research artifacts
  • Examine the claim drift detection mechanism and whether it could flag inconsistencies in astronomical reasoning
Abstract

AI systems can increasingly automate scientific workflows, but the reasoning that links prior evidence, generated ideas, experiments and final claims often remains implicit inside model inference. Here we introduce Xcientist, a research harness that externalizes research synthesis and experimental validation into inspectable, contract-governed processes. Xcientist organizes literature evidence, idea states, implementation plans, ablation records and repair traces as persistent research artifacts, so that generated mechanisms can be grounded, executed, tested and revised without losing their evidential basis. We identify claim drift as a failure mode of automated research, where runnable artifacts no longer support the mechanism originally claimed. Across training-free memory systems, graph-structured traffic forecasting and multi-scale physics-informed neural networks, Xcientist preserves traceable trajectories from problem formulation to mechanism design, validation and bounded revision. These results suggest that AI scientists should be evaluated not only by their final artifacts, but by whether their synthesis and validation processes remain attributable, inspectable and scientifically accountable.

ThousandWorlds: A benchmark for climate emulation of potentially habitable exoplanets

Edward T. Stevenson , Mei Ting Mak , Eric Wolf , Denis E. Sergeev , Tobi Hammond , N. J. Mayne , Miles Cranmer

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

This paper addresses the problem that global climate models (GCMs) for exoplanets are computationally expensive, limiting the interpretation of atmospheric signatures. It introduces ThousandWorlds, an ML-ready benchmark dataset of ~1800 simulations from five GCMs, mapping planet parameters to 3D atmospheric fields. For astronomy, this is directly relevant because it provides a testbed for developing fast emulators that could accelerate the interpretation of JWST exoplanet spectra. You could imagine training a neural network on ThousandWorlds to predict atmospheric temperature or cloud structure from a few planetary parameters, then using that emulator to rapidly explore the parameter space of a newly observed exoplanet. A key caveat is that the benchmark currently covers only a limited set of planet types (potentially habitable), and the best-performing baselines are Gaussian processes, not deep learning, suggesting that off-the-shelf methods may not work well.

Reason

This paper directly addresses a key bottleneck in exoplanet climate modeling by providing a benchmark for machine-learning emulators, which is highly relevant to automated scientific discovery and could transfer to other astronomical forward-modeling problems.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Look for the specific planet parameters and atmospheric fields included in the dataset to assess coverage for your exoplanet of interest.
  • Check the evaluation protocol that measures performance relative to inter-GCM disagreement, which is a realistic metric for emulator usefulness.
  • Examine the performance of the seven baselines to see if any deep learning method approaches the GP performance, or if the problem truly requires non-deep approaches.
Abstract

The search for life beyond Earth will depend on detecting faint signatures in the atmospheres of potentially habitable exoplanets. Interpreting those signatures requires understanding the host planet's climate: the same molecule may signal life on one planet and abiotic chemistry on another. Global climate models (GCMs) provide this understanding, but individual runs can require up to millions of core-hours and substantial domain expert time. Machine-learning emulators could remove this bottleneck, but progress has been limited by the absence of a curated, multi-model exoclimate dataset. We introduce ThousandWorlds, an ML-ready benchmark for exoclimate emulation and for the broader regime of low-data, multi-simulator, parameter-to-field regression. The dataset contains approximately 1800 simulations from five GCMs, mapping eight planet parameters to 3D atmospheric fields including temperature, humidity, winds, clouds, and radiation. Three nested subsets define progressively harder challenges: single-simulator regression, multi-simulator regression with complete observations, and multi-simulator regression with structured missingness. We propose two evaluation protocols: one for ranking methods, and one that measures performance relative to the disagreement between GCMs themselves. We evaluate seven baselines spanning simple methods, deep learning, and Gaussian processes. SproutRAG: Attention-Guided Tree Search with Progressive Embeddings for Long-Document RAG

Amirhossein Abaskohi , Issam H. Laradji , Peter West , Giuseppe Carenini

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

This paper asks how to build a retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) system that can retrieve relevant information at the right granularity (sentence, paragraph, section) from long documents without costly LLM calls. It introduces SproutRAG, which learns a binary chunking tree from inter-sentence attention and uses hierarchical beam search to retrieve candidates at multiple levels. For astronomy, this is highly relevant because astronomical literature (e.g., instrument papers, survey descriptions) is long and multi-scale, and an AI agent that can retrieve the right level of detail (e.g., a specific calibration step vs. an overview of the instrument) would be very useful. You could imagine using SproutRAG to build a RAG system for the JWST documentation or for a specific subfield like high-redshift galaxy papers. A key caveat is that the framework requires training on the target document corpus, which may be computationally expensive, and the attention-based tree construction may not work well on highly structured documents (e.g., tables, equations).

Reason

This paper proposes a hierarchical RAG framework that learns to retrieve at multiple granularities without extra LLM calls. This is directly relevant to building AI agents for literature review and data analysis in astronomy, where papers and technical documentation are long and multi-scale.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Check whether the attention-guided tree construction works on documents with heavy mathematical notation or tables, which are common in astronomy papers.
  • Look for the computational cost of training the tree structure and whether it scales to a large corpus like all arXiv astrophysics papers.
  • Examine the four benchmarks (scientific, legal, open-domain) to see if any are close to the astronomy domain, and check the improvement over baselines.
Abstract

Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) systems must balance retrieval granularity with contextual coherence, a challenge that existing methods address through LLM-guided chunking, single-level context expansion, or hierarchical summarization. These approaches variously depend on costly LLM calls during indexing or retrieval, limit context aggregation to a single granularity level, or introduce information loss through summarization. We present SproutRAG, an attention-guided hierarchical RAG framework that addresses this trade-off by organizing sentence-level chunks into progressively larger but semantically coherent units, using learned inter-sentence attention to construct a binary chunking tree. Unlike prior approaches that rely on external LLMs, fixed context expansion, or lossy summarization, SproutRAG learns which attention heads and layers best capture semantic document structure, enabling multi-granularity retrieval without additional LLM calls or compressed summaries. At retrieval time, SproutRAG uses hierarchical beam search to retrieve candidates at multiple granularities, capturing multi-sentence relevance beyond flat retrieval. The framework is trained end-to-end with a joint objective that improves both embeddings and tree structure. Experiments across four benchmarks spanning scientific, legal, and open-domain settings demonstrate that SproutRAG improves information efficiency (IE) by 6.1% on average over the strongest baseline. CaVe-VLM-CoT: An Interpretable Vision-Language Model Framework

Sneha Rao , Shaina Raza , Dhanesh Ramachandram

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

This paper aims to reduce hallucinations in Vision-Language Models (VLMs) by enforcing evidence-grounded reasoning. The core idea is a five-stage closed-loop pipeline (Extractor, Retriever, Solver, Citation Injector, Verifier) where ungrounded claims trigger targeted re-retrieval, and a suite of 23 metrics (including CaVeScore) measures retrieval quality and citation faithfulness. For astronomy, this could be applied to multimodal tasks like verifying that a VLM's description of a JWST image is actually supported by the image data and associated catalog entries. A concrete workflow would be using this framework to build a system that answers questions about galaxy morphology or AGN properties from JWST images and spectra, with citations to specific regions or spectral lines. A key caveat is that the method was evaluated on general science QA (ScienceQA, MMMU) and may need adaptation for specialized astronomical data formats and domain-specific citation structures.

Reason

The paper presents a modular, reflection-based agentic-RAG framework with citation grounding and verification, which directly maps to interests in LLM agents for scientific workflows and retrieval-augmented generation.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Check whether the framework's verification step can be adapted to domain-specific knowledge bases (e.g., NASA ADS, SIMBAD) for citation grounding.
  • Look for ablation studies showing the contribution of each stage (Extractor, Retriever, etc.) to overall accuracy and citation faithfulness.
  • Examine the CaVeScore metric definition to see if it can be applied to multimodal astronomical data (images + spectra + text).
Abstract

Vision-Language Models (VLMs) remain prone to hallucinations, producing fluent but visually unfaithful outputs. Existing chain-of-thought and retrieval-augmented methods only partially address this, as they neither enforce step-level citation grounding nor route verification failures back to retrieval for correction. We present CaVe-VLM-CoT, a modular reflection-based agentic-RAG framework that enforces evidence-grounded reasoning through a five-stage closed-loop pipeline: Extractor, Retriever, Solver, Citation Injector, and Verifier, in which detected ungrounded claims trigger structured feedback to the Extractor for targeted re-retrieval. Since no existing framework jointly measures retrieval quality, step-wise citation faithfulness, and cross-modal grounding, we propose a suite of 23 component-wise metrics across all stages, anchored by CaVeScore, a composite metric weighting accuracy, citation precision and recall, attribution, and evidence grounding. Without any architectural or prompt modifications, CaVe-VLM-CoT achieves 87.1\% accuracy and 56.6\% CaVeScore on ScienceQA , and 55.2\% accuracy and 35.7\% CaVeScore on MMMU (30 subjects).

MCompassRAG: Topic Metadata as a Semantic Compass for Paragraph-Level Retrieval

Amirhossein Abaskohi , Raymond Li , Gaetano Cimino , Peter West , Giuseppe Carenini , Issam H. Laradji

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

This paper asks how to improve retrieval precision in RAG systems without increasing latency, especially for deep research tasks over large, heterogeneous corpora. The core idea is to enrich chunk embeddings with topic metadata and train a lightweight retriever via LLM-teacher distillation, enabling topic-aware retrieval without extra LLM calls at inference time. For astronomy, this could power an LLM agent that searches across thousands of arXiv papers, telescope documentation, and data release notes to answer specific questions (e.g., 'What are the known biases in JWST NIRSpec flux calibration?'). A concrete workflow would be building a literature review agent that retrieves relevant paragraphs from a large corpus of astronomy papers, using topic metadata to avoid mixing unrelated concepts. A key caveat is that the method is evaluated on general-domain benchmarks (e.g., HotpotQA, 2WikiMultihopQA), not on scientific or astronomy-specific corpora, so performance on domain-specific terminology and citation-heavy text is unknown.

Reason

This paper improves retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) with topic metadata, which is directly relevant to building LLM agents for literature review and scientific discovery in astronomy.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Check if the topic metadata is automatically generated or requires manual annotation; automatic generation would be essential for scaling to astronomy literature.
  • Look for ablation studies showing the contribution of topic metadata vs. standard dense retrieval.
  • Examine whether the method handles multi-modal queries (e.g., text + image captions) or only text queries.
  • Check the computational cost of training the lightweight retriever; is it feasible for a small research group?
Abstract

Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) systems depend critically on how documents are chunked and searched. Fine-grained chunks can improve retrieval precision but expand the search space, increasing latency and cost; larger chunks reduce the number of candidates but make dense similarity less reliable, as the representation for each chunk mixes multiple topics and introduces more semantic noise. This trade-off becomes especially limiting in deep research tasks, where retrieval must be both fast and precise across large, heterogeneous corpora. We introduce MCompassRAG, a metadata-guided retrieval framework that uses topic-level signals as a semantic compass for selecting relevant evidence. Instead of relying only on cosine similarity between queries and noisy chunk embeddings, MCompassRAG enriches chunk representations with topic metadata in the same embedding space and trains a lightweight retriever through LLM-teacher distillation. At inference time, MCompassRAG performs topic-aware retrieval without additional LLM calls, improving both efficiency and evidence quality. Across six complex retrieval benchmarks, MCompassRAG improves information efficiency (IE) by 8.24% on average with over 5 times lower latency than the strongest efficient RAG baselines. Anomaly Detection for Sparse and Irregular Multivariate Time Series with Latent SDEs

Martin Uray , Dominik Geng , Florian Graf , Stefan Huber , Roland Kwitt

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

The paper tackles anomaly detection in multivariate time series that are often sparse, irregularly sampled, or partially observed, a common problem in astronomy. It uses a generative approach based on latent Stochastic Differential Equations (SDEs), which model the data as a continuous-time stochastic dynamical system, naturally handling missing observations and irregular sampling. This is directly applicable to astronomical light curves (e.g., from LSST, ZTF, or TESS) where observations are unevenly spaced. An astronomer could use this method to detect anomalous transients, variable stars, or AGN flares in survey data without needing to interpolate or bin the data. A key caveat is that the method is demonstrated on six benchmark datasets (likely industrial/cybersecurity), not astronomical data, so performance on real astrophysical time series needs verification. The method also assumes cyclic behavior, which may not suit all astronomical sources.

Reason

This paper directly addresses anomaly detection in sparse, irregularly sampled multivariate time series using latent SDEs, which is highly relevant to astronomical light curves, transient detection, and survey data from LSST/Rubin.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Look for experiments on astronomical or astrophysical time series data, or any discussion of domain transfer to scientific datasets.
  • Check the computational cost and scalability to large survey datasets (e.g., millions of light curves from LSST).
  • Examine how the anomaly score is defined and whether it can be interpreted physically (e.g., distinguishing novae from AGN flares).
  • Check if the latent SDE model can incorporate known physical priors (e.g., periodicities, power-law noise).
Abstract

Multivariate time series anomaly detection (MTSAD) is critical for a wide range of application areas, such as industrial monitoring, cybersecurity, or healthcare. Real-world data is often sparse, irregularly sampled or partially observed, yet existing methods assume uniformly sampled time series. We propose a generative approach based on Latent SDEs that projects the observed time series on a continuous-time stochastic dynamical system, directly being able to handle missing observations and irregular sampling, while also naturally capturing possible cyclic behavior that many real-world use cases inherently possess. Experiments on six anomaly benchmark datasets show that our proposed method ranks first among state-of-the-art baselines. We further demonstrate that our method remains robust under severe data sparsity, while performance significantly degrades for the tested baseline methods. These results highlight latent SDEs as a natural inductive bias for anomaly detection in multivariate time series, especially in presence of real-world irregularities.
