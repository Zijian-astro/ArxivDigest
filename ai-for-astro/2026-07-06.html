Grounded autonomous research: a fault-tolerant LLM pipeline from corpus to manuscript in frontier computational physics

Haonan Huang

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

The paper presents a fault-tolerant LLM pipeline that autonomously conducts research from a corpus of 11,083 condensed-matter physics papers to a publication-grade manuscript with novel findings. It uses 47 fresh-context sessions across six phases, with 2,162 literature-consultation events, and incorporates adversarial review and reproduction calibration. For astronomy, this pipeline could be adapted to autonomously analyze JWST data, generate hypotheses about high-redshift galaxies, and write papers. The method is grounded in literature and uses redundancy for fault tolerance. However, it requires significant domain adaptation and human intervention for reproduction failures. The approach is highly relevant for automated scientific discovery in astronomy.

Reason

Directly addresses autonomous research pipelines for computational physics, with clear relevance to automated scientific discovery in astronomy.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Check if the pipeline can be adapted to astronomical literature and data (e.g., JWST catalogs, spectra)
  • Look for details on the calibration checkpoints and how they ensure numerical confrontation with literature
  • See if the adversarial review mechanism can catch hallucinations specific to astronomical claims
  • Evaluate the fault tolerance primitives and whether they scale to larger, multi-modal astronomical datasets
Abstract

Autonomous-research agents have demonstrated end-to-end LLM automation in machine-learning sandboxes where execution provides calibration. Frontier physical science differs categorically: physical reasoning underlies every methodology choice, toolchains are often underdocumented, and calibration must come from external literature anchors - which unscaffolded agents cite but do not confront, hallucinating plausible, unverifiable results from internal priors. We present a pipeline that runs end-to-end from a corpus of 11,083 recent condensed-matter physics arXiv papers to a publication-grade manuscript with three substantive physics findings (here on altermagnetic piezomagnetism): the agent autonomously conceives a research direction by mapping the corpus, calibrates methodology by reproducing published references, conducts novel first-principles computations, and writes the manuscript - grounded in literature throughout, across 47 fresh-context sessions in six phases sharing only on-disk state, with 2,162 literature-consultation events. Fault tolerance emerges from redundancy: fresh-context isolation, distributed grounding, and adversarial review catch what any single session misses; pre- and post-pilot stages are fully autonomous, and pilot requires bounded human intervention only at reproduction failures - operational knowledge curation, not scientific direction. Two paired failure modes - a pre-architecture baseline and a no-pilot ablation - isolate structurally enforced numerical confrontation at calibration checkpoints as the operative grounding mechanism. The primitives, characterized failure modes, and quantified intervention pattern lay a foundation for autonomous research in high-stakes scientific domains beyond computational physics.

IsoSci: A Benchmark of Isomorphic Cross-Domain Science Problems for Evaluating Reasoning versus Knowledge Retrieval in LLMs

Samir Abdaljalil , Erchin Serpedin , Hasan Kurban

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

This paper asks whether chain-of-thought reasoning truly improves scientific problem-solving or just retrieves domain knowledge better. It introduces ISOSCI, a benchmark of science problem pairs that share the same logical structure but require different domain knowledge. The key finding is that 91.3% of reasoning gains are knowledge-dependent, not structure-invariant, challenging the assumption that reasoning alone helps. For astronomy, this matters because an LLM agent analyzing JWST spectra must distinguish when it needs to recall galaxy formation physics versus when it can reason step-by-step. You could use this framework to test whether an LLM agent truly reasons about SED fitting or just memorizes common templates. A caveat is that the benchmark covers general science, not astronomy specifically, so domain-specific validation is needed.

Reason

Directly evaluates LLM reasoning vs. knowledge retrieval, which is central to building reliable research agents for astronomy workflows.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Check whether the benchmark includes any astronomy or astrophysics problem pairs, or if the logic transfers directly.
  • Look for the specific reasoning-mode gains table to see which model families and sizes were tested.
  • Examine the chain-of-thought prompting strategy used; see if it is applicable to multi-step astronomical data analysis tasks.
Abstract

We introduce ISOSCI, a benchmark of isomorphic cross-domain science problem pairs that separates reasoning ability from domain knowledge retrieval in LLM evaluation. Each pair shares identical logical structure but requires different domain-specific knowledge, enabling controlled attribution of reasoning-mode gains. Across five model pairs spanning four model families, we find that 91.3% of reasoning-mode gains are knowledge-dependent rather than structure-invariant (63/69 gains; Wilson 95% CI [82.3%, 96.0%]), directly challenging the assumption that chain-of-thought reasoning improves short-horizon procedural scientific problem-solving. Reasoning toggles on highly capable models provide less than 5 percentage points accuracy gain across all domains, and a reasoning-specialized model (o3-mini) that outperforms its standard counterpart on GPQA Diamond (+19.2 percentage points) underperforms on ISOSCI (-24.7 percentage points), showing that benchmark choice determines conclusions about reasoning utility. Hidden Forgetting in Continual Multimodal Learning: When Accuracy Survives but Grounding Fails

Qianyu Chen , Canran Xiao , Runxuan Tang

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

This paper asks whether multimodal LLMs that are continually fine-tuned on new tasks forget not just old answers, but also how they used visual/textual evidence to arrive at those answers. The authors find that standard accuracy metrics can stay high while the model silently shifts its reliance to different, less appropriate evidence channels—a phenomenon they call 'hidden evidence-use forgetting'. They propose a new training method, RCL, that freezes a previous version of the model as a reference and uses counterfactual tests to estimate which evidence channels each version relies on, then jointly optimizes for correct answers and stable evidence use. For astronomy, this is directly relevant to any workflow where a model is fine-tuned on new survey data (e.g., JWST vs. HST) and we need to ensure it still correctly uses features like spectral lines or morphological shapes. A concrete workflow would be fine-tuning a vision-language model on JWST data for galaxy classification, then using RCL to verify it hasn't silently stopped using key spectral features. A caveat is that the method requires storing previous model checkpoints and running counterfactual interventions, which may be computationally expensive for very large models.

Reason

Directly studies continual learning in multimodal LLMs, which is relevant for adapting foundation models to evolving astronomical tasks and datasets. The concept of 'hidden evidence-use forgetting' is analogous to an astronomical model silently shifting which data features (e.g., spectra vs. photometry) it relies on.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Check whether the paper's experiments include multimodal data types analogous to astronomy (e.g., charts, documents) or only natural images/text.
  • Look for quantitative metrics on 'reliance drift' and 'dominant evidence flips' that could translate to tracking which features (e.g., flux ratios, colors) a model uses.
  • See if the method's reliance estimation via counterfactual interventions is feasible for large models (e.g., 7B+ parameters) or only demonstrated on smaller ones.
Abstract

Multimodal large language models must continually adapt to evolving tasks and domains, yet standard continual learning metrics mainly measure whether old answers remain correct, leaving the stability of multimodal grounding largely unexamined. We study this overlooked failure mode and ask whether a continually adapted MLLM can preserve not only what it answers, but also how it uses visual, textual, OCR, chart, and document evidence. We identify \emph{hidden evidence-use forgetting}, where answer accuracy is retained while the model silently shifts toward different or less grounded evidence channels, and propose \textsc{RCL}, a replay-free reliance-constrained continual learning framework. \textsc{RCL} freezes the previous checkpoint as a behavioral reference, estimates teacher and student evidence-reliance profiles through counterfactual channel interventions, and jointly optimizes task learning, prediction preservation, and reliance preservation without adding inference-time cost. Across CoIN, COAST, MCITlib, and an evidence-sensitive multimodal stream, \textsc{RCL} consistently improves final performance and reduces forgetting over replay-free, PEFT, routing, and memory-assisted baselines, while substantially lowering modality reliance drift, dominant evidence flips, and hidden forgetting rates. These results suggest that robust continual multimodal learning requires preserving the evidence path behind correct answers, not merely the answers themselves.

CheckRLM: Effective Knowledge-Thought Coherence Checking in Retrieval-Augmented Reasoning

Dingling Xu , Ruobing Wang , Qingfei Zhao , Yukun Yan , Zhichun Wang , Daren Zha , Shi Yu , Zhenghao Liu , Shuo Wang , Xu Han , Maosong Sun

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

This paper proposes CheckRLM, a framework that improves the reliability of reasoning language models by checking factual claims against external knowledge during inference and correcting errors. It uses retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) to detect and fix knowledge inconsistencies in the reasoning chain. For astronomy, this is highly relevant for building LLM agents that perform literature review, synthesize findings, or generate analysis code, where factual accuracy is critical. The method could be applied to verify claims about astronomical sources, redshifts, or physical parameters against trusted databases. However, the paper evaluates on general knowledge tasks, not scientific domains. Before investing time, check if the framework can be adapted to domain-specific knowledge bases (e.g., SIMBAD, NED, ADS).

Reason

Knowledge-thought coherence checking in RAG; directly applicable to improving reliability of LLM agents for astronomy literature review and data analysis.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Check if the paper evaluates on any scientific or domain-specific knowledge bases.
  • Look for analysis of how the refinement mechanism handles domain-specific terminology and data formats.
  • Examine the computational cost of the checking and correction process for long reasoning chains.
Abstract

Reasoning Language Models (RLMs) have significantly improved performance on complex tasks by extending the reasoning chain. However, these chains are prone to containing factual errors, particularly in knowledge-intensive tasks. To address this issue, we propose CheckRLM, a framework that improves the reliability of the reasoning process through Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) by timely checking and correcting factual errors. Specifically, CheckRLM extracts factual claims from the reasoning chain to identify and localize subtle knowledge inconsistencies during inference. Upon detection of errors, a refinement mechanism performs minimal-cost yet precise corrections by leveraging external knowledge, ensuring coherence between the reasoning chain and correct knowledge. Extensive experiments demonstrate that CheckRLM substantially outperforms existing baselines, exhibiting a strong capability to mitigate error accumulation in long-horizon reasoning with lower costs. InvSplat: Inverse Feed-Forward Scene Splatting

Polina Karpikova , Wenjing Bian , Haofei Xu , Hendrik Lensch , Andreas Geiger

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

The paper presents InvSplat, a feed-forward framework for inverse rendering that predicts 3D Gaussian primitives with material attributes (albedo, metallic, roughness) from multi-view images. It enables relighting and novel view synthesis without per-scene optimization. For astronomy, this could reconstruct 3D structures of nebulae or galaxies from multi-band images, enabling physical modeling of dust and gas. The method's feed-forward nature is efficient for large surveys. However, it is designed for synthetic and real-world objects, not astronomical scenes with emission lines or complex radiative transfer.

Reason

Inverse rendering for 3D scene reconstruction with material properties could be applied to astronomical imaging for modeling nebula, galaxies, or planetary surfaces.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Check if the Gaussian splatting representation can handle astronomical features like diffuse emission and point sources
  • Look for evaluation on multi-view consistency, which is relevant for astronomical imaging from different telescopes
  • See if the material estimation network can be adapted for astronomical reflectance or emission properties
Abstract

Inverse rendering aims to recover both 3D geometry and physically meaningful material properties from images, enabling applications such as relighting and novel view synthesis. Optimization-based methods achieve high fidelity but require costly per-scene fitting, while image-space learning-based approaches often suffer from multi-view inconsistencies and lack an explicit 3D representation for stable novel view rendering. We present a feed-forward multi-view reconstruction framework for inverse rendering that directly predicts a structured 3D Gaussian representation with intrinsic material attributes. Each Gaussian primitive is parameterized by mean, normal, opacity, rotation, scale, albedo, metallic, and roughness, enabling a disentangled and physically grounded scene representation. Our model integrates priors from a material estimation network with a multi-view 3D reconstruction backbone, allowing joint prediction of geometry and reflectance parameters in a single forward pass. Experiments on synthetic and real-world datasets demonstrate improved multi-view consistency compared to 2D baselines, accurate material recovery, and stable novel view rendering. Our representation further supports physically-based relighting and more faithful modeling of view-dependent effects compared to existing RGB-based feed-forward reconstruction methods.
