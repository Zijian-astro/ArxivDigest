A General-Purpose VLM Can Teach an Astronomy Foundation Model to Better Recognize Galaxy Morphology

Dichang Zhang , Jiaqi Deng , Yixuan Shao , Yuanpeng Liu , Jiali Cui , Zhiqiang Lao , Heather Yu , Liang Peng , Simon Birrer , Dimitris Samaras

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

The paper asks whether a general-purpose vision-language model (VLM) can provide weak supervision to improve galaxy morphology classification when human labels are scarce. It introduces a survey-oriented VQA benchmark and shows that a VLM teacher consistently improves Zoobot, an astronomy foundation model, across two survey domains and annotation budgets. This matters for astronomy because it offers a label-efficient way to adapt foundation models to new surveys like LSST and Roman. A concrete workflow could be using a VLM to generate pseudo-labels for morphology classification on JWST or Rubin images. The main caveat is that the VLM is not reliable enough to replace human annotators, so it is best used as a teacher in a semi-supervised setting.

Reason

The paper directly addresses using a general-purpose VLM to teach an astronomy foundation model for galaxy morphology classification, which aligns with multimodal models and astronomical source classification.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Check whether the VQA benchmark includes JWST-like imaging or only optical surveys
  • Look for details on how the VLM teacher's uncertainty is used in training
  • Assess the performance gains for low annotation budgets, which are critical for new surveys
Abstract

Existing astronomy foundation models provide strong galaxy representations, but adapting them to new survey conditions and survey-specific morphology recognition tasks still requires substantial human supervision. We show that VLM-based VQA systems contain meaningful visual-semantic priors that can serve as weak supervision for downstream morphology classifiers and improve morphology classification under limited human-label budgets. We first introduce a survey-oriented VQA benchmark spanning two representative imaging regimes and evaluate state-of-the-art VLMs on galaxy morphology questions. The results show that these models capture useful morphology signals and informative uncertainty, but are not sufficiently reliable to replace human annotators. Motivated by this finding, we use a general-purpose VLM as a morphology teacher for Zoobot, an astronomy foundation model pretrained on large-scale Galaxy Zoo annotations. Across two survey domains and multiple annotation budgets, the VLM teacher consistently improves Zoobot's downstream morphology classification. These results demonstrate that a general-purpose VLM provides knowledge complementary to an astronomy foundation model and can teach it to better recognize galaxy morphology under limited human supervision. The resulting pipeline is designed for label-efficient adaptation to forthcoming large-scale surveys, including the Vera C. Rubin Observatory's Legacy Survey of Space and Time (LSST) and the Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope. The benchmark and code are publicly available at this https URL .

Beyond Solution-Centric Search: Adaptive Inquiry and Knowledge Revision for Autonomous ML Engineering

Shaokang Fu , Yulong Tao , Linbo Jin , Jiarong Zhao , Qiming Shi , Tianjun Pan , Haonan Li , Chengyu Wang , Jia Wu , Chengfu Huo

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

This paper tackles the problem of LLM agents failing on long-horizon research tasks because they organize solution search rather than accumulating understanding. The main idea is to replace solution-centric search with an evolving information state, using an inquiry-revision loop where the agent probes unknowns and maintains revisable claims. This matters for astronomy because it offers a blueprint for autonomous agents that can iteratively refine hypotheses about JWST data or multi-wavelength catalogs. A concrete workflow could be an agent that plans observations, runs SED fits, and updates its knowledge of a source's nature. Before investing time, check whether the benchmark tasks (MLE-Bench) are representative of astronomy's data complexity and whether the 12-hour budget is realistic.

Reason

Directly addresses LLM-based agents for long-horizon autonomous research, which is a core interest for automated scientific discovery. The information-centric paradigm and knowledge revision loop could transfer to astronomy workflows like iterative data analysis or literature review.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Check if the information state representation is general enough to handle astronomical data types (images, spectra, tables).
  • Look for details on how epistemic actions are chosen and whether they could be adapted to querying databases or running simulations.
  • Inspect the evaluation metrics beyond any-medal rate, especially failure modes on long-horizon tasks.
Abstract

Long-horizon autonomous research tasks such as machine learning engineering require systems to make interdependent decisions under a limited budget. Existing LLM-based agents typically organize candidate-solution improvement through tree, graph, or chain structures, meaning that the search process determines how information is acquired and managed. We call this design solution-centric search and propose instead the information paradigm, in which an evolving information state represents the system's understanding of the task and guides solution improvement. We instantiate this paradigm in Iris, an inquiry-revision loop. For information acquisition, Iris generates local action plans from the current information state and uses epistemic actions to probe decision-critical unknowns without modifying the retained solution. For information management, Iris synthesizes observations across experiments into task knowledge composed of revisable claims with explicit scope and status. It updates this knowledge as new evidence arrives and constructs each decision context from raw evidence, structured summaries, or task knowledge at the required level of detail. On MLE-Bench, Iris attains a 64.9% any-medal rate under a 12-hour budget, the highest among compared systems. Across four tasks spanning harness engineering and model post-training, Iris also demonstrates cross-domain generalization.

SearchMaster: Grounded and Regulated Self-Play for Search Agents

Wentao Tan , Qiong Cao , Jiaqi Wang , Nan Duan

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

This paper tackles the problem of training LLM-based search agents without human-written tasks or expert demonstrations. The main idea is a self-play framework where a single LLM generates, solves, and verifies its own search tasks, with controls to avoid pseudo multi-hop questions and shallow browsing. For astronomy, this could help build agents that autonomously retrieve and synthesize multi-source information from literature or databases. A concrete workflow could be an agent that searches for JWST papers on high-redshift galaxies, verifies evidence chains, and summarizes findings. Before investing time, check if the search environment can be adapted to domain-specific databases and if the self-play quality holds for specialized astronomical queries.

Reason

Directly relevant to LLM agents for search and retrieval, which can transfer to astronomical literature/data retrieval workflows. The self-play training and verification methods could inspire automated scientific discovery agents.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Check whether the search environment is generic or can be adapted to domain-specific corpora
  • Look for details on how evidence chains are constructed and whether they require domain knowledge
  • Examine the GRPO optimization setup and its computational cost for training
Abstract

Training LLM-based search agents requires high-quality search data: tasks that demand genuine multi-hop retrieval and trajectories that use search tools effectively. Existing pipelines often depend on human-written tasks, expert demonstrations, or stronger teacher models. We present SearchMaster, a self-play framework that trains a single LLM from search tasks it generates, solves, and verifies in a local search environment. The key challenge is that self-generated tasks and rollouts can yield misleading signals: pseudo multi-hop questions, success-rate difficulty estimates that ignore search depth, and rollouts with excessive opening but little targeted evidence acquisition. SearchMaster addresses these failure modes with three controls. An Evidence-Chain Generator (ECG) grounds task generation in explicit cross-document evidence chains to reduce pseudo multi-hop questions. A Search-Depth Reward (SDR) scores task difficulty by the search depth of successful rollouts rather than success rate alone, keeping retained tasks search-intensive. An Over-Opening Penalty (OOP) regulates tool use by discouraging excessive document opening, avoiding long but shallow browsing. Verified Proposer and Solver rollouts are then jointly optimized with GRPO. Across six deep-search benchmarks, SearchMaster improves a Qwen3.5-9B backbone from 38.19% to 51.52% average accuracy, with a 30.1-point gain on BrowseComp-Plus. These results show that grounded and regulated self-play can provide effective search-agent training data without human-labeled QA pairs or expert demonstrations. The code is available at this https URL .

ReasonCast: Towards Explainable Time Series Forecasting with Reasoning

Seunghan Lee , Jun Seo , Jaehoon Lee , Junhyeok Kang , Sangjun Han , Sungdong Yoo , Minjae Kim , Tae Yoon Lim , Dongwan Kang , Hwanil Choi , Soonyoung Lee , Wonbin Ahn

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

The paper argues for a task-fused model that jointly produces numerical forecasts and text explanations in a single response. It introduces ReasonTS-Bench with five fundamental patterns and ReasonCast, a recipe to fine-tune LLMs for this joint task. This matters for astronomy because it could enable models to forecast astronomical time series while explaining the reasoning, aiding in interpretation of transients or variable sources. A concrete workflow could be using ReasonCast to predict and explain the evolution of supernova light curves. However, the benchmark is synthetic, so real astronomical data may present additional challenges.

Reason

Time series forecasting with explainable reasoning, directly relevant to astronomical time series analysis. The joint generation and understanding approach could be useful for interpreting light curves or spectral time series.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Check if the model can handle irregularly sampled and noisy astronomical time series.
  • Look for details on how the reasoning chain is generated and whether it is verifiable.
  • Assess the computational cost of fine-tuning LLMs for this task.
Abstract

Most time series (TS) models are specialized for a single task, either understanding (i.e., returning text answers about a TS) or generation (i.e., returning a numeric forecast). Only recently have unified models begun to handle the two within a single architecture. Even these models, however, produce the two outputs as task-separated paths and cannot predict a series and explain why that prediction arises within a single coherent response. In this paper, we argue for a task-fused model that jointly produces 1) prediction (generation) and 2) selfexplanation (understanding), thereby integrating 1) numerical TS forecasting and 2) interpretable text reasoning within a single response. To enable the systematic study of this capability, we present both a benchmark and a recipe that jointly address the two tasks. The benchmark, ReasonTS-Bench, identifies five fundamental patterns underlying TS and enables the joint evaluation of both tasks. ReasonCast, our recipe for finetuning any LLM to perform both tasks jointly, yields a model that generates a reasoning chain and a forecast together in a single autoregressive pass. Extensive experiments show that ReasonCast outperforms both LLMs and TS models on prediction accuracy while producing verifiable, causal reasoning. Code is available at: this https URL .

GeoCore-9B: Towards Geo-Aware Generative Foundation Models in Earth Observation

Jeonghyeok Do , Munchurl Kim

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

The paper introduces GeoCore-9B, a 9-billion-parameter generative foundation model trained from scratch on Earth observation data. It uses a Flow Matching-based Diffusion Transformer and conditions on text and geospatial metadata, with a geospatial semantic alignment loss. This matters for astronomy because similar generative models could be trained on astronomical images for tasks like image synthesis or denoising. A concrete workflow could be adapting this model to generate synthetic galaxy images conditioned on physical parameters. However, the model is trained on Earth observation, so significant adaptation is needed for astronomical data.

Reason

Generative foundation model for Earth observation, directly relevant to multimodal models for astronomical imaging. The text-conditioned generation and geospatial metadata could transfer to astronomical surveys.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Check if the model can handle the different data distribution and noise characteristics of astronomical images.
  • Look for details on the conditioning mechanism and whether it can be extended to physical parameters.
  • Assess the computational resources required for training and inference.
Abstract

Existing generative models for earth observation (EO) predominantly rely on fine-tuning natural image priors, which limits their scalability and introduces perspective biases that conflict with geospatial constraints. To address this, we introduce GeoCore-9B, a 9-billion-parameter generative foundation model, which is the first of its scale to be trained from scratch exclusively on EO data. Unlike previous EO foundation models, GeoCore-9B is built upon a Flow Matching-based Diffusion Transformer (DiT) and natively conditions generation on text descriptions and continuous geospatial metadata, including ground sample distances, latitudes, and longitudes. To overcome the convergence and spatial disorientation challenges of training at this scale, we propose a Geospatial Semantic Alignment loss. This objective distills structural Earth surface priors (e.g., terrain and urban areas) from a frozen specialist teacher network, constraining the diffusion latent trajectory during training without adding inference overhead. Pre-trained on the global-scale Git-10M dataset, GeoCore-9B demonstrates strong downstream versatility. Beyond standard proxy generative tasks, we show that GeoCore-9B can be effectively adapted for practical EO applications, including highly challenging tasks such as cloud removal and SAR-to-optical cross-modal translation. Extensive evaluations confirm that GeoCore-9B establishes new state-of-the-art performance in both visual fidelity and geographic structural accuracy.
