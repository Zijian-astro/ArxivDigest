From I/O to Code with Discovery Agent

Yihong Dong , Jiaru Qian , Haoran Zhang , Peixu Wang , Binhua Li , Zhi Jin , Yongbin Li , Ge Li , Xiaokang Yang , Xue Jiang

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

The paper tackles the problem of automatically synthesizing a program from input-output examples (IO2Code), which is harder than natural-language-to-code because the model must infer the underlying logic from behavior alone. The core idea is to frame program synthesis as an evolutionary search where an LLM acts as a mutation operator, guided by execution errors and a 'Transformation Priority Premise' that biases the LLM toward the simplest hypothesis first. For astronomy, this could automate the discovery of fitting functions, data reduction pipelines, or even physical models from observational data. A concrete workflow would be to provide a set of input-output pairs from a JWST spectrum and have the agent propose a model (e.g., a line profile or SED component) that reproduces the data. A key caveat is that the method is currently tested on simple programming benchmarks, not scientific data, so its robustness to noisy or high-dimensional astronomical inputs is unknown.

Reason

This paper directly addresses automated scientific discovery via LLM agents, which is a core interest. The method of evolutionary search over program space with LLM as mutation operator could transfer to tasks like automated data reduction or spectral fitting in astronomy.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Check whether the method is demonstrated on any scientific or continuous-valued data, or only on discrete programming tasks.
  • Look for discussion of how the search handles noisy or incomplete input-output examples, which are common in astronomy.
  • Examine the mutation prior: does it explicitly incorporate domain-specific knowledge, or is it purely syntactic?
Abstract

The automatic synthesis of a program from any form of specification is regarded as a holy grail of computer science. Fueled by LLMs, NL2Code has achieved tremendous success, yet the fundamentally more challenging task of synthesizing programs from input-output behavior, which we refer to as IO2Code, remains largely unsolved. Whereas NL2Code can exploit the semantic alignment between natural language and code acquired during pretraining, IO2Code requires recovering underlying principles from concrete computational behavior, navigating a vast and underspecified hypothesis space. To address this, we propose DIO-Agent, a discovery agent for IO2Code. Our method frames IO2Code as an evolutionary search over discrete program space, in which an LLM serves as the mutation operator and concrete error signals from execution guide each mutation. To prevent the search from wandering into structurally complex yet incorrect dead ends, we introduce the Transformation Priority Premise as a mutation prior that biases the LLM toward the simplest hypothesis consistent with current evidence, progressively escalating from constants to conditionals to iteration only when simpler constructs are insufficient. To facilitate systematic study, we further construct an IO2CodeBench spanning multiple difficulty levels. Extensive experiments show that DIO-Agent consistently outperforms both traditional program-by-example method and SOTA evolution-agent baselines across all difficulty levels and various LLMs, while substantially surpassing test-time scaling strategies with equivalent sampling budgets.

Njord: A Probabilistic Graph Neural Network for Ensemble Ocean Forecasting

Daniel Holmberg , Joel Oskarsson , Erik Larsson , Fredrik Lindsten , Teemu Roos

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

The paper asks how to produce probabilistic forecasts for chaotic ocean dynamics. It introduces Njord, which combines a deep latent variable model with a graph neural network to generate ensemble forecasts in a single forward pass. The method is applied globally at 0.25° resolution and regionally at 2 km. For astronomy, this approach could be adapted to forecast astronomical phenomena like solar activity, space weather, or gravitational wave events. The uncertainty estimates are crucial for scientific decision-making. A limitation is that the method is designed for gridded geophysical data and may need modification for irregular astronomical data like galaxy catalogs.

Reason

The paper presents a probabilistic graph neural network for ocean forecasting, directly relevant to uncertainty estimation in geophysical and astronomical forecasting.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Look for details on how the K-means cluster meshes handle irregular geometry
  • Check whether the latent variable framework can be applied to non-gridded data
  • Examine the computational scaling to larger grids or higher resolutions
Abstract

Ocean dynamics are inherently chaotic, yet existing machine learning ocean models produce only deterministic forecasts. We introduce Njord, a probabilistic data-driven model for ocean forecasting, applicable to both global and regional domains. Njord combines a deep latent variable framework with a graph neural network architecture, enabling sampling each forecast step in a single forward pass. We apply Njord globally at 0.25° resolution and regionally to the Baltic Sea at 2 km resolution. To scale to these large ocean grids we introduce K-means cluster meshes that adapt to irregular sea surface geometry. Experiments demonstrate strong performance on both domains compared to deterministic machine learning baselines, while also providing uncertainty estimates from the sampled ensemble forecasts. On the global OceanBench benchmark, Njord achieves the lowest errors on average across upper-ocean variables when evaluated against real-world observations, with the largest improvements in surface temperature prediction.

Towards Generalization of Block Attention via Automatic Segmentation and Block Distillation

Shuaiyi Li , Zhisong Zhang , Yan Wang , Lei Zhu , Dongyang Ma , Chenlong Deng , Yang Deng , Wai Lam

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

This paper tackles the problem of efficiently handling long contexts in retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) by using block attention, where input text is segmented into blocks that cannot attend to each other. The authors propose a semantic segmentation dataset and a lightweight segmenter to automatically partition text into meaningful blocks, and a block distillation training framework that uses a frozen full-attention teacher to guide the block-attention student. This is highly relevant for astronomy because RAG is a key technique for building LLM agents that can query large astronomical databases and literature. The method could be used to build an astronomy-specific RAG system that efficiently retrieves and processes information from long papers or survey catalogs. A key limitation is that the segmenter is trained on general text categories and may need fine-tuning for astronomical terminology.

Reason

Directly addresses block attention for long-context RAG, which is highly relevant to LLM-based literature review and data analysis in astronomy.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Check if the semantic segmentation dataset includes scientific or technical text categories.
  • Look for evaluation on long-context QA tasks that are similar to astronomy literature review.
  • Examine the block distillation training framework for its computational cost and scalability.
Abstract

Block attention, which processes the input as separate blocks that cannot attend to one another, offers significant potential to improve KV cache reuse in long-context scenarios such as Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG). However, its broader application is hindered by two key challenges: the difficulty of segmenting input text into meaningful, self-contained blocks, and the inefficiency of existing block fine-tuning methods that risk degrading performance. To address these, we first construct SemanticSeg, a large and diverse semantic segmentation dataset containing over 30k instances across 16 categories-including books, code, web text, and conversations with text lengths ranging from 2k to 32k. Using this dataset, we train a lightweight segmenter to automatically partition text into human-instinct-aligned blocks with controllable granularity. Second, we propose block distillation, a training framework that is more efficient than block fine-tuning, which uses a frozen full-attention teacher model to guide the block-attention student. This framework integrates three novel components: block sink tokens to mitigate information loss at block boundaries, block dropout to leverage training signals from all blocks, and token-level loss weighting to focus learning on block-attention-sensitive tokens. Experiments across multiple models and benchmarks demonstrate that our segmenter outperforms heuristic and statistical baselines, and block distillation achieves near-full-attention performance under block attention, establishing a practical and scalable pathway for deploying block attention.

When and Why Adversarial Training Improves PINNs: A Neural Tangent Kernel Perspective

Yuan-dong Cao , Chi Chiu SO , Jun-Min Wang , He Wang

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

This paper investigates why adversarial training improves physics-informed neural networks (PINNs) and proposes a new analysis framework based on neural tangent kernels. The core idea is that the discriminator in GANs influences the training dynamics of PINNs, and the authors develop a practical training algorithm that significantly reduces training pathologies. This is highly relevant for astronomy because PINNs are used for solving differential equations in astrophysics, such as modeling stellar structure or gravitational lensing. The method could improve the accuracy and training stability of PINNs for astrophysical applications. A key limitation is that the method is evaluated on benchmark PDEs, not on astrophysical problems.

Reason

Adversarial training for physics-informed neural networks is directly relevant for solving differential equations in astrophysics, such as radiative transfer or N-body simulations.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Check if the method is evaluated on any scientific or domain-specific PDEs.
  • Look for discussion of transfer to astrophysical applications.
  • Examine the neural tangent kernel analysis and its implications for training dynamics.
Abstract

Physics-informed neural networks (PINNs) are powerful surrogates for differential equations but are notoriously difficult to train due to spectral bias, stiffness, and poor accuracy on high-frequency or multiscale solutions. Adversarial training based on generative adversarial networks (GANs) has recently gained surprisingly strong empirical results in improving training, but the underlying mechanisms remain elusive. To this end, we propose a new analysis framework for adversarially trained PINNs, based on the key observation of how the discriminator in GANs can influence the training dynamics of PINNs. The framework first provides a much needed theoretical grounding to why and when adversarial training is effective in PINNs, then presents a unified analysis of GANs variants in such training, and finally leads to a new, practical, efficient training algorithm for PINNs. Empirical results demonstrate that our method can significantly reduce the pathology of PINNs training, thereby providing better models with superior performances, often several magnitudes more accurate than alternative methods.

Sparse Autoencoders enable Robust and Interpretable Fine-tuning of CLIP models

Fabian Morelli , Arnas Uselis , Ankit Sonthalia , Seong Joon Oh

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

This paper proposes a method for robust fine-tuning of CLIP models using sparse autoencoders to regularize changes to visual representations. The core idea is to penalize the addition and removal of semantically meaningful features identified by a sparse autoencoder, preventing catastrophic forgetting and enabling interpretable fine-tuning. This is highly relevant for astronomy because CLIP models can be used for zero-shot classification of astronomical images, and fine-tuning them on astronomical data could improve performance. The method could be used to adapt CLIP for galaxy morphology classification or anomaly detection in JWST images. A key limitation is that the method is evaluated on ImageNet and distribution shift benchmarks, not on astronomical data.

Reason

Sparse autoencoders for interpretable fine-tuning of CLIP models are highly relevant for adapting vision-language models to astronomical data.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Check if the method is evaluated on any scientific or domain-specific datasets.
  • Look for discussion of transfer to remote sensing or astronomical imaging.
  • Examine the sparse autoencoder training and its impact on interpretability.
Abstract

Large-scale pre-trained vision-language models like CLIP demonstrate remarkable zero-shot performance across diverse tasks. However, fine-tuning these models to improve downstream performance often degrades robustness against distribution shifts. Recent approaches have attempted to mitigate this trade-off, but often rely on computationally expensive text-guidance. We propose a novel method for robust fine-tuning, SAE-FT, which operates only on the model's visual representations. SAE-FT regularizes changes to these representations by penalizing the addition and removal of semantically meaningful features identified by a Sparse Autoencoder trained on the pre-trained model. This constraint prevents catastrophic forgetting and makes the fine-tuning process interpretable, enabling direct analysis of semantic changes. SAE-FT is both mechanistically transparent and computationally efficient, matching or exceeding state-of-the-art performance on ImageNet and its associated distribution shift benchmarks. Optimized Three-Dimensional Photovoltaic Structures with LLM guided Tree Search

Michael P. Brenner , Lizzie Dorfman , John C. Platt

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

This paper demonstrates a workflow combining a coding agent (AntiGravity) with an LLM-driven tree search (ERA) to autonomously design high-efficiency 3D photovoltaic structures. The key idea is to use the coding agent to reproduce known physics calculations, then use tree search to explore design variations, with iterative patching to eliminate reward hacking (non-physical solutions). For astronomy, this workflow could be adapted to optimize telescope observing strategies, design instrument configurations, or discover new astrophysical models. A concrete workflow could involve using an LLM agent to set up a radiative transfer simulation, then tree search to find parameters that match observed spectra. A caveat is that the paper's physics engine is relatively simple (optics for solar panels), and scaling to complex astrophysical simulations may require significant engineering.

Reason

This paper combines LLM agents with tree search for scientific discovery in a physics domain, directly relevant to automated scientific discovery for astronomy.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Check whether the tree search algorithm (ERA) is general enough to handle continuous parameter spaces common in astrophysical modeling.
  • Look for details on how reward hacking was detected and patched, as this is critical for scientific validity.
  • See if the coding agent can be adapted to use astronomical simulation codes (e.g., CLOUDY, RADMC-3D) or if it requires a simple score function.
Abstract

We present a case study for how AI coding systems can be used to generate novel scientific hypotheses. We combine a generic coding agent (Google's AntiGravity) with an LLM-driven tree search algorithm (Empirical Research Assistance / ERA) to autonomously generate high-efficiency three-dimensional photovoltaic (3DPV) structures that overcome losses limiting flat solar panels at mid-latitudes. These structures operate by presenting favorable angles to the sun throughout the day, and for illustrative purposes we focus on optimizing performance for a single solar day. Our workflow begins by using AntiGravity to reproduce calculations \cite{bernardi2012solar} showing that 3DPV can have energy densities much higher than stationary flat PV panels. We use these initial designs as the starting point for large scale tree search, where we seek improved solutions and score them for their diurnal yield. The initial tree search leads to nominally more efficient solutions, yet they are caused by algorithmic reward hacking, arising from non-physical design features such as structurally levitating disconnected tiers and exploitations of the discretizations in the optics solver. To counteract this, we develop a workflow where the coding agent iteratively patches the physics engine with constraints to eliminate reward hacking. With reward-hacking eliminated, ERA discovers a series of designs with various constraints and improved performance, including optimal designs with different fixed collector areas, optimizing zenith tracking and avoiding self shadowing. Combining coding agents with tree search (ERA) provides a powerful platform for scientific discovery, for problems whose solutions can be empirically evaluated with a score function.
