## 1. MetaResearcher: Scaling Deep Research via Self-Reflective Reinforcement Learning in Adversarial Virtual Environments

- arXiv ID: `2606.19893`
- Authors: Wei Yu , Suxing Liu , Minjie Yu , Jiahao Wang , Zhijian Zheng , Haocheng Deng , Bing Li
- Score: 9
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19893
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.19893
- Subjects: Artificial Intelligence (cs.AI)
- Reason: This paper directly addresses deep research agents, self-reflective reinforcement learning, and multi-agent swarms for autonomous information gathering and synthesis, which are highly relevant to automated scientific discovery and LLM agents for literature review and data analysis in astronomy.

Short digest:
This paper proposes MetaResearcher, a framework for scaling deep research agent training across four dimensions: an evolving virtual world with adversarial misinformation, discovery-oriented tasks beyond fact retrieval, a self-reflective meta-reward mechanism, and a heterogeneous multi-agent swarm architecture. The core idea is to train agents to develop source credibility assessment, temporal conflict resolution, and collaborative research strategies through reinforcement learning in adversarial environments. This is highly relevant to astronomy because it could enable automated literature review, hypothesis generation, and contradiction resolution across astronomical datasets and publications. For example, an astronomer could use such an agent to synthesize findings from JWST papers on little red dots, identify conflicting results, and propose new observational tests. A key limitation is that the framework is designed for general research tasks and may require significant adaptation to handle domain-specific astronomical data formats, coordinate systems, and instrument characteristics. Before investing time, check whether the agent can ingest astronomical catalogs, spectra, and images, or if it is limited to text-based literature.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Look for evaluation on GAIA and Xbench-DS benchmarks to see if the agent handles multi-step research tasks. Check whether the Evolving Virtual World includes astronomical data or only general web text. Examine the Heterogeneous Multi-Agent Swarm architecture to see if Scout, Filter, and Synthesizer models can be specialized for astronomy workflows. Look for discussion of tool use (e.g., API calls to databases, code execution) that could be adapted for astronomical data analysis.

## 2. Language-Instructed Vision Embeddings for Controllable and Generalizable Perception

- arXiv ID: `2606.19584`
- Authors: Chengzhi Mao , Xudong Lin , Wen-Sheng Chu
- Score: 8
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19584
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.19584
- Subjects: Computer Vision and Pattern Recognition (cs.CV)
- Reason: Paper proposes language-instructed vision embeddings (LIVE) that use language to dynamically guide vision encoders, directly relevant to multimodal models for astronomy (e.g., combining images and text queries for source classification or anomaly detection).

Short digest:
This paper addresses the problem that vision foundation models are static feature extractors, requiring large downstream models for task adaptation. It proposes LIVE, which uses language instructions to dynamically guide the vision encoder at inference time, producing task-centric embeddings without retraining. This reduces visual hallucinations and improves generalization to unseen instructions. This matters for astronomy because it could enable a single vision encoder to perform multiple tasks (e.g., morphology classification, anomaly detection, source finding) based on natural language queries from a user. A concrete workflow would be to use LIVE to query JWST images for 'little red dots' or 'high-redshift galaxy candidates' without retraining the encoder. A caveat is that the paper evaluates on general vision tasks (VQA, MMVP) and not on astronomical images; the method's performance on scientific images with different statistics is unknown. Before investing time, check if the method requires paired language-image data for training, which is scarce in astronomy.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Look for experiments on out-of-distribution images or domains beyond natural images.
- Check whether the language guidance is effective for fine-grained visual features (e.g., morphological details) or only for coarse categories.
- Verify if the method can be applied to grayscale or multi-band astronomical images without modification.

## 3. TerraMARS: A Domain-Adapted Small-Language-Model Pipeline for Mars Terraforming Literature

- arXiv ID: `2606.19700`
- Authors: Jyotsna Singh , Ash Black , Jeff Larsen , Scott R. Saleska
- Score: 8
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19700
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.19700
- Subjects: Computation and Language (cs.CL)
- Reason: Domain-adapted small language model for Mars terraforming literature; directly relevant to using LLMs for scientific literature extraction in astronomy.

Short digest:
This paper presents TerraMARS, a pipeline that uses a fine-tuned small language model (Gemma 3 1B with QLoRA) to extract structured information from Mars science literature into JSON format. The system uses multistage retrieval and chunking to answer domain-specific questions and convert unstructured text into machine-readable data. For astronomy, this is directly applicable to extracting physical parameters from exoplanet or galaxy literature, building knowledge graphs for high-redshift galaxies, or automating literature reviews for JWST results. The pipeline could be adapted to extract spectroscopic redshifts, emission-line fluxes, or morphological classifications from papers. However, the current domain is Mars terraforming, not astronomy, and the extraction accuracy needs improvement. The use of QLoRA for domain adaptation is a practical approach that could be replicated with astronomy-specific datasets.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Check the accuracy of information extraction on Mars-specific questions and whether the pipeline handles numerical constraints (e.g., temperature ranges).
- Look for details on the chunking and retrieval strategy, which would need adaptation for astronomy papers with complex figures and tables.
- Examine the QLoRA fine-tuning recipe to assess feasibility of retraining on astronomy literature.

- Reason: Paper provides a systematic evaluation of black-box uncertainty estimation methods for LLMs, which is directly relevant for building trustworthy AI agents for astronomy.

Short digest:
This paper addresses the problem of estimating uncertainty in LLM outputs when only the API is accessible (no logits or hidden states). It systematically reviews and benchmarks 24 black-box UE methods across 4 models and 4 datasets, finding that no single method dominates but hybrid methods combining multiple signals perform well. For astronomy, this is crucial for building reliable research agents that can flag uncertain classifications or literature extractions. A concrete workflow would be using a black-box UE method to assess the confidence of an LLM agent's answer when asked 'Is this galaxy an AGN?' based on a paper abstract. The main caveat is that the benchmark uses general NLP tasks (e.g., question answering, summarization), and the performance of these UE methods on domain-specific astronomical tasks (e.g., extracting redshifts from text) is unknown.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Check the list of UE methods: are any of them practical for a research agent (e.g., sampling-based or verbalization-based) without requiring multiple API calls?
- Look for the evaluation on 'explanation-based' methods: do they provide calibrated uncertainty or just verbose justifications?

- Score: 8
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19911
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.19911
- Subjects: Artificial Intelligence (cs.AI) ; Computation and Language (cs.CL); Information Retrieval (cs.IR)
- Reason: This paper proposes a multi-agent transactive memory framework for storing and retrieving agent trajectories, which is highly relevant to LLM agents for scientific workflows and reproducible research. The idea of sharing procedural knowledge across agents could accelerate astronomical data analysis pipelines.

Short digest:
This paper proposes Multi-Agent Transactive Memory (MATM), a framework for population-level storage and retrieval of LLM agent trajectories, where producer agents contribute trajectories to a shared repository and consumer agents retrieve them to improve task execution. The key idea is to extend retrieval-augmented generation to agent-generated artifacts, enabling reuse of procedural knowledge across heterogeneous agent populations. This is highly relevant to astronomy because it could enable a community of AI agents to share successful analysis workflows for JWST data, such as SED fitting or source classification. For example, an agent that successfully deblends a crowded field could store its trajectory for other agents to reuse. A limitation is that the framework is evaluated on interactive environments (ALFWorld, WebArena) and may need adaptation for astronomical data analysis tools. Before investing time, check if the trajectory representation can capture the complex dependencies of astronomical data pipelines (e.g., calibration, stacking, fitting).

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Look for the trajectory representation and how procedural knowledge is encoded. Check the retrieval mechanism and whether it supports semantic search over trajectories. Examine the downstream task performance improvement and reduction in interaction steps. Look for discussion of scalability to large agent populations and whether the framework handles heterogeneous agent capabilities.

For astronomy, this could automate literature review and synthesis tasks, such as generating a report on recent JWST high-redshift galaxy findings. I could imagine using it to produce a structured summary of papers on little red dots or AGN from a set of retrieved abstracts. A key caveat is that the method is designed for text-based research and may need adaptation to handle domain-specific terminology or multimodal inputs like spectra and images.

Full-text inspection checklist:

This paper addresses the lack of large-scale multimodal datasets for synthetic aperture radar (SAR) by creating SARLO-80, a dataset of 119,566 triplets of very-high-resolution SAR, aligned optical imagery, and natural-language captions. The data is processed from Umbra spotlight acquisitions to 80cm slant-range resolution. For astronomy, this demonstrates a pipeline for creating aligned multimodal datasets from different imaging modalities, which could be adapted for JWST, HST, and ALMA data. The dataset supports cross-modal retrieval and conditional generation tasks. However, SAR physics differs from astronomy, so the specific alignment methods may not transfer directly.

Full-text inspection checklist:

- Look for the caption generation pipeline: are captions template-based or free-form? Could this be adapted for astronomical source descriptions?
