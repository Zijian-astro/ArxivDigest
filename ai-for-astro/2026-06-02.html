VESTA: Visual Exploration with Statistical Tool Agents

William Rudman , Abhishek Divekar , Kanishk Jain , Sebastian Joseph , Stella S. R. Offner , Matthew Lease , Kyle Mahowald , Greg Durrett , Junyi Jessy Li

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

The paper asks how to automate quantitative model fitting in scientific workflows using vision-language models (VLMs). It introduces VESTA, a framework that equips VLMs with a dynamically growing exploration toolkit of data transformations, visualizations, and statistical tests. The key idea is that the VLM actively explores data before and during refinement, creating and reusing diagnostic tools. For astronomy, this is directly relevant: VESTA is evaluated on real-world astronomy tasks including modeling initial mass functions and gravitational-wave chirp signals. An astronomer could imagine using VESTA to automatically fit a model to their data, with the VLM suggesting transformations and diagnostics. A limitation is that VESTA requires a VLM with strong visual reasoning capabilities, and the dynamic tool creation may be computationally expensive. The paper introduces the DAWN benchmark with astronomy tasks, making the connection explicit.

Reason

Directly addresses automated statistical model fitting with VLMs, includes astronomy benchmark tasks (IMF, gravitational waves), and uses dynamic tool creation.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Check the DAWN benchmark details: what astronomy tasks are included and how they are evaluated.
  • Look for examples of dynamically created tools and whether they are interpretable and reusable for other astronomy problems.
  • Examine the VLM architecture and whether it can handle astronomical data formats (e.g., FITS files, spectra).
  • Check if the framework is open-source and whether it can be extended to custom astronomical tools or databases.
Abstract

Fitting quantitative models to data is a central step in scientific workflows, yet it remains one of the least automated. Recent agent-based systems leverage language and vision-language models (VLMs) to iteratively propose and refine statistical models, but these systems struggle on more challenging modeling tasks. To address these limitations, we introduce VESTA: Visual Exploration with Statistical Tool Agents, a framework that equips VLMs with a dynamically growing exploration toolkit to guide model refinement through data transformations, hypothesis-driven visualizations, and robust statistical tests. Unlike prior systems that rely on iterative critique alone, VESTA actively explores data before and during refinement by selecting or creating diagnostic tools, which accumulate in the model's context and can be reused later. We evaluate VESTA against established baselines in three toolkit configurations: no tools, static expert-written tools, and dynamic model-written tools. To support this evaluation, we introduce DAWN (Dataset for Automated Workflows and Numerical Modeling), a benchmark targeting distribution fitting and time series modeling with varying difficulty tiers, and culminating in real-world astronomy tasks including modeling initial mass functions and gravitational-wave chirp signals. We find that VESTA's dynamic tool creation outperforms prior agentic pipelines, with the largest gains on complex and domain-specific tasks. We further show that dynamically generated tools are substantially more sophisticated than those produced by existing visual tool-creation systems, covering more diagnostic categories per function and strongly preferring visual outputs that the VLM critic can reason over directly.

Zamba2-VL Technical Report

Hassan Shapourian , Kasra Hejazi , Olabode M. Sule , Beren Millidge

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

This paper presents Zamba2-VL, a suite of vision-language models that combine Mamba2 state-space layers with a few shared transformer blocks. The key innovation is a hybrid architecture that achieves near-linear prefill compute and very low time-to-first-token, making it suitable for on-device or edge deployment. For astronomy, this could enable real-time analysis of JWST images or spectra with natural language queries, or rapid classification of sources in large survey data. A concrete workflow would be using Zamba2-VL to generate captions or answer questions about astronomical images, or to perform zero-shot classification of galaxy morphologies. A caveat is that the model is trained on general image understanding benchmarks, not astronomical data, so performance on specialized tasks like identifying little red dots or AGN features may require fine-tuning.

Reason

Vision-language model with fast inference, directly applicable to multimodal astronomical data analysis.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Check if the model supports image+text inputs for multimodal queries
  • Look for benchmark results on scientific or domain-specific image understanding tasks
  • Verify the model's ability to handle high-resolution images common in astronomy
Abstract

We present Zamba2-VL, a suite of vision-language models built on Zamba2, a hybrid language-model architecture combining Mamba2 state-space layers with a small number of shared transformer blocks. Across a broad range of image understanding, reasoning, OCR, grounding, and counting benchmarks, Zamba2-VL is competitive with leading Transformer-based open-weight VLMs of comparable scale, including the Molmo2, Qwen3-VL, and InternVL3.5 families, and substantially outperforms prior SSM-based and hybrid VLMs such as VL-Mamba, Cobra, and mmMamba. Inheriting the near-linear prefill compute and small, near-constant recurrent state of its Zamba2 backbone, Zamba2-VL delivers roughly an order of magnitude lower time-to-first-token (TTFT) than these Transformer baselines at matched parameter scale, with the efficiency gap most pronounced at the smaller 1.2B and 2.7B scales most relevant to on-device and edge deployment. We release three models -- 1.2B, 2.7B, and 7B -- together with inference code at this https URL .

Improving Visual Grounding in Remote Sensing via Cluster-Guided Refinement and Model Ensemble Voting

Panav Shah , Geet Sethi , Ashutosh Gandhe

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

This paper tackles visual grounding in remote sensing imagery, where the goal is to locate image regions corresponding to natural language descriptions. The core idea is to combine a specialized remote sensing grounding model (RemoteSAM) with a general-purpose segmentation model (SAM3) in a refinement pipeline, plus an ensemble voting strategy. This is directly relevant to astronomy because it could enable querying astronomical images with natural language (e.g., 'find all spiral galaxies with bright cores'). One could imagine using this to build a system that locates specific astronomical sources in JWST or HST images based on textual descriptions. A caveat is that the method is designed for Earth observation imagery and may need retraining on astronomical data.

Reason

Directly addresses visual grounding in remote sensing imagery, which is highly relevant for astronomical source detection and classification from survey images.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Check whether the method handles small objects and varying scales, which are common in astronomical images.
  • Look for details on the ensemble voting strategy and whether it could be adapted for multi-wavelength data.
  • Verify if the refinement pipeline preserves spatial accuracy needed for precise source localization.
Abstract

Visual grounding aims to locate image regions that correspond to natural language descriptions and is a key component of interpretable vision systems. In remote sensing imagery, grounding is particularly challenging due to complex scenes, small objects, and large variations in scale. Relying on a single model is often insufficient to address these diverse challenges. In this work, we propose two grounding pipelines, Sequential Grounding Refinement (SGR) and Cluster-Aware Grounding Refinement (CGR), that combine the complementary strengths of RemoteSAM, a visual grounding model specialized for remote sensing, and SAM3, a powerful general-purpose segmentation model. Our approach first uses RemoteSAM to obtain an initial estimate of object location, which is then refined using SAM3 to produce more accurate and spatially consistent segmentations. Additionally, we explore an ensemble strategy based on majority voting across six diverse grounding pipelines, each with distinct capabilities. This multi-model framework improves robustness and significantly enhances localization accuracy. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed pipelines and ensemble approach outperform individual models, leading to more reliable and precise visual grounding predictions.

Chunking Methods on Retrieval-Augmented Generation - Effectiveness Evaluation Against Computational Cost and Limitations

Mateusz Śmigielski (1), Michał Rajkowski (1), Mateusz Zbrocki (1), Michał Bernacki-Janson (1), Karol Kunicki (1), Julianna Godziszewska (1), Maciej Piasecki (1), Konrad Wojtasik (1) ((1) Department of Artificial Intelligence, Faculty of Information and Communication Technology, Wrocław University of Science and Technology, Wrocław 50-370, Poland)

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

This paper asks how different chunking strategies (fixed-size, semantic, etc.) affect the performance of retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) systems, and what the trade-offs are between effectiveness and computational cost. It systematically evaluates a wide range of chunking methods and highlights overlooked issues. For astronomy, this is highly relevant because RAG is a key technique for building research agents that can retrieve and synthesize information from the astronomical literature, telescope proposals, or instrument manuals. An astronomer could use the findings to optimize a RAG pipeline for querying papers on JWST data reduction or high-redshift galaxy classification. A caveat is that the evaluation is on general-domain text, not scientific documents, and the optimal chunking may differ for technical astronomy papers with equations, tables, and figures.

Reason

Directly evaluates chunking methods for retrieval-augmented generation, which is a core component of building LLM agents for literature review and data analysis in astronomy.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Check if the paper provides guidelines for choosing chunking methods based on document type or query characteristics.
  • Look for the specific chunking methods tested and whether any are particularly suited for technical or scientific text.
  • Examine the computational cost metrics (e.g., latency, memory) for each method.
  • Consider whether the findings are likely to transfer to astronomy-specific corpora (e.g., arXiv papers, ADS abstracts).
Abstract

Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) has demonstrated significant capabilities in enhancing the performance of Large Language Models (LLMs). One of the key tasks in RAG systems is the chunking process. Traditionally, fixed-size chunking and semantic chunking have been the standard approaches. However, interest in chunking strategies has been increasing, leading to a growing number of proposed methods that often claim improved performance over these conventional techniques. Many of these approaches are tailored to specific use cases and data types, with limited evidence of their effectiveness across diverse scenarios. As a result, it remains challenging to directly compare different techniques and assess their relative strengths. To the best of our knowledge, this study is the first to systematically evaluate the effectiveness of a wide range of chunking methods and emphasize the underlying challenges of chunking strategies in RAG systems. While chunking is commonly treated as a simple preprocessing step, we show that it introduces a range of impactful and often overlooked issues.

Cohort-Scale Neural Atlases of Ultrasound Video

Zhuorui Zhang , Roger Pallarès-López , Xuan Wu , Praneeth Namburi , Brian W. Anthony

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

This paper asks how to create a shared canonical representation for ultrasound video across a cohort of subjects, reducing the need for per-frame expert annotation. It proposes a cohort-scale neural atlas that uses DINOv3 features and per-video Generative Latent Optimization embeddings, trained jointly over thousands of frames. The main result is that the method enables accurate annotation transfer from the atlas to new videos with few or no labels. For astronomy, this is highly relevant because the same idea could be applied to astronomical image stacks (e.g., multi-epoch JWST observations) or multi-filter images, creating a shared atlas for source detection, morphology measurement, or anomaly detection. An astronomer could imagine using a similar pipeline to register images from different telescopes or filters to a canonical coordinate system, then transfer labels (e.g., segmentation masks for galaxies) with few-shot learning. A caveat is that the method is demonstrated on ultrasound video, not astronomical images, and the DINOv3 backbone may not capture astronomical features like SEDs or spectral lines.

Reason

The method uses DINOv3 features and neural atlases for cohort-scale video analysis, which could transfer to astronomical image analysis, especially for large survey data like JWST or Roman.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Check if the DINOv3 backbone is pre-trained on natural images and whether fine-tuning on astronomical data would be needed.
  • Look for the interpretability of the learned embeddings (e.g., linear projections) and whether they capture meaningful astronomical variations.
  • Examine the computational cost for training on large astronomical datasets (e.g., thousands of JWST images).
  • Consider whether the atlas representation can handle the dynamic range and noise characteristics of astronomical data.
Abstract

Ultrasound is the most widely used real-time imaging modality in clinical practice, yet per-frame video annotation remains a major bottleneck: expert labels are scarce and costly, and image appearance varies with speckle, shadowing, attenuation, and operator-dependent probe pose. This is especially limiting because clinically relevant information is often dynamic, from left-ventricular motion in echocardiography to muscle and bone kinematics in musculoskeletal imaging. Population atlases can amortize annotation cost by registering observations to a shared canonical coordinate system, but existing neural atlas methods mainly target single videos, small test-time image sets, or object-centric image collections. We introduce a cohort-scale neural atlas for ultrasound video: a single canonical chart with per-video Generative Latent Optimization embeddings, trained jointly over thousands of frames in DINOv3 feature space. Across five cardiac and musculoskeletal datasets with point landmarks and segmentation masks, our method learns coherent canonical templates and enables accurate atlas-space annotation transfer. On EchoNet-Dynamic and MSK-Bone, it supports single- and few-shot transfer with accuracy competitive with strong dense-correspondence baselines, while training in minutes on a single consumer GPU. The learned embeddings are interpretable: linear projections reveal structured cohort variation, image-decoder interpolation produces anatomically plausible intermediate frames, and test-time latent inversion reconstructs held-out frames through the atlas. These results suggest that cohort-scale neural atlases offer a practical, interpretable representation for reducing expert annotation burden in ultrasound video analysis.

Efficient RAG with Intent-Aware Retrieval and Semantics-Preserving Chunking

Fachrina Dewi Puspitasari , Chaoning Zhang , Jiaquan Zhang , Zhicheng Wang , Hafiz Shakeel Ahmad Awan , Rizwan Qureshi , Jewon Lee , Tae-Ho Kim , Yang Yang

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

This paper tackles the problem of information insufficiency in Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) systems, where LLMs retrieve chunks of text from a database to answer queries. The authors propose InSemRAG, which uses an intention-aware retriever that adaptively weights different retrieval methods based on the query's intent, and a semantics-preserving chunking module that repairs broken text chunks. For astronomy, this could improve systems that retrieve relevant papers, catalog entries, or observational notes to assist in data analysis or literature review. You could imagine using this to build a research assistant that retrieves the most relevant JWST papers or calibration notes for a given science question. A key caveat is that the benchmarks are general NLP tasks (HotPotQA, FEVER), so performance on specialized astronomical text needs verification.

Reason

Directly addresses RAG, a key method for augmenting LLMs with external knowledge, which is highly relevant for astronomical literature and data retrieval workflows.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Check if the method is evaluated on domain-specific (e.g., scientific or astronomical) text, not just general NLP benchmarks.
  • Look for details on how the 'intention-aware' retrieval is implemented and whether it can be adapted to multi-modal queries (text + image).
  • Examine the computational cost and latency, especially if the system would need to run on large astronomical databases.
Abstract

The demand for powerful instruction following and reasoning capability of large language models (LLMs) has promoted rapid development of retrieval-augmented generation (RAG). The RAG system assists LLM generation by retrieving chunks of query-fit supplementary knowledge from an external database. Conventional RAG systems, however, suffer from information insufficiency due to two factors, which are intent-agnostic retrieval and information fragmentation. Our work proposes a RAG framework, termed InSemRAG, that addresses these challenges via an iterative retrieve-and-check mechanism with two supporting modules, an intention-aware retriever (IAR) and semantics-preserving chunking (SPC). IAR implements a dynamic hybrid retrieval method that adaptively weights the retrieval channels based on the query intent, while SPC performs detection and reparation to the damaged evidence chunks to preserve the semantic integrity. To alleviate the computational latency brought by our iterative mechanism, we leverage small language models (SLMs). Extensive experiments across several benchmark datasets consistently demonstrate the competitiveness of our method against recent state-of-the-art RAG mechanisms. Particularly, our method achieves significant gains on multi-hop and evidence-sensitive tasks, with a 2.65-point improvement in F1 on HotPotQA and a 1.5-point increase in accuracy on FEVER. Our method also achieves competitive performance to Multi-Hop RAG with 4.32$\times$ lower latency with the utilization of SLM.

Self-Revising Discovery Systems for Science: A Categorical Framework for Agentic Artificial Intelligence

Fiona Y. Wang , Markus J. Buehler

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

This paper develops a category-theoretic framework for agentic scientific discovery, where discovery is a verified regime transition that preserves old artifacts and identifies residual content beyond functorial transport. It instantiates the framework in two systems: Builder/Breaker (for protein mechanics) and CategoryScienceClaw (a proof-carrying knowledge-computation graph for materials science). For astronomy, this framework could be used to build an AI agent that not only retrieves and analyzes data but also revises its own representational assumptions (e.g., switching from a template-fitting to a machine-learning-based redshift estimator when evidence accumulates). A concrete workflow would be to implement a CategoryScienceClaw-like system for JWST data, where the agent manages typed skills (e.g., SED fitting, source detection, spectral line identification), open needs (e.g., unknown redshift, ambiguous AGN classification), and workflow mutation (e.g., switching from photometric to spectroscopic analysis). A major caveat is that the framework is abstract and requires significant engineering to instantiate for a new domain; the paper's examples are in materials science, not astronomy. The category theory may be a barrier for practical adoption.

Reason

Directly addresses self-revising AI discovery systems for science, with a category-theoretic framework and instantiations in materials science; highly relevant to building automated scientific discovery agents for astronomy.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Look at the CategoryScienceClaw instantiation: how are typed skills, artifacts, and open needs defined, and could they be mapped to astronomical concepts?
  • Check the Builder/Breaker example: how does the Minimum Description Length gate work, and could it be applied to model selection in SED fitting?
  • Examine the 'verified regime transition' mechanism: what constitutes a verified transition, and how is the comparison between old and new regimes performed?
  • Look for any discussion of scalability or computational cost: can the framework handle the scale of modern astronomical surveys?
Abstract

Scientific discovery is not only answer generation but revision of the representational regime in which evidence, artifacts, operations, and verifiers are typed. We develop a category-theoretic account of agentic discovery for materials science. In a fixed regime b with schema category S_b, the system state is a copresheaf I_t: S_b -> Set, and provenance is the category of elements \int_{S_b} I_t. Fixed-regime operation is an update on such states, endofunctorial only when provenance-preserving refinements are specified and preserved. Discovery is instead a verified regime transition u: S_b -> S_b': old artifacts are preserved, transported by the left Kan extension Lan_u I_t, and compared with the post-transition state to identify residual content beyond functorial transport. This separates retrieval, search, and discovery without subjective novelty. We instantiate the framework in two systems. In Builder/Breaker, a protein-mechanics world model is revised under a Minimum Description Length gate; the accepted law expresses within-chain flexibility as all-mode elastic compliance conditioned by slow collective-mode participation, or mode-conditioned compliance. In CategoryScienceClaw, typed skills, artifacts, open needs, workflow mutation, gates, stress tests, and public discourse become a proof-carrying knowledge-computation graph. A fiber-network example records candidate models, rejected alternatives, an AIC gate, perturbation tests, and an accepted orientation-tensor anisotropic stiffness surrogate over an isotropic fiber-count descriptor. Together, the cases show how category theory can be both a mathematical language for discovery and an engineering specification for self-revising AI discovery systems.

TimeSage-MT: A Multi-Turn Benchmark for Evaluating Agentic Time Series Reasoning

Yaxuan Kong , Qingren Yao , Yuqi Nie , Yichen Li , Yilei Shao , Stefan Zohren , Anna Vettoruzzo , Joaquin Vanschoren , Ming Jin , Qingsong Wen

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

This paper introduces TimeSage-MT, a multi-turn benchmark for evaluating LLM agents on time series reasoning tasks across 8 real-world domains. It includes 240 tasks and 2,680 dialogue turns, spanning basic exploration to decision-oriented analysis. The benchmark reveals sharp performance drops on decision-oriented tasks due to failures in memory, uncertainty handling, and domain-based decision making. For astronomy, this benchmark could be adapted to evaluate agents that analyze light curves, detect transients, or make follow-up observation decisions. The structured agent TimeSage with a time series skill library provides a template for building astronomy-specific agents. A key caveat is that the benchmark domains are not astronomical, so transferability is uncertain. The paper provides a reproducible pipeline for creating new benchmarks, which could be used to build an astronomy-specific version.

Reason

Directly relevant: benchmark for LLM agents on time series reasoning, which is crucial for astronomical time-domain analysis and decision-making.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Check if the benchmark pipeline can be adapted to astronomical time series (e.g., light curves from JWST or LSST).
  • Look for the skill library design to see if it includes operations like period finding, anomaly detection, or forecasting.
  • Verify if the evaluation protocol supports multi-turn interactions with external tools (e.g., databases, simulators).
  • Check for any discussion of uncertainty quantification in the agent's reasoning.
Abstract

Time series data inform critical decisions across many real-world domains. While large language model (LLM) agents can analyze data through natural language and tools, it remains unclear whether they can conduct reliable time series analysis across multi-turn conversations. Existing benchmarks focus on single-step tasks such as forecasting and anomaly detection, overlooking practical workflows where user goals evolve, agents must build on prior analyses, and conclusions emerge from accumulated evidence. In this work, we introduce TimeSage-MT, a multi-turn benchmark for agentic time series reasoning with 240 tasks and 2,680 dialogue turns across 8 real-world domains, spanning basic exploration to decision-oriented analysis. TimeSage-MT is built through a reproducible pipeline that converts real-world time series data into multi-turn conversations with verifiable answers. It provides a unified evaluation protocol and public leaderboard for comparing time series agentic systems. To demonstrate the benchmark's utility, we evaluate frontier LLMs alongside TimeSage, a novel structured agent equipped with a comprehensive time series skill library. The results show sharp performance drops on decision-oriented tasks, driven by failures in memory, uncertainty handling, and domain-based decision making. TimeSage-MT exposes critical gaps in current agentic reasoning and provides a rigorous foundation for future development.
