Title: Explore Before Committing: Hypothesis-Guided Search for Deep Research Agents

arXiv ID: 2609.01294
Score: 8
Reason: Directly relevant to deep research agents, which could be used for literature review and automated scientific discovery in astronomy.
Short digest: This paper addresses a failure mode in deep-research agents where they commit to an early search direction without gathering enough comparative evidence. The main idea is to generate lightweight hypotheses as soft search hints, explore them in bounded independent branches, and compare evidence before committing, improving search efficiency and accuracy. Title: Beyond Language Priors: Diagnosing and Fixing Visual-Origin Hallucinations in Multimodal LLM
Authors: Peiyang Xu , Xiaopei Zhu , Jun Zhu , Xiaolin Hu
arXiv ID: 2609.00231
Score: 7
Reason: Directly addresses visual-origin hallucinations in multimodal LLMs, which is crucial for reliable analysis of astronomical images where subtle features may be misrepresented. The proposed ACFT method for reducing hallucinations could improve trust in LMM outputs for JWST data interpretation.
Short digest: This paper challenges the common belief that hallucinations in multimodal large language models (MLLMs) are solely due to language priors, showing instead that incorrect visual feature extraction and image-text misalignment also play a key role. The authors introduce Adversarial Contrastive Fine-Tuning (ACFT), which uses targeted adversarial perturbations to create aligned positive-negative pairs for contrastive learning, reducing hallucinations without extra inference cost. For astronomy, this matters because LMMs are increasingly used to interpret JWST images, and reducing visual-origin hallucinations could make their outputs more reliable for tasks like identifying high-redshift galaxies or AGN. A practical workflow could involve fine-tuning an LMM on astronomical image-caption pairs with ACFT to improve accuracy in source classification. However, the method is tested on COCO, so its transferability to astronomical data needs validation, and the adversarial perturbations may not capture the unique noise characteristics of JWST images.
Full-text inspection checklist: Check whether the ACFT method is evaluated on any non-COCO datasets or if the authors discuss domain transfer limitations.; Look for details on how the adversarial perturbations are generated and whether they could be adapted to astronomical image noise properties.; Examine the diagnostic metrics (cosine similarity, Smooth Grad-CAM) for evidence that visual-origin hallucinations are indeed reduced in practice.
