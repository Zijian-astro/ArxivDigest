## 1. SciOrch: Learning to Orchestrate Expert LLMs for Solving Frontier Multimodal Scientific Reasoning Tasks

- arXiv ID: `2606.15872`
- Authors: Jingru Guo , Xiangyuan Xue , Lian Zhang , Wanghan Xu , Siki Chen , Philip Torr , Wanli Ouyang , Lei Bai , Zhenfei Yin
- Score: 9
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.15872
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.15872
- Subjects: Computation and Language (cs.CL)
- Reason: Directly addresses scientific reasoning with LLMs, using an orchestrator to combine multiple expert models. Highly relevant to automated scientific discovery and research agents for astronomy.

Short digest:
This paper tackles the challenge of frontier scientific reasoning, where even the best LLMs fall short. It presents SciOrch, a framework that trains a lightweight 8B model to orchestrate calls to multiple commercial LLMs, decomposing questions and synthesizing answers. The orchestrator is trained using MCTS and GRPO to optimize the delegation strategy. For astronomy, this could enable an agent that combines specialized models for tasks like source classification, SED fitting, and literature review. A concrete workflow could be a multi-model agent that queries different LLMs for interpreting JWST spectra, cross-checking results, and producing a final analysis. A caveat is the API cost and latency of calling multiple commercial models, though the paper claims lower cost than typical multi-agent methods.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Look for the specific scientific domains tested (SGI-Reasoning, Scientists' First Exam) to assess overlap with astronomy.
- Check the MCTS-based training details to see if the orchestrator can be adapted to domain-specific sub-tasks.
- Examine the API cost analysis to evaluate feasibility for large-scale astronomy projects.

## 2. SING: Synthetic Intention Graph for Scalable Active Tool Discovery in LLM Agents

- arXiv ID: `2606.16591`
- Authors: Qiao Xiao , Haochen Shi , Yisen Gao , Wenbin Hu , Huihao Jing , Tianshi Zheng , Baixuan Xu , Ziheng Zhang , Weiqi Wang , Haoran Li , Jiaxin Bai , Yangqiu Song
- Score: 9
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.16591
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.16591
- Subjects: Computation and Language (cs.CL)
- Reason: Directly addresses retrieval-augmented tool selection for LLM agents, which is highly relevant to building research agents for astronomy that can query databases, run codes, and access APIs.

Short digest:
This paper asks how LLM agents can dynamically discover and select the right tools from a large, evolving ecosystem (hundreds to thousands of APIs) rather than relying on a fixed, predefined inventory. It proposes SING, an intention-aware active tool discovery framework that builds an intention-tool graph linking user intentions, tool capabilities, and tool collaboration patterns, then retrieves tools according to the agent's evolving task state. For astronomy, this is directly applicable: an astronomy research agent could use SING to dynamically select the right data analysis tool (e.g., a photometric redshift code, a spectral fitting routine, a catalog cross-matching service) based on the current research question and intermediate results. A concrete workflow would be an agent that, when asked 'Find little red dots in this JWST field and fit their SEDs', uses SING to first retrieve a source detection tool, then a photometry tool, then an SED-fitting tool, all without hardcoding the pipeline. A key caveat is that SING requires a pre-built intention-tool graph, which would need to be constructed for astronomy-specific tools and APIs.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Look for the graph construction method: how are intentions, tool capabilities, and collaboration patterns defined and linked?
- Check whether the retrieval is truly dynamic (i.e., updates as the agent's state changes) or only at the start of a task.
- Examine the evaluation on real-world tool-use benchmarks to see if the method scales to hundreds of tools without performance degradation.

## 3. VeriGraph: Towards Verifiable Data-Analytic Agents

- arXiv ID: `2606.16603`
- Authors: Jiajie Jin , Zhao Yang , Wenle Liao , Yuyang Hu , Guanting Dong , Xiaoxi Li , Yutao Zhu , Zhicheng Dou
- Score: 9
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.16603
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.16603
- Subjects: Computation and Language (cs.CL) ; Artificial Intelligence (cs.AI)
- Reason: Directly addresses verifiability and traceability of LLM-based data-analytic agents, which is crucial for reproducible scientific research in astronomy.

Short digest:
This paper asks how to make LLM-based data-analytic agents verifiable, so that their numerical conclusions and qualitative judgments can be audited and reproduced. It proposes VeriGraph, a framework that constructs an explicit heterogeneous evidence directed acyclic graph (DAG) during execution, connecting raw data, computed results, and natural-language claims. Three evidence-expansion primitives (computational, grounding, derivational) are introduced, and a graph-based policy optimization strategy with a composite reward supervises answer correctness, computational integrity, and derivational coherence. For astronomy, this is directly applicable: an astronomy research agent that queries catalogs, runs analysis code, and generates scientific claims could use VeriGraph to produce an auditable trail from raw JWST images to published conclusions. A concrete workflow would be an agent that, when asked 'What is the redshift distribution of little red dots in this field?', produces a DAG showing the exact SQL queries, photometric redshift code, and derived claims, enabling full reproducibility. A key caveat is that VeriGraph requires the agent to be built with the specific evidence-expansion primitives, which may require significant engineering for astronomy-specific tools.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Look for the definition of the three evidence-expansion primitives: how are computational, grounding, and derivational expansions implemented for tabular and code-based data?
- Check whether the Grounding Rate metric (87.61%) is evaluated on claims that are typical for scientific analysis (e.g., 'The mean redshift is 2.3') or only on simple factual claims.
- Examine the graph-based policy optimization: does the composite reward require ground-truth annotations for computational integrity and derivational coherence, or can it be self-supervised?

For astronomy, this could enable efficient automated hyperparameter tuning or code optimization for data analysis pipelines. ## 5. Retrievable Gradients: Continual Post-Training Without Cumulative Weight Drift

- arXiv ID: `2606.15734`
- Authors: Weihang Su , Jiacheng Kang , Jingyan Xu , Qingyao Ai , Jianming Long , Hanwen Zhang , Bangde Du , Xinyuan Cao , Min Zhang , Yiqun Liu
- Score: 8
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.15734
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.15734
- Subjects: Computation and Language (cs.CL) ; Artificial Intelligence (cs.AI); Information Retrieval (cs.IR); Machine Learning (cs.LG)
- Reason: This paper proposes a method for continual post-training without weight drift, which could be highly relevant for updating astronomical models with new survey data without catastrophic forgetting.

Short digest:
This paper addresses the problem of continual post-training for language models, where updating parameters with new knowledge can cause catastrophic forgetting. The authors propose ReGrad, which treats gradients as retrievable units of knowledge, pre-computing document-specific gradients offline and retrieving only query-relevant gradients at inference time for temporary weight adaptation. This approach combines the benefits of retrieval-augmented generation with parametric knowledge integration, avoiding cumulative weight drift. For astronomy, this could enable models to incorporate new observational data or literature without retraining, which is valuable for large surveys like JWST, Roman, or Rubin. A concrete workflow could involve using ReGrad to update a model with new spectral or imaging catalogs, allowing it to answer queries about new objects without full retraining. The method requires pre-computing gradients for new documents, which may be computationally intensive, and the meta-learning objective may need tuning for astronomical data.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Check whether the paper provides experiments on domain-specific datasets that resemble astronomical data (e.g., scientific literature or structured knowledge).
- Look for details on the computational cost of pre-computing gradients and the scalability to large document collections.
- Examine the meta-learning objective and whether it can be adapted to multimodal data (images, spectra, catalogs).

Short digest:
This paper presents I-GUIDE Smart Search, a production multimodal retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) system for geospatial knowledge discovery. It combines OpenSearch indexes, a Neo4j knowledge graph, and an iterative RAG pipeline with memory-aware query augmentation, reasoning, and hallucination checking. The system handles heterogeneous artifacts: datasets, maps, notebooks, software, and publications. For astronomy, this is directly relevant as a template for building a RAG system that searches across astronomical catalogs, papers, code repositories, and provenance links. The paper reports deployment lessons on spatial metadata quality, graph provenance, retrieval routing, and latency-cost tradeoffs that transfer to astronomical cyberinfrastructure. A limitation is the 25-second p50 latency due to 20-50 LLM calls per query, which may need optimization for interactive astronomy use.

- Reason: Addresses spectral bias in neural networks for high-frequency and multiscale problems, directly relevant to astronomical data analysis (e.g., spectral fitting, SED fitting, oscillatory signals).

Short digest:
This paper tackles the problem of spectral bias in deep neural networks, where they fail to capture high-frequency or oscillatory patterns. It proposes RepNet, a reparameterization of the first hidden layer's weights and biases to control initial slope scales and partition point distributions, enabling adaptive frequency scaling during training. For astronomy, this is directly relevant because many astrophysical signals (e.g., spectral lines, light curves, SEDs) contain high-frequency or multiscale features that standard neural networks struggle to learn. A concrete workflow could be using RepNet within a physics-informed neural network (PINN) to fit galaxy SEDs or spectral line profiles from JWST data. A key caveat is that the method is demonstrated on synthetic and PDE problems, not yet on real astronomical data, so performance on noisy, irregularly sampled observations needs validation.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Check if the method is tested on noisy or irregularly sampled data, as astronomical observations often are
- Look for quantitative comparisons on multiscale function approximation—how much improvement over vanilla DNNs?
- Assess whether the reparameterization is compatible with common astronomical data formats and loss functions

## 8. LOCUS: Local Visual Cue Search for Enhancing Fine-Grained Perception in Multimodal Large Language Models

- arXiv ID: `2606.16586`
- Authors: Zhou Tao , Fang Zhang , Zewen Ding , Shida Wang , Xiaokun Sun , YongXiang Hua , Haoyu Cao , Linli Xu
- Score: 8
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.16586
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.16586
- Subjects: Computer Vision and Pattern Recognition (cs.CV)
- Reason: Directly addresses fine-grained visual perception in MLLMs, which is critical for analyzing high-resolution JWST images and detecting small, faint sources like little red dots.

Short digest:
This paper asks why multimodal large language models (MLLMs) still fail on fine-grained visual tasks even with high-resolution inputs. It identifies 'visual context rot' where decisive local evidence exists but is not reliably selected by the model. The authors propose LOCUS, a training framework that teaches MLLMs to internalize local evidence search by providing a local crop as a visual cue during training and optimizing the model to recover its spatial location in the full image using an IoU-based reward. For astronomy, this could directly improve MLLM-based analysis of JWST images where small, high-redshift galaxies or AGN require fine-grained localization and classification. A concrete workflow would be using LOCUS-trained MLLMs to answer questions like 'Is there a compact red source in this JWST NIRCam image?' or to locate little red dots. A key caveat is that LOCUS requires training with paired crop-full-image data, which may need custom dataset construction for astronomical images.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Look for the IoU-based reward formulation and whether it can be adapted to bounding-box or segmentation tasks common in astronomy.
- Check whether the method is evaluated on any natural images with small objects (e.g., birds, insects) that mimic the scale of high-redshift sources.
- Examine the attention analysis figures to see if the model truly focuses on task-relevant regions after LOCUS training.
