## 1. Frozen Foundation-Model Embeddings Discard Small-Lesion Signal in Chest Radiography: Implications for Pre-Deployment Evaluation

- arXiv ID: `2606.11606`
- Authors: Raajitha Muthyala , Zhenan Yin , Alekhya Jilla , Frank Li , Theo Dapamede , Bardia Khosravi , Mohammadreza Chavoshi , Judy Gichoya , Saptarshi Purkayastha
- Score: 8
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11606
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.11606
- Subjects: Computer Vision and Pattern Recognition (cs.CV)
- Reason: Directly investigates signal loss in frozen foundation model embeddings for medical imaging, with clear parallels to astronomical imaging where small, low-contrast sources (e.g., high-redshift galaxies, little red dots) are critical.

Short digest:
This paper systematically probes frozen vision transformer (ViT) embeddings to understand where small-scale, low-contrast signal is lost in chest radiography. It finds that global aggregation (CLS token or patch-mean) discards small-lesion signal, but this signal is recoverable from patch tokens when a region of interest is known. For astronomy, this is crucial: JWST images contain small, faint sources (e.g., high-redshift galaxies, AGN) that could be similarly suppressed by frozen foundation models. A concrete workflow would be to use patch-level features instead of global embeddings for detecting and characterizing little red dots or other compact sources in JWST images. A key caveat is that the study is on medical images, not astronomical ones, and the nature of 'signal' (lesions vs. galaxies) differs; the findings need validation on astronomical data.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Check if the paper provides any analysis of the spatial scale at which signal is lost—is there a characteristic size threshold?
- Look for the perturbation panel details: what types of small-scale perturbations were tested, and how do they relate to astronomical sources (e.g., PSF size, surface brightness)?
- Examine the region-aware probe: does the method require a perfect region of interest, or is it robust to imperfect localization?

## 2. TimeRouter: Efficient and Adaptive Routing of Time-Series Foundation Models

- arXiv ID: `2606.11625`
- Authors: Kanghui Ning , Yushan Jiang , Kashif Rasul , Anderson Schneider , Yuriy Nevmyvaka , Dongjin Song
- Score: 8
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11625
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.11625
- Subjects: Machine Learning (cs.LG)
- Reason: Directly addresses routing between time-series foundation models, which is highly relevant for astronomical time-series analysis (e.g., light curves, variable stars, AGN variability) and for building agentic systems.

Short digest:
This paper presents TimeRouter, a framework for efficiently routing between multiple pretrained time-series foundation models (TSFMs) without invoking an LLM at inference time. It uses a lightweight learned routing head, selective gating, and ensemble fallback to adaptively select the best expert model for each forecasting task. For astronomy, this is directly applicable: different TSFMs may excel at different types of light curves (e.g., periodic variables, stochastic AGN, transients). A concrete workflow could involve using TimeRouter to combine several TSFMs for classifying variable stars or predicting AGN flares from LSST or TESS data. A key caveat is that the paper evaluates on the GIFT-EVAL benchmark (general time-series), not astronomical datasets, and the TSFMs used may not be pretrained on astronomical data.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Check if the paper provides any analysis of which TSFMs are selected for different time-series characteristics (e.g., periodicity, trend, noise level).
- Look for details on the routing head architecture—is it interpretable? Can we see why a particular model is chosen?
- Examine the computational overhead of the routing framework: is it truly lightweight enough for real-time or large-scale astronomical surveys?

## 3. Parameter-Efficient Adapter Tuning for Tabular-Image Multimodal Learning

- arXiv ID: `2606.11682`
- Authors: Jiaqi Luo
- Score: 8
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11682
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.11682
- Subjects: Computer Vision and Pattern Recognition (cs.CV) ; Machine Learning (cs.LG)
- Reason: Directly addresses tabular-image multimodal learning with parameter-efficient adapters, which could be applied to astronomical datasets combining image (e.g., JWST) and tabular (e.g., photometric catalog) data.

Short digest:
1. The paper tackles the problem of efficiently combining tabular data (like structured catalog attributes) with image data for multimodal learning, without the high cost of full fine-tuning. 2. The main idea is TI-Adapter, which freezes pretrained encoders and inserts small trainable adapter modules after the tabular embedding and within the image branch, achieving competitive performance with far fewer parameters. 3. For astronomy, this could enable efficient fine-tuning of models on JWST or Roman images paired with photometric or spectroscopic catalog features, such as redshift, magnitude, or color. 4. A concrete workflow would be taking a pretrained vision encoder (e.g., for galaxy morphology) and a tabular encoder for catalog data, then using TI-Adapter to jointly fine-tune on a specific survey like COSMOS or CEERS. 5. A key caveat is that the paper tests on 20 general tabular-image datasets, not astronomical ones, so performance on high-redshift galaxy or AGN classification tasks is unknown and would require validation.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Check if the adapter architecture supports different types of tabular features (e.g., continuous, categorical) common in astronomical catalogs.
- Look for ablation studies on adapter placement and whether the method scales to very large tabular feature spaces (e.g., hundreds of photometric bands).
- Examine whether the method can handle missing data in tabular inputs, which is common in astronomical surveys.

## 4. Search Discipline for Long-Horizon Research Agents

- arXiv ID: `2606.11522`
- Authors: Adithya Srinivasan , Devesh Paragiri
- Score: 7
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11522
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.11522
- Subjects: Artificial Intelligence (cs.AI) ; Machine Learning (cs.LG)
- Reason: Paper directly addresses a critical failure mode in research agents that optimize aggregate metrics, which is highly relevant for automated scientific discovery in astronomy where multi-dimensional validity (e.g., across redshift bins, galaxy types) is common.

Short digest:
This paper identifies a fundamental problem in autoresearch agents: when scientific validity is multi-dimensional (e.g., across regions or cohorts), optimizing an aggregate metric can select a candidate that inverts the structure underneath, breaking the model for important subgroups. It demonstrates this inversion on an ecosystem model where the top-scoring candidate collapses boreal regions while a slightly lower-scoring one preserves them. The proposed solution is a search-discipline protocol with an external control loop that audits candidates on disaggregated behavior after the agent has decided. For astronomy, this is directly relevant: an agent optimizing a global metric like average photometric redshift accuracy might select a model that fails for high-redshift galaxies or little red dots. The external audit loop could be implemented to check performance on specific subpopulations (e.g., by redshift, color, or morphology) before accepting a candidate model or pipeline.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Look for the formal definition of 'inversion' and how to detect it in practice, especially for astronomical metrics like classification accuracy across redshift bins.
- Check whether the external control loop requires a separate validation dataset or can work with the same data used for optimization.
- Examine the computational overhead of the audit loop: does it require full re-evaluation on all subgroups, or can it be approximated?
- Look for examples of how the protocol handles multiple competing validity dimensions (e.g., accuracy vs. completeness vs. physical plausibility).

## 5. Physics-Distilled Neural Network enabled by Large Language Models for Manufacturing Process-Property Predictive Modeling

- arXiv ID: `2606.11605`
- Authors: Ge Song , Kiarash Naghavi Khanghah , Anandkumar Patel , Rajiv Malhotra , Hongyi Xu
- Score: 7
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11605
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.11605
- Subjects: Machine Learning (cs.LG) ; Artificial Intelligence (cs.AI)
- Reason: Uses LLMs to extract physics priors from literature and distills them into a lightweight model for process-property prediction, directly relevant to automated scientific discovery and data-scarce scenarios in astronomy.

Short digest:
This paper addresses the problem of predicting process-property relationships in manufacturing with limited data. The key idea is to use LLMs to extract analytical physics priors from scientific literature, which are then integrated into a privileged teacher model (with a Graph-Masked Attention layer). This teacher's knowledge is distilled into a lightweight student predictor for fast, real-time inference. For astronomy, this is highly relevant: one could use LLMs to extract physical priors (e.g., SED templates, dust attenuation laws) from the literature and distill them into a model for predicting galaxy properties from sparse photometry. A concrete workflow could be using an LLM to read papers on high-redshift galaxy SEDs, extract prior knowledge, and train a fast student model for photometric redshift estimation. A key caveat is that the paper demonstrates this on manufacturing processes, not astronomical data, and the quality of LLM-extracted priors may be lower for complex astrophysical phenomena.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Check how the LLM is prompted to extract physics priors—is the process automated and reproducible?
- Look for the Graph-Masked Attention layer details: can it handle the irregular sampling and missing data common in astronomical catalogs?
- Examine the distillation procedure: is the student model interpretable, or is it a black box? How is the uncertainty in the teacher's knowledge propagated?
