CodeGrep: An RL-Trained Retrieval Agent for LLM Coding Agents

Wuya Chen , Yihao yang , Yang Cao , Yue Lin

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

The paper tackles the inefficiency of LLM coding agents spending too many tokens on repository exploration. It introduces CodeGrep, a 14B retrieval agent trained with reinforcement learning to issue parallel search and read calls, improving efficiency while preserving solve rates. For astronomy, this could help automate data analysis pipelines where agents need to find relevant code or data files. A concrete workflow could be using CodeGrep to speed up an agent that searches through large astronomical software repositories. A key caveat is that the method is designed for code repositories, so its transfer to astronomical data files or catalogs may require adaptation.

Reason

Directly addresses improving efficiency of LLM coding agents via a trained retrieval agent, which is relevant to automated scientific workflows and research agents for astronomy. The method of training a retrieval agent with RL could transfer to retrieving relevant data or code in astronomical analysis pipelines.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Check if the retrieval agent can be adapted to non-code file types like FITS or catalogs
  • Look for details on the training data and whether it can be generated from astronomical workflows
  • Inspect the precision threshold analysis to see if it applies to different retrieval domains
Abstract

Modern LLM coding agents such as Claude Code and OpenHands share a common inefficiency: they spend much of their token budget finding the file to patch, rather than patching it. On SWE-Bench Verified, a 30B OpenHands agent averages 23 rounds and 631K tokens per resolved issue, with many calls spent on grep, glob, and view_file during repository exploration. We introduce CodeGrep, a 14B retrieval agent trained end-to-end with GRPO to issue multi-turn parallel grep, glob, and read tool calls and return candidate files to a frozen downstream coding agent. On all 500 SWE-Bench Verified instances, CodeGrep preserves resolve rate while substantially improving efficiency: 27.0% versus 25.8% for the no-retrieval baseline, with 15% fewer rounds and 19% fewer tokens on resolved instances. Across retrievers, downstream utility follows a precision threshold: BM25 with precision 0.375 degrades the agent, Jina with precision 0.445 is neutral, and CodeGrep with precision 0.677 crosses the threshold at which retrieval begins to reduce rollout cost. To enable this study, we mine supervision from 67K open-source agent trajectories using CATM and build a Git-worktree environment for multi-turn agent RL. In our setting, applying the efficiency signal at the advantage layer rather than the reward layer reduces KL drift and translates cleanly into downstream efficiency. We will release the model, training pipeline, RL environment, and evaluation harnesses.

Align-RAG: Alignment Is All You Need for TSFM In-Context Learning

Mohammad Asadi , Soheil Hor , Bardiya Akhbari , Jack W. O'Sullivan , Tahoura Nedaee , Layne C. Price , Raviteja Anantha , Euan Ashley , Ehsan Adeli

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

This paper asks whether frozen time-series foundation models can effectively use retrieved examples without learned fusion modules. The main idea is a closed-form alignment of retrieved past-future windows (amplitude rescaling and phase shift) before feeding them into the model's context. This matters for astronomy because it offers a lightweight way to adapt pre-trained models to new survey data, such as variable star classification or transient detection. A concrete workflow could be using this method to forecast light curves from JWST or LSST without fine-tuning. However, the method assumes time-series data with clear periodic or trend structure, so its applicability to irregularly sampled astronomical data needs checking.

Reason

Directly relevant to retrieval-augmented generation for time-series forecasting, which can be applied to astronomical light curves or time-domain surveys. The training-free alignment method could reduce computational costs in adapting foundation models to new astronomical datasets.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Check if the method handles irregular time sampling or missing data, common in astronomical light curves
  • Look for experiments on datasets with non-stationary or multi-scale patterns similar to astronomical variability
  • Assess whether the closed-form alignment is robust to noise levels typical in survey data
Abstract

Retrieval-augmented forecasting promises to adapt frozen Time Series Foundation Models (TSFMs) to new domains without fine-tuning, but recent methods typically rely on learned fusion modules, i.e., trained adapters that merge retrieved examples into the backbone's forecast, based on the assumption that frozen backbones cannot dynamically incorporate retrieved context on their own. We show this assumption is unnecessary. We introduce Align-RAG, a training-free method that applies a closed-form per-pair amplitude rescaling and integer-lag phase shift to retrieved past-future windows before they enter a frozen backbone's context. With no learned parameters, Align-RAG outperforms the state-of-the-art trained retrieval adapter on a frozen Chronos-Bolt on all seven datasets of the standard benchmark (avg -3.75% MSE), showing that the gains previously attributed to learned fusion are recoverable without any training. Align-RAG further improves zero-shot MSE on four additional frozen TSFMs with various architectures by 2.5% to 13.7% per backbone with no per-backbone tuning. To probe why alignment helps, we compare the frozen backbone's prediction shift under aligned demonstrations to the closed-form ridge prediction shift on the same pairs. We find that aligned demonstrations induce prediction shifts that track a closed-form ridge predictor on the same pairs, with a future-shuffle control ruling out a futures-averaging account. Together, these results indicate that frozen TSFMs already support dynamic in-context use of retrievals, and that closed-form alignment should be the default baseline for retrieval-augmented forecasting before any fusion module is trained. Contextual Information Policy Optimization for Search Agents

Xingyu Guo , Wei Chen , Linlin Yang , Baochang Zhang

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

This paper proposes Contextual Information Policy Optimization (CIPO) to align LLM search agents with external evidence use, reducing confirmation bias. It assigns dense, turn-level credit to reasoning actions influenced by retrieved information. For astronomy, this could improve AI agents that retrieve and reason over astronomical literature or data. A concrete workflow might be to use CIPO to train an agent that retrieves relevant papers and synthesizes findings for a specific astrophysical question. The main limitation is the need for a reward signal that measures evidence grounding, which may require careful design for scientific tasks.

Reason

Focuses on evidence-oriented reinforcement learning for search agents, which is highly relevant to research agents for literature review and data analysis in astronomy.

TruthLens: Object Hallucination Detection via Self-Evaluating Truthfulness Scores in LVLMs

Yanqi Wu , Runhe Lai , Xinhua Lu , Qichao Chen , Zhiping Zhou , Jia-Xin Zhuang , Weijiang Yu , Ruixuan Wang

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

The paper addresses object hallucination in large vision-language models (LVLMs), where models generate objects not present in the image. The main idea is TruthLens, a self-evaluation framework that repurposes a special token to output a truthfulness score for each object, trained with a simple MSE loss while preserving generation quality. This matters for astronomy because LVLMs could be used to describe or analyze JWST images, and hallucinated sources could mislead scientific interpretation. A concrete workflow could be using TruthLens to verify object detections in JWST images before downstream analysis. A caveat is that the method is trained on limited object categories and may need fine-tuning on astronomical objects.

Reason

Focuses on detecting hallucinations in large vision-language models, which is relevant to reliability of multimodal models for astronomical image analysis. The self-evaluation method could transfer to verifying model outputs on JWST data.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Check whether the method requires fine-tuning on domain-specific data or works out-of-the-box on unseen categories
  • Look for evaluation metrics beyond AUROC, such as precision-recall, on diverse image types
  • Inspect if the truthfulness score is calibrated for low-confidence detections typical of faint astronomical sources
Abstract

Despite the remarkable progress of large vision language models (LVLMs), object hallucination remains a fundamental challenge that hinders their trustworthy deployment. A key finding motivates our work: real and hallucinated object tokens are clearly separable in hidden representations, yet this separability is largely lost at the language-modeling (LM) head. We propose TruthLens, a self-evaluation framework that teaches the LM head to expose a per-object truthfulness signal without any auxiliary model or additional inference cost. Concretely, a rarely-used special token is repurposed as a reference token. For each object-token position, we extract the log-probability assigned to this special token by the LM head, and define its difference from a predefined constant as the truthfulness score. The model is then fine-tuned with an MSE objective that drives scores toward 1 for real objects and 0 for hallucinated ones, while a divergence constraint preserves the original generation capability. Despite being trained on only a limited set of object categories, TruthLens generalizes effectively to benchmarks with substantially larger label spaces. Extensive experiments across multiple LVLMs demonstrate state-of-the-art performance; notably, on Qwen2.5-VL-7B, TruthLens outperforms the previous best method on MS-COCO by over 17\% in AUROC. SkillTFM: Gated Skill Evolution for Training-Free Adaptation of Tabular Foundation Models

Yi He , Zhengkang Guan , Anpeng Wu , Peng Cui , Fei Wu , Kun Kuang

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

This paper proposes SkillTFM, a training-free system that adapts tabular foundation models to new datasets via gated skill evolution, without parameter updates. It improves AUC on boundary settings and real-world forecasting. For astronomy, this could be applied to photometric redshift estimation or source classification from multi-wavelength catalogs. A concrete workflow might be to use SkillTFM to adapt a pre-trained tabular model to a new survey catalog without fine-tuning. The main limitation is that the method is designed for tabular data and may need adaptation to handle missing values or heterogeneous feature types common in astronomy.

Reason

Focuses on tabular foundation models and training-free adaptation, which is highly relevant to astronomical catalogs and tabular data analysis.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Check if the skill bank and gated evolution are described in enough detail to adapt to astronomical catalogs.
  • Look for details on how the method handles distribution shifts, which are common in survey data.
  • Assess whether the improvements are significant for typical astronomical tabular tasks like photo-z.
Abstract

Tabular data are ubiquitous in real-world applications and are crucial for data-driven prediction and decision-making across science, industry, finance, healthcare, and public services. Tabular foundation models (TFMs) have emerged as a promising paradigm for general-purpose tabular learning, offering reusable predictors across diverse datasets and substantially reducing the need for task-specific training, tuning, and model development. However, their practical deployment remains constrained by distribution shifts, heterogeneous feature semantics, and task-specific patterns that are difficult to capture without costly fine-tuning or additional labeled data. To this end, we propose SkillTFM, a training-free system that shifts TFM adaptation from parameter updates to the gated evolution of agentic skills. The core of SkillTFM is a verifiable and extensible skill bank that couples boundary evidence identification with gated skill evolution: the former characterizes task structure and base-model failure patterns, whereas the latter retrieves and extends reusable skills subject to explicit validation. Across simulated boundary settings and real-world electricity-price forecasting, SkillTFM improves AUC by 0.128--0.142, raises nonlinear-boundary AUC from 0.699 to 0.898. Furthermore, experiments across TFM backbones demonstrate the effectiveness and generality of SkillTFM.
