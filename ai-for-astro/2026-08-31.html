When Evidence Shapes Collaboration: Knowledge-Conditioned Topology Generation for Multi-Agent Systems

Yangxiao Jiang , Jiarun Fan , Mingcong Xu , Yanxi Guo , Jiwen Feng , Shanqing Xu , Mengchen Qian , Wei Chen , Xiaojin Zhang

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

This paper addresses the problem of designing collaboration topologies for multi-agent LLM systems, where existing methods rely on parametric knowledge and treat retrieval as reactive rather than structural. The main idea is K-GAT, a neuro-symbolic framework that integrates external evidence into autoregressive graph generation, formulating topology design as knowledge-conditioned structure learning. This matters for astronomy because multi-agent systems could be used for automated literature review or data analysis pipelines, where external knowledge from catalogs or papers should shape agent interactions. A concrete workflow could be building an agent team for JWST data analysis where agents retrieve relevant papers or calibration data and their collaboration structure is guided by that evidence. A caveat is that the method is tested on general knowledge benchmarks, not astronomy-specific tasks, so transferability needs validation.

Reason

Directly relevant to LLM agents for scientific workflows, especially knowledge-conditioned topology generation could apply to multi-agent research systems for astronomy. The GPQA benchmark is knowledge-intensive, similar to literature review tasks.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Check if the method requires a specific knowledge base or retrieval mechanism that could be adapted to astronomy literature or catalogs
  • Look for details on how the topology generator handles heterogeneous evidence types (e.g., text, tables, images)
Abstract

Multi-Agent Systems (MAS) have recently moved from static workflows toward dynamically generated collaboration topologies. However, existing topology generation methods rely primarily on the parametric knowledge of large language models, with external search or retrieval used only as a reactive tool rather than an explicit determinant of collaboration structure. This leads to structure-knowledge misalignment, where systems exhibit redundant interactions or insufficient verification in knowledge-intensive tasks. We propose K-GAT (Knowledge-Guided Agent Topology Generator), a neuro-symbolic framework that formulates collaboration topology design as a knowledge-conditioned structure learning problem, integrating external evidence directly into autoregressive graph generation. Extensive experiments on knowledge-intensive benchmarks demonstrate K-GAT's efficiency and effectiveness: notably on the expert-level GPQA dataset, K-GAT outperforms the LLM-Debate baseline by a substantial margin of +15.7% in accuracy, while consuming less than half the computational tokens.

MAIL: Memory-driven, Adaptive, Incremental, and Literature-grounded Framework for Hypothesis Generation in Chemistry

Mahdi Babaei , Xueshen Li , Yutao Kuang , Jolene P. Reid , Yu Gan

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

This paper addresses the problem of generating novel hypotheses from scientific literature. It introduces MAIL, a memory-augmented, adaptive, incremental framework that uses LLMs to reason over evolving knowledge. For astronomy, this could be used to generate hypotheses about high-redshift galaxies or AGN from the literature. The concrete workflow would be adapting MAIL to astronomical literature to propose new observational targets or theoretical models. The main caveat is that the method is evaluated on chemistry datasets, so transfer to astronomy requires validation.

The paper presents an LLM-based framework for hypothesis generation in chemistry, which is directly relevant to automated scientific discovery and could transfer to astronomy.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Check if the framework can handle astronomical terminology and data types
  • Look for any discussion of generalizing to other scientific domains
Abstract

The ever-expanding volume of the chemical literature offers unprecedented opportunities to generate novel and impactful hypotheses. However, the bottleneck lies in efficiently navigating this vast knowledge base to formulate high-quality, experimentally meaningful insights. While Large Language Models (LLMs) show promise for this task, existing methods often rely on static inspiration corpora, predefined heuristics, or laborious human-in-the-loop pipelines and decision-support frameworks that limit scalability and novelty. In this work, we propose an automated approach, a Memory-augmented, Adaptive, Incremental, and Literature-grounded (MAIL) framework for hypothesis generation in chemistry. Our MAIL method formulates hypothesis generation as a temporally grounded, memory-driven reasoning process, where hypotheses emerge from an evolving conceptual path that continuously accumulates and reinterprets prior knowledge. We evaluated the MAIL framework on a public TOMATO-Chem dataset and a newly curated and disseminated high-novelty nature/science challenge (HN-NS) dataset. Across both datasets, MAIL generates structurally coherent and mechanistically plausible hypotheses, achieves the highest MIOS and MPOS by more effectively recovering the central ideas and methodological elements of the historical target hypotheses, and obtains the highest overall expert-evaluation scores for scientific quality. These results demonstrate the potential of LLMs to autonomously explore chemical domains and generate hypotheses that are both innovative and chemically plausible.

Time Capsule of Testable Human Knowledge: 41 Years of Jeopardy! in a Single Free Local Model

David Noever , Forrest McKee

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

This paper tests whether a single open-weight LLM can answer a large dataset of Jeopardy! clues, finding it achieves 67% accuracy. The main idea is that a portable, free model can serve as a knowledge snapshot, similar to IBM's Watson but more accessible. For astronomy, this could inform how well LLMs handle general knowledge questions, but it does not address domain-specific data analysis. A potential workflow might involve using such a model for literature-based fact-checking, but its relevance to JWST or survey data is limited. The main caveat is that the model's performance on domain-specific scientific queries is not tested here.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Check if the model's performance is broken down by knowledge domain, especially science or astronomy categories.
  • Look for any analysis of failure modes on questions requiring numerical or spatial reasoning.
Abstract

In 2011, IBM's Watson was something like a sealed capsule of its era's queryable knowledge. Its DeepQA system defeated the strongest human Jeopardy! champions, but the knowledge that let it do so lived in a curated billion-document corpus running on a cluster of POWER7 servers, frozen at build time and impossible to move or copy. We show that the same kind of artifact, a snapshot of what a culture can answer, is now portable and essentially free. We evaluate a single 9 GB open-weight model (Qwen2.5-14B, 4-bit) against the complete open Jeopardy! clue dataset, 529,939 clues across all 41 broadcast seasons from 1984 to 2025. To our knowledge this is the first time a model has been run over the full corpus. The 41 years mark only how long the questions were collected. What they test is far older and broader: the accumulated body of human general knowledge a culture considers worth knowing, from ancient history and dead languages to science, literature, and geography, with a verified answer for every item. The model answers 67.0% of all clues under a strict forced-response protocol with exact and fuzzy matching, and exceeds 85% on factoid categories. We treat training-data exposure as something both systems share rather than a flaw unique to language models. Watson's case is in fact the more extreme one. Its corpus was assembled to contain Jeopardy answers and it was tuned on past clues, and it could not answer anything outside that curated distribution. The decisive test is whether a model can answer clues that did not exist when it was built. On clues aired after its training cutoff, the local model holds 65% and Claude Opus 4.8 holds 95%, while Watson by construction scores zero. The capability survives the move from a server room to a file you could seal in a time capsule, and unlike Watson it is not frozen to its own moment.

Dandelion: A Spherical Flower for Neural Simulation of Planetary Dynamics

Till Muser , Giovanni Abati , Ivan Dokmanić

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

This paper proposes a spherical neural PDE solver called Dandelion that uses warp-based feature transport instead of convolutions, aiming to handle dynamics on spheres more accurately than Euclidean architectures. It introduces a benchmark suite of natively spherical PDE datasets and shows Dandelion performs best or second-best across them. For astronomy, this could be useful for modeling spherical phenomena like planetary atmospheres or large-scale cosmic structure, though it is not directly tied to JWST or high-redshift galaxy analysis. A concrete workflow might be applying it to spherical harmonics-based SED fitting or atmospheric modeling of exoplanets. However, its relevance to my specific interests in JWST and LLM-based analysis is limited, and the computational overhead may be significant.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Check if the benchmark datasets include any astrophysical or planetary examples
  • Look for details on how the warp-based approach handles irregular spherical grids
  • Assess whether the method could be adapted to spherical galaxy morphology or CMB analysis
Abstract

Many dynamical processes unfold on the sphere but the default scientific machine learning architectures are Euclidean. Applying these architectures on a regular lat-lon grid causes problems: Cartesian convolutions become distorted at high latitude; 2D FFTs in Fourier neural operators incorrectly assume double periodicity; Cartesian positional encodings in ViTs distort spherical geodesic distances. Recent work moves towards natively spherical primitives, including spherical convolutions (e.g., DeepSphere or DISCO), Spherical Fourier Neural Operators (SFNOs), and geodesic attention. Here we propose Dandelion, a spherical version of Flower, a warp-based neural PDE solver. Layers of Dandelion predict a tangent-plane displacement and transport features along great circles. We obtain a U-Net-like structure by implementing hierarchical pooling entirely in the spherical-harmonic domain. There are thus no convolutions: spatial mixing is achieved only through spherical coordinate changes, or warps. To compare Dandelion with existing spherical architectures, we release an evolving benchmark suite of challenging, natively-spherical PDE datasets including a modified Galewsky jet, anomalous chained turbulence, Cahn-Hilliard decomposition, spherical Riemann shocks, Held-Suarez dry atmospheric transport and global ocean dynamics. This new benchmark fills the gap in existing spherical datasets which are either too small and stylized, or much too large (ERA5) for model iteration. Dandelion is best or second-best on every dataset, and the gap to non-warp baselines widens with resolution: at $256\times 512$, Dandelion and Flower2D occupy the top two slots in both single-step prediction and rollout.

Semantic Head Specialization Guides Hybrid ViT Attention for Multimodal LLMs

Chenhong He , Lei Li , Shicheng Li , Hanglong Lv , Lingpeng Kong , Qi Liu , Tong Yang , Shuhuai Ren

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

The paper studies how attention heads in Vision Transformers specialize into object and background roles, proposing a metric to quantify this. It introduces a hybrid attention mechanism that matches full attention performance with less compute. For astronomy, this could inform efficient processing of JWST images in multimodal models, but the direct application is not immediate.

Reason

Focuses on ViT attention design for multimodal LLMs, which is relevant to LMMs but not directly to astronomy workflows.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Check if the hybrid attention design can be adapted to astronomical image sizes or resolutions
  • Look for any experiments on scientific or remote-sensing images beyond natural images
Abstract

Hybrid attention dominates frontier LLMs, yet Vision Transformers (ViTs) in multimodal LLMs lack a satisfactory hybrid design, with no consensus on why certain attention patterns work better. To fill this gap, we study ViT attention heads and find they differentiate into object- and background-specialist roles, a pattern most pronounced under full attention; we call this Semantic Head Specialization (SHS). We propose SHS-Index to quantify this specialization, show that it distinguishes full-attention from chunk-window ViTs, and find that it strongly tracks downstream benchmark performance. We then identify three structural factors that shape SHS---window interaction, token serialization, and local softmax allocation---and use them as design principles for hybrid attention. Guided by these factors, we design Ariadne Attention, a hybrid that matches full attention on 22 image and video tasks at 6.5x less attention compute. Our findings establish head specialization as a measurable property for diagnosing and designing principled hybrid ViT attention at the multimodal-LLM scale.
