## 1. MOF-Sleuth: Tool-Grounded Reward Alignment for Explainable Fine-Grained MOF CIF Auditing

- arXiv ID: `2607.19935`
- Authors: Yu Liu , Zhiwei Yang , Diandian Guo , Kun Peng , Fangfang Yuan , Cong Cao , Chaozhuo Li , Zhiyuan Ma , Yanbing Liu , Guobin Zhao
- Score: 8
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19935
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.19935
- Subjects: Artificial Intelligence (cs.AI)
- Reason: This paper directly applies LLM agents to scientific data auditing (MOF CIF files), which is a clear example of automated scientific discovery. The tool-grounded reward alignment and evidence-grounded explanation approach could transfer to astronomical data validation.

Short digest:
This paper addresses the problem of detecting subtle chemical and structural errors in large metal-organic framework (MOF) databases, which can compromise downstream simulation and machine learning results. The core idea is MOF-Sleuth, a reinforcement-guided CIF auditing agent with two modules: a deterministic Forensic Lab that derives composition, geometry, connectivity, occupancy, coordination, and charge evidence, and a Sleuth reasoning engine that uses this evidence to produce an evidence-grounded explanation, error types, and a binary decision. Reward-guided reinforcement learning turns tool measurements into chemical explanation-level supervision, rewarding not only the final answer but also cited chemical evidence and evidence-supported diagnoses. For astronomy, this approach could be directly adapted to validate JWST spectroscopic or imaging data products, detect anomalies in high-redshift galaxy catalogs, or audit SED fitting results with evidence-grounded explanations. An astronomer could imagine using a similar agent to check for subtle errors in photometric redshift estimates, spectral line identifications, or source classifications in large survey catalogs. The main caveat is that the method is designed for MOF-specific CIF files and would require adaptation to astronomical data formats and domain-specific validators.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Look for the specific reward functions and how they incorporate tool measurements into the RL training loop.
- Check whether the Forensic Lab module is domain-agnostic or requires MOF-specific chemistry knowledge.
- Examine the Chem-GD metric to see if it could be adapted for astronomical data validation.
- Look for any discussion of transferability to other scientific domains or data formats.

## 2. PercepCap: Video Captioner with Structured Spatio-Temporal Perception

- arXiv ID: `2607.20389`
- Authors: Yifan Xu , Zihao Wang , Zhixiao Wang , Jiaming Zhang , Yichun Yang , Desen Meng , Yuanxing Zhang , Pengfei Wan , Limin Wang
- Score: 8
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20389
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.20389
- Subjects: Computer Vision and Pattern Recognition (cs.CV)
- Reason: The paper proposes a video captioning framework that makes spatiotemporal perception explicit before generating captions, which directly relates to multimodal models that combine visual and textual data, a key interest for astronomical image analysis and automated description.

Short digest:
The paper asks how to make video captioning models produce explicit, verifiable perceptual evidence (object trajectories and temporal events) before generating the final caption, rather than just outputting a description from a black-box model. The main idea is a 'perceive-describe' chain where the model first outputs a structured perception trace (e.g., 'object A moves from left to right at time t1-t2') and then generates the caption conditioned on that trace, trained with a two-stage supervised fine-tuning and reinforcement learning approach. For astronomy, this could be adapted to generate structured descriptions of astronomical phenomena from time-domain surveys (e.g., 'a transient brightens in the southern arm of the galaxy between frames 10 and 20'), or to produce captions for JWST images that explicitly mention detected sources and their positions. A concrete workflow would be feeding a sequence of JWST NIRCam images of a supernova into such a model to get a caption like 'a point source appears at (x,y) in band F200W and fades over 3 epochs'. A limitation is that the method is designed for natural videos with clear object trajectories, and astronomical images may have very different noise, resolution, and semantic structure.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Check whether the perception trace (object trajectories and events) is grounded with bounding boxes and timestamps, and how this grounding is obtained for training data.
- Look for details on the reinforcement learning reward function and whether it could be adapted to domain-specific metrics (e.g., accuracy of source positions or flux measurements).
- See if the paper discusses generalization to videos with many small, faint objects (like astronomical sources) versus a few salient objects.

## 3. SoftReason: A Fully Differentiable Neuro-Soft-Symbolic Deductive Reasoning Architecture over High-Dimensional Perceptual Data

- arXiv ID: `2607.20402`
- Authors: Wael AbdAlmageed
- Score: 8
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20402
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.20402
- Subjects: Artificial Intelligence (cs.AI)
- Reason: The paper presents a differentiable neuro-symbolic reasoning architecture that can handle high-dimensional perceptual data and knowledge graph evidence, which directly aligns with interests in automated scientific discovery and multimodal models for astronomy.

Short digest:
The paper asks how to perform deductive reasoning when premises are not given as discrete symbols but must be inferred from high-dimensional inputs like images, and when background knowledge is available from a knowledge graph. The main idea is SoftReason, a fully differentiable architecture that represents the deductive state as a 'soft interpretation tensor' over candidate constants and predicates, allowing perception (e.g., from a vision model) to propose probabilistic facts and the knowledge graph to provide high-confidence evidence, all while keeping the entire reasoning chain trainable via gradient descent. For astronomy, this could be transformative: one could build a system that takes a JWST image, detects sources (perception), queries a knowledge graph of known AGN or high-redshift galaxy properties, and then performs differentiable reasoning to classify the source or infer its redshift. A concrete workflow would be feeding a cutout of a 'little red dot' into a vision encoder, getting probabilistic facts like 'object is compact' and 'object has red color', then using a knowledge graph of AGN signatures to deduce 'object is likely an AGN' with end-to-end training. A limitation is that the paper instantiates the framework only on Knowledge-aware Visual Question Answering (KVQA), a dataset of natural images and questions, and scaling to astronomical images with their unique noise and resolution may require significant adaptation.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Check whether the framework supports continuous or probabilistic predicates (e.g., 'redshift > 5') or only categorical ones, as many astronomical properties are continuous.
- Look for details on how the knowledge graph is constructed and whether it can be dynamically updated with new astronomical discoveries.
- See if the paper discusses computational scalability to large knowledge graphs or high-resolution images, as JWST data can be large.
- Examine the training data requirements: does the method need a large set of (image, question, answer) triples, or can it leverage existing astronomical catalogs and knowledge bases?

## 4. Expert-Guided Forecast Editing for Time-Series Foundation Models

- arXiv ID: `2607.19659`
- Authors: Hung Le , Minh Hoang Nguyen , Manh Nguyen , Huu Hiep Nguyen , Dai Do
- Score: 7
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19659
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.19659
- Subjects: Machine Learning (cs.LG)
- Reason: Time-series foundation models and expert-guided forecast editing are directly relevant to astronomical time-domain analysis, such as light curve forecasting for variable stars, AGN, or transients. The method could be applied to JWST time-series observations.

Short digest:
This paper introduces DEFT, a framework for editing forecasts from frozen time-series foundation models using sparse expert feedback. It balances exploiting the model's predictive distribution with structured exploration in a trend-seasonal decomposition space. For astronomy, this could be applied to light curve forecasting for variable stars, AGN, or exoplanet transits, where an astronomer could provide expert feedback on a few candidate forecasts. An astronomer could imagine using DEFT to refine light curve predictions for a high-redshift supernova or to forecast the next outburst of a tidal disruption event. The main caveat is that the paper evaluates on general forecasting benchmarks and a molecular dynamics case study, not on astronomical light curves, so the method's performance on irregularly sampled, noisy astronomical data is unknown.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Check if the trend-seasonal decomposition is appropriate for astronomical light curves with irregular sampling and measurement noise
- Look for discussion of how the method handles missing data or non-periodic variability, which are common in astronomy

## 5. Reference-Free Evaluation of Reasoning in Open-Ended Question Answering

- arXiv ID: `2607.19678`
- Authors: Guneet Singh Kohli , Yuxiang Zhou , Michael Sejr Schlichtkrull , Gregory E Dean , Maria Liakata
- Score: 7
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19678
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.19678
- Subjects: Computation and Language (cs.CL) ; Artificial Intelligence (cs.AI); Machine Learning (cs.LG)
- Reason: Reference-free evaluation of reasoning in LLM outputs is directly relevant to building research agents for astronomy that need to verify their own reasoning. The hypergraph-based audit framework could be applied to evaluating LLM-generated scientific analyses.

Short digest:
This paper proposes a reference-free framework for auditing LLM reasoning traces by decomposing them into segments, labeling premise-target relations using Natural Language Inference, and organizing these into a hypergraph for backward AND-OR search. It is evaluated on mathematical reasoning and a new medical reasoning benchmark. For astronomy, this could be used to verify the reasoning of LLM agents that analyze JWST spectra or generate scientific conclusions from multi-wavelength data. An astronomer could imagine using this to audit an LLM agent's step-by-step reasoning when classifying a little red dot or estimating a photometric redshift. The main caveat is that the framework is evaluated on deductive and medical reasoning, and its performance on astronomy-specific reasoning with domain knowledge (e.g., SED fitting, spectral line identification) is not tested.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Check if the NLI component can handle domain-specific astronomical terminology and concepts
- Look for discussion of how the hypergraph audit handles probabilistic or uncertain reasoning, which is common in astronomy
