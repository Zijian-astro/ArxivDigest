## 1. How Do Agents Fail on AutoResearch: End-to-End Diagnostic Evaluation on 100 Real-World Frontier Research Tasks

- arXiv ID: `2608.14905`
- Authors: Yanlin Fei , Nazhou Liu , Xinmiao Yu , Shaolong Chen , Lei Li , Rahul Thapa , Madalina Ciobanu , Qingqing Mao , Ritankar Das
- Score: 9
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14905
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.14905
- Subjects: Computation and Language (cs.CL)
- Reason: Directly addresses AutoResearch, which is central to my interest in automated scientific discovery. The failure taxonomy and evaluation on real research tasks are highly relevant for building research agents for astronomy.

Short digest:
This paper introduces AutoResearchEval, a benchmark of 100 real-world research tasks across 7 domains, evaluating agentic research systems end-to-end. The main idea is to provide process-level annotations and a failure taxonomy, finding that agents lack a metacognitive loop. For astronomy, this is highly relevant because it identifies common failure modes in autonomous research, which could inform the design of astronomy-specific agents. A concrete workflow could be using this benchmark to evaluate an agent that analyzes JWST data and writes papers. The main caveat is that the tasks are not astronomy-specific, so the failure patterns may differ in scientific domains with specialized data.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Check if the failure taxonomy includes domain-specific issues, such as data handling or visualization
- Look for any details on the agent-as-a-judge pipeline, which could be used for evaluating astronomical research agents

- Score: 8
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14881

- Subjects: Artificial Intelligence (cs.AI) ; Computation and Language (cs.CL)
- Reason: Directly addresses personalized auto-research, which aligns with my interest in research agents for scientific discovery. The framework for conditioning research on researcher context could be applied to astronomy.

Short digest:
This paper introduces the problem of personalized auto-research, where AI co-scientists tailor every stage of research to an individual researcher. The main idea is a framework that uses graph-grounded researcher representations to personalize retrieval, hypothesis search, experimentation, writing, and review. For astronomy, this is highly relevant because it could create agents that understand an astronomer's prior work and methods, improving discovery. A concrete workflow could be a co-scientist that suggests new analyses for JWST data based on an astronomer's past papers. The main caveat is that the framework is conceptual, with no implementation or evaluation, so practical benefits are unproven.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Check if the paper provides any concrete examples of personalization in a scientific domain
- Look for a discussion of how to represent researcher context, such as publication graphs or code repositories

## 3. Historical Backtesting for Scientific Question Discovery: A Protocol and Astronomy Pilot

- arXiv ID: `2608.16795`
- Authors: Hui Mao
- Score: 8
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16795
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.16795
- Subjects: Computational Engineering, Finance, and Science (cs.CE) ; Artificial Intelligence (cs.AI)
- Reason: Directly targets automated scientific question discovery with a falsifiable evaluation protocol, highly relevant to research agents and scientific discovery in astronomy.

Short digest:
This paper tackles the problem of evaluating AI systems that generate scientific research questions, proposing a historical backtesting protocol where questions are frozen before future literature is available and scored against that future. The main idea is to use temporally isolated corpora to objectively determine if generated questions were answered, refuted, or ignored, avoiding subjective expert scores. For astronomy, this could help build and validate AI-driven research agents that propose novel, testable questions for JWST or survey data. A concrete workflow would be to use this protocol to benchmark an LLM-based question generator on high-redshift galaxy or AGN topics. However, the low inter-rater agreement on the outcome taxonomy suggests that the evaluation labels themselves need careful design before trusting results.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Check the exact definition of the outcome taxonomy (answered, partially addressed, etc.) and how it handles ambiguous cases
- Look for the released astronomy instances and whether they include JWST-era topics
- Assess the generator decomposition (evidence-structure-first vs LLM-only) and whether it can be applied to my own question-generation pipelines

## 4. HyMem: Hierarchical Context Management for Long-Horizon Agents via Information Isolation

- arXiv ID: `2608.15703`
- Authors: XinQi Wang , Jinwei Xiao , Sijia Cui , Hongming Zhang , Yanna Wang , Qingyang Zhang , Bo Xu
- Score: 7
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.15703
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.15703
- Subjects: Artificial Intelligence (cs.AI)
- Reason: Directly addresses long-horizon agent context management, which is key for research agents in astronomy that must handle complex multi-step analyses and literature reviews. The hierarchical context separation could improve reliability of LLM-driven scientific workflows.

Short digest:
This paper tackles the problem of LLM agents losing track of high-level goals when their context fills with execution details over long tasks. It introduces HyMem, a framework that separates context into functional layers—planning, execution, and reasoning—so that intermediate steps don't clutter the persistent plan. For astronomy, this could help an agent maintain a clear research plan while running many data-analysis steps, e.g., fitting SEDs or querying catalogs. I could imagine using it to build a research agent that iteratively analyzes JWST spectra while keeping the original science question in focus. However, the method is tested on general benchmarks (GAIA, Browsecomp-plus), not scientific tasks, so I should check if the hierarchical design transfers to domain-specific workflows with long tool-use chains.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Check whether the hierarchical context design is model-agnostic and can be applied to open-source LLMs used in astronomy
- Look for ablation studies showing which component (planning vs. reasoning isolation) contributes most to performance gains
- See if the memory management module supports structured summaries of astronomical data products (e.g., spectra, images) or only text

- Score: 6

- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.15962
- Subjects: Computation and Language (cs.CL) ; Computer Vision and Pattern Recognition (cs.CV)
- Reason: The paper addresses long-context reasoning with selective visual-text compression, which could be relevant for processing large astronomical datasets or documents, but the application is generic and not astronomy-specific.

Short digest:
This paper tackles the computational cost of long-context reasoning in LLMs by selectively compressing text into images and retrieving text only where needed. The main idea is a framework that learns to choose query-relevant images through visual scanning, balancing efficiency and precision. This could matter for astronomy by enabling efficient processing of long multi-wavelength catalogs or literature, but the method is not validated on scientific data. A concrete workflow could be compressing long astronomical survey descriptions for question-answering agents. A caveat is that the compression may lose fine details needed for precise scientific analysis.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Check if the method can handle domain-specific text like astronomical papers or data descriptions.
- Look for any evaluation on long documents with technical jargon or tables.
