## 1. Evidence Blindness in Direct Corpus Interaction: Persistent Navigation with AtlasNav

- arXiv ID: `2608.24764`
- Authors: Hongyu Guo , Zhiyu Zheng , Zhao Cao
- Score: 8
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24764
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.24764
- Subjects: Artificial Intelligence (cs.AI)
- Reason: Directly relevant to LLM agents for literature review and data analysis, which is a key interest for automated scientific discovery in astronomy.

Short digest:
The paper addresses the problem of evidence blindness in direct corpus interaction, where agents fail to surface or use relevant evidence. It introduces AtlasNav, a persistent multi-view corpus navigation framework that organizes the corpus once and allows adaptive navigation per query. This matters for astronomy because it can improve LLM agents that search and synthesize astronomical literature and data, making them more effective and cost-efficient.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Check if the Corpus Atlas construction can handle large astronomical databases like ADS or VizieR.
- Look for details on how the framework scales with corpus size and query complexity.

- Score: 7
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24753

- Subjects: Computation and Language (cs.CL) ; Artificial Intelligence (cs.AI)
- Reason: Directly relevant to RAG evaluation, which is key for LLM-based research agents in astronomy that retrieve and synthesize literature or data.

Short digest:
The paper introduces a Bayesian framework to evaluate RAG systems by modeling retrieval success, abstention, and answer correctness jointly. It decomposes errors to reveal behavioral differences between systems that look similar under marginal metrics. This matters for astronomy because it provides a principled way to assess and improve LLM agents that retrieve and reason over astronomical literature or catalogs.

## 3. CAFE: Self-Improving Search Agents Need Co-Evolving Feedback

- arXiv ID: `2608.24794`
- Authors: Boyang Liu , Senjie Jin , Peixin Wang , Zhangyue Yin , Yibo Wang , Yuhao Zhou , Xinbing Liang , Shizheng Zhu , Yuhui Wang , Jingqi Tong , Zhiheng Xi , Jiazheng Zhang , Clive Bai , Clarenceai , Blaze Chen , Tao Gui , Qi Zhang , Xuanjing Huang
- Score: 7
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24794
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.24794
- Subjects: Artificial Intelligence (cs.AI)
- Reason: Directly addresses improving search agents via co-evolving feedback, relevant to LLM agents for scientific workflows and literature review. The concept of feedback-conditioned recovery could transfer to astronomy research agents that need to correct their retrieval or analysis steps.

Short digest:
This paper tackles the problem that outcome-supervised search agents lack intermediate feedback, causing errors to compound. It introduces CAFE, where a shared-parameter model alternates between being a search agent and a critic, using online RL and offline preference optimization to co-evolve feedback. This matters for astronomy because research agents that search literature or data could benefit from self-improving feedback loops. I could imagine applying it to an agent that retrieves and analyzes JWST papers or spectra, where intermediate corrections improve final results. However, the benchmarks are generic search tasks, so I should check if the method scales to domain-specific, multi-step scientific reasoning.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Check whether the feedback mechanism is domain-agnostic or requires task-specific reward design
- Look for ablation results showing how feedback quality changes over training
- Inspect if the method is evaluated on long-horizon tasks similar to scientific literature review
