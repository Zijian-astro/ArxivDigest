## 1. CASCADE: Case-Based Continual Adaptation for Large Language Models During Deployment

- arXiv ID: `2605.06702`
- Authors: Siyuan Guo , Yali Du , Hechang Chen , Yi Chang , Jun Wang
- Score: 8
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06702
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.06702
- Subjects: Artificial Intelligence (cs.AI) ; Computation and Language (cs.CL); Machine Learning (cs.LG)
- Reason: Directly addresses deployment-time learning for LLM agents via episodic memory, which is highly relevant to building research agents for automated scientific discovery in astronomy.

Short digest:
This paper asks how LLM agents can continue learning from experience during deployment without retraining model parameters. The core idea is CASCADE, a framework that gives agents an explicit episodic memory and treats experience reuse as a contextual bandit problem, balancing exploration and exploitation with theoretical guarantees. For astronomy, this could enable a research agent that improves its ability to search literature, analyze JWST spectra, or classify sources over time as it encounters more data. Concretely, one could imagine deploying an agent to autonomously analyze a stream of new JWST observations, where it learns which analysis strategies work best from past successes and failures. A key caveat is that the paper tests on 16 diverse tasks (medical, legal, code, web) but not on scientific data analysis, so the transfer to astronomy workflows needs validation.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Check whether the episodic memory stores structured astronomical knowledge (e.g., source properties, SED templates) or only generic task descriptions.
- Look for ablation studies showing how the exploration-exploitation trade-off is tuned and whether it can be adapted to long-running scientific workflows.
- Examine the computational overhead of the episodic memory relative to the base LLM, especially for large-scale survey data.
- Verify if the framework supports multimodal inputs (images, spectra) or only text, which is critical for JWST analysis.

## 2. When Does Critique Improve AI-Assisted Theoretical Physics? SCALAR: Structured Critic--Actor Loop for Agentic Reasoning

- arXiv ID: `2605.06772`
- Authors: Vasilis Niarchos , Constantinos Papageorgakis , Alexander G. Stapleton , Sokratis Trifinopoulos
- Score: 8
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06772
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.06772
- Subjects: Artificial Intelligence (cs.AI) ; Human-Computer Interaction (cs.HC); High Energy Physics - Phenomenology (hep-ph); High Energy Physics - Theory (hep-th)
- Reason: Directly studies LLM agents for theoretical physics reasoning with a structured critic-actor loop, highly relevant to research agents and automated scientific discovery.

Short digest:
The paper asks how researcher-agent interaction affects AI-assisted theoretical physics problem-solving. It introduces SCALAR, a pipeline where an Actor proposes solutions, a Critic provides iterative feedback, and a Judge evaluates the transcript. This matters for astronomy because similar agentic loops could be used to automate SED fitting, spectral classification, or literature review for JWST data. One could imagine using SCALAR to iteratively refine photometric redshift estimates or classify little red dots by having the agent propose and critique solutions. A key caveat is that the study uses quantum field theory problems, so transfer to astronomy workflows requires adaptation of the problem representation and evaluation metrics.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Look for the specific prompting strategies used for the Critic (constructive vs. strict vs. adversarial) and whether they are described in enough detail to replicate.
- Check if the paper provides the full transcript examples or only aggregate scores, to assess the quality of feedback.
- Examine the Judge evaluation criteria: are they based on exact answer matching or partial credit, and could a similar rubric be designed for astronomy tasks?

## 3. Kurtosis-Guided Denoising Score Matching for Tabular Anomaly Detection

- arXiv ID: `2605.06955`
- Authors: Victor Livernoche , Jie Zan , Reihaneh Rabbany
- Score: 8
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06955
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.06955
- Subjects: Machine Learning (cs.LG) ; Artificial Intelligence (cs.AI)
- Reason: Directly addresses anomaly detection in tabular data, which is highly relevant to astronomical source classification and outlier detection in survey catalogs. The kurtosis-based noise scaling is a practical, hyperparameter-light method that could transfer to JWST or Rubin/LSST data.

Short digest:
This paper tackles the problem of detecting anomalous points in tabular data, a common task in astronomy for finding rare or unusual sources in survey catalogs. The core idea is a denoising score matching (DSM) approach, where a neural network learns the 'score' (gradient of the data density) from noise-corrupted samples; a high score magnitude at a test point indicates it is inconsistent with the training distribution and thus anomalous. The key innovation is a per-feature noise scaling scheme based on kurtosis (a measure of tail heaviness), which adapts the noise level to each feature's distribution shape without requiring expensive hyperparameter tuning. For astronomy, this could be applied to detect anomalous galaxies, AGN, or artifacts in multi-wavelength catalogs from surveys like JWST, Roman, or Rubin. A concrete workflow would be to train K-DSM on a clean set of known source types (e.g., stars, normal galaxies) and then flag sources with high anomaly scores for spectroscopic follow-up or further inspection. A caveat is that the method is demonstrated on standard ML benchmarks, not astronomical data, so its performance on noisy, missing, or highly imbalanced astronomical catalogs needs verification.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Check whether the method is evaluated on datasets with similar characteristics to astronomical catalogs (e.g., high dimensionality, missing values, class imbalance).
- Look for ablation studies showing the impact of the kurtosis-based noise scaling versus fixed or other adaptive noise schemes.
- Examine the sensitivity of the anomaly detection performance to the choice of the noise scale parameter and whether the method is robust to contamination of the training set.

- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.07323

- Subjects: Artificial Intelligence (cs.AI) ; Machine Learning (cs.LG); Neural and Evolutionary Computing (cs.NE); Symbolic Computation (cs.SC)
- Reason: Directly relevant to automated scientific discovery using LLMs for equation discovery from observational data.

Short digest:
This paper tackles the problem of discovering governing differential equations from observational data, a core challenge in scientific machine learning. It proposes DoLQ, a method that uses a multi-agent LLM architecture to propose, optimize, and evaluate candidate equations. A Scientist Agent leverages an LLM to conduct both qualitative (physical plausibility) and quantitative (accuracy) evaluations. The method achieves superior performance on multi-dimensional ODE benchmarks. For astronomy, this could be applied to discover dynamical models from time-series observations, such as light curves or spectral variability. The multi-agent approach with LLM-based evaluation is a novel way to incorporate domain knowledge into equation discovery. However, the paper only tests on synthetic ODE benchmarks, not real astronomical data.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Check if the method can handle noisy or sparse observational data typical in astronomy.
- Look for any experiments on real-world scientific datasets beyond synthetic ODEs.
- Examine the LLM's ability to incorporate physical constraints (e.g., conservation laws) into the evaluation.
- Check the computational cost and scalability to large datasets.

## 5. Anisotropic Modality Align

- arXiv ID: `2605.07825`
- Authors: Xiaomin Yu , Yijiang Li , Yuhui Zhang , Hanzhen Zhao , Yue Yang , Hao Tang , Yue Song , Xiaobin Hu , Chengwei Qin , Shuicheng Yan , Hui Xiong
- Score: 8
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.07825
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.07825
- Subjects: Multimedia (cs.MM) ; Computer Vision and Pattern Recognition (cs.CV)
- Reason: Directly addresses modality alignment for multimodal models, which is crucial for combining astronomical images, spectra, and catalogs.

Short digest:
This paper tackles the problem of the 'modality gap' in multimodal contrastive models, where representations from different modalities (e.g., images and text) are not interchangeable. The main finding is that the gap is not a simple shift but an anisotropic structure along a few dominant directions. The authors propose AnisoAlign, a method to correct this gap by aligning source representations to the target modality's distribution while preserving semantic structure. For astronomy, this is highly relevant: it could enable training multimodal models that combine JWST images, spectra, and catalog data without requiring large paired datasets. An astronomer could use this to build a model that, given a spectrum, retrieves similar images or vice versa. A caveat is that the method is demonstrated on general vision-language data, not astronomical data, and may require adaptation.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Check if the anisotropic correction is computationally feasible for high-dimensional astronomical data (e.g., spectra with thousands of wavelength bins).
- Look for any discussion of how to choose the number of dominant directions for correction, as this may be domain-dependent.

## 6. AgentEscapeBench: Evaluating Out-of-Domain Tool-Grounded Reasoning in LLM Agents

- Authors: Zhengkang Guo , Yiyang Li , Lin Qiu , Xiaohua Wang , Jingwen Xv , Dongyu Ru , Xiaoyu Li , Xiaoqing Zheng , Xuezhi Cao , Xunliang Cai
- Score: 8
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.07926
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.07926
- Subjects: Artificial Intelligence (cs.AI)
- Reason: Directly evaluates LLM agents on long-horizon tool-use reasoning, which is highly relevant to building research agents for automated scientific workflows.

Short digest:
This paper introduces AgentEscapeBench, a benchmark to test whether LLM agents can infer, execute, and revise novel tool-use procedures under long-range dependency constraints. The tasks involve a directed acyclic dependency graph over tools and items, requiring agents to track hidden state and propagate intermediate results. For astronomy, this is directly relevant because it provides a diagnostic framework for evaluating agents that could automate multi-step data analysis pipelines (e.g., fetching JWST images, running SED fitting, querying catalogs). A concrete workflow would be testing an agent on a simplified version of this benchmark before deploying it on real astronomical tasks. A key limitation is that the benchmark tasks are synthetic and not domain-specific; performance may not directly transfer to astronomical tool use without adaptation.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Check the dependency graph complexity and how it maps to real astronomical workflows (e.g., multi-catalog queries, sequential data processing).
- Look for trajectory analysis breakdowns: which types of errors (state tracking, clue adherence, result propagation) are most common and how they might be mitigated.
- Examine whether the benchmark supports custom tool definitions that could be adapted for astronomy-specific tools (e.g., astroquery, TOPCAT, or custom APIs).
- Check the human performance baseline to calibrate expectations for agent capabilities on long-horizon tasks.

Short digest:
This paper tackles the problem of manually designing test-time scaling (TTS) strategies for LLMs, which is inefficient and unexplored. The key idea is an environment-driven framework, AutoTTS, that automatically discovers TTS strategies by constructing a tractable control space and providing cheap feedback. For astronomy, this could automate the design of reasoning strategies for LLM agents performing data analysis or literature review, improving accuracy-cost tradeoffs. A concrete workflow would be using AutoTTS to discover optimal branching and pruning strategies for an LLM agent analyzing JWST spectra. A limitation is that the framework is demonstrated on mathematical reasoning and may require adaptation to scientific domains with different feedback signals.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Check if the discovered strategies generalize to held-out benchmarks and model scales.
- Look for details on the feedback mechanism and how it can be adapted to scientific tasks.
- Examine the cost and time requirements for discovery to assess feasibility for astronomy workflows.
