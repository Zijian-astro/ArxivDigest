## 2. Origins of Extreme Emission-Line Ratios in z > 3 Galaxies: Insights from the Lumen Model

- arXiv ID: `2605.06769`
- Authors: Lucie Scharré , Michaela Hirschmann , Adèle Plat , Stephane Charlot , Rachel S. Somerville , Emma Curtis-Lake , Gabriella De Lucia , Miroslava Dessauges-Zavadsky , Anna Feltre , Marion Farcy , Natalia Lahén , Aswin P. Vijayan , Stephen M. Wilkins
- Score: 9
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06769
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.06769
- Subjects: Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
- Reason: This paper directly addresses extreme emission-line ratios in z>3 galaxies using the Lumen model, which is highly relevant to high-redshift galaxy ISM conditions and AGN contributions.

Short digest:
The central question is what physical mechanisms produce extreme [OIII]/Hbeta and [OIII]/[OII] ratios in z~3-8 galaxies. The main result is that high ionization parameters from massive star clusters, combined with alpha-enhancement and AGN contributions, reproduce the observed offsets. This matters for my work because it provides a framework for interpreting JWST emission-line diagnostics and distinguishing AGN from star-formation in high-redshift sources.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Look for the model predictions for N2-BPT and O32-R23 diagrams and how they compare to JWST data.
- Check whether AGN contributions are modeled and how they affect the extreme line ratios.
- Examine the role of gas density and ionization parameter in the model.

- arXiv ID: `2605.07976`
- Authors: Rosa M. Mérida , Marcin Sawicki , Gaia Gaspar , Chris J. Willott , Kartheik G. Iyer
- Score: 9
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.07976
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.07976
- Subjects: Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
- Reason: Directly tests the BH* model for LRDs using JWST/NIRSpec PRISM spectroscopy, which is central to understanding compact red sources and AGN at high redshift.

Short digest:
Does the BH* model, where LRDs are accreting BHs in neutral-gas envelopes, hold up under self-consistent UV-to-optical spectral fitting? The fit to The Cliff (z=3.55) naturally yields a BH*-like solution with a low-mass, star-forming host and weak AGN UV leakage. This matters because it provides a quantitative test of a key LRD model, informing how we interpret compact red sources and early BH-host co-evolution.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Check the Bagpipes fitting diagnostics, especially the posterior distributions for BH and host parameters.
- Look for the inferred BH-to-stellar mass ratio and its evolution with redshift.
- Examine the dust attenuation law assumptions and how they affect stellar mass and AV estimates.
