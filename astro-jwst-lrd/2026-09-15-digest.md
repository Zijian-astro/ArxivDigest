## 1. Host Galaxy UV Emission and Dust Reddening in Little Red Dots

- arXiv ID: `2609.13444`
- Authors: Emmanuel Durodola , Ryan C. Hickox , Jonathan Cohn , David M. Alexander
- Score: 10
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13444
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.13444
- Subjects: Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
- Reason: This paper directly studies little red dots with JWST NIRSpec spectroscopy, focusing on UV emission, dust reddening, and AGN versus host-galaxy interpretations, which is central to your research.

Short digest:
The paper asks whether the UV emission in little red dots originates from the host galaxy or from AGN-related processes. It finds that narrow-line Balmer decrements, UV slopes, and break strengths indicate UV emission from dust-reddened galaxy scales, with the most extreme LRDs forming a distinct sub-population. This is highly relevant for interpreting LRD SEDs, AGN contamination, and black-hole/galaxy co-evolution at high redshift.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Look for the Balmer decrement measurements and their comparison to case B recombination
- Check the SED-fitting evidence for dust-attenuated stellar templates versus AGN templates
- Examine the UV slope versus Balmer break strength diagnostic and how it separates LRD sub-populations
- Assess whether the sample selection and NIRSpec gratings could bias the host-galaxy interpretation

## 2. A Standard Ultraviolet Continuum Can Hide Supercritical Accretion in GN-z11

- arXiv ID: `2609.15967`
- Authors: Samik Mitra , Ramananda Santra
- Score: 9
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15967
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.15967
- Subjects: High Energy Astrophysical Phenomena (astro-ph.HE) ; Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (astro-ph.CO); Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
- Reason: This paper directly addresses GN-z11, a high-redshift AGN/black-hole candidate observed with JWST, and questions UV-continuum-based black-hole mass inference. It is highly relevant to early black-hole growth, supercritical accretion, and AGN interpretation at cosmic dawn.

Short digest:
The paper asks whether the standard UV continuum from GN-z11 can hide supercritical accretion and bias black-hole mass estimates. It derives a criterion showing the supercritical transition can lie inside the UV-emitting region, making continuum Eddington masses model-dependent upper bounds and lowering the inferred mass by up to an order of magnitude. This matters for my work because it directly affects how we interpret broad-line and continuum masses of high-redshift black-hole candidates, including LRDs and little red dots.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Look for the closed-form criterion comparing photon-production radius and supercritical transition radius
- Check the composite disk models and how the UV slope changes when the inner flow is replaced
- Examine the additional JWST sources and whether their mass hierarchies support the same conclusion
- Assess assumptions about thin-disk efficiency, viewing angle, and broad-line versus continuum mass systematics

## 5. The New Dark Matter Density Profile from JWST JADES Galaxies

- arXiv ID: `2609.14071`
- Authors: Aritra Sanyal , Farook Rahaman
- Score: 7
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.14071
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.14071
- Subjects: Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA) ; General Relativity and Quantum Cosmology (gr-qc)
- Reason: This paper uses JWST JADES NIRSpec observations of high-redshift galaxies to derive a dark matter density profile, directly involving JWST high-redshift galaxy spectroscopy and galaxy evolution at cosmic noon.

Short digest:
The paper asks what dark matter density profile can be inferred directly from JWST JADES NIRSpec observations of galaxies at 1.5 <= z <= 3.5. It derives a cusp-free, redshift-dependent modified exponential profile with a central density varying by less than 15% across the redshift range, suggesting a universal dark matter core saturation density. This is relevant to my work because it uses JWST NIRSpec spectroscopy of high-redshift galaxies and addresses galaxy evolution at cosmic noon, though it focuses on dark matter rather than AGN or LRDs.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Check how H-alpha velocity dispersions are extracted from NIRSpec G235M/G395M spectra and whether AGN contamination is addressed
- Look for the stacking procedure and whether it accounts for galaxy heterogeneity or AGN
- Check the assumptions in the rotation-curve reconstruction and whether they are valid for high-redshift disk galaxies
- Look for any discussion of baryonic physics and its impact on the inferred dark matter profile

- Score: 6
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.14460

- Subjects: Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
- Reason: This paper studies nitrogen-loud quasars and broad-line region metallicities using DESI spectroscopy, which is relevant to AGN metallicity and broad-line region physics but not directly to JWST high-redshift observations or LRDs.

Short digest:
The paper asks whether nitrogen-loud quasars reflect high overall metallicity, enhanced nitrogen abundance, or both. It finds that N-loud quasars have both high BLR metallicity and enhanced relative nitrogen abundance, with the discrepancy between N V/C IV and (Si IV+O IV])/C IV metallicities increasing at high metallicity. This is relevant to my work on AGN and broad-line regions, though it uses DESI rather than JWST and focuses on z ~ 2-4 quasars rather than cosmic dawn.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Check the metallicity diagnostics and whether they are applicable to JWST NIRSpec AGN spectra at higher redshift
- Look for discussion of broad-line region structure and whether it connects to LRD broad-line AGN
- Check the control sample matching and whether selection biases affect the conclusions
- Look for any implications for nitrogen enrichment in early black-hole growth scenarios
