Title: The JWST early galaxy crisis resolved by a reionization degeneracy
Authors: Zihan Wang , Huanyuan Shan
arXiv ID: 2605.03635
Score: 10
Reason: Directly addresses JWST's early galaxy crisis, reionization, and high-z galaxy formation, core to research interests.
Short digest: Can the JWST early galaxy crisis be resolved by a reionization degeneracy? The product f_esc × f_⋆,0 is constrained, excluding the crisis threshold at 4.5σ. This provides a quantitative framework for interpreting JWST observations of z>10 galaxies and their ionizing escape fractions.
Full-text inspection checklist: Look for the profile-likelihood analysis and confidence intervals on f_esc; Check the UV luminosity function constraints and how f_⋆,0 is independently measured; Examine the predicted f_esc(z) evolution and how JWST Cycle 3-4 observations can distinguish scenarios

Title: Environmental Quenching of High-Redshift Galaxies: Interpreting JWST Observations with Simulations
Authors: Aleyna Döven , Mohammadreza Ayromlou , Cristiano Porciani
arXiv ID: 2605.03008
Score: 8
Reason: Directly relevant: uses simulations to interpret JWST observations of quenched high-redshift galaxies, including low-mass systems at z>3, which is central to my interests.
Short digest: What is the origin of quenching in low-mass high-redshift galaxies observed by JWST? The paper finds that environmental quenching via satellite processes is the primary mechanism, with simulations producing quenched galaxies consistent with JWST data. This matters for my work because it provides a physical interpretation for the quenched population seen in JWST deep fields and connects to galaxy/AGN co-evolution.
Full-text inspection checklist: Check the comparison between simulated quenched fractions and JWST observations at z>3.; Look for the role of ram-pressure stripping and gas depletion in satellite quenching.; Examine the fraction of temporarily quenched galaxies that later merge or rejuvenate.

Authors: James M. Sullivan , Greg L. Bryan , Matthew C. Smith , Jake S. Bennett , Drummond B. Fielding , Bryan A. Terrazas , Sophie Koudmani , Rachel S. Somerville , Michaela Hirschmann
arXiv ID: 2605.03154
Score: 7
Reason: Directly relevant: presents a new AGN feedback model for cosmological simulations, which is central to understanding early black-hole growth and galaxy/AGN co-evolution at high redshift.
Short digest: How to model high specific energy AGN feedback in cosmological simulations? The Arkenstone BH model captures hot, high-energy outflows that suppress star formation by counteracting gas inflow. This matters for my work because it provides a framework for simulating AGN feedback that can be compared to JWST observations of high-redshift AGN and quenched galaxies.
Full-text inspection checklist: Check the implementation of hot, high specific energy feedback and its interaction with cold gas.; Look for the suppression of star formation and the impact on gas inflow from the CGM.; Examine the validation against isolated galaxy simulations and future multiphase extensions.

Title: Built to Rest: The Evolving Star-Forming Main Sequence Requires Episodic Quiescence or Late Assembly
Authors: Lucas C. Kimmig , Jesse van de Sande , Rhea-Silvia Remus , Klaus Dolag , Rebecca Davies , Deanne Fisher
arXiv ID: 2605.02997
Score: 6
Reason: The paper models the star-forming main sequence out to z~6, which is relevant to high-redshift galaxy evolution, though it is analytical rather than observational.
Short digest: How long do galaxies remain on the star-forming main sequence and how do their star formation histories evolve? The analytical model shows that low-mass early-forming progenitors must become very massive if they stay on the main sequence, and that intermediate-mass galaxies require late assembly or quiescent phases. This matters for my work because it provides a framework for interpreting star formation histories of high-redshift galaxies observed by JWST.
Full-text inspection checklist: Check the analytical model assumptions and how they compare to observational SFMS data at z>3.; Look for tracks connecting galaxies from z=6 to z=0 and their implications for quiescent phases.
