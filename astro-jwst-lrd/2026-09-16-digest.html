Title: A Dark-matter Origin of Little Red Dots: Early Seeding and Super-Bondi Accretion
Authors: Hua-Peng Gu , Fangzhou Jiang , Xian Chen , Ran Li , Zi-Xiang Jia
arXiv ID: 2609.16122
Score: 9
Reason: This paper directly addresses little red dots, early SMBH seeding, and super-Bondi accretion, which are central to my interests in LRDs and early black-hole growth. It offers a dark-matter-based formation scenario that could be tested against JWST LRD demographics.
Short digest: The paper asks whether the abundance, early emergence, and extreme black-hole-to-stellar mass ratios of little red dots can be explained without conventional baryonic seeding. It claims that self-interacting dark matter can produce stellar-mass seeds and sustain super-Bondi dark-matter accretion to supermassive scales by the LRD epoch. This matters for my work because it provides an alternative physical framework for interpreting JWST LRD candidates and their host-galaxy underluminosity.
Full-text inspection checklist: Check the assumptions and parameter choices in the SIDM halo-evolution model, especially seeding timescales and concentration requirements; Look for predicted LRD abundance, redshift distribution, and black-hole mass functions that can be compared with JWST observations; Assess whether the model makes testable predictions for stellar hosts, AGN signatures, or multiwavelength counterparts; Check how the paper treats major-merger disruption and whether the required halo conditions are plausible in JWST deep fields

Title: Lyman-$α$ forest 1D flux power spectrum constraints on QSO-assisted reionization models
Authors: Vid Iršič , Avery Meiksin
arXiv ID: 2609.16992
Score: 9
Reason: This paper directly connects JWST-discovered faint QSOs and Little Red Dots to Lyman-alpha forest constraints on QSO-assisted reionization and IGM heating. It is highly relevant to early black-hole growth, LRDs, and high-redshift AGN demographics.
Short digest: The paper asks what Lyman-alpha forest flux power spectrum measurements imply about the spectral properties and IGM heating from faint QSOs and Little Red Dots during HeII reionization. It finds that temperature boosts from HeIII regions improve agreement with data, while a hard-spectrum contribution from faint JWST QSOs with M_UV > -21.6 is excluded at 2 sigma for large boosts. For your work, this provides a valuable external constraint on LRD and faint-AGN contribution to reionization and early black-hole growth.
Full-text inspection checklist: Check the assumed LRD and faint-QSO UV luminosity functions, spectral slopes, and escape fractions used in the reionization model; Look for the Lyman-alpha forest power spectrum data points and redshift range used for constraints; Examine how HeII/HeIII temperature boosts are modeled and whether the conclusions depend on hard versus soft spectra; Assess whether the paper provides any observational predictions or selection implications for JWST LRD/AGN samples

Title: X-ray to Mid-IR Spectral Energy Distributions: A Catalog of X-ray Selected AGNs in the XMM-COSMOS Survey
Authors: Şeyda Şen , Eda Sonbas , Ece Kilerci , Hasan Avdan , Kalvir S. Dhuga , Ersin Göğüş , W. N. Brandt
arXiv ID: 2609.17383
Score: 9
Reason: This paper directly uses JWST/NIRCam imaging to decompose AGN SEDs into accretion-disk, torus, and polar-dust components for X-ray-selected AGN, closely matching my interests in AGN SED fitting, dust attenuation, and JWST observations.
Short digest: The paper asks how the IR-to-X-ray connection in Type 1 AGN can be cleanly assessed by separating accretion-disk, torus, and polar-dust emission. It finds that JWST/NIRCam coverage reduces host-galaxy contamination and reveals a steeper 2 keV-to-NIR luminosity relation at 1 micron due to starlight dilution. This matters for my work because it demonstrates how JWST NIR imaging improves AGN SED decomposition and host contamination control in high-redshift AGN studies.
Full-text inspection checklist: Look for the SED-fitting diagnostics that separate AGN torus, polar dust, and stellar host contributions; Check whether the JWST/NIRCam subset is large enough to support the claimed steeper relation; Examine the 1 micron luminosity relation plots and host-galaxy contamination corrections; Check whether the sample includes high-redshift or primarily low-redshift Type 1 AGN

Title: Jet Feedback and the Self-Regulated Growth of Black Holes Embedded in AGN Disks
Authors: Marguerite Epstein-Martin , Kung-Yi Su , Zoltan Haiman , Rosalba Perna
arXiv ID: 2609.16167
Score: 6
Reason: This paper studies black-hole growth and jet feedback in AGN disks, which connects to early SMBH growth and AGN feedback physics. It is theoretical/simulation-based rather than JWST observational, but relevant to black-hole seeding and self-regulated growth.
Short digest: The paper asks whether jet feedback from stellar-mass black holes embedded in AGN disks can self-regulate their growth and remain confined within the disk. It finds that jet-inflated cocoons stall near the Bondi radius and limit growth to within an order of magnitude of the Eddington rate, though inner-disk accretion is set by tidal shear. For my work, it provides theoretical context for rapid black-hole growth and feedback in AGN environments, which may inform interpretations of high-z AGN and LRD accretion scenarios.
Full-text inspection checklist: Check the simulation setup, cooling regimes, and assumptions about jet mass loading and velocity; Look for predicted accretion-rate histories and Eddington ratios relevant to high-z SMBH growth; Assess whether the results can be scaled to supermassive black holes or high-redshift AGN disks; Check the limitations for inner-disk regions and whether breakout or runaway growth remains possible

Title: Disentangling Stellar Mass and Environmental Effects on eROSITA AGN Activity using multi-wavelength data from GAMA, WISE, GALEX, and DESI Legacy Survey
Authors: Navya Saraswat , Shadab Alam , Subhabrata Majumdar
arXiv ID: 2609.16333
Score: 6
Reason: This paper studies AGN activity, environment, and stellar mass using multiwavelength surveys, which is relevant to AGN selection, classification, and galaxy/AGN co-evolution. It is not JWST high-z focused, but it is closely related to AGN diagnostics and survey methodology.
Short digest: The paper asks whether AGN activity depends on environment independently of stellar mass. It finds that X-ray-selected AGNs show a clear environmental trend, while WISE-selected and broad-line AGNs do not, and that AGN fraction versus SFR varies strongly with environment and selection method. For my work, it is useful for understanding AGN selection biases and how different AGN tracers may affect high-z AGN and LRD classification.
Full-text inspection checklist: Check the definitions of X-ray, WISE, and broad-line AGN samples and their selection biases; Look for the δ_rank overdensity metric and how it is computed within stellar-mass bins; Assess whether the environmental trends could be tested at high redshift with JWST surveys; Check for discussion of AGN fraction versus SFR and Eddington ratio across environments

arXiv ID: 2609.16951
Score: 6
Reason: This paper studies FIR-bright quasars at 1 < z < 4 with ALMA and multi-wavelength SED fitting, addressing SMBH-host co-evolution, AGN-driven winds, and obscured-to-unobscured transitions, which connects to my interests in AGN co-evolution and dust-reddened AGN, though it is not JWST or cosmic-dawn.
Short digest: The paper asks what the star-formation and UV/optical properties of FIR-bright quasars reveal about SMBH-host co-evolution. It finds extreme starbursts with SFRs of 500-3000 solar masses per year, weak starburst-accretion coupling, and evidence for AGN-driven winds, placing these sources in a transitional blowout phase. This is relevant to my work on AGN feedback, obscured AGN, and galaxy/AGN co-evolution, though at lower redshift than my JWST focus.
