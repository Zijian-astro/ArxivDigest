Short digest:
The central question is how gas-phase metallicity evolves with stellar mass and redshift at z=1-10. The main result is a canonical MZR with decreasing metallicity at higher redshift, and the identification of 50 EMPG candidates including two Little Red Dots with broad Hα. This matters for my work because it provides new calibrations for metallicity, identifies EMPGs that may host early black holes, and includes LRDs that are key targets for studying AGN at cosmic dawn.

- Examine the EMPG candidates and their sSFR trends, which may indicate stochastic star formation or AGN feedback.
- Look for the stacking methodology and how auroral line detections are used.

## 2. Spiral arms across cosmic time: JWST measurements of the pitch angles of spiral galaxies at $z<3.5$

- arXiv ID: `2606.11315`
- Authors: Vicki Kuhn , Yicheng Guo , Sophie Rentschler , Maxmillian Castillo , Gourab Nandi , Ellie Dugdale , Tsinat Mitiku
- Score: 9
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11315
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.11315
- Subjects: Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
- Reason: This paper uses JWST data (CEERS and JADES) to study spiral galaxy pitch angles at z<3.5, directly relevant to high-redshift galaxy morphology and evolution.

Short digest:
The central question is how spiral arm pitch angles evolve with cosmic time. The main result is no significant redshift evolution overall, but massive systems show winding up with time, and pitch angle correlates with galaxy properties only at z<1.25. This matters for my work because it provides a morphological perspective on galaxy evolution at high redshift using JWST, and the sample includes massive galaxies that may host AGN.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Look for the pitch angle measurement method (SpArcFiRe) and its reliability at high redshift.
- Check whether AGN hosts or little red dots are identified in the sample and if their pitch angles differ.
- Examine the redshift bins and how the transition at z~1 is established.
- Look for figures showing pitch angle vs. stellar mass, sSFR, and redshift.

Central question: What role does turbulence play in regulating star formation in z~3 dusty galaxies? Why it matters: Provides high-resolution constraints on star formation scaling relations and turbulence in high-redshift galaxies, directly applicable to JWST studies of early galaxy evolution.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Examine CO and 870μm continuum maps for spatial offsets between gas and dust
- Check kinematic modeling (V_max/σ) and evidence for rotation vs. mergers
- Look for star formation scaling relations on kpc scales and comparison to turbulence models

The central question is how the size-mass relation of quiescent galaxies evolves at z>3. The main result is that size decreases with redshift and wavelength, and stellar mass and redshift alone predict size with large scatter. ## 5. pop-cosmos: Disentangling galaxy properties from observables using data-driven approaches

- arXiv ID: `2606.11308`
- Authors: Benedict Van den Bussche , Sinan Deger , Hiranya V. Peiris , Stephen Thorp , Daniel J. Mortlock , Boris Leistedt , Anik Halder , Madalina N. Tudorache , Gurjeet Jagwani
- Score: 7
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11308
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.11308
- Subjects: Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA) ; Instrumentation and Methods for Astrophysics (astro-ph.IM)
- Reason: This paper uses a VAE to disentangle galaxy properties from SEDs, which is directly relevant to SED fitting, source classification, and understanding degeneracies in galaxy/AGN observations.

Short digest:
The central question is how many independent degrees of freedom the rest-frame optical SED contains. The main result is that five dimensions suffice: stellar mass, recent star formation, dust, and two ionization state parameters. This matters for my work because it provides a data-driven framework to break degeneracies in SED fitting of high-redshift galaxies and AGN.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Look for how the VAE latent dimensions correlate with specific spectral features (e.g., emission lines, continuum breaks).
- Check whether the model includes AGN components or is limited to pure stellar populations.
- Examine the mutual information analysis that justifies the five-dimensional claim.

## 7. Interpretable Neural Marked Statistics for Cosmological Inference

- arXiv ID: `2606.11295`

- Score: 5
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11295
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.11295
- Subjects: Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (astro-ph.CO) ; Machine Learning (cs.LG)
- Reason: This paper uses machine learning (neural marking) for cosmological inference, which is relevant to AI-for-astronomy workflows, but the focus is on cosmology (σ8, Ωm) rather than high-redshift galaxies or AGN.

Short digest:
The central question is how to recover cosmological information beyond the power spectrum using marked statistics. The main result is that a neural marking scheme improves constraints on σ8 and Ωm by factors of 2.9 and 1.8. This matters for my work as it demonstrates an AI method for parameter inference that could be adapted to galaxy/AGN SED fitting.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Check how the neural marks are physically interpreted and whether the method can be applied to galaxy/AGN data.
- Look for validation tests on simulated data that show robustness to systematics.
