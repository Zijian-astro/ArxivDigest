## 1. A Massive Galaxy at the Edge of Feedback-Free Efficiency

- arXiv ID: `2607.29589`
- Authors: K. Aditya , Kartick C. Sarkar
- Score: 9
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29589
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.29589
- Subjects: Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
- Reason: Directly studies a spectroscopically confirmed high-redshift galaxy at z=9.31 with stellar mass, SFE, and compact morphology, matching interests in JWST high-z galaxies and early galaxy formation. Uses UNCOVER data, a priority survey.

Short digest:
The paper asks whether a z=9.31 galaxy can reach the maximum baryon conversion efficiency predicted by the feedback-free starburst scenario. It finds the star-formation efficiency is 20-60%, 2-6 times above empirical predictions, with properties consistent with FFB conditions. This matters for my work as it provides a concrete observational test of early galaxy formation efficiency and stellar mass assembly at cosmic dawn.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Check how SFE is derived and whether it accounts for dust attenuation and AGN contamination
- Look for the spectroscopic diagnostics confirming redshift and stellar properties
- Examine the compactness and stellar age measurements for consistency with FFB predictions

## 2. Determining Total Infrared Luminosities from Submm Measurements of High Redshift Galaxies

- arXiv ID: `2607.28875`
- Authors: Maria Emilia De Rossi , George H. Rieke , Christina C. Williams , Volker Bromm , Jianwei Lyu , Meredith A. Stone , Yang Sun , Christopher N. A. Willmer , Yongda Zhu
- Score: 8
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28875
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.28875
- Subjects: Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
- Reason: This paper directly addresses SED fitting and total infrared luminosity corrections for high-redshift galaxies, which is crucial for estimating star formation rates in dusty environments relevant to my work on JWST high-z galaxies.

Short digest:
The central question is how to correct submm-based infrared luminosities for missing mid-IR emission in high-z galaxies. The main result is a correction factor of ~1.6-1.7, larger for z>4 luminous galaxies. This matters because accurate SFR estimates are essential for studying galaxy evolution and AGN host properties.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Look for the simulation setup and how luminosity density controls SED shape.
- Check the derivation of correction factors and their dependence on redshift and luminosity.
- Verify whether the corrections are validated against observed high-z SEDs.
