- Reason: This is a JWST/HST/Spitzer study of a nuclear transient in a z=7.19 red quasar, directly hitting my interests in high-redshift AGN, early black-hole growth, broad-line regions, dust, and galaxy/AGN co-evolution. It is highly relevant to little red dots, red quasars, and time-domain AGN studies at cosmic dawn.

Short digest:
The paper asks what caused a long-lived nuclear transient in the z=7.19 red quasar GNz7q. It finds that the multi-band evolution is best explained by a tidal disruption event around a roughly 3e7 solar-mass black hole, with a dust echo and a transient broad-line region. For my work it matters because it provides a time-domain view of episodic black-hole fueling and dusty nuclear environments at cosmic dawn, with direct implications for interpreting red quasars, LRDs, and broad-line AGN variability.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Look for the JWST/NIRSpec broad Balmer-line analysis and how it establishes a transient, non-virialized broad-line region.
- Check the panchromatic light-curve modeling, including the t^-5/3 decline and dust-echo interpretation.
- Assess the multiwavelength variability evidence across HST, Spitzer, and JWST and how it rules out superluminous supernovae and stochastic quasar variability.
- Check the black-hole mass, host-galaxy properties, and whether the event informs LRD or red-quasar classification.

For my work it matters because it provides an empirical SED template for vetting high-redshift dropout candidates and highlights transient contamination in high-z galaxy searches.

## 3. TRICEPS. I. Tracing cold gas, dust, and stars at sub-kpc scales in massive star-forming galaxies at z = 4-5

- Subjects: Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA) ; Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (astro-ph.CO)
- Reason: This is a multiwavelength survey of massive star-forming galaxies at z=4-5 with ALMA and JWST/NIRCam/MIRI, directly relevant to high-redshift galaxy formation, dust, gas, and stellar mass distributions. It connects to my interests in JWST deep-field galaxy studies and galaxy/AGN co-evolution at cosmic dawn.

Short digest:
The paper asks how cold gas, dust, and stars are distributed at sub-kpc scales in massive star-forming galaxies at z=4-5. It introduces the TRICEPS survey and finds regular rotation ubiquitous in massive star-forming galaxies when the Universe was less than about 1.5 Gyr old, with disturbed kinematics in interacting pairs. For my work it matters because it provides resolved gas/dust/stellar constraints at cosmic dawn and a benchmark for interpreting JWST morphologies, SEDs, and possible AGN contamination in massive high-redshift systems.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Look for the JWST/NIRCam and MIRI imaging analysis, including how stellar mass and star-formation distributions are derived.
- Check the [CII] and dust-continuum morphology/kinematics diagnostics and how they compare with stellar distributions.
- Assess whether any targets show AGN-like or compact red components that could contaminate the star-forming interpretation.
- Check the sample selection and whether it overlaps with JWST deep fields such as CEERS, JADES, or COSMOS-Web.

Short digest:

For my work it matters as a practical tool for finding and vetting figures relevant to JWST high-redshift galaxies, AGN, and LRD literature, and as a model for LLM-assisted literature analysis.

- Assess whether the figure-level search can be queried for specific JWST diagnostics such as SEDs, spectra, or color-color plots.
- Check whether the tool is openly available and how it could integrate into a literature-review workflow.

## 5. SQuIGG$\vec{L}$E: Spatially resolved NIRSpec Pa$α$ reveals that the most CO-luminous, $z\sim0.7$ post-starburst galaxies exhibit falling---but still active---star formation behind optically thick dust

- Authors: David J. Setton , Jenny E. Greene , Rachel Bezanson , Katherine A. Suess , Justin S. Spilker , Vincenzo R. D'Onofrio , Elia Cenci , Robert Feldmann , Alex J. Geiger , Andy D. Goulding , Mariska Kriek , Anika Kumar , Yuanze Luo , Desika Narayanan , Margaret E. Verrico , Pengpei Zhu
- Score: 6
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31307
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.31307
- Subjects: Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
- Reason: Uses JWST/NIRSpec IFU spectroscopy to study dust-obscured star formation and possible AGN/shock ionization in massive post-starburst galaxies. Relevant to AGN contamination, dust attenuation, and spatially resolved SED/emission-line diagnostics, though at z~0.7 rather than cosmic dawn.

Short digest:
The paper asks what the true instantaneous star formation rates are in the most CO-luminous z~0.7 post-starburst galaxies, where rest-UV/optical data suggest quiescence. Spatially resolved NIRSpec Pa-alpha/Br-gamma/Br-beta yields SFRs of 27-80 Msun/yr, with ~30-40% of emission possibly from AGN or shocks and significant dust obscuration (A_Pa-alpha~1-2). It matters for my work because it demonstrates how JWST IFU emission-line mapping can separate obscured star formation from AGN/shock ionization and correct rest-optical-based classifications.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Check the [FeII]/Pa-alpha diagnostic thresholds and spaxel maps used to argue for AGN or shock ionization versus star formation
- Look for the dust attenuation derivation from Pa-alpha/Br-gamma/Br-beta ratios and whether a nebular attenuation curve is assumed
- Check how the SFRs compare to SED-fit and spectrophotometric values, and whether AGN contributions were included in the SED fits
- Look for spatially resolved maps showing where the obscured star formation is located relative to the rest-optical emission

## 7. SQuIGG$\vec{L}$E: Ubiquitous Cool Gas Outflows with Large Mass-Loading Factors in $z\sim0.7$ Post-starburst Galaxies

- arXiv ID: `2609.31308`
- Authors: Yuanze Luo , Justin S. Spilker , David J. Setton , Timothy Heckman , Rachel Bezanson , Vincenzo R. D'Onofrio , Alex Geiger , Andy D. Goulding , Jenny E. Greene , Kate Rowlands , Katherine A. Suess , Elizabeth Taylor , Margaret E. Verrico , Andrew Weldon , Vivienne Wild , Pengpei Zhu
- Score: 5
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31308
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.31308
- Subjects: Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
- Reason: Studies cool gas outflows traced by Fe II and Mg II absorption in z~0.5-0.9 post-starburst galaxies, connecting outflows to rapid quenching and possible AGN activity. Relevant to quenching and galaxy/AGN co-evolution but at intermediate redshift and using ground-based stacked spectra rather than JWST.

Short digest:
The paper asks whether cool gas outflows are ubiquitous in intermediate-redshift post-starburst galaxies and how they relate to quenching timescales. Stacked spectra show blueshifted Fe II and Mg II absorption in nearly all bins, with large mass-loading factors (1-120) and outflow velocities declining with time since quenching, suggesting relic outflows from earlier starburst or AGN activity. It matters for my work as a low-redshift analog for outflow-driven quenching that may inform interpretation of high-redshift JWST outflows and AGN feedback.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Check the stacking methodology and how outflow velocities and mass-loading factors are derived from Fe II/Mg II absorption
- Look for evidence distinguishing starburst-driven versus AGN-driven outflow energetics
- Check whether the outflow detection significance and velocity measurements account for stellar absorption contamination
- Look for comparisons to high-redshift JWST outflow samples and quenching timescale arguments
