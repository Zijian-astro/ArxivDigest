- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.15901

- Subjects: Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
- Reason: Directly relevant: a large JWST/NIRSpec PRISM + NIRCam spectrophotometric catalogue of z>2 galaxies with stellar masses, SFRs, and dust attenuation, exactly the SED-fitting and physical-property framework I use for high-redshift galaxies and AGN hosts.

Short digest:
The paper asks how star formation, stellar mass, and dust build up from cosmic noon to cosmic dawn, using joint spectrophotometric modelling of ~12,000 z>2 galaxies. It releases a BAGPIPES-based catalogue of stellar masses, SFRs on multiple timescales, and Av, showing that the dustiest galaxies are massive, strongly star-forming, and that attenuation-curve slope depends on Av and sSFR. This matters because it provides a large, spectroscopically informed reference sample and methodology for interpreting my JWST high-redshift and LRD/AGN-host SED fits.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Look for the SED-fitting setup, priors, and how AGN contamination is (or is not) treated in the BAGPIPES runs
- Check the Av and attenuation-curve slope diagnostics and whether they separate dusty star-forming galaxies from possible AGN
- Examine the ugi colour-colour diagrams and where quiescent/dusty/recently quenched galaxies fall
- Check the catalogue release details and selection function for reuse in my own samples

## 4. Anisotropic Radiation in Super-Eddington Active Galactic Nuclei: The Responsivity of Broad-Line Regions to Continuum Variability

- arXiv ID: `2610.15255`
- Authors: Feng-Na Fang , Pu Du , Jian-Min Wang
- Score: 8
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.15255
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.15255
- Subjects: Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
- Reason: This paper directly addresses anisotropic ionizing radiation in super-Eddington AGN and explicitly discusses implications for JWST little red dots, connecting BLR reverberation physics to LRD interpretation.

Short digest:
The paper asks whether the ionizing radiation incident on broad-line region clouds in super-Eddington AGN is anisotropic relative to the observer's line of sight. It finds that H-beta responsivity in super-Eddington AGN implies ionizing fluxes differing significantly from the line-of-sight continuum, unlike sub-Eddington AGN. This matters for my work because it offers a physical mechanism that could explain the compact broad-line and reddened AGN signatures seen in JWST little red dots.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Look for the quantitative comparison between inferred BLR ionizing flux and observed line-of-sight continuum in super- versus sub-Eddington AGN
- Check the discussion connecting anisotropic ionizing radiation to JWST little red dots and whether it predicts specific emission-line ratios or continuum shapes
- Look for the sample selection and reverberation-lag measurements used to derive H-beta responsivity
- Check whether the paper provides diagnostics that could distinguish anisotropic BLR illumination from dust reddening in LRD SEDs

Short digest:
The paper asks whether mergers or in-situ star formation dominate stellar mass growth at cosmic dawn. It finds that mergers contribute less than 10% of stellar mass density evolution at z>~5, with in-situ star formation dominating. This matters for my work because it provides theoretical context for interpreting JWST-observed high-redshift galaxy assembly and AGN co-evolution.

Full-text inspection checklist:

- Check the ex-situ stellar mass fractions across models and their implications for high-z galaxy SEDs
- Examine the treatment of AGN feedback and its effect on quenching at high redshift
- Assess whether the simulations cover the mass and redshift range relevant to LRDs and compact red sources

- Reason: It uses unsupervised deep learning and anomaly detection on AGN light curves, relevant to AI-for-astronomy and AGN variability, though it is low-redshift and not JWST-based.
