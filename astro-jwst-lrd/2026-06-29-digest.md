Short digest:
This paper presents JWST/NIRSpec spectroscopy of a metal-poor dwarf galaxy with extreme mid-IR colors, finding no AGN signatures. It shows that compact nuclear starbursts can mimic AGN in WISE diagnostics. This is crucial for distinguishing true AGN from starbursts in high-z compact red sources like LRDs.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Look for the NIRSpec line detections and limits on coronal lines (e.g., [Ne V], [O IV]).
- Check the photoionization models and why low metallicity cannot explain the weak high-ionization lines.
- Examine the spatial extent of the nuclear emission and PAH features.
- See if the paper provides diagnostic diagrams or criteria to separate starbursts from AGN in JWST data.

- Reason: Directly addresses supermassive star formation, heavy black hole seeds, and connects to Little Red Dot galaxies observed by JWST.

Short digest:
This paper uses SEEDZ simulations to study how rapid galaxy assembly leads to supermassive stars and heavy black hole seeds. It finds that only a tiny fraction of SMSs need be visible to JWST to explain the observed LRD population. This is highly relevant because it directly links theoretical seed formation to JWST's LRD observations.

- Examine the spatial resolution and whether the 10 pc inflow criterion is robust.
- See if the paper discusses observational discriminants between SMS-seeded black holes and other LRD models.

- Reason: Directly discusses curvaton non-Gaussianity as a scenario for supermassive black hole seeds and explicitly mentions 'Little Red Dots observed by JWST'.

Short digest:
Can curvaton non-Gaussianity produce supermassive black hole seeds? Strongly positive non-Gaussianity allows seeds at peak amplitudes ~10^-5, compatible with COBE/FIRAS bounds. This provides a new primordial scenario for JWST Little Red Dots, directly relevant to early black-hole growth studies.

- Check the non-Gaussian mapping F(zeta_G) for each potential
- Look for PBH mass function predictions and comparison with LRD properties
- Verify COBE/FIRAS mu-distortion constraints on peak amplitude

## 5. Too shy to spin? Cosmic wallflowers as proto-globular clusters

- arXiv ID: `2606.27426`
- Authors: Floor van Donkelaar , Lucio Mayer , Pedro R. Capelo
- Score: 5
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27426
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.27426
- Subjects: Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA) ; High Energy Astrophysical Phenomena (astro-ph.HE)
- Reason: Simulation of high-redshift star clusters at z~7.6, relevant to early galaxy formation and black hole seeds.

Short digest:
This simulation study investigates rotational properties of star-forming clusters at z~7.6, distinguishing disc-born and filament-born 'cosmic wallflowers'. It finds that weakly rotating, gas-rich wallflowers are proto-globular cluster candidates, while denser ones may link to massive black hole seeds. This matters because it connects early cluster dynamics to black hole seed formation, relevant to JWST studies of compact sources.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Examine the criteria for identifying proto-globular clusters vs. black hole seed candidates.
- Look for predictions of observable signatures (e.g., compactness, metallicity) that JWST could test.
- Check the simulation resolution and whether it resolves sub-pc scales relevant for seed formation.
