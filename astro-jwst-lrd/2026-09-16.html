A Dark-matter Origin of Little Red Dots: Early Seeding and Super-Bondi Accretion

Hua-Peng Gu , Fangzhou Jiang , Xian Chen , Ran Li , Zi-Xiang Jia

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

The paper asks whether the abundance, early emergence, and extreme black-hole-to-stellar mass ratios of little red dots can be explained without conventional baryonic seeding. It claims that self-interacting dark matter can produce stellar-mass seeds and sustain super-Bondi dark-matter accretion to supermassive scales by the LRD epoch. This matters for my work because it provides an alternative physical framework for interpreting JWST LRD candidates and their host-galaxy underluminosity.

Reason

This paper directly addresses little red dots, early SMBH seeding, and super-Bondi accretion, which are central to my interests in LRDs and early black-hole growth. It offers a dark-matter-based formation scenario that could be tested against JWST LRD demographics.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Check the assumptions and parameter choices in the SIDM halo-evolution model, especially seeding timescales and concentration requirements
  • Look for predicted LRD abundance, redshift distribution, and black-hole mass functions that can be compared with JWST observations
  • Assess whether the model makes testable predictions for stellar hosts, AGN signatures, or multiwavelength counterparts
  • Check how the paper treats major-merger disruption and whether the required halo conditions are plausible in JWST deep fields
Abstract

The "Little red dots" (LRDs) are a population of accreting supermassive black holes (SMBHs) in the early Universe which often exhibit undermassive or even undetectable stellar hosts. Their early emergence, high space density, and extremely large black-hole-to-stellar mass ratios pose a serious challenge to conventional seeding scenarios that rely on baryon for both the formation and growth of black holes. Here we demonstrate that the above anomalies can be naturally resolved if dark matter is self-interacting. We apply a fully relativistic, non-equilibrium halo-evolution model, first developed in our earlier work, to trace the complete gravothermal evolution of self-interacting dark matter (SIDM) halos, from the initial collapse into BH seeds to the subsequent accretion of dark matter. We find that in highly concentrated halos assembled before reionization, gravothermal collapse efficiently produces stellar-mass black-hole seeds within a few hundred million years. Remarkably, and contrary to standard expectations for dark-matter accretion, heat conduction in SIDM then sustains a prolonged super-Bondi inflow that drives these seeds to supermassive scale by the LRD epoch, without baryonic assistance. The halo conditions required for completing these processes, together with the probability of avoiding major mergers that disrupt gravothermal evolution, result in an SMBH population consistent with the observed abundance and redshift distribution of LRDs. Our findings establish a pathway in which SMBHs are seeded and assembled primarily from dark matter, well before substantial galaxies form around them, thereby offering both a compelling physical explanation for LRDs and a new observational probe of dark-matter microphysics.

Lyman-$α$ forest 1D flux power spectrum constraints on QSO-assisted reionization models

Vid Iršič , Avery Meiksin

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

The paper asks what Lyman-alpha forest flux power spectrum measurements imply about the spectral properties and IGM heating from faint QSOs and Little Red Dots during HeII reionization. It finds that temperature boosts from HeIII regions improve agreement with data, while a hard-spectrum contribution from faint JWST QSOs with M_UV > -21.6 is excluded at 2 sigma for large boosts. For your work, this provides a valuable external constraint on LRD and faint-AGN contribution to reionization and early black-hole growth.

Reason

This paper directly connects JWST-discovered faint QSOs and Little Red Dots to Lyman-alpha forest constraints on QSO-assisted reionization and IGM heating. It is highly relevant to early black-hole growth, LRDs, and high-redshift AGN demographics.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Check the assumed LRD and faint-QSO UV luminosity functions, spectral slopes, and escape fractions used in the reionization model
  • Look for the Lyman-alpha forest power spectrum data points and redshift range used for constraints
  • Examine how HeII/HeIII temperature boosts are modeled and whether the conclusions depend on hard versus soft spectra
  • Assess whether the paper provides any observational predictions or selection implications for JWST LRD/AGN samples
Abstract

Recent JWST observations have revealed a large population of faint Quasi-Stellar Objects (QSOs) at redshifts 4 < z < 6, including a highly reddened sub-population of Little Red Dots. We exploit the sensitivity of the Lyman-$\alpha$ forest 1D flux power spectrum to thermal fluctuations in the Intergalactic Medium (IGM) to place statistical constraints on the spectral properties of the QSOs. By post-processing simulated Lyman-$\alpha$ forest spectra from the Sherwood-Relics suite of Lyman-$\alpha$ forest simulations with added HeII reionization by QSOs, in conjunction with published precision measurements of the Lyman-$\alpha$ forest 1D flux power spectrum between 4.2 < z < 5.0, we find that the addition of temperature boosts of the IGM within the HeIII regions improves agreement with the measured power spectra. While the contribution of the Little Red Dot population to HeII reionization is consistent with both a mild temperature boost of $\Delta T_b=1\times10^4$ K for soft spectra QSOs and $\Delta T_b=2\times10^4$ K for hard spectra QSOs, a contribution from the larger population of faint QSOs found by JWST having $M_{\rm UV} > -21.6$ is excluded at the 2$\sigma$ level if their spectra are sufficiently hard to boost the IGM temperature in HeIII regions by $\Delta T_b=2\times10^4$ K or greater.

X-ray to Mid-IR Spectral Energy Distributions: A Catalog of X-ray Selected AGNs in the XMM-COSMOS Survey

Şeyda Şen , Eda Sonbas , Ece Kilerci , Hasan Avdan , Kalvir S. Dhuga , Ersin Göğüş , W. N. Brandt

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

The paper asks how the IR-to-X-ray connection in Type 1 AGN can be cleanly assessed by separating accretion-disk, torus, and polar-dust emission. It finds that JWST/NIRCam coverage reduces host-galaxy contamination and reveals a steeper 2 keV-to-NIR luminosity relation at 1 micron due to starlight dilution. This matters for my work because it demonstrates how JWST NIR imaging improves AGN SED decomposition and host contamination control in high-redshift AGN studies.

Reason

This paper directly uses JWST/NIRCam imaging to decompose AGN SEDs into accretion-disk, torus, and polar-dust components for X-ray-selected AGN, closely matching my interests in AGN SED fitting, dust attenuation, and JWST observations.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Look for the SED-fitting diagnostics that separate AGN torus, polar dust, and stellar host contributions
  • Check whether the JWST/NIRCam subset is large enough to support the claimed steeper relation
  • Examine the 1 micron luminosity relation plots and host-galaxy contamination corrections
  • Check whether the sample includes high-redshift or primarily low-redshift Type 1 AGN
Abstract

We present a component-resolved analysis of the IR--X-ray connection in a sample of 104 spectroscopically confirmed Type~1 AGN. By combining \xmm, \swift/UVOT, and new high--angular resolution \jwst/NIRCam imaging for a subset of the sources, we constructed detailed SEDs in which the IR emission was explicitly decomposed into accretion-disk, torus, and polar-dust components. This approach enables us to isolate the intrinsic AGN-IR luminosities with minimal host-galaxy contamination, providing a clean assessment of the physical relationship between the accretion flow, circumnuclear dust, and the X-ray corona. By incorporating JWST/NIRCam observations into our SED decomposition, we significantly reduce host-galaxy contamination and isolate the nuclear infrared emission with greater fidelity, particularly at near-infrared (NIR) wavelengths. This improvement is most apparent at 1 $\mu\mathrm{m}$, where sources with JWST coverage exhibit a markedly steeper relation between $\nu$L$_{\nu}$ (2 keV) and the NIR luminosity than those without JWST data. This behavior is naturally explained by starlight dilution, whereby host-galaxy emission dominates lower-resolution measurements.

Cosmic Noon Galaxies in the Hubble Ultra Deep Field with MIRI Wide-Field Slitless Spectroscopy

S. Kendrew , G. Östlin , L. Colina , P. Rinaldi , J. Álvarez-Márquez , L. A. Boogaard , J. Melinder , J. P. Pye , A. Alonso Herrero , K. I. Caputi , A. Crespo Gómez , M. Garcia Marín , S. Gillman , S. Harish , E. Iani , D. Langeroodi , A. Labiano , P. G. Pérez-González , P. van der Werf , F. Walter , G. Wright , T. Greve , M. Güdel , T. Henning , P.-O. Lagage , T. P. Ray , B. Vandenbussche , E. van Dishoeck , S. Alberts , R. Fernandez Aranda , A. Petric , I. Shivaei

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

The paper asks how well MIRI wide-field slitless spectroscopy can characterize Cosmic Noon galaxies and the 3.3 μm PAH feature. It obtains spectra of 47 galaxies with confirmed redshifts up to z=3.712 and finds that PAH luminosities follow established correlations with IR luminosity and SED-derived star formation rates. For my work, it is useful for understanding MIRI WFSS capabilities, dust-obscured star formation diagnostics, and how to interpret mid-IR emission in high-z galaxy samples.

Reason

This paper uses JWST MIRI WFSS to study Cosmic Noon galaxies and PAH emission, which is relevant to JWST spectroscopy, dust attenuation, star formation, and galaxy evolution. It is not about LRDs or AGN specifically, but it is closely related to JWST survey methodology and SED/emission-line diagnostics.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Check the MIRI WFSS data reduction, calibration uncertainties, and redshift confirmation methodology
  • Look for PAH line measurements and correlations with IR luminosity and SFR
  • Assess whether the sample or methods can be extended to AGN or LRD hosts
  • Check for discussion of source confusion, blending, or AGN contamination in the WFSS spectra
Abstract

We present results from a survey of the Hubble Ultra-Deep Field using the Wide-Field Slitless Spectroscopic (WFSS) capability of the Mid-Infrared Instrument (MIRI) on JWST, demonstrating the capabilities of this new mode. We describe the data reduction and calibration methodology, and estimate calibration uncertainties. From our observations we obtain spectra of 47 galaxies with confirmed spectroscopic redshifts, with a maximum z_spec of 3.712. In the final sample we target in particular the 3.3 um Polycyclic Aromatic Hydrocarbon (PAH) feature, which has recently gathered interest as a star formation rate indicator and diagnostic for the dust grain size distribution in star forming galaxies from the local Universe to intermediate redshifts. The feature falls into the WFSS wavelength region for redshifts 0.67 to 3.1 - providing full coverage of the peak star formation ``Cosmic Noon'' era (1 < z < 3) and connecting dust properties in this critical galaxy evolution period with local-Universe and low-redshift observations. Using the galaxies in our sample in this redshift regime, we test correlations identified in lower-redshift samples in the near-infrared or targeted programs in the mid-infrared, finding the WFSS spectra, even with large calibration uncertainties, show good agreement with complementary samples. The 3.3 um PAH luminosities follow previously established correlations with total IR luminosity and SED-derived star formation rates, confirming this feature's power as tracer of dust-obscured star formation. Our work illustrate the potential of the MIRI WFSS mode for studies of Cosmic Noon-era galaxies in particular in an observationally efficient way.

Jet Feedback and the Self-Regulated Growth of Black Holes Embedded in AGN Disks

Marguerite Epstein-Martin , Kung-Yi Su , Zoltan Haiman , Rosalba Perna

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

The paper asks whether jet feedback from stellar-mass black holes embedded in AGN disks can self-regulate their growth and remain confined within the disk. It finds that jet-inflated cocoons stall near the Bondi radius and limit growth to within an order of magnitude of the Eddington rate, though inner-disk accretion is set by tidal shear. For my work, it provides theoretical context for rapid black-hole growth and feedback in AGN environments, which may inform interpretations of high-z AGN and LRD accretion scenarios.

Reason

This paper studies black-hole growth and jet feedback in AGN disks, which connects to early SMBH growth and AGN feedback physics. It is theoretical/simulation-based rather than JWST observational, but relevant to black-hole seeding and self-regulated growth.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Check the simulation setup, cooling regimes, and assumptions about jet mass loading and velocity
  • Look for predicted accretion-rate histories and Eddington ratios relevant to high-z SMBH growth
  • Assess whether the results can be scaled to supermassive black holes or high-redshift AGN disks
  • Check the limitations for inner-disk regions and whether breakout or runaway growth remains possible
Abstract

Accretion disks in active galactic nuclei (AGN) are expected to host embedded stellar-mass black holes (BHs) whose gas capture rates can be highly super-Eddington. Without feedback, they would rapidly grow and deplete the disk, but at such high feeding rates, radiation is advected inward with the flow. We test whether the resulting accretion-powered jet and its shocked cocoon can (i) self-regulate the BH's growth rate and (ii) break out of the AGN disk. Using GIZMO, we perform three-dimensional simulations of a $10\,{\rm M}_\odot$ BH, embedded in dense AGN-disk gas, launching a particle-spawned jet whose mass flux scales with the accretion rate. We explore a range of jet velocities, ambient temperatures and densities, and three cooling regimes. We find that the jet-inflated cocoon stalls near the Bondi radius and maintains a modest aspect ratio and the self-regulated mass flux is determined by momentum balance at the Bondi radius. Cooling controls the mode of self-regulated accretion, from large-amplitude oscillation cycles at low metallicity to weak, quasi-steady modulation at solar metallicity. Scaling our results to outer disk conditions implies that mechanical feedback limits BH growth to within an order of magnitude of the Eddington rate -- largely preventing the overgrowth problem. Over the same disk region, the cocoon remains confined within the disk. In the inner disk, accretion onto the embedded BHs is limited by tidal shear rather than the Bondi radius, and our simulations no longer apply. Whether runaway growth and cocoon breakout remains possible in these inner regions needs further study.

Disentangling Stellar Mass and Environmental Effects on eROSITA AGN Activity using multi-wavelength data from GAMA, WISE, GALEX, and DESI Legacy Survey

Navya Saraswat , Shadab Alam , Subhabrata Majumdar

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

The paper asks whether AGN activity depends on environment independently of stellar mass. It finds that X-ray-selected AGNs show a clear environmental trend, while WISE-selected and broad-line AGNs do not, and that AGN fraction versus SFR varies strongly with environment and selection method. For my work, it is useful for understanding AGN selection biases and how different AGN tracers may affect high-z AGN and LRD classification.

Reason

This paper studies AGN activity, environment, and stellar mass using multiwavelength surveys, which is relevant to AGN selection, classification, and galaxy/AGN co-evolution. It is not JWST high-z focused, but it is closely related to AGN diagnostics and survey methodology.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Check the definitions of X-ray, WISE, and broad-line AGN samples and their selection biases
  • Look for the δ_rank overdensity metric and how it is computed within stellar-mass bins
  • Assess whether the environmental trends could be tested at high redshift with JWST surveys
  • Check for discussion of AGN fraction versus SFR and Eddington ratio across environments
Abstract

We use a stellar-mass complete sample of $\sim 36{,}000$ galaxies from GAMA/eFEDS to test whether AGN activity depends on environment independent of stellar mass. To isolate this effect, we introduce $\delta_{\rm rank}$, a novel local overdensity metric defined within narrow stellar mass bins. Our main result is that X-ray-selected AGNs show a clear environmental trend, the AGN fraction rises from $\sim 4\%$ in low-density regions to $\sim 6\%$ in high-density regions, with a sharp transition at $\delta_{\rm rank} \sim 0.4$. This environmental dependence is absent for WISE-selected and broad-line AGN, and Eddington ratios show no variation across environments. We also found that for galaxies with $\mathrm{SFR} > 15\,M_\odot\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1}$, X-ray AGN fraction is approximately 5%, 60% and 20% in low, mid and high $\delta_{\rm rank}$ galaxies. We show that relationship between AGN fraction and SFR varies dramatically with both environment and selection method. We conclude that environment on scales of $\sim 5h^{-1}\,\mathrm{Mpc}$ regulates X-ray AGN triggering efficiency.

An ALMA Band 7 survey of SDSS/Herschel quasars in Stripe 82: II. The nature of FIR-bright quasars

Evanthia Hatziminaoglou , Hugo Messias , Duncan Farrah , Anna Feltre , Dennis Koopmans , Ismael Perez-Fournon , Rhimon Souza , Lingyu Wang

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

The paper asks what the star-formation and UV/optical properties of FIR-bright quasars reveal about SMBH-host co-evolution. It finds extreme starbursts with SFRs of 500-3000 solar masses per year, weak starburst-accretion coupling, and evidence for AGN-driven winds, placing these sources in a transitional blowout phase. This is relevant to my work on AGN feedback, obscured AGN, and galaxy/AGN co-evolution, though at lower redshift than my JWST focus.

Reason

This paper studies FIR-bright quasars at 1 < z < 4 with ALMA and multi-wavelength SED fitting, addressing SMBH-host co-evolution, AGN-driven winds, and obscured-to-unobscured transitions, which connects to my interests in AGN co-evolution and dust-reddened AGN, though it is not JWST or cosmic-dawn.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Look for SED-fitting diagnostics that separate AGN and starburst contributions to the FIR
  • Check whether the paper provides spectroscopy or only photometric evidence for the wind signatures
  • Examine the CIV, Lya, and MgII equivalent-width measurements and their interpretation
  • Check the ALMA submm counterpart identification and how blending or confusion is handled
Abstract

We investigate the star formation properties and ultraviolet (UV)/optical spectral characteristics of 142 far-infrared (FIR)-bright quasars at redshifts 1 < z < 4 to probe supermassive black hole (SMBH) and host galaxy co-evolution. By combining multi-wavelength observations with high-resolution ALMA submillimeter (submm) data, we overcome source confusion and robustly constrain star formation rates (SFRs). The inclusion of ALMA data to the Spectral Energy Distribution fitting reduces SFR estimates by ~20% for quasars with single submm counterparts and by a factor >2 for FIR-blended multi-component systems, while reducing FIR luminosity uncertainties by ~18%. The revised host SFRs span 500 and 3000 solar masses per year (median 1080 solar masses per year), confirming these systems are extreme starbursts. The very shallow positive correlation between starburst and accretion luminosities suggests that SMBH accretion and star formation in the host are not coupled on the timescales probed by the observations. Compared to the general population of FIR-faint quasars, FIR-bright sources have a factor of three higher detection rates at 1.4 GHz, predominantly following the FIR-radio correlation, and reduced CIV and Lya equivalent widths with unaffected MgII, suggesting AGN-driven winds that leave MgII relatively unaffected. Together, these results place FIR-bright quasars in a brief transitional phase, bridging heavily obscured "blowout" galaxies and optically unobscured blue quasars, where bulge assembly and SMBH growth peak simultaneously during ongoing stellar assembly.
