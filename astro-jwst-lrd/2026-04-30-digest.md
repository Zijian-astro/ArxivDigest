- Reason: Directly studies spectroscopic variability in a z~7 LRD using JWST/NIRSpec, addressing key debates on LRD nature, AGN vs. star formation, and black hole properties at high redshift.

Short digest:
Does the ionizing source in LRDs vary on short timescales? This paper reports ~30% continuum and broad-line flux variability and 42% [OIII] flux change over ~13 rest-days in a z~7 LRD, confirmed by multiple epochs. This implies direct sightlines from the accretion disk to nebular gas beyond the BLR, challenging galaxy-dominated interpretations and requiring new black hole mass inference methods.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Check the flux calibration and MSA slit placement tests that rule out systematic artifacts.
- Look for the comparison of [OIII] and broad-line flux changes across the multiple epochs (C3PO, THRILS, RUBIES, CAPERS).
- Examine the discussion on covering fractions and implications for BLR geometry.

- Reason: Directly studies Lyα emission during reionization, a key observable for high-z galaxies and the IGM state, relevant to JWST studies of reionization-era emitters.

Short digest:
How can double-peaked Lyα emission be observed during reionization when the IGM is mostly neutral? The authors show that sightlines through underdense voids allow blue-side photons to transmit, with a global probability ~3e-3 at 80% ionization. This provides a sensitive probe of the ionizing background at late reionization stages, relevant for interpreting JWST Lyα observations.

Full-text inspection checklist:

- Look for the predicted evolution of double-peaked fraction with ionization fraction and ionizing background intensity.
- Examine whether the simulation includes radiative transfer effects beyond the ionization equilibrium assumption.

- arXiv ID: `2604.26750`
- Authors: Koki Otaki , Raffaella Schneider , Simone Bianchi , Joris Witstok , Luca Graziani , Marco Limongi , Roberto Maiolino
- Score: 8
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.26750
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.26750
- Subjects: Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
- Reason: This paper directly addresses dust enrichment and extinction curves in high-redshift galaxies, relevant to JWST observations of UV attenuation and dust properties.

Short digest:
The central question is whether core-collapse supernovae can produce the flat UV attenuation curves observed in high-redshift galaxies. The main result is that reverse shock processing destroys small grains, making amorphous carbon dominant and flattening extinction curves. This matters for interpreting JWST dust attenuation and grain properties in early galaxies.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Check comparison of model extinction curves to JWST observed attenuation curves
- Look for dust-to-stellar mass ratio predictions and their evolution
- Examine the role of reverse shock in shaping grain size distributions
