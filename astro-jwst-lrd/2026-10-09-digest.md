## 1. JWST CAPERS: Spectroscopic evaluation of very high redshift galaxy candidates

- arXiv ID: `2610.12322`
- Authors: Callum T. Donnan , Mark Dickinson , Lorenzo Napolitano , Pablo Arrabal Haro , Anthony J. Taylor , Hollis B. Akins , Ryan Begley , Davide Bevacqua , Adam C. Carnall , Marco Castellano , Fergus Cullen , James S. Dunlop , Steven L. Finkelstein , Carter Flayhart , Mauro Giavalisco , Michaela Hirschmann , Ray A. Lucas , Derek J. McLeod , Ross J. McLure , Casey Papovich , Laura Pentericci , Borja Pérez-Díaz , Thomas M. Stanton , L. Y. Aaron Yung
- Score: 10
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12322
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.12322
- Subjects: Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
- Reason: This paper directly evaluates JWST/NIRSpec spectroscopy of very high-redshift galaxy candidates and discusses photometric selection, UV LF priors, and spectroscopic confirmation, which is central to my research.

Short digest:
The paper evaluates selection methodology for z>=8.5 galaxy candidates using 183 sources with secure NIRSpec PRISM redshifts from CAPERS. It finds that a UV LF prior on the inferred p(z) raises the confirmation rate from about 35% to 77% and confirms 48 sources at z>=8.5, with the highest at z=12.98. It matters greatly for my work because it provides spectroscopic validation of JWST/NIRCam photometric selection and directly informs high-redshift galaxy candidate classification and UVLF studies.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Look for the detailed comparison of selection criteria, including SNR, Lyman-alpha break strength, and Delta chi-squared cuts.
- Check the spectroscopic confirmation statistics and any sources that were rejected as low-redshift interlopers.
- Examine the UV LF prior implementation and how it affects completeness and contamination.
- Look for any discussion of emission-line detections, especially in sources without clear Lyman-alpha breaks, and whether any confirmed sources show AGN-like features.

## 3. ACORN I. Massive Black Hole Seeding and Tidal Disruption Events from Star Clusters in Cosmological Simulations

- arXiv ID: `2610.12398`
- Authors: Yihao Zhou , Tiziana Di Matteo , Claire E. Williams , Aklant Kumar Bhowmick , Marta Reina-Campos , Carl L. Rodriguez , Elena González Prieto , Laura Blecha , Rupert Croft
- Score: 8
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12398
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.12398
- Subjects: Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA) ; High Energy Astrophysical Phenomena (astro-ph.HE)
- Reason: Directly addresses massive black hole seeding at z>6 motivated by JWST star cluster discoveries, connecting to early SMBH growth and galaxy/AGN co-evolution. The M_BH-M_galaxy scaling for low-mass galaxies is relevant to high-redshift black hole candidates.

Short digest:
The paper asks how massive black hole seeds form in high-redshift star clusters and how this affects MBH growth, mergers, and tidal disruption events. It introduces a runaway-collision seeding model in cosmological simulations, producing seeds of 10^3-10^5.6 Msun and enhancing merger and TDE rates. This matters because it provides a physical seeding channel for the early black holes JWST is now detecting and predicts observable signatures relevant to LISA and low-mass seed growth.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Check the predicted MBH seed mass function and occupation fraction at z>6 for comparison with JWST AGN/LRD candidate number densities
- Look for the M_BH-M_galaxy scaling plots at low galaxy masses and whether they are compared to observational constraints
- Examine the TDE luminosity and rate predictions for low-mass seeds and whether they could be observable with current or future facilities
- Check how the seeding model depends on metallicity, cluster mass, and environment, and whether it can produce the compact red sources seen by JWST

## 5. Data Representation Matters: Optimizing Machine Learning for X-ray Source Classification by Leveraging Spatio-Spectral Information

- arXiv ID: `2610.12317`
- Authors: Maggie Lieu , Ivan Valtchanov , Marguerite Pierre , Esin Gulbahar , Elias Koulouridis , Iván Muñoz Rodríguez , Lucio Chiappetti
- Score: 7
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12317
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.12317
- Subjects: Instrumentation and Methods for Astrophysics (astro-ph.IM)
- Reason: This paper applies machine learning to X-ray source classification of AGN and galaxy clusters, which is relevant to automated AGN selection and interpretable AI-for-astronomy workflows, though it is X-ray rather than JWST.

Short digest:
The paper benchmarks 1D, 2D, 2D+1D, and 3D CNNs for classifying simulated XMM-Newton observations of AGN and galaxy clusters. It finds that 3D CNNs on spatio-spectral event cubes perform most robustly and that interpretability analysis shows the model learns to distinguish thermal cluster emission from non-thermal AGN power laws. It matters for my work because it demonstrates data representation choices and interpretability methods for AGN classification that could inform JWST AGN selection and source classification.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Look for the performance metrics and cross-validation stability of the 3D CNN versus lower-dimensional baselines.
- Check the GradCAM interpretability results to see whether the learned features correspond to physically meaningful AGN versus cluster diagnostics.
- Examine how the method handles background characterization and whether it could be adapted to JWST photometric or spectroscopic AGN selection.
- Confirm whether the simulated XMM-Newton setup includes realistic AGN spectra and obscuration relevant to high-redshift AGN.

## 7. DeepDISC on LSST Data Preview 1: Scene-Level Detection, Deblending, and Star/Galaxy Classification in the ECDFS and EDFS Fields

- arXiv ID: `2610.10986`
- Authors: Prince Yadav , Yaswant Sai Ejjagiri , Xin Liu , Grant Merz , the LSST Dark Energy Science Collaboration
- Score: 6
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10986
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.10986
- Subjects: Instrumentation and Methods for Astrophysics (astro-ph.IM)
- Reason: This paper applies a deep-learning framework to LSST Data Preview 1 for detection, deblending, and star/galaxy classification, which connects to my interest in AI-for-astronomy and automated source classification. It is not JWST high-redshift AGN or LRD science, but the methodology is relevant to survey source classification.

Short digest:
The paper asks whether the DeepDISC scene-level deep-learning framework can jointly detect, deblend, and classify sources in real LSST DP1 images. It finds that fine-tuning on real DP1 data substantially improves completeness, reaching about 84% galaxy and 49-74% star completeness depending on matching, with performance comparable to a Random Forest classifier. This matters for my work because automated classification and deblending are directly relevant to JWST survey source selection, AGN/LRD candidate identification, and handling blends in deep fields.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Look for diagnostics separating stars, galaxies, and possible AGN or compact red sources in the classification output.
- Check whether the framework can be adapted to JWST NIRCam/MIRI bands or high-redshift source classification.
- Examine the unrecognized-blend fraction and how it compares with JWST deep-field blending issues.
- Check whether the paper provides probability outputs or uncertainty estimates useful for candidate selection.
