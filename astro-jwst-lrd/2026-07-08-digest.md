## 2. Executable verification through formalized expert reasoning in astronomical spectroscopy

- arXiv ID: `2607.06128`
- Authors: Haosong Wang , Ting Tan , Ji Yao , Jiajun Zhang , Qian Zheng , Christophe Yeche , Jean-Paul Kneib , Huanyuan Shan
- Score: 8
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06128
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.06128
- Subjects: Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (astro-ph.CO) ; Instrumentation and Methods for Astrophysics (astro-ph.IM)
- Reason: This paper introduces FORMA, an executable verification protocol for astronomical spectroscopy that uses formalized expert reasoning, which is directly relevant to AI/automated classification and spectral inference for astronomy.

Short digest:
The central question is how to automate scientific verification in astronomical spectroscopy beyond prediction. The main result is FORMA, which reconstructs expert reasoning into an auditable workflow that achieves 95.5% agreement with experts on DESI data. This matters for my work because it provides a framework for automated, auditable classification of ambiguous spectra, applicable to JWST high-redshift galaxy and AGN surveys.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Check the credibility score threshold and its relation to classification reliability.
- Look for examples of spectral evidence extraction and physical audit steps.
- Examine how the method handles ambiguous cases and compares to expert adjudication.

- Subjects: Instrumentation and Methods for Astrophysics (astro-ph.IM)
- Reason: This paper explores image-text alignment for radio galaxy morphologies using vision-language models, which is directly relevant to AI-for-astronomy and automated classification.

Short digest:
The central question is whether text captions can capture the same morphological information as radio galaxy images. The main result is that caption-based classification of FR-I/FR-II galaxies performs similarly to images. This matters for my work because it demonstrates the use of multimodal AI for galaxy classification, which could be extended to JWST data for high-redshift galaxies and AGN.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Check the classification performance metrics and comparison between image-only and caption-only models.
- Look for details on the domain-specific prompt and LoRA fine-tuning approach.
- Examine the dataset (MiraBest) and whether the method could generalize to other morphological classes.

- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.06315
- Subjects: Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
- Reason: This paper discusses low-frequency radio recombination lines from galaxies and AGN over cosmic time, which is relevant to AGN and galaxy evolution, but not directly JWST-based.

Short digest:
The central question is how low-frequency RRLs can provide a multi-phase view of interstellar gas in galaxies and AGN out to z~6. The main result is that RRLs trace ionized and molecular gas and are observable with SKA pathfinders. This is partially relevant to my work because it addresses gas and AGN at high redshift, but uses radio rather than JWST observations.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Check the detection examples at z~1 and the predicted observability at higher redshift.
- Look for discussion of how RRLs distinguish AGN activity from star formation.
- Examine the survey strategies and frequency ranges that will be accessible with SKA.
