## 1. Formation of Heavy Seed Black Holes and Little Red Dots-like Compact Clusters in Metal-enriched Star-forming Regions

- arXiv ID: `2609.00437`
- Authors: Sunmyon Chon , Shingo Hirano , Ke-Jung Chen , Volker Springel
- Score: 9
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00437
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.00437
- Subjects: Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA) ; Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (astro-ph.CO)
- Reason: Directly addresses heavy seed BH formation in metal-enriched environments and links to LRD-like compact clusters, aligning with your interests in early BH growth and LRDs.

Short digest:
The paper asks whether heavy seed BHs can form in metal-enriched, clustered star-forming regions. Using cosmological radiation-hydro simulations, it finds that intense LW radiation enables seed formation in weakly metal-enriched gas, and that massive clusters can exhibit V-shaped spectra similar to LRDs. This matters for your work because it provides a theoretical framework connecting heavy seeds, compact clusters, and LRD-like emission, which you can test against JWST observations.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Check the simulation's treatment of LW radiation and metal enrichment in seed-forming regions.
- Look for the synthetic spectra of massive clusters and their comparison to observed LRD SEDs.
- Examine the properties of off-center LRD-like systems and their lensed counterparts.

Short digest:
The paper asks how Lyα visibility evolves with redshift and what physical properties of LAEs drive this trend. It finds that LAE properties are stable while the full galaxy population evolves toward LAE-like properties at higher z, implying galaxy evolution boosts Lyα escape. This matters for your work because it provides a framework to separate galaxy-evolution effects from IGM attenuation when interpreting Lyα in high-z JWST samples.

Full-text inspection checklist:

- Look for the redshift evolution plots of X_Lyα in the two UV luminosity bins and the significance of the decline at z>6.
- Examine the Attenuation-Free Model assumptions and how they separate galaxy evolution from IGM attenuation.

- Authors: Francisco Pozo Nuñez , Eduardo Bañados , Swayamtrupta Panda , Amit Kumar Mandal , Bozena Czerny , Jochen Heidt
- Score: 6
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00278
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.00278
- Subjects: Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
- Reason: Studies accretion-disk and BLR sizes in luminous quasars at z>2, relevant to early black-hole growth and AGN physics, though not JWST-based.

Short digest:
The paper compares AD and BLR sizes in two high-z quasars using continuum reverberation mapping and interferometry. It finds disk sizes consistent with thin-disk models for one quasar but inflated for another, providing constraints on BH mass and accretion. This matters for your work as it offers physical scales for high-z AGN, though at lower z than your typical targets.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Check the lag measurements and uncertainties for each quasar.
- Look for the comparison between inferred disk sizes and thin-disk predictions.
- Examine the R_BLR/R_AD ratios and their implications for BLR kinematics.
