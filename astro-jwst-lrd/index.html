A dust-reddened quasar in a [C II]-luminous host at z=6.55

Daming Yang , Joseph F. Hennawi , Rychard Bouwens , Timo Kist , Eduardo Bañados , Hiddo S. B. Algera , Aaron J. Barth , Sarah E. I. Bosman , Frederick B. Davies , Anna-Christina Eilers , Xiaohui Fan , Victoria A. Fawcett , Yuming Fu , Anniek J. Gloudemans , Francesco Guarneri , Bin Jia , Elia Pizzati , Huub Röttgering , Jan-Torge Schindler , Feige Wang , Jinyi Yang

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

The paper asks what the nuclear and host-galaxy properties are of the dust-reddened quasar J1440+0019 at z=6.55. JWST/NIRSpec reveals an SMC-like extinction law with E(B-V)=0.20, a black-hole mass of log(MBH/Msun)=9.04, Eddington ratio ~0.8, and an ionized outflow, while ALMA shows an extremely [C II]-luminous, dust-rich host. This is highly relevant because it provides a benchmark for identifying and characterizing reddened, rapidly accreting quasars and their hosts during reionization, directly informing my work on high-redshift AGN and galaxy/AGN co-evolution.

Reason

This paper presents JWST/NIRSpec and ALMA observations of a dust-reddened quasar at z=6.55, directly matching my interests in high-redshift obscured AGN, black-hole growth, and galaxy/AGN co-evolution. It also addresses SED fitting, extinction, and host-galaxy properties at cosmic dawn.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Look for the NIRSpec spectral fit, extinction law, and broad Mg II line measurements
  • Check the black-hole mass, bolometric luminosity, and Eddington-ratio derivations
  • Examine the ALMA [C II] and dust-continuum constraints on the host galaxy
  • Assess how the object compares to blue quasars and LRDs in the MBH-Mdyn plane
Abstract

Most known quasars at $z>6$ were selected through their rest-frame UV emission. Only a small number of reddened quasars have been identified at these redshifts, leaving their nuclear and host-galaxy properties poorly constrained. We present JWST/NIRSpec spectroscopy and ALMA band 6 observations of the quasar J1440+0019 at $z=6.5488$, revealing its dust-reddened nature and jointly characterizing its nucleus and host galaxy. The continuum is best described by an SMC-like extinction law with $E(B-V)=0.20\pm0.03$. Three absorption systems detected in N V, Si IV, and C IV are blueshifted by approximately $900$--$3600$ km s$^{-1}$ and likely trace an ionized nuclear outflow. The broad Mg II line and extinction-corrected continuum yield a black-hole mass of $\log(M_{\rm BH}/M_\odot)=9.04\pm0.42$, a bolometric luminosity of $L_{\rm bol}\simeq1.1\times10^{47}$ erg s$^{-1}$, and an Eddington ratio of $\lambda_{\rm Edd}\simeq0.8$. ALMA observations further reveal its host galaxy to be among the most [C II]-luminous known at $z>6$, with $L_{\mathrm{[C II]}}=(7.21\pm0.25)\times10^9 L_\odot$. For a fiducial dust temperature of 47 K and emissivity index $\beta=1.6$, the dust continuum implies $L_{\rm TIR}=8.9\times10^{12} L_\odot$ and $M_{\rm dust}=4.0\times10^8 M_\odot$. The inferred dynamical mass places J1440+0019 in the region of the $M_{\rm BH}$--$M_{\rm dyn}$ plane occupied by high-redshift blue quasars. These observations reveal a moderately reddened, rapidly accreting quasar in a luminous, dust-rich host during reionization. Its measured nuclear and host-galaxy properties provide an observational reference for identifying and characterizing similar systems in future systematic searches with wide-area infrared surveys.

An empirical calibration of the spectral resolution of JWST NIRSpec PRISM

Hang Zhou , Xin Wang , Yuxuan Pang , Shengzhe Wang , Karl Glazebrook , Themiya Nanayakkara , Hu Zhan

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

The paper asks how the effective spectral resolution of JWST NIRSpec PRISM varies with wavelength and source morphology. Using archival galaxies from z~0.5 to 10 and the [O III] doublet, it finds the empirical resolution exceeds pre-launch JDox values by factors of 1.03-1.63 and provides a wavelength- and morphology-dependent calibration. This matters for my work because it affects how I interpret emission-line widths, broad-line AGN, and kinematic measurements in NIRSpec PRISM spectra of high-redshift sources.

Reason

This paper empirically calibrates JWST NIRSpec PRISM spectral resolution, directly relevant to my NIRSpec spectroscopy of high-redshift galaxies and AGN. Accurate line-width and resolution knowledge is essential for broad-line and emission-line measurements.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Look for the empirical resolving-power curve versus wavelength and how it compares to JDox
  • Check the multi-Gaussian fitting procedure and FWHM measurement methodology
  • Examine the source-size/morphology dependence and the root-sum-square formula
  • Assess whether the calibration is applicable to compact LRDs and broad-line AGN
Abstract

We present an empirical calibration of the effective spectral resolution of the JWST NIRSpec PRISM disperser based on a large sample of galaxies drawn from archival observations. Our sample spans a wide redshift range, $0.5 \lesssim z \lesssim 10$, enabling a wavelength-dependent characterization of the PRISM resolving power across the full bandpass using the [O III]$\lambda\lambda4959,5007$ doublets. To ensure robust measurements, we impose strict signal-to-noise requirements on both continuum and emission-line detections. Emission-line profiles are fitted with multi-Gaussian models, and the full width at half maximum (FWHM) is measured for each source. The derived empirical resolution curve shows a systematic offset from the nominal pre-launch estimate, lying between the tabulated instrumental resolution and the point-source shutter limit. We also discuss how source size affects the final spectral resolution, and provide an analytical formula that combines wavelength and morphology dependence in a root-sum-square framework. This work delivers a population-averaged, statistically robust calibration of the effective PRISM spectral resolution for typical galaxy observations. The resulting calibration is directly applicable to statistical analyses of galaxy spectra obtained with NIRSpec PRISM. Our empirical calibration exceeds the pre-launch JDox values by a factor of 1.03 to 1.63, depending on source morphology, and provides the first wavelength and morphology dependent calibration of the NIRSpec PRISM spectral resolution.

A Comprehensive JWST Slitless Spectroscopic Census of $\sim$12,000 Emission-Line Galaxies at Redshift $0\sim9$ in COSMOS

Shuqi Fu , Zijian Zhang , Danyang Jiang , Jie Chen , Linhua Jiang , Chenxi Bao , Yihe Wang , Ronaldo Laishram

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

The paper asks how to build a large, homogeneous slitless-spectroscopic census of emission-line galaxies across cosmic time with JWST. It presents 12,275 sources at z~0-9 with F444W grism spectroscopy, a multi-step identification framework using spectra, images, SEDs, and spatial information, and CNN/LLM-assisted classification. This is highly relevant for my work on high-redshift emission-line galaxies and AGN, providing a reference catalog and methodology for line identification and source classification.

Reason

This is a JWST/NIRCam WFSS census of ~12,000 emission-line galaxies at z~0-9 in COSMOS-Web, directly relevant to high-redshift spectroscopy, emission-line diagnostics, and AGN activity. It also uses CNN and LLM methods for source identification, matching my AI-for-astronomy interests.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Look for the emission-line diagnostic diagrams and how [O III], Halpha, and other lines are used to separate star-forming galaxies from AGN.
  • Check the CNN/LLM identification framework details, including training data, performance metrics, and confusion/contamination rates.
  • Examine the redshift distribution and number counts at z>6 to assess overlap with LRD and high-redshift AGN samples.
  • Look for composite spectra and faint line detections that could inform broad-line or obscured AGN searches.
Abstract

Emission-line galaxies provide key diagnostics of star formation, chemical enrichment, and AGN activity across cosmic time. We present a systematic census of emission-line sources using JWST/NIRCam wide-field slitless spectroscopy (WFSS) from the COSMOS-3D program, which covers a large contiguous area of 0.33 deg$^2$ and provides F444W grism spectroscopy over $3.9$--$5.1\ \mu$m. By combining slitless spectroscopy with deep multi-band imaging data, we construct a homogeneous spectroscopic sample of 12,275 objects at redshift 0--9. The typical $3\sigma$ line-flux limit reaches $3.2\times10^{-18}\ {\rm erg\ s^{-1}\ cm^{-2}}$. To address source confusion inherent to slitless spectroscopy, we develop a multi-step identification framework that combines information from spectra, images, spectral energy distributions, and spatial distributions. We introduce convolutional neural network and large language model to emulate the human identification process and improve efficiency. The resulting catalog provides spectroscopic redshifts, emission-line measurements, and derived physical properties for all sources. The F444W WFSS enables the detection of multiple emission lines, including primarily [O III], H$\alpha$, He I $\lambda10830$, [S III] $\lambda\lambda9531,9069$, Pa$\beta$, Pa$\alpha$, Br$\beta$, PAH, Br$\alpha$, allowing a continuous census of galaxies across cosmic time. We construct composite spectra in several redshift bins and detect a variety of faint spectral features that are difficult to identify in individual spectra, including higher-order hydrogen recombination lines, CO absorption bands, and [Fe II] lines. This catalog provides a key reference dataset for studies of galaxy evolution and AGN activity, and demonstrates the power of WFSS for constructing large, unbiased spectroscopic samples over wide areas. The full catalog will be publicly available online upon publication.

A Single Line-Emitter Identified in an Unbiased Search with JWST/NIRSpec: Multi-Object Coronagraphy for Slitless Spectroscopy

Mason S. Huberty , Claudia Scarlata , Matthew J. Hayes , Alaina Henry , Danielle A. Berg , Vieri Cammelli , Richard Ellis , Anne E. Jaskot , Yu-Heng Lin , Vihang Mehta , Matilde Mingozzi , Pierluigi Monaco , Alberto Saldana-Lopez , Aayush Saxena , Jonathan C. Tan , Alice Young

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

The paper asks whether an unbiased JWST/NIRSpec slitless search can identify previously unknown line emitters. It reports a strong 1.60 micron emission line with no imaging counterpart and an extreme EW lower limit of 7620 A, discussing possible AGN and transient explanations. This matters for my work because it demonstrates a new NIRSpec technique for finding compact line emitters and highlights the classification challenges for high-z AGN and unusual sources.

Reason

This is a JWST/NIRSpec slitless spectroscopy detection of a single-line emitter in the HUDF, directly relevant to high-z emission-line searches, source classification, and possible AGN/transient interpretations.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Look for the line identification diagnostics and why [OII], [OIII], Halpha, and Lyalpha are disfavored
  • Check the imaging non-detection limits and how the EW lower limit is derived
  • Look for the AGN and transient scenarios discussed and their observational tests
  • Check whether follow-up spectroscopy or additional lines are proposed or available
Abstract

We report the detection of a previously unknown object with a single emission line within the Hubble Ultra Deep Field, using a new JWST NIRSpec technique called Multi-Object Coronagraphy for Slitless Spectroscopy (MOCSS) with the G140M/F100LP observing mode. The MOCSS technique involves opening almost all the shutters on the NIRSpec microshutter array, while simultaneously closing the shutters that lie on top of known low redshift objects, facilitating a wide field slitless spectroscopic search without contamination from bright objects. This observational technique has enabled the detection of a strong emission line in four different dither positions and in two position angles, and thus cannot be artificial. This line is detected at 1.60 $\rm \mu m$ with a line flux of $[2.5\pm 0.3]\times10^{-18} \rm erg\ s^{-1} cm^{-2}$ and a FWHM of $13.2\pm2.0$Å, and does not correspond to any known source with some of the deepest HST and JWST imaging in existence. Based upon the absence of imaging detection, we place a lower limit on the observed frame equivalent width (EW) of 7620Å on this object, which strongly limits the number of possible lines that this line can be. Given the wavelength, an EW limit that is near the ceiling of expectation, and the lack of other lines in the bandpass, this line cannot be easily explained by [OII] at $z=3.29$, [OIII] at $z=2.20$, H$\alpha$ at $z=1.44$, and Ly$\alpha$ at $z=12.16$. We discuss possible AGN and transient phenomena that could explain the finding.

The road to $H_0$, $Ω_m$, and $w$ with a global neutrino-telescope network

Pavel Zhelnin , Gonzalo Herrera , Nicholas Kamp , Carlos A. Argüelles

Abstract

The newly measured correlation between the neutrino and X-ray luminosities of Seyfert galaxies has yielded the first neutrino-based measurement of the Hubble constant. Building on this result, we forecast the reach of a global neutrino-telescope network by 2030, utilizing full-sky IceCube exposures combined with KM3NeT-ARCA and Baikal-GVD. We find that this 2030 configuration sharpens the neutrino-measured Hubble constant from today's $H_0 = 66^{+44}_{-37}$ to $H_0 =66^{+15}_{-13}$ km s$^{-1}$ Mpc$^{-1}$. Additionally, we project that the disk-corona slope of the neutrino X-ray luminosity relation ($L_\nu\propto L_X^\beta$) will reach $\beta=0.72\pm0.04$, enabling a $\geq \!5\sigma$ exclusion of the calorimetric value $\beta=1$. Finally, we show that, if a high-redshift population of neutrino sources, such as JWST's "little red dots," is identified and obeys the same luminosity relation, we can obtain a new independent measurement of the matter density $\Omega_m$ and the dark energy equation-of-state parameter $w$.

Exploring Case B I: The Footprints of Hidden Radiation Fields in H I Balmer Decrements

Maryam Dehghanian , Fransisco Guzman , Peter van Hoof , Chamani Gunasekera , Gary Ferland

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

The paper asks what additional physics can produce Balmer decrement departures from Case B in high-redshift systems. It shows that a soft non-ionizing radiation field from cool sources can strongly perturb Halpha/Hbeta and Hgamma/Hbeta ratios in a way distinguishable from dust reddening. This matters for my work because Balmer line ratios are key diagnostics for dust attenuation and AGN contamination in high-z galaxy spectra.

Reason

This paper explores departures from Case B Balmer decrements due to hidden radiation fields, directly relevant to interpreting high-redshift emission-line ratios and AGN/nebular diagnostics.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Look for the parameter space of blackbody temperatures and radiation fields that produce the largest effects
  • Check the predicted Halpha/Hbeta and Hgamma/Hbeta trajectories versus dust reddening
  • Assess whether the models apply to unresolved high-z systems or only resolved nebulae
  • Look for any discussion of AGN or hidden broad-line region contributions
Abstract

Theory predicts that H I emission from photoionized clouds should lie close to the Case B limit established by Menzel and Baker, and observations of nearby nebulae have largely confirmed this expectation. However, recent observations of more distant high-redshift systems have revealed a small fraction with striking departures from Case B. What additional physics might be at work in these objects? This paper is part of a series exploring the physical processes that can produce such departures. In Paper I (D26), we show that Case B remains remarkably robust over a broad range of physical conditions, consistent with observations of nearby nebulae. Here, we explore one additional ingredient: a soft, non-ionizing radiation field. Such a continuum can strongly perturb the Balmer spectrum. The largest effects occur for radiation fields resembling log(Teff K)\sim 3.2-4.2 blackbodies, where photoexcitation from the metastable 2s level alters the hydrogen level populations and subsequent radiative cascade. The resulting Halpha/Hbeta and Hgamma/Hbeta ratios follow a different trajectory from dust reddening, allowing the two effects to be distinguished using multiple Balmer lines. In unresolved systems, a cool source located near the line-emitting gas may provide an additional radiation field distinct from the hot stars responsible for ionization. Balmer decrements may therefore carry information about otherwise hidden components of the radiation field surrounding the emitting gas.

When dust growth takes over: the origin of dust in post-reionization galaxies

Omima Osman , Gabriella De Lucia , Andreas. L. Faisst , Lizhi Xie , Fabio Fontanot , Michaela Hirschmann

arXiv PDF

Short Digest

The paper asks what physical processes drive rapid dust enrichment in post-reionization galaxies. It finds that efficient interstellar-medium dust growth, not stellar dust production alone, is required to reproduce observed dust scaling relations, with a transition around z~8-9. This matters for my work as context on dust build-up and attenuation in high-z galaxies, though it does not address AGN or compact red sources.

Reason

This paper models dust enrichment in post-reionization galaxies at z~4-6 using the ACJ sample, relevant to dust attenuation and galaxy evolution but not directly to JWST high-z AGN or LRDs.

Full-Text Inspection Checklist

  • Look for the dust mass-stellar mass and dust-to-gas relations used as constraints
  • Check how the model treats dust growth, destruction, and stellar dust production
  • Assess whether the ACJ sample includes any AGN or compact red sources
  • Check the redshift range and whether any z>6 or cosmic-dawn implications are discussed
Abstract

The post-reionization epoch ($z\sim4$-6) marks a critical phase in galaxy evolution, during which galaxies rapidly assemble their stellar mass, enrich their interstellar medium, and build up substantial dust reservoirs. The physical processes responsible for such rapid dust enrichment remain uncertain. We investigate the dust enrichment history of the ALPINE-CRISTAL-JWST (ACJ) sample using the semi-analytic galaxy formation model GAlaxy Evolution and Assembly (GAEA), which follows galaxy assembly, chemical enrichment, and dust evolution. We select model galaxies matching the observed stellar masses, star formation rates, and metallicities of the ACJ sample, compare their dust properties with observed scaling relations, and trace their enrichment histories and environments. The model reproduces the observed dust mass-stellar mass relation and the dust-to-stellar mass, dust-to-gas, and dust-to-metal ratios as functions of stellar mass and metallicity. Reproducing these constraints requires efficient dust growth in the interstellar medium; stellar dust production alone cannot account for the observed dust reservoirs, even under the most optimistic assumptions. The combination of stellar mass, star formation rate, and metallicity provides a good predictor of galaxy dust content. Our model suggests rapid dust build-up consistent with the previously noted rapid metal enrichment, with evidence for a transition around $z\sim8$-9 below which dust growth becomes the dominant dust formation channel. Finally, ACJ-like galaxies preferentially reside in locally overdense environments, suggesting that environment may have contributed to their accelerated evolution. Taken together, these results support rapid and coupled stellar, metal, and dust build-up in ACJ galaxies during the post-reionization era.
