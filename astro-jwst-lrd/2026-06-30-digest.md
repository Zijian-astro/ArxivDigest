- Reason: Directly studies little red dots (LRDs) at high redshift using JWST, finding no evolution in number density from cosmic dawn to cosmic noon.

Short digest:
Does the number density of little red dots evolve from cosmic dawn to cosmic noon? No significant evolution is found for LRDs with L_bol > 3e44 erg/s at z>2. This matters because it challenges models of black hole seed formation and suggests LRDs may represent a high-accretion phase in mature black holes, directly informing early SMBH growth and galaxy/AGN co-evolution.

## 2. General Relativistic Shock Wave Solutions with Black Hole Formation: The Singular Isothermal Sphere Case

- arXiv ID: `2606.29607`
- Authors: Chien-Ting J. Chen (1), Michael J. Cai (2), Fabio Pacucci (3) ((1) USRA/NASA MSFC, (2) Google, (3) CfA/SAO)
- Score: 8
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29607
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.29607
- Subjects: High Energy Astrophysical Phenomena (astro-ph.HE) ; Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (astro-ph.CO); Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA); General Relativity and Quantum Cosmology (gr-qc)
- Reason: This paper directly addresses early SMBH growth, direct-collapse black hole formation, and explicitly mentions JWST's Little Red Dots, which are central to my research interests.

Short digest:
The central question is how shock waves during the collapse of a singular isothermal sphere lead to black hole formation. The main result is that general-relativistic shock solutions yield accretion rates suppressed by a factor of 5-7 and energy release up to 10% of rest mass. This matters for my work because it provides an analytical energy budget for direct-collapse black hole seeds and connects to the properties of JWST's Little Red Dots.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Check the derived accretion rate suppression factor and its dependence on sound speed.
- Look for the energy release at the shock and comparison to radiative efficiency.
- Examine how the results are linked to Little Red Dots and high-redshift black hole candidates.
