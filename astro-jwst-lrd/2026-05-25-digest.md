## 1. SDSS+JWST Census of Stellar and Nebular Dust Attenuation at $z \sim 0$-7: Mass Dependence and Redshift Evolution

- arXiv ID: `2605.23049`
- Authors: Jie Song , Masami Ouchi , Tomokazu Kiyota , Chenghao Zhu , Xu Kong , Yurina Nakazato
- Score: 10
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23049
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.23049
- Subjects: Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
- Reason: Directly uses JWST/JADES spectroscopy to study dust attenuation in galaxies at z~1.5-7, with mass dependence and redshift evolution, perfectly matching my interests in SED fitting, dust attenuation, and high-redshift galaxy properties.

Short digest:
How does dust attenuation depend on galaxy stellar mass and redshift from z~0 to 7? The paper finds a universal extinction relation with no significant redshift evolution, and identifies M*~10^9 Msun as a transition mass where nebular attenuation rises more steeply than stellar attenuation. This matters for my work because it provides critical constraints on dust attenuation laws and the stellar-to-nebular color excess ratio for high-redshift galaxies, directly applicable to SED fitting of LRDs and compact red sources.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Examine the Balmer decrement and A_V measurements from JADES spectra for galaxies at z~1.5-7.
- Look for the mass-dependent trends in A_V, nebular and A_V, stellar, especially the transition at M*~10^9 Msun.
- Check the derived f = E(B-V)_stellar/E(B-V)_nebular values and how they vary with mass.

## 2. No Blue without Red: Evolutionary Properties of Super-Early Galaxies

- arXiv ID: `2605.22914`
- Authors: A. Ferrara , G. Rodighiero , S. Carniani , Z. Zhang , M. Kohandel , B. Das
- Score: 9
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22914
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.22914
- Subjects: Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
- Reason: Directly studies JWST super-early galaxies at z>10, including red and blue populations, and discusses AGN interpretation for compact sources, closely matching my interests in LRDs, compact red sources, and early galaxy evolution.

Short digest:
What physical origin explains the UV diversity of super-early galaxies at z>10? The paper proposes an evolutionary sequence from dust-obscured 'Red Monster' to UV-bright 'Blue Monster' phases driven by radiation-pressure outflows, with compact sources possibly AGN-dominated. This matters for my work because it provides a unified framework for interpreting LRDs and compact red sources at cosmic dawn.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Check the sample of 32 spectroscopically confirmed galaxies and their derived physical properties (halo mass, SFE, metallicity).
- Look for the specific interpretation of EGS-z11-R0 as an obscured-phase system and the discussion of compact sources as AGN candidates.
- Examine the outflow extent and super-Eddington conditions that drive the proposed evolutionary sequence.
