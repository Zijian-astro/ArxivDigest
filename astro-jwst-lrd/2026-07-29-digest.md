## 1. Reaching the Metallicity Floor at $z\sim 10$: Lensed Star Clusters at Cosmic Dawn and Cosmic Noon

- arXiv ID: `2607.24952`
- Authors: Raul Jimenez , Elena Tomasetti , Carmela Lardo , Licia Verde
- Score: 10
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24952
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.24952
- Subjects: Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA) ; Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (astro-ph.CO)
- Reason: Directly studies lensed star clusters at z=9.625 (Cosmic Dawn) using JWST-like photometry, relevant to high-redshift galaxies and early star formation.

Short digest:
Central question: What are the metallicities and formation redshifts of star clusters at Cosmic Dawn and Cosmic Noon? Main result: GEMS clusters at z=9.625 have [Z/H]~-2.3, consistent with the Milky Way GC metallicity floor, while Sparkler clusters at z=1.378 are more enriched. Why it matters: Provides direct constraints on early star cluster formation and metallicity enrichment at z~10, informing our understanding of galaxy assembly and AGN environments at cosmic dawn.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Check the SED fitting and Bayesian analysis for metallicity constraints.
- Look for the deconvolution photometry method and cluster candidate selection.
- Examine the comparison with stellar-population models including binary evolution.

This provides key constraints on AGN-galaxy co-evolution and highlights JWST's power to uncover hidden AGN populations.

- Reason: Directly studies Lyα escape in Hα-selected star-forming galaxies at z~5.5 using JWST/NIRCam and VLT/MUSE, relevant to high-redshift galaxy formation and reionization-era galaxies.

Short digest:
What is the Lyα escape fraction in typical star-forming galaxies at z~5.5? Using JWST/NIRCam F430M-selected Hα emitters, they find a population-averaged upper limit of <0.32, with Lyα detections associated with dust-free systems and overdensities. This matters for understanding Lyα visibility and the role of environment in the post-reionization era.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Check the Hα selection method using JWST/NIRCam F430M excess and the sample size/coverage.
- Look for the Lyα escape fraction measurements and upper limits, and the correlation with dust attenuation and SFR.
- Examine the overdensity structure and how local environment may facilitate Lyα escape.

The central question is the radio morphology of three high-redshift (z>4) quasars with compact radio features. ## 6. How massive and clumpy must a quasar wind be to create emission line blueshifts?

- arXiv ID: `2607.25035`
- Authors: James H. Matthews
- Score: 6
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25035
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.25035
- Subjects: Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA) ; High Energy Astrophysical Phenomena (astro-ph.HE)
- Reason: The paper studies quasar wind properties and C IV emission line blueshifts, which is relevant to understanding broad-line AGN and quasar feedback at high redshift.

Short digest:
The central question is how much mass, momentum, and energy quasar winds must carry to produce blueshifted C IV emission lines. The main result is that the wind must have mass outflow rates ~50 f_V times the accretion rate, favoring clumpy winds or swept-up ambient medium. This matters for my work because it provides constraints on wind properties that can be tested with JWST spectroscopy of high-redshift broad-line AGN.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Look for the derived mass outflow rates and clumping factors
- Check whether the models are compared to any high-redshift quasar observations
- Examine the 2D numerical model setup and assumptions about wind geometry
