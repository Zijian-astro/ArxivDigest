## 1. Combining datasets with different ground truths using Low-Rank Adaptation to generalize image-based CNN models for photometric redshift prediction

- arXiv ID: `2601.00146v1`
- Authors: Vikram Seenivasan, Srinath Saikrishnan, Andrew Lizarraga, Jonathan Soriano, Bernie Boscoe, Tuan Do
- Score: 8
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.00146v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.00146v1
- Subjects: Instrumentation and Methods for Astrophysics (astro-ph.IM); Machine Learning (cs.LG)
- Reason: Applies LoRA (a machine learning technique) to combine imaging datasets for photometric redshift prediction, directly relevant to AI-for-astronomy and galaxy survey analysis.

Short digest:
Central question: Can Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) improve photometric redshift estimation by combining datasets with different ground truths? Main result: LoRA fine-tuning yields ~2.5x less bias and ~2.2x less scatter than traditional transfer learning. This matters because it offers an efficient way to leverage pretrained models for redshift inference in large JWST surveys.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Check the comparison of LoRA vs. full retraining on bias and scatter metrics.
- Look for details on the CNN architecture and how LoRA adapters were integrated.
- Examine the datasets used (photometric vs. spectroscopic ground truths) and their relevance to JWST fields.
