- Subjects: Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
- Reason: This paper directly addresses Little Red Dots (LRDs) with JWST/NIRSpec observations, detecting Ca triplet absorption and arguing for an optically thick atmosphere. It is highly relevant to my research on LRDs, compact red sources, and AGN classification.

Short digest:
The paper presents new evidence for an optically thick atmosphere surrounding LRDs via Ca triplet absorption at rest-frame 8500A in JWST/NIRSpec data of 17 LRDs. It detects CaT absorption in three individual sources with average EW ~ -5 A, which can be matched by hydrostatic atmosphere models at low metallicity or non-hydrostatic gas structures. This directly informs my work on LRD physical nature, AGN vs. stellar interpretations, and the use of metal absorption lines as probes of gas conditions and enclosed mass in LRDs.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Look for the NIRSpec grating spectra and the CaT absorption measurements for individual LRDs
- Check the hydrostatic atmosphere model fits and the derived metallicity and effective temperature constraints
- Look for any discussion of the continuum origin (central engine vs. stellar) and its implications for AGN classification
- Check whether the paper provides any enclosed mass estimates or dynamical constraints from the absorption lines

## 2. Constraining Inflation with Little Red Dots

- arXiv ID: `2609.26553`
- Authors: Dionysios Karagiannis , P. Daniel Meerburg
- Score: 10
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26553
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.26553
- Subjects: Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (astro-ph.CO)
- Reason: This paper directly uses JWST-discovered Little Red Dots as a proposed tracer for inflation constraints, making it highly relevant to my interest in LRDs, high-redshift surveys, and early black-hole/galaxy co-evolution. It also connects to wide-field near- to mid-infrared spectroscopy, which is central to JWST follow-up.

Short digest:
The central question is whether LRD clustering can constrain primordial non-Gaussianity better than current CMB bounds. The authors forecast that a 14,000 deg2 LRD spectroscopic survey over 4<z<9 could achieve sigma(fNL_loc)=0.32, improving on Planck by a factor of 16. This matters for my work because it treats LRDs as a powerful cosmological and survey tracer, reinforcing the need to understand their nature, selection, and spectroscopic follow-up.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Look for the assumed LRD redshift distribution, bias, and number density used in the forecast
- Check how the authors model nuisance parameters and whether LRD AGN contamination is considered
- Examine the bispectrum covariance treatment and any assumptions about survey systematics
- Assess whether the paper discusses the physical nature of LRDs or only their clustering as a tracer

## 4. Gone with the wind? Potential gas inflow in the broad-line region of a QSO at $z=0.287$ traced by time-varying Balmer absorption

- arXiv ID: `2609.25224`
- Authors: Xihan Ji , Dominic J. Walton , Francesco D'Eugenio , Ignas Juodžbalis , Vasily Belokurov , Andrew C. Fabian , Roberto Maiolino , Jiamu Huang , Douglas N.C. Lin
- Score: 7
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25224
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.25224
- Subjects: Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
- Reason: This paper reports time-varying Balmer absorption in a QSO broad-line region, revealing gas inflow and obscuration, which is relevant to my interests in AGN feeding, broad-line regions, and obscured AGN. It is at low redshift but provides physical insight applicable to high-redshift AGN.

Short digest:
The paper reports spectroscopic observations of a QSO at z=0.287 showing time-varying Balmer absorption decomposed into stationary and dynamic components, interpreted as gas inflow in the broad-line region. The inferred mass inflow rate is comparable to the accretion rate, and X-ray observations reveal strong obscuration consistent with a warm absorber or merger-driven galactic-scale obscuration. This provides a local analog for understanding AGN feeding and obscuration physics that may apply to high-redshift broad-line AGN and LRDs.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Look for the spectroscopic decomposition of stationary vs. dynamic Balmer absorption components
- Check the X-ray-to-bolometric luminosity ratio and obscuration column density estimates
- Look for any discussion of applicability to high-redshift broad-line AGN or LRDs
- Check whether the inflow interpretation is supported by multi-epoch spectroscopy or only two epochs

## 5. Exploring the influence of reionisation-era galaxies on their local intergalactic medium using the Sherwood-Relics simulations

- arXiv ID: `2609.26483`
- Authors: Hamish Anderson , Laura C. Keating , Luke Conaboy , Xiangyu Jin , James S. Bolton , Ewald Puchwein
- Score: 7
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26483
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.26483
- Subjects: Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
- Reason: This paper connects reionization-era galaxies to their local IGM using simulations tuned to JWST ASPIRE [OIII] emitters, which is relevant to high-redshift galaxy formation and reionization-era observations. It is not directly about LRDs or AGN, but it informs the broader cosmic dawn galaxy/IGM context.

Short digest:
The central question is how reionization-era galaxies influence Lyman-alpha forest transmission in their surrounding IGM. Using Sherwood-Relics simulations and ASPIRE-like [OIII] emitters, the authors find an overdense, more opaque IGM at small radii and a more ionized IGM at larger radii, with large cosmic-variance scatter. This matters for my work because it provides a simulation framework for interpreting JWST high-z galaxy environments and IGM transmission, even though it does not address LRDs or AGN directly.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Look for the radial ranges and physical scales where IGM transmission is enhanced or suppressed around [OIII] emitters
- Check how the ASPIRE survey selection is implemented and whether it matches JWST/NIRSpec observational constraints
- Examine the treatment of cosmic variance and realization-to-realization scatter
- Assess whether the results can be connected to AGN or LRD environments rather than only star-forming [OIII] emitters

- Reason: This paper develops a data-driven galaxy population prior for photometric redshifts using generative models, which is relevant to my interest in SED fitting, photometric redshifts, source classification, and AI-for-astronomy. It is not JWST-specific or AGN-focused, but the methodology could inform high-z photometric redshift and SED inference.

Short digest:
The central question is whether a purely data-driven generative model can learn the galaxy SED prior accurately enough for Stage-IV photometric redshift calibration. The authors train a probabilistic autoencoder with a normalising flow on mock spectra and find per-mille-level mean redshift biases under controlled conditions. This matters for my work because improved photometric redshift priors and SED modeling are directly relevant to JWST high-z galaxy and AGN classification, even though this paper does not address AGN or LRDs.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Look for how the generative model handles noisy spectra and whether it can be extended to low-S/N JWST photometry
- Check the assumptions about galaxy SED diversity and whether AGN or dusty sources are included
- Examine the self-organising map tomographic binning and its robustness to outliers
- Assess whether the method could be adapted for LRD or compact red source selection
