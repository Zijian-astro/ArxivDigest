## 1. Chaotic accretion can (still) explain early supermassive black hole growth

- arXiv ID: `2610.07947`
- Authors: Kastytis Zubovas , Matas Tartėnas
- Score: 8
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07947
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.07947
- Subjects: Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA) ; Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (astro-ph.CO)
- Reason: This directly addresses early supermassive black hole growth at z>7, a core part of my research on early black-hole growth and galaxy/AGN co-evolution at cosmic dawn.

Short digest:
The paper asks whether chaotic accretion can explain the most extreme high-redshift SMBH masses given recent z>7 discoveries. It finds that in 7.6<z<10.6 the most massive SMBHs could grow with an exponential timescale of ~32 Myr from seeds below 100 solar masses, consistent with chaotic accretion if growth was nearly continuous. This is highly relevant to my work on early SMBH growth and provides testable predictions for high-redshift AGN observations, though it is theoretical rather than JWST-based.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Look for the compilation of z>7 SMBH mass estimates and their uncertainties
- Check the assumptions about radiative efficiency, spin, and gas supply in the chaotic accretion model
- Assess the predicted observables (spins, host-galaxy correlations) and whether they are testable with JWST
- Look for how the model distinguishes itself from massive-seed and super-Eddington scenarios

## 2. Halo Mass Function Constraints on Early Galaxy and AGN Populations in the JWST Era

- arXiv ID: `2610.08731`
- Authors: Elan Yogeswaren , Robert Schaefer
- Score: 8
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08731
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.08731
- Subjects: Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (astro-ph.CO) ; Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
- Reason: It directly addresses JWST-era massive galaxies and luminous AGN at high redshift through halo mass function constraints, relevant to early galaxy/AGN populations and cosmic-dawn structure formation.

Short digest:
The paper asks whether halo mass function predictions can consistently accommodate JWST massive galaxies and luminous AGN at high redshift. It develops a framework linking the linear power spectrum to halo abundance and shows that modest changes in host mass, baryonic efficiency, BH occupation, or HMF calibration strongly affect predicted abundances. This matters for interpreting JWST AGN and massive-galaxy candidates, including whether their host halos exist in sufficient numbers at cosmic dawn.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Look for quantitative comparisons to JWST AGN and massive-galaxy abundance measurements
- Check assumptions about BH occupation fraction, duty cycle, and baryonic conversion efficiency
- Examine how ST versus KST halo mass function choices change high-z predictions
- Look for implications for individual luminous AGN or LRD candidates at z>8

- Reason: This paper uses cosmological simulations of early black-hole seeding and merger-driven growth at z>=10, directly relevant to early SMBH growth and galaxy/AGN co-evolution, though it is simulation-focused rather than JWST observational.

Short digest:
The paper asks how AGN feedback prescriptions affect hierarchical black-hole assembly and merger rates at high redshift. It finds that strong feedback suppresses repeated mergers, while no-feedback models produce runaway merger-driven growth, creating distinct high-redshift merger populations. For my work, this informs theoretical expectations for early black-hole growth and the seeding/feedback degeneracies that JWST AGN and LRD observations may help constrain.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Check the predicted BH mass function and merger rates at z>=10 for comparison with JWST black-hole candidates
- Look for how feedback prescriptions affect BH occupation fractions and accretion-driven growth
- Check whether the simulations make any predictions relevant to LRD or compact AGN demographics

## 4. Radiation pressure powers quasar broad absorption line winds but fails to drive galaxy feedback

- arXiv ID: `2610.07624`
- Authors: Braden Seefeldt-Gail , Lluís Mas-Ribas , Norman Murray
- Score: 6
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07624
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.07624
- Subjects: Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
- Reason: This is a quasar broad-absorption-line wind and AGN feedback study using SDSS data; it directly addresses AGN physics and galaxy/AGN co-evolution, though not at high redshift or with JWST.

Short digest:
The paper asks whether radiation-pressure-driven quasar winds can provide the feedback needed to quench star formation in massive galaxies. Using 81,388 BAL troughs from SDSS-IV DR16, it detects line locking in 60-70% of troughs but finds the median kinetic luminosity is only 0.01% of bolometric luminosity, far below feedback requirements. This challenges a primary mechanism invoked in galaxy-formation models and is relevant to my interest in AGN feedback and galaxy/AGN co-evolution, though it is not a high-redshift or JWST study.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Look for the line-locking detection statistics and how they constrain radiation-pressure driving
- Check the kinetic-luminosity estimates and assumptions about outflow geometry and distances
- Assess whether the feedback conclusions are extrapolated to high-redshift quasar populations
- Look for any discussion of obscured or broad-line AGN relevant to LRD interpretations

## 5. The $\mathrm{[O\ III]}\lambda5007$ Equivalent Width as an Ionization-Parameter Diagnostic Across Cosmic Time

- arXiv ID: `2610.08427`
- Authors: Dian P. Triani , Lisa J. Kewley
- Score: 6
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08427
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.08427
- Subjects: Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
- Reason: This paper develops the [O III]λ5007 equivalent width as an ionization-parameter diagnostic for high-redshift galaxies, directly relevant to JWST emission-line studies and ISM conditions. It connects to my interest in SED fitting, emission lines, and high-redshift galaxy classification.

Short digest:
The paper asks whether [O III]λ5007 equivalent width can serve as an ionization-parameter diagnostic alternative to O32 across cosmic time. The main claim is that EWO3 is highly sensitive to ionization parameter and less pressure-dependent than O32 in the low-metallicity high-ionization regime, with calibrations favoring higher gas pressures at high redshift. It matters for my work because JWST high-redshift galaxies often lack the broad wavelength coverage needed for O32, so EWO3 could be a practical diagnostic for ISM conditions and AGN contamination in LRDs and compact sources.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Look for the EWO3 calibrations as functions of metallicity and gas pressure, and whether they are applicable to JWST wavelength coverage.
- Check whether the paper provides diagnostics that separate AGN from star-forming ionization in high-redshift sources.
- Look for comparisons with observed high-redshift galaxy samples and whether the pressure evolution is supported by JWST data.
- Assess whether the BPASS binary-inclusive models change the inferred ionization parameters for metal-poor galaxies.

## 6. Galaxy merger-driven signatures in massive black hole pair hosts across cosmic time

- arXiv ID: `2610.08709`
- Authors: Pranav Satheesh , Aklant K. Bhowmick , Laura Blecha
- Score: 6
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08709
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.08709
- Subjects: Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
- Reason: It addresses merger-driven black-hole accretion and MBH-pair hosts across cosmic time, connecting galaxy/AGN co-evolution and high-redshift BH growth, though it is simulation-based rather than JWST observational.

Short digest:
The paper asks how galaxy mergers enhance star formation and black-hole accretion in massive black-hole pair hosts. Using IllustrisTNG-50 and BRAHMA, it finds merger-driven sSFR enhancements persist to z~6 but sBHAR enhancements depend strongly on BH seeding and are absent in BRAHMA. This informs expectations for JWST-detected dual or accreting black holes at cosmic dawn and the role of seeding models in interpreting high-z AGN.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Look for predicted AGN fractions and sBHAR enhancements at z>4 relevant to JWST AGN samples
- Check how BH seeding prescriptions affect observable dual-AGN or pair-host signatures
- Examine whether the simulations make testable predictions for JWST compact red sources or LRDs

- Reason: This is a COSMOS2020 study of environment-dependent galaxy evolution out to z=4, relevant to high-redshift galaxy formation and survey science but not specifically JWST, LRDs, or AGN.

Short digest:
The paper investigates how stellar mass and star formation activity depend on cosmic-web topology from z=0.4 to z=4 using COSMOS2020. It finds environmental quenching at z<1, a transitional regime at 1<z<2, and a mild reversal toward enhanced star formation in clusters at z>2. This is relevant to my interest in high-redshift galaxy formation and survey-based environmental studies, though it lacks JWST data and AGN/LRD focus.

Full-text inspection checklist:
- Check the mass-completeness limits and how they affect the z>2 environmental trends
- Look for whether AGN contamination is addressed in the SFR or mass estimates
- Assess whether the cosmic-web classification could be applied to JWST deep fields like COSMOS-Web
- Check the significance of the claimed reversal of environmental trends at z>2
