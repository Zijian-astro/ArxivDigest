Title: Free-Free Radio Emission from Little Red Dots as a Probe of Ionized Gas
Authors: Takumi S. Tanaka , Kohei Inayoshi , Zu Yan , Tomokazu Kiyota , Yuichi Harikane , John D. Silverman
arXiv ID: 2609.20913
Score: 10
Reason: This directly addresses little red dots, their ionized gas, and black-hole accretion environments, which are central to my research on LRDs and early black-hole growth. It also proposes radio/mm observables that can constrain LRD physical conditions beyond JWST bands.
Short digest: The paper asks whether free-free radio emission can independently probe the ionized gas surrounding little red dots. It argues that free-free self-absorption produces a spectral turnover sensitive to electron column density and ionized-region size, with predicted ALMA/ngVLA observables for representative LRDs. This matters because it offers a new multiwavelength test of LRD models and helps connect JWST-selected red sources to their gas geometry and accretion physics.
Full-text inspection checklist: Look for the assumed LRD geometry, density profile, and ionization parameters used in the free-free predictions; Check how the two representative LRDs are chosen and whether they are local or high-redshift analogs; Examine the predicted flux levels and whether they are detectable with ALMA/ngVLA in realistic exposure times; Look for caveats about synchrotron contamination or AGN radio emission confusing the free-free interpretation

Reason: This paper directly studies JWST/NIRCam-selected ultra-red LRD-like galaxies in a z=4.3 protocluster, with implications for LRD classification, dust, AGN versus stellar emission, and environmental quenching.
Short digest: The paper asks what the nature is of 11 newly identified ultra-red galaxies in the SPT2349-56 protocluster at z=4.30. arXiv ID: 2609.20963
Score: 9
Reason: This is a JWST/JADES search for Pop III signatures using He II emitters, directly relevant to high-redshift galaxy spectroscopy, emission-line diagnostics, AGN screening, and resolved SED fitting. It connects to reionization-era galaxies and early stellar populations.
Short digest: The paper asks whether hybrid Pop III-Pop II signatures can be found in strong He II-emitting galaxies at z=3-7 in JADES. This matters because it demonstrates resolved SED fitting and UV line diagnostics for extreme high-redshift galaxies, with direct implications for distinguishing AGN, starbursts, and unusual stellar populations in JWST data.

arXiv ID: 2609.21939
Score: 7
Reason: This paper provides stellar yields for rapidly rotating Pop III stars specifically for interpreting JWST high-redshift nitrogen-rich galaxies like GN-z11, CEERS-1019, and GS 3073, directly relevant to my work on high-redshift galaxy formation and JWST spectroscopy.
Short digest: The central question is what CNO yields rapidly rotating Pop III stars produce for the high-redshift universe. The authors find that high nitrogen yields do not necessarily imply high N/O ratios because PISN models also eject large oxygen masses, and they provide a CNO-focused prompt-enrichment grid. This matters for my work because it offers a physical framework to interpret nitrogen-rich JWST sources and their stellar populations.
