Title: On the quenching of LRD X-ray emission by both Compton-thick gas and high accretion rates
Authors: Albert Sneppen , Darach Watson , James H. Matthews , Stuart A. Sim
arXiv ID: 2605.15263
Score: 10
Reason: Directly addresses LRDs, X-ray non-detection, and uses radiative transfer to model their SEDs, which is central to your interests in JWST high-z AGN.
Short digest: Why are LRDs X-ray undetected? The paper finds that both Compton-thick gas (NH~10^25 cm^-2) and intrinsically weak X-ray emission (k_bol,X>30) are needed to suppress X-rays. This matters because it constrains the physical nature of LRDs and their accretion mode.
Full-text inspection checklist: Check the predicted X-ray attenuation vs. Balmer-break strength and metallicity.; Look for the derived NH column densities and how they compare to optical spectral constraints.; Examine the assumed intrinsic X-ray SEDs and the k_bol,X values used.

Authors: Shuaiyi Li , Zheng Cai , Yunjing Wu , Fujiang Yu , Xiaojing Lin , Jingyang Men , Xiaoyang Wei
arXiv ID: 2605.15712
Score: 8
Reason: Directly studies a z=2.96 protocluster using JWST/NIRCam slitless spectroscopy and multi-wavelength data, with analysis of stellar masses, SFRs, and environmental effects relevant to high-redshift galaxy assembly.
Short digest: Does a z=2.96 protocluster show accelerated galaxy assembly? The paper finds a +0.2 dex stellar mass excess and SFR enhancement in low-mass members, but no quenching yet.
